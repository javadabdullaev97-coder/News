Tim Cook has only weeks left as Apple's chief executive: starting
September 1, 2026, the company will be led by John Ternus, Apple's senior
vice president of hardware engineering. Apple Newsroom
[announced](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/04/tim-cook-to-become-apple-executive-chairman-john-ternus-to-become-apple-ceo/)
the move on April 20, 2026.

Cook has run the company since 2011, when he succeeded Steve Jobs. After
the handover, he will become executive chairman of the board. In his new
role, Cook will continue working on select areas of Apple, including
engagement with policymakers in various countries, according to the
[press release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/04/tim-cook-to-become-apple-executive-chairman-john-ternus-to-become-apple-ceo/).
Apple's board of directors approved the decision unanimously.

Ternus has worked at Apple since 2001 — 25 years. During that time, he
oversaw development of the iPad, AirPods, and several generations of the
iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch. Board Chairman Arthur Levinson called Cook's
tenure "unprecedented" in the press release. According to Levinson, it
turned Apple into "the best company in the world" — the company's own
words.

Cook gave an interview to CBS News that aired August 14. It was recorded
the day before in Houston at the opening of an Apple manufacturing
center — in the conversation, Cook described how he views his own legacy
as the company's chief executive: "Legacy is described by others, not by
yourself. So I hope people will say I was a good and decent man," Cook
[said](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/14/apple-ceo-tim-cook-reflects-on-legacy-apple/).

Ternus himself said in Apple's press release that he is "deeply grateful
for the opportunity to carry Apple's mission forward" — as the company
quoted him.
