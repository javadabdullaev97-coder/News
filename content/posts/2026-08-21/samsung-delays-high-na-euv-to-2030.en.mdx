Samsung Electronics has delayed its transition to next-generation High-NA EUV lithography machines until the roughly 1-nanometer process node — expected around 2030, [DigiTimes reported](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260820VL217/samsung-high-na-euv-production-1nm-high-na.html) on August 20.

High-NA EUV is an advanced version of the machines that use ultraviolet light to print a chip's circuit pattern onto a silicon wafer — a key step in manufacturing modern processors. The smaller the process node, the more compact and energy-efficient the resulting chips are.

Samsung had previously planned to introduce such machines as early as the 2-nanometer and 1.4-nanometer nodes but pushed them to a later node, [DigiTimes wrote](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260813PD213/samsung-high-na-euv-production-1nm-2030.html). For the 2nm and 1.4nm nodes, the company will keep using conventional EUV machines and multi-patterning — printing a single circuit pattern in several passes instead of one, [SamMobile wrote](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

The delay was announced by Park Chan-min, a lead process development specialist at Samsung's Semiconductor R&D Center. He spoke at the Next-Generation Lithography + Patterning (NGL 2026) conference, [SamMobile reported](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

## Why Samsung Is in No Hurry

A single High-NA EUV machine costs $350 million to $400 million — significantly more than conventional EUV lithography machines, [SamMobile wrote](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

Beyond the price, the supporting ecosystem for the new equipment isn't ready yet: masks, protective pellicle films and specialized materials require joint development with suppliers, [SamMobile reported](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

In High-NA EUV machines, numerical aperture — a parameter that determines the sharpness and precision of circuit printing — has been increased from 0.33 to 0.55. That makes it possible to print thinner lines in a single pass instead of several, [SamMobile explains](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

## What Competitors Are Doing

Intel already uses High-NA EUV for some process layers in manufacturing 2-nanometer-class chips, [SamMobile reported](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/).

TSMC uses such machines only for research and development and plans commercial adoption by 2029-2030, [SamMobile wrote](https://www.sammobile.com/news/samsung-1nm-chip-push-adoption-most-advanced-machines/). That's roughly the same timeline as Samsung's.
