Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan on August 15 in Kadamjai approved the composition of a working group on exchanging the villages of Chon-Gara and Tash-Dobo, according to reports cited by several outlets; there is no official confirmation as of publication.

According to media reports, the joint working commission was headed on the Kyrgyz side by First Deputy Minister of Labor, Social Security and Migration Kamchybek Dosmatov. On the Uzbek side, co-chair duties went to First Deputy Minister of Internal Affairs of Uzbekistan Botir Kudratkhodjaev. The group is reported to include representatives of relevant ministries, agencies and local government bodies of both countries.

According to media reports, the working group will handle the citizenship of residents in the territories being exchanged, their resettlement, and the transfer of state property. Its tasks will also include registering citizens' property rights, land use, and social protection of the population.

## Background

The exchange of the villages of Chon-Gara and Tash-Dobo is part of a broader package of border agreements. On July 30 in Cholpon-Ata, Presidents Shavkat Mirziyoyev and Sadyr Japarov [signed](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9472) the Treaty on Sections of the Uzbek-Kyrgyz State Border. The package also included the Treaty on Allied Relations and other bilateral documents.

The joint working group had already begun registering village residents on August 3 — as LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/04/uz-kg-village-transfer-chongara-tashdobo) at the time, about 2,500 residents, most of them ethnic Kyrgyz, were affected, and they would receive Kyrgyz citizenship once registration was complete. Under the same package of agreements, Kyrgyzstan had earlier transferred a plot to Uzbekistan for the construction of a road between the villages of Say and Tayan — Kommersant [reported](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8763096) this detail, citing Askat Alagozov, press secretary to the Kyrgyz president. According to media reports, on August 15 the working group's composition was expanded and formalized within the same set of agencies.

The exact number of residents, the area of land being transferred and received, and the procedure for the agreements taking effect have not been officially disclosed by either side.

A request on these questions has been sent to the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Uzbekistan; the article will be updated once a response is received.
