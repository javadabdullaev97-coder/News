Houthi forces launched missile and drone strikes on August 6 against government troop positions in Yemen's Marib and Hadramaut provinces — according to [AFP](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864421), at least 58 people were killed (as of the morning of August 7).

## Strikes on Yemen and Saudi Arabia

The Houthis struck with ballistic missiles and drones positions of government forces loyal to Yemen's internationally recognized Presidential Leadership Council. Residential blocks as well as weapons and ammunition depots were hit, [AFP reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864421). A spokesman for the Houthi military command said the attack was a response to an alleged buildup of Yemeni military forces backed by Saudi Arabia.

Separately, in the Najran region in southwestern Saudi Arabia, shelling injured [11 people](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864422). Among them were seven Saudi nationals, including a woman and a 4-year-old child with second-degree burns, as well as two Egyptian nationals, a Yemeni national and a Pakistani national. Turki al-Malki, official spokesman for the coalition to restore legitimacy in Yemen, accused the Houthis of violating international humanitarian law.

## Intelligence reports preparations for new attacks

Saudi Arabia said it had detected plans by the Houthis and "Iran-backed formations" — including Iraqi armed groups and forces linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps — to carry out coordinated strikes on civilian sites, energy infrastructure, airports and ports across the kingdom, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107961), citing an Al Arabiya source familiar with the intelligence. According to the source, movements of missiles and drones have been recorded, indicating preparations for possible simultaneous attacks. The kingdom said it would take "all necessary measures" in response to any aggression.

## Riyadh forms maritime coalition

Amid the escalation, Saudi Arabia announced on August 6 the appointment of a commander for a multilateral maritime defense coalition. The coalition is responsible for the security of shipping in the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden and the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait. The post was taken, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/saudi-arabia-names-commander-for-maritime-defence-coalition), by Rear Admiral Abdullah bin Salem al-Shehri (Interfax refers to him as a major general). He will head the joint command, coordination among member states and planning of operations to protect maritime corridors.

Nine states signed the declaration establishing the coalition on July 30: Kuwait, Somalia, Qatar, Turkey, Pakistan, Jordan, Egypt, Sudan and Djibouti, [Interfax reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107858). According to Al Jazeera, the number of founding member states has reached 14 — in addition to those listed, the list also includes Saudi Arabia itself, Yemen, Bahrain, Bangladesh and the Comoros. A meeting of military planners from member states is scheduled for August 12–13 to complete formal procedures and prepare for the signing of a joint declaration.

The initiative was created in response to the escalating conflict between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis, which has included attacks on tankers in the region.
