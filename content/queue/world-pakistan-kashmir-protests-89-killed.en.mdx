The death toll from protests in Pakistan-administered Azad Kashmir has reached 89 since unrest began in June, according to data as of August 9, the Joint Awami Action Committee (JAAC) said.

## What the protests are about

Demonstrators in Azad Kashmir are demanding regional autonomy and the withdrawal of federal troops, [Kommersant reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8875657), citing JAAC. The protests are rooted in the region's longstanding political disenfranchisement and high unemployment: Azad Kashmir formally has its own government, but key decisions are made in Islamabad.

Protests are led by JAAC, a coalition that Pakistani authorities declared a banned organization in early summer, accusing it of "involvement in terrorism." The decision followed the committee's call for supporters to join a protest march.

## Timeline and casualties

Unrest in the region has continued since June. According to JAAC, more than 40 protesters have died since late July, when elections to the local parliament began in Azad Kashmir. The organization also reports numerous people with severe injuries.

The death toll may still change: the figure of 89 comes from the protest committee itself, not from Pakistani authorities.

## Government response

Pakistani authorities responded to the protests with a curfew, an internet shutdown in the region, and restrictions on journalists' work. Islamabad accused protesters of acting "in India's interests."

The Financial Times writes that the wave of violence in the region has become a test for Pakistan's military rulers.
