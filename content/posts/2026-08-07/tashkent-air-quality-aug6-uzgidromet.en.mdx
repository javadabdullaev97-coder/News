Uzgidromet recorded PM10 and PM2.5 concentrations in Tashkent's ambient air within Uzbekistan's maximum permissible concentration (MPC) limits at 6 p.m. on August 6, despite reports of a "yellow" reading on international air quality indexes.

## What the Automated Stations Recorded

According to a [Uzgidromet bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21893) published on the evening of August 6, automated monitoring stations recorded a concentration of fine particulate matter PM10 at 60 µg/m³ in Tashkent's ambient air as of 6 p.m. The maximum permissible concentration (MPC) for this indicator is 300 µg/m³. The concentration of the smaller PM2.5 particles was 17 µg/m³, against an MPC of 60 µg/m³. The agency stated explicitly that neither indicator exceeded the MPC.

Uzgidromet's full official name is the Hydrometeorological Service Agency under the National Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan for Ecology and Climate Change. The agency publishes air quality bulletins from Tashkent's automated monitoring stations on its Telegram channel several times a day.

## Two Standards, Two Different Scales

Uzbekistan assesses air quality using a system of maximum permissible concentrations (MPC): the threshold for PM2.5 is set at 60 µg/m³ per day, and for PM10 at 300 µg/m³. These values are notably more lenient than the current [World Health Organization guidelines](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034433), revised in 2021. The WHO recommends not exceeding 15 µg/m³ of PM2.5 per day and 5 µg/m³ as an annual average.

The 17 µg/m³ of PM2.5 recorded by Uzgidromet is 13% above the WHO's daily reference level and more than three times above its annual standard. At the same time, the level remains more than three times below Uzbekistan's current MPC. International air quality indexes (AQI), which many smartphone apps rely on, are calculated on scales close to WHO guidelines rather than Uzbekistan's MPC. As a result, the same level of air pollution can simultaneously fall within the norm under Uzbekistan's regulations and appear notably higher on the stricter international scale. That explains the discrepancy between the official bulletin and the readings that circulated in messaging apps that same evening.
