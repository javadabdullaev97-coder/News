The death toll from two earthquakes that struck Venezuela on June 24 has passed 6,100 — National Assembly President Jorge Rodríguez [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/death-toll-from-venezuela-earthquakes-passes-6000) 6,125 deaths as of August 3.

## Rescue operation: over 6,400 pulled from rubble

Two earthquakes measuring magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 [struck](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/death-toll-from-venezuela-earthquakes-passes-6000) on June 24, several dozen seconds apart. The coastal state of La Guaira and the capital, Caracas, were hit hardest.

According to Rodríguez, rescuers [pulled](https://www.rbc.ru/rbcfreenews/6a71322c9a7947a71720e5bb) 6,462 people from the rubble, and about 61,000 people received medical and other assistance. A total of 190 buildings were destroyed and another 856 damaged — figures also [confirmed](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8861867) by Kommersant. By August 4, 16.5% of the rubble from collapsed structures had been cleared.

The World Bank [estimated](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/death-toll-from-venezuela-earthquakes-passes-6000) direct physical damage from the earthquakes at $19.6 billion.

## United States resumes deportation flights to Venezuela

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), part of the Department of Homeland Security, [conducted](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/03/world/americas/venezuela-us-deportation-flights-resume.html) the first deportation flight since the earthquakes on August 3 — a plane carrying 147 Venezuelan citizens landed in the country. The agency confirmed the operation but declined to comment on plans for future flights, citing operational security considerations.

The flights had been suspended after a plane carrying 146 deportees arrived in Venezuela on June 24, just hours before the earthquakes. The deportees were housed in a building in La Guaira. The building collapsed during the tremors, killing dozens of deportees under the rubble. Neither U.S. nor Venezuelan authorities have published a complete passenger list for that flight, an official death toll among them, or a list of survivors.

According to the NYT, human rights advocates [criticized](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/03/world/americas/venezuela-us-deportation-flights-resume.html) the resumption of flights as premature, saying authorities had not clarified safety protocols following the June 24 disaster.
