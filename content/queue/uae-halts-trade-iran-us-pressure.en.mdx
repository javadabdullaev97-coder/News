The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the United Arab Emirates suspended all trade and financial operations with Iran on the evening of August 19, citing "escalations that undermine regional and international peace and security," [Bloomberg reports](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/iran-risks-losing-key-economic-150832336.html).

The UAE is one of Iran's largest regional trading partners. At the same time, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington intends to apply "unprecedented economic pressure" on Tehran, [Bloomberg reports](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/iran-risks-losing-key-economic-150832336.html).

According to the agency, President Donald Trump's administration is shifting its focus "from military strikes to maximum financial pressure on the oil-rich country." According to an expert consulted by Bloomberg, the UAE's suspension of trade could inflict greater damage on Iran than US and European Union sanctions.

Separately, a NATO official said on August 19 that the alliance is "prepared to respond to any threat and will always do what is necessary to defend all allies," [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/nato-prepared-to-defend-allies-amid-report-of-iranian-strike-plans).

The statement came after the Financial Times reported that Iran had considered strikes on US military facilities in Europe in the event of further escalation of the war. The alliance official said NATO's air defense systems intercepted ballistic missiles fired from Iran toward Turkey several times in 2026.
