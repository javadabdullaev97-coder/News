Apple will move all apps in the European Union to a single set of commercial terms with commissions ranging from 5% to 26%, replacing several options currently in effect, starting October 1, 2026, [the company announced](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/).

Developers who sell digital goods and services in iOS and iPadOS apps in the European Union will move to the new terms. The terms apply to everyone starting October 1, 2026, and can be signed voluntarily before that date, according to [a document for developers](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/) on Apple's website.

## New Commission Structure

The standard commission for purchases through Apple's In-App Purchase payment system will be 26%. For payments through a third-party payment system within the app, the rate drops to 20%. If a user follows a link to pay outside the app, the commission is 15%. These figures are listed in [the terms for developers in the EU](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/). Participants in reduced-fee programs, including the Small Business Program, pay less: 15% for purchases through Apple In-App Purchase, 10% for alternative payments and 10% for redirects.

For apps distributed outside the App Store — through alternative app marketplaces or web distribution — Apple is introducing a new Core Technology Commission. It will amount to 5% of sales of digital goods and services within such apps, [the company said](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gmws0jgp).

## What Is Being Removed From the Terms

Apple is eliminating two previous fees — the Initial Acquisition Fee for acquiring a new user and the Store Services Fee for App Store services. This is stated in [the document for developers](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/). The Core Technology Fee is also changing — the previous charge for each app installation above a certain threshold. It is being replaced by a sales-based commission — the same 5% Core Technology Commission.

## Context: Dispute With the European Commission

Apple said the new terms are the result of "close engagement with the European Commission." According to the company, they resolve disagreements with the regulator over commercial terms and alternative app distribution in the European Union. This is described in [Apple's press release](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/).

Since 2024, Apple has been changing iOS, iPadOS, Safari and the App Store in the European Union to comply with the Digital Markets Act (DMA). This is a European law that requires large technology platforms to open their ecosystems to third-party services and developers. Apple [notes this](https://developer.apple.com/support/apps-in-the-eu/) in its description of the EU terms.

## Who This Affects and When It Takes Effect

Developers can sign the new terms starting now. The new rates themselves will take effect on October 1, 2026, for everyone distributing apps in the European Union, [Apple clarified](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/).
