Attorneys general and governors of 25 US states sued on August 3 over tariffs on goods from 60 trading partners, imposed by the Donald Trump administration under the guise of combating forced labor.

## What the States Are Challenging

The lawsuit was filed with the [US Court of International Trade](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-governor-hochul-announce-lawsuit-against-trump) — signatories include New York, California, Arizona, Oregon, Colorado, Illinois, Michigan, Washington and about a dozen and a half other states, as well as the governors of Kentucky and Pennsylvania.

The tariffs took effect in late July — according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/trump-administration-sued-by-25-states-over-new-tariffs-on-trading-partners), on July 24; according to the [New York Attorney General's press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-governor-hochul-announce-lawsuit-against-trump), on July 23. They were imposed by the Office of the US Trade Representative (USTR) following an investigation that began in March 2026 and covered partners accounting for [99.4% of US imports](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/july/fact-sheet-ustr-section-301-action-response-failure-60-economies-ban-imports-produced-forced-labor). The rate is 10% for partners that partially restrict imports of goods made with forced labor, and 12.5% for the rest — among the 60 partners covered.

## The "Cover" Argument

The plaintiffs cite a February 20, 2026 US Supreme Court ruling: the court [held](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/trump-administration-sued-by-25-states-over-new-tariffs-on-trading-partners) that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not give the president the authority to impose tariffs unilaterally — that ruling struck down Trump's earlier "Liberation Day" tariffs.

The states argue that the new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 are an attempt to recreate the same restrictions under a different pretext, rather than a genuine measure against forced labor.

New York Attorney General Letitia James said: "After losing at the Supreme Court, the administration is once again trying to illegally raise taxes on families and businesses with another round of tariffs" — [Al Jazeera quotes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/trump-administration-sued-by-25-states-over-new-tariffs-on-trading-partners).

New York Governor Kathy Hochul added in the [press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-and-governor-hochul-announce-lawsuit-against-trump) that the tariffs are a "tax on working families" that raises prices on groceries, essential goods and building materials.

## The Administration's Position

White House spokesman Kush Desai responded that a trading partner's failure to enact and actually enforce a ban on imports of goods made with forced labor "is unreasonable and burdens US trade" — [the statement is cited by Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/trump-administration-sued-by-25-states-over-new-tariffs-on-trading-partners).

## What's Next

The states are asking the court to declare the tariffs unlawful, block their enforcement and refund tariff fees already paid. No hearing date has been set yet.
