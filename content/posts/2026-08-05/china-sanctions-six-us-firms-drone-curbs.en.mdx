China's Ministry of Commerce on August 5 imposed countersanctions on six American organizations and tightened export controls on drones and related technologies bound for the United States.

## Who China Added to the Sanctions List

Under [Ministry of Commerce Order No. 2 of 2026](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-05/10672370.shtml), the countersanctions list includes six organizations: Applied DNA Sciences (Stony Brook, New York), Stratum Reservoir (Houston, Texas), Altana Technologies (Brooklyn, New York), the Responsible Business Alliance (Alexandria, Virginia), Verite Group (Sterling, Virginia) and the human rights organization Human Rights in China (New York). The Ministry of Commerce accused them of facilitating US sanctions against Chinese companies over allegations of forced labor in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region.

The decision is based on China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law (Articles 3, 4, 6, 9, 10 and 15) and its implementing regulations. Organizations and individuals in China are barred from conducting transactions or cooperating with the listed entities.

In a separate [Order No. 3](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2026/art_5536d924b28442f3ad90db8d764bab0d.html), published the same day, the Ministry of Commerce added a seventh entity to the countersanctions list — Compliance Testing LLC of Mesa, Arizona. The ministry said the company assisted the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in measures against Chinese manufacturers.

## Restrictions on Drone Exports to the US

At the same time, China's Ministry of Commerce [announced](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-05/10672368.shtml) tighter controls on exports of drones, their key components and related technologies included on China's dual-use goods list. Shipments of such products to the US now undergo case-by-case review — simplified licensing procedures no longer apply to them. The measure took effect on August 5.

The ministry justified the decision by citing the need to protect national security and the country's interests, as well as compliance with international non-proliferation obligations — referencing China's Export Control Law and the Regulations on the Export Control of Dual-Use Items.

## What Preceded the Countermeasures

Beijing described the countersanctions as a response to Washington's actions. In recent weeks, the US Federal Communications Commission expanded its so-called Covered List — a list of equipment barred from certification in the US — to include foreign power inverters and advanced robotics products, including humanoid robots. Restrictions hit, among others, Chinese manufacturers AgiBot, Unitree Robotics, UBTech and Leju Robotics. China accounted for about a third of US power inverter imports in 2024, and Chinese suppliers provided more than 80% of the over 13,000 robots shipped worldwide in 2025.

In parallel, according to foreign media reports, the Trump administration is preparing a ban on imports of new models of Chinese data center components — primarily optical transceivers, which transmit data over fiber-optic lines inside server facilities. The FCC is drafting the measure, with publication possible by the end of the year, but the decision has not been finalized and could be changed or withdrawn.

## Beijing's Position

China's Ministry of Commerce characterized the measures as generally restrained but warned that if Washington continues imposing new restrictions on China, Beijing will respond with further countermeasures.

The state-run People's Daily called Washington's actions unilateral pressure that distorts market economy rules, and said US measures under the pretext of "non-discrimination" or "national security" are in fact discriminatory pressure on Chinese companies.
