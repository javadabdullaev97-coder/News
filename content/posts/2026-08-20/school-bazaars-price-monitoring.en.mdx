Uzbekistan's Competition and Consumer Rights Protection Committee this week began monitoring prices at seasonal school bazaars across the country ahead of the start of the academic year on September 1. The agency [urged](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12600) sellers not to unreasonably inflate prices on textbooks, school uniforms, stationery and other school supplies.

Seasonal "school bazaars" have opened at general education schools, farmers' markets and shopping complexes, [the committee said in a post](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12600). Parents and students go there for textbooks, notebooks, pens and school uniforms ahead of the academic year, which in Uzbekistan traditionally starts on September 1.

According to the committee, the monitoring is meant to protect consumer rights, support competition and ensure the availability of quality goods. The agency recommended that parents and students pay attention to product quality, manufacturer and price when making purchases. Entrepreneurs trading at school bazaars were reminded of their obligation to sell quality products at justified prices. They must also give buyers complete information about goods and respect consumer rights.

If a price at a school bazaar appears inflated without explanation, complaints [can be filed](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12600) with the Competition Committee's single hotline — 1159.

The agency did not name specific fines for violations, citing general requirements of legislation on competition and consumer rights protection. The committee did not specify when the monitoring will end: seasonal school bazaars will continue operating until the start of the academic year on September 1.
