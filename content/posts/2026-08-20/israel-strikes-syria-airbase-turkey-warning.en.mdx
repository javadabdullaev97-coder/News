Israel carried out eight strikes overnight into August 18 on the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Syria's Idlib province, saying it had prevented the deployment of Turkish troops there. The strikes hit the base's runway and storage buildings; there were no casualties, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/eight-air-strikes-hit-airbase-in-syrias-idlib) reports.

A statement from the office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel and Syria had "agreed to a status quo in security matters." However, Damascus was "on the verge of breaching" that understanding by allowing Turkish troops to deploy at the base. According to the office, Israel had "repeatedly warned Syria" that such a deployment would pose a threat to Israel's security, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/nx-s1-5937400/israel-strikes-syria) reports.

## Reaction from Damascus and Ankara

Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani said the strikes had no security justification. He called them "an unjustified provocation, a violation of Syria's sovereignty, and a direct threat to regional and global stability," [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/nx-s1-5937400/israel-strikes-syria) reports. He said Damascus would continue "our diplomatic path to dissuade Israel from this policy," [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/eight-air-strikes-hit-airbase-in-syrias-idlib) reports.

Turkey's Directorate of Communications called the accusations against Ankara "frivolous." It said they aim to "legitimise Israel's unlawful air strikes targeting the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Syria," [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/eight-air-strikes-hit-airbase-in-syrias-idlib) reports. Turkey's Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned the strike and called on the international community "to take a more decisive stance to end Israel's escalating aggression against Syria," [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/nx-s1-5937400/israel-strikes-syria) reports.

## International reaction

US Ambassador to Turkey and special envoy for Syria Tom Barrack said Washington was "deeply concerned" about the strikes. He called them "an unnecessary escalation that does not advance regional stability," [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/nx-s1-5937400/israel-strikes-syria) reports. Qatar, Egypt and Saudi Arabia also condemned the strikes, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/19/nx-s1-5937400/israel-strikes-syria) reports.

This was Israel's first strike on Syrian government assets since March 2026, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/eight-air-strikes-hit-airbase-in-syrias-idlib) notes. Israel has carried out hundreds of air strikes on Syrian territory since the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime in December 2024, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/eight-air-strikes-hit-airbase-in-syrias-idlib) reports.
