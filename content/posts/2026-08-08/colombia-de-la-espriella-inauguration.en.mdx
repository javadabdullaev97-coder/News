Right-wing politician Abelardo de la Espriella was sworn in as president of Colombia in Cali on August 7, succeeding leftist Gustavo Petro. That same day, the United States announced [$1 billion in aid](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) to fight drug trafficking.

## A Ceremony Outside the Capital

The inauguration [took place in an auditorium at Santiago de Cali University](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups), rather than in Bogotá, where presidents traditionally take the oath before Congress. According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924775/colombias-new-president-vows-to-remake-the-country-and-challenge-its-fragile-peace), 11,000 troops secured the ceremony, and the city banned drone flights and private vehicle traffic.

De la Espriella, 48, is a lawyer and businessman known by the nickname El Tigre ("The Tiger"). He won the June 21 runoff against leftist candidate Iván Cepeda by a margin of roughly [250,000 votes — about one percentage point](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924775/colombias-new-president-vows-to-remake-the-country-and-challenge-its-fragile-peace). Outgoing President Petro disputed the election results without providing evidence, NPR notes.

## "The Option of Dialogue Is Completely Exhausted"

In his inaugural address, de la Espriella [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) he would "relentlessly defeat narco-terrorism," adding: "The option of dialogue is completely exhausted." He called control over the country's territory one of his government's fundamental priorities — a phrase [cited by NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924775/colombias-new-president-vows-to-remake-the-country-and-challenge-its-fragile-peace).

Announced measures include [resuming aerial spraying and drone operations against coca plantations](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924775/colombias-new-president-vows-to-remake-the-country-and-challenge-its-fragile-peace), ending peace talks with armed groups, and building large new prisons. The new administration also [plans to cut government spending by up to 40%](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) and revive the oil and gas industry, including hydraulic fracturing projects.

Human rights advocates cited by [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/colombias-new-president-reflects-latin-americas-right-wing-shift) fear the hardline approach will lead to extrajudicial killings and hit rural Indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities hardest — echoing past security crackdowns in the country.

## $1 Billion From the US and a New Alliance

The US State Department announced a plan [to provide $1 billion in aid](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) as part of a security package for the new administration; the decision requires Congressional approval, and details of how the funds will be allocated have not yet been disclosed. Colombia simultaneously announced it would join the ["Shield of the Americas"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) — a military alliance of right-wing governments in the region against drug trafficking, established by the Trump administration.

Trump backed de la Espriella even before the election. At the inauguration, the American delegation [was led by Acting US Attorney General Todd Blanche](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/colombias-new-president-reflects-latin-americas-right-wing-shift), who called the moment a "revival of a great partnership." Guests at the ceremony included Argentine President Javier Milei, Ecuadorian President Daniel Noboa, Chilean President José Antonio Kast, Spain's King Felipe VI, and FIFA President Gianni Infantino.

According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups), the new president also plans to restore diplomatic relations with Israel and sever them with Cuba and Nicaragua. Protests against the new course took place in several cities across the country.

## Context: A Right-Wing Shift in the Region

The change of power in Colombia continues a broader right-wing shift in Latin America: according to analysts cited by [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/colombias-new-president-reflects-latin-americas-right-wing-shift), roughly half a dozen conservative leaders have recently come to power in the region, and no significant disagreements are expected between their administrations and Washington.

[Petro became Colombia's first leftist president in 2022](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/in-historic-shift-gustavo-petro-sworn-in-as-colombias-first-leftist-president) and repeatedly clashed with the Trump administration during his term — notably over operations against drug traffickers in the Caribbean and over the situation in Gaza.
