Uzbekistan's president on August 3 signed a resolution "On Measures to Improve the Efficiency of Hazardous Waste Management," under which the recycling rate for radioactive and chemically hazardous waste of hazard classes I–IV must rise to 20%, with no deadline specified.

## What the Resolution Establishes

The document sets a goal of [raising to 20 percent](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154) the recycling rate for radioactive waste generated by the mining, metallurgical and chemical industries, the oil and gas and power sectors, and other manufacturing enterprises, as well as for chemically hazardous waste classified under hazard classes I–IV. The resolution does not specify the current recycling level from which this figure is meant to rise — the document contains no precise data on the current share of such waste being recycled. Nor does the resolution set a specific deadline for reaching the 20% target: the figure is not tied to a date anywhere in the document.

The resolution took effect on August 4, the day after it was signed, [according to](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154) the document's record on lex.uz.

## How Waste Will Be Tracked

Starting October 1, 2026, organizations that generate hazardous waste must compile annual statistical reports, [the resolution states](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154). The document also introduces quarterly reporting for them to the territorial departments of the relevant agency, due by the 20th of the month following the reporting period. The indicators for such reporting are drawn from annual waste management plans, which organizations are required to develop only starting January 1, 2027.

By January 1, 2027, authorities plan to create an information system with a digital passport for hazardous waste, which should let agencies monitor the movement of waste "from the point of generation to the place of disposal" in real time and integrate data with the Unified Reporting System. From the same date, January 1, 2027, organizations are required to develop annual waste management plans jointly with the relevant agency — with targets for reducing waste generation volumes, increasing the share of waste recycled, and transferring waste to specialized organizations.

## Who Is Responsible for Implementation

The [Committee for Industrial, Radiation and Nuclear Safety under the Cabinet of Ministers](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154) has been designated the authorized body in the field. The operator is the Agency for Waste Management and Circular Economy Development under the National Committee for Ecology and Climate Change of the Republic of Uzbekistan — investment projects of enterprises that accumulate hazardous waste will now be implemented jointly with this agency.

A Directorate for Hazardous Waste Management is being established under the agency — a state institution operating on off-budget financing. The resolution sets a [40 percent bonus](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154) on directorate employees' monthly salaries for work in hazardous conditions. The Republican Center for Radioactive Waste Storage, previously under the Institute of Nuclear Physics of the Academy of Sciences, is also being transferred into the agency's system — a fact also reported, citing the resolution, by the [poytaxt_uz Telegram channel](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51280).

Decommissioned special-purpose facilities — including former uranium mines and tailings storage sites — are being transferred free of charge onto the agency's balance sheet, along with funding for their environmental remediation. Land plots under such facilities are exempt from land tax, according to the resolution. Oversight of compliance with the indicators set out in the annual plans has been assigned to the State Inspectorate for Environmental Control.

## What's Next

Under the [resolution](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8372154), an inventory of decommissioned special-purpose facilities must be completed within two months, and the structure of the new directorate must be developed within one month. The requirements for annual waste management plans and the creation of a digital passport take effect January 1, 2027 — from the same date, organizations' quarterly reporting will be based on the indicators set out in these plans. The document does not specify a deadline for reaching the 20% recycling target.
