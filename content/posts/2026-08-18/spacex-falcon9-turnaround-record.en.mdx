SpaceX launched two reusable Falcon 9 rockets 38 minutes apart on the evening of August 15, 2026 — from Cape Canaveral in Florida and Vandenberg Space Force Base in California.

The first rocket lifted off from Cape Canaveral's SLC-40 launch complex at 9:12 p.m. Eastern time (01:12 UTC on August 16), [Spaceflight Now reported](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/15/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-satellite-replenishment-mission-for-globalstar-from-cape-canaveral/). It placed eight Globalstar communications satellites into orbit. Its first stage, booster B1090, flew for the fourteenth time.

Thirty-eight minutes later, at 9:50 p.m. Eastern time (01:50 UTC), a second Falcon 9 lifted off from SLC-4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base, [the outlet wrote](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/15/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-satellite-replenishment-mission-for-globalstar-from-cape-canaveral/). It carried a payload for the U.S. Space Force under the USSF-366 program. The service did not disclose the payload's contents before launch. A spokesperson for the Space Force's Space Systems Command told the outlet the mission was carried out "under a previously awarded contract." The contract falls under the National Security Space Launch, Phase 3, Lane 1 program — how the Pentagon procures launches for reconnaissance and military satellites. The second mission's booster, B1088, marked Falcon 9's 650th landing in the company's history.

Spaceflight Now called the interval between the two launches a record for SpaceX — "a record 38.5 minutes between launches," [the outlet's coverage said](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/15/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-satellite-replenishment-mission-for-globalstar-from-cape-canaveral/). SpaceX itself did not issue a separate statement about the record: the description came from the trade publication, which covered both launches live and tracks the company's launch statistics.

The Globalstar launch was the 52nd orbital liftoff from Florida's Space Coast in 2026, [Spaceflight Now noted](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/15/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-satellite-replenishment-mission-for-globalstar-from-cape-canaveral/).

SpaceX's previous record for the interval between launches was about 65 minutes, [Space.com reported](https://www.space.com/spacex-back-to-back-rocket-launches-landings-after-booster-touchdown-failure) in 2024.
