Apple on August 13 proposed to the court that it be allowed to charge up to 15% on purchases iPhone users make through links that bypass the App Store — the company's app marketplace. The proposal was submitted as part of the long-running [Epic Games v. Apple](https://9to5mac.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/Epic_Games_Inc_v_Apple_Inc__candce-20-05640__1708.0.pdf) case in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California.

This concerns so-called external purchase links — buttons inside an app. They direct users to a developer's website to pay for a subscription or purchase, bypassing Apple's built-in payment system. Developers won the right to include such links under a court ruling in the case, which Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers has been overseeing since 2020. The dispute began with a lawsuit filed by Epic Games (case No. 4:20-cv-05640) accusing Apple of monopolistic practices in the App Store.

In the filing, Apple proposes four commission tiers. For most apps — 15%. For subscription renewals and participants in Apple's partner programs — the Video Partner Program, News Partner Program and Mini Apps Partner Program — 10%. For developers in the Small Business Program, which covers companies with annual revenue under $1 million — 5%.

In the filing, Apple cites an "expert analysis" of the App Store market to justify the proposed rates. According to that analysis, "a significant number of U.S. developers, who account for the bulk of App Store revenue, will be able to operate profitably through off-store links at the proposed rates."

The proposed rates are not a ruling but Apple's position in the dispute: Judge Gonzalez Rogers must still review and either approve or reject them. In parallel, Apple is preparing an appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court. According to court filings, the deadline to file a petition for review is September 14, 2026.
