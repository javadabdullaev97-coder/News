Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif are set to meet in Jeddah on Friday, August 7, to sign a trilateral pact, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiyes-erdogan-to-visit-saudi-arabia-what-to-expect).

## What's known about the agreement

None of the three governments has officially confirmed the contents of the document, Al Jazeera [notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiyes-erdogan-to-visit-saudi-arabia-what-to-expect): the parties may settle for a more modest joint statement rather than a full-fledged pact. According to regional media cited by the outlet, the talks are focused on maritime security, the fallout from the US and Israel's war against Iran, and defense cooperation — Ankara has not disclosed details.

If the agreement is signed as announced, it would extend to Turkey the Strategic Mutual Defense Agreement that Saudi Arabia and Pakistan [signed](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiyes-erdogan-to-visit-saudi-arabia-what-to-expect) in September 2025 — a document that equates an attack on one country with an attack on both, Al Jazeera writes. Interfax, citing Anadolu, [notes](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108030) that the earlier agreement opens potential access for Saudi Arabia to Pakistan's nuclear capability.

Erdogan arrived in Jeddah on Friday for a one-day visit, Interfax notes. Pakistan Foreign Ministry spokesman Tahir Andrabi [said](https://www.euronews.com/2026/08/07/saudi-arabia-turkiye-and-pakistan-to-sign-joint-defence-pact-amid-regional-escalation) that "although the visit comes amid escalating tensions in the Persian Gulf, its significance extends beyond the current crisis," Euronews reports.

## Context: the US-Iran war

The Jeddah meeting comes against the backdrop of the war between the US and Iran, which has been underway since February 28, 2026, and has disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz — on August 5–6 Iran [warned](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/us-iran-diplomatic-friction-gulf-warning-aug2026) Gulf states of strikes on energy infrastructure in the event of a new US attack, LEAP News reported. Qatar, Oman and Saudi Arabia have previously publicly urged Washington to resume talks with Tehran.

## What's next

The exact text and the parties' commitments have not yet been published. There has been no official confirmation from Ankara, Riyadh or Islamabad about the contents of the document as of this report — the parties are expected to disclose the final form of the agreement (a full-fledged pact or a joint statement) following the Jeddah meeting.
