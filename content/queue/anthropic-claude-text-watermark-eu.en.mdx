Anthropic on August 14 [explained](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark) the mechanism behind the invisible watermarks it embeds in text generated by Claude. The technology is designed to meet the requirements of Article 50 of the EU AI Act on labeling AI-generated content.

The watermark works not by adding characters but by choosing words. When generating text, the model receives several equally valid word options. Instead of a standard random-number generator, it draws from a sequence that depends on a special key and the preceding words in the text. Word choice still looks random to the reader, but the key can be used to check whether the sequence is consistent with how Claude selects words, the company said.

According to Anthropic, the method adds no extra tokens and does not slow down the model: generation speed and cost stay the same. The watermarks cannot be tied to a specific user, organization or conversation — they can only identify the probability that a text was partly written by Claude, not which parts a person wrote.

## Google DeepMind's technology

Anthropic uses "a version of the SynthID-Text approach" — the company's name for the watermarking technology [developed by Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/watermarking-ai-generated-text-and-video-with-synthid/). According to Anthropic, the method is described in a DeepMind paper published in Nature in 2024. When tested on a portion of Gemini chatbot traffic, DeepMind found no statistically significant difference in answer quality compared with the unmarked model, Anthropic said. The method's code, according to the same data, is open through an updated Responsible Generative AI Toolkit.

The watermark covers all Claude models released after August 2, 2026. For earlier models, Anthropic is rolling out the feature gradually, in a transition mode. The company said it plans to release a public API for verifying the watermark later — no launch date has been set yet.

## What the EU requires

[Article 50 of the EU AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) requires providers of generative AI systems to label synthetic text, audio, images and video in a machine-readable format. Content must be recognizable as AI-generated. The rule took effect on August 2, 2026. For models already on the market before that date, the deadline was pushed to December 2, 2026. The penalty for violations is up to 15 million euros or 3% of a company's annual global turnover, whichever is greater.

In July 2026, Anthropic signed the EU Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content. It is a voluntary code of practice; around 190 companies and organizations signed it along with Anthropic.

Anthropic acknowledges limitations to the method. Short texts and factual fragments carry fewer watermarks. When editing already-finished human-written text, the system may not detect any trace of AI involvement at all. Code is also watermarked less often: there is less room to choose between words without hurting accuracy.
