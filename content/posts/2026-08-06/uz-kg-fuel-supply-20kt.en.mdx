Kyrgyz Cabinet of Ministers Chairman Adylbek Kasymaliev said on August 4, in a podcast by the state news agency Kabar, that talks with Uzbekistan's prime minister had discussed supplies of up to 20,000 tons of fuel per month.

## What Kyrgyzstan's Cabinet Chief Said

Kasymaliev made the statement in a podcast by the [Kabar news agency](https://ru.kabar.kg/news/kitaj-uzbekistan-azerbajdzhan-kyrgyzstan-rasshiryaet-napravleniya-importa-topliva/). According to him, Kyrgyzstan imports more than 90% of the fuel it consumes, and rising prices in the Middle East along with reduced supplies from Russia are forcing Bishkek to seek new partners.

"We also held talks with the prime minister of Uzbekistan — they have the capacity to supply 20,000 tons of fuel per month," Kasymaliev said.

Abdulla Aripov has been prime minister of Uzbekistan since December 2016 — according to [Fergana.agency](https://fergana.agency/news/148433/), the talks were held with him. There has been no official confirmation from the Uzbek delegation of the content of the talks or the 20,000-ton figure.

## Other Directions: Belarus, China, Azerbaijan

Uzbekistan is not the only partner Bishkek is negotiating with on fuel. According to Kasymaliev, Belarus is already supplying fuel to Kyrgyzstan, and the first five trucks of fuel have arrived from China. Delivery from China is planned to shift from road to rail transport — the sides are discussing the details. Talks are also underway with Azerbaijan, though without specific volumes mentioned. Fuel also comes in from Europe and Turkey, but, according to the cabinet chief, transit through third countries is more expensive.

To contain retail prices, Kyrgyzstan's government has developed a subsidy mechanism for diesel fuel, automotive gas, and AI-92 gasoline. Kasymaliev noted that after subsidies, diesel and AI-92 cost 20–25 soms less in Kyrgyzstan than in Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, with the difference covered by the national budget.

## Uzbekistan's Position Not Yet Announced

As of publication, Uzbekistan's Cabinet of Ministers, the Ministry of Energy of Uzbekistan, and Uzbekneftegaz had not issued their own statements on the talks with Kyrgyzstan over fuel supplies. All the figures and details of the talks cited here come from the Kyrgyz side.
