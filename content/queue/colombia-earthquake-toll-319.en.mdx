Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella on August 20 [declared a state of emergency](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/prezident-kolumbii-obuyavil-rezhim-chp-v-ekonomike-v-15-departamentah-strany) in the economy for 30 days across 15 departments affected by the August 10 earthquake.

Authorities have also revised the casualty count. As of August 20, the death toll had passed 310. [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1110270) puts it at 314 dead and 262 missing, while [Euronews](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/prezident-kolumbii-obuyavil-rezhim-chp-v-ekonomike-v-15-departamentah-strany) reports at least 319 dead and about 260 missing. 4,437 people were injured, and more than 115,000 residents were left homeless or otherwise affected, [Euronews reports](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/prezident-kolumbii-obuyavil-rezhim-chp-v-ekonomike-v-15-departamentah-strany). [LEAP News previously reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/18/colombia-earthquake-toll-289-damage) that the death toll had reached 289 as of August 17, with the president putting direct damage at $9.6 billion.

## State of Emergency Gives President Power to Issue Decrees Without Parliament

The decree covers the departments of Antioquia, Caldas, Cauca, Chocó, Quindío, Risaralda, Cundinamarca, Huila, Valle del Cauca, Tolima, Putumayo, Norte de Santander, Bolívar, Nariño and Sucre, [Euronews reports](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/prezident-kolumbii-obuyavil-rezhim-chp-v-ekonomike-v-15-departamentah-strany). The state of emergency allows the president to issue decrees without parliamentary approval for 30 days — such powers can remain in effect for up to 90 days a year, the outlet notes. Colombia's constitutional court will need to assess whether the use of these powers was justified.

## Chocó Still Lacks Roads to Some Affected Areas

Chocó, in western Colombia, is the earthquake's epicenter and one of the country's poorest regions. As of 2024, 67.4% of its residents were living in multidimensional poverty even before the disaster, [Euronews writes](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/colombia-reconstruction-es-team). About 70,000 people in the department were affected: 3,128 homes were destroyed and about 19,000 more damaged. Also damaged were 65 medical facilities and 277 schools. Some rural areas remain inaccessible, reachable only by river because roads have been destroyed.

"We are dealing with a population that has suffered a triple blow — from the earthquake, armed conflict and natural disasters," said UNICEF emergency specialist Olga Lucía Zuluaga, [Euronews reports](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/20/colombia-reconstruction-es-team).

## What's Next

The state of emergency in the economy will remain in effect for 30 days from the date the decree was signed. Colombia's constitutional court must assess whether the use of emergency powers was justified.
