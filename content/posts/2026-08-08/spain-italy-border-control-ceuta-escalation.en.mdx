Spain introduced passport, visa and nationality checks for passengers arriving from Italy at ports and airports from midnight on August 8 — a response to measures Rome imposed over the Ceuta crisis.

## What Madrid decided

Spain's Ministry of Interior [announced](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/italy-vows-to-maintain-border-checks-despite-spains-countermeasures-threat) that enhanced checks for arrivals from Italy would take effect at 00:00 on August 8 and last until September 7. The timeframe may be revised depending on how the situation develops. Madrid [explained](https://www.bbc.com/russian/articles/crk5r8lj74mo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) the decision as a response to "persistent illegal migration pressure" from Italy.

There is no direct land border between Spain and Italy, so the checks will mainly affect ferry and airline passengers. Migration expert Gerald Knaus, interviewed by DW, [called](https://www.dw.com/en/ceuta-spain-sets-up-border-controls-on-travelers-from-italy/a-78282840?maca=en-rss-en-world-4025-rdf) the measure "pure populism" and "completely bizarre." He said it could only be explained by domestic political pressure on Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni from right-wing parties, not by the logic of border policy.

## How the dispute began

Italy was the first to introduce temporary checks for arrivals from Spain — Meloni [announced the decision](https://www.ft.com/content/f3050d94-6b78-4cf2-8b07-cf8903aacc7f?syn-25a6b1a6=1), citing the risk of migrants stranded in Spain's Ceuta enclave crossing into Italy. The trigger was the crisis LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/ceuta-crisis-death-toll-100-russia-probe) on August 7. Since late July, about 72,000 migrants have crossed into the enclave; as of August 6, roughly 100 people had died attempting to reach Ceuta.

Spain gave Rome 48 hours to lift the restrictions, threatening "proportionate measures." Italy [refused](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/italy-vows-to-maintain-border-checks-despite-spains-countermeasures-threat) to comply with the ultimatum: the country's authorities said they would not accept "ultimatums or outside pressure" on matters of security and borders. Rome said it intends to maintain the checks until at least August 15, citing national security considerations. After Italy's refusal, Madrid introduced its own controls.

## What's next

Italy's checks on arrivals from Spain remain in place until at least August 15; Spain's last until September 7. LEAP News will continue following the dispute between Madrid and Rome and the reaction of other EU member states.
