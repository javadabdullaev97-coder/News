Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said August 5 for the first time that the two sides had agreed on route coordinates through the Strait of Hormuz with Oman, and that a joint statement was in its final stage of drafting.

## What Exactly Tehran Said

"The geographic coordinates of the route proposed by the two sides have been agreed," Baghaei said at a press briefing, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/iran-says-hormuz-talks-with-oman-in-final-stages-as-route-approved). According to him, talks with Oman have been underway for about two months, and the joint statement recording the main agreements is "at the final stage of review and drafting," South China Morning Post [reports](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3363094/iran-and-oman-agree-new-hormuz-route-tehran-says). Baghaei did not give a date for signing the final document.

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said a significant part of the agreed route runs through Iranian territorial waters, with certain sections passing through Omani waters, and that the route itself is intended for use over a period of two to four months or longer, Al Jazeera [writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/iran-says-hormuz-talks-with-oman-in-final-stages-as-route-approved).

## Tehran's Caveat: An Agreement Is Not a Safety Guarantee

Baghaei stressed that reaching an understanding with Oman does not in itself mean the strait has become safe for ship passage. "The factors that make the Strait of Hormuz unsafe — primarily the US naval blockade and other actions against Iran — remain in place," he said, SCMP [reports](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3363094/iran-and-oman-agree-new-hormuz-route-tehran-says).

Neither Al Jazeera nor SCMP cite any confirmation of the agreement from Omani authorities; the headlines of BBC and FT point to the same one-sided attribution — all reports on the final route coordinates are based solely on statements from the Iranian side.

## How This Differs From LEAP News' Previous Report

LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-progress) August 5 that Iran and Oman were "making progress" in talks: the discussion covered a general scheme with ships entering via the Iranian route and exiting via the Omani one, as well as a dispute over the size of port fees — there was no specificity about the route itself at that time. Baghaei's new statement names, for the first time, a concrete technical result of the talks — agreed coordinates — and points to a "final drafting" stage for the text of the joint statement. The question of port fees, raised by the two sides in late July, is not separately mentioned in the August 5 reports from Al Jazeera and SCMP — it is unclear whether it was included in the agreed text of the joint statement.

## What's Next

A date for signing and publishing the joint statement between Iran and Oman has not yet been announced. The US naval blockade in the strait, which LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-progress) on earlier, remains in place according to Baghaei — it is this blockade, not the lack of a route, that continues to pose a threat to shipping safety, according to Tehran. About 20% of global oil consumption passes through the Strait of Hormuz — LEAP News [wrote](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/03/iran-oman-hormuz-talks-final-stage) about this August 3, citing the US Energy Information Administration. Uzbekistan does not receive oil through the strait directly but depends on world prices when importing petroleum products.
