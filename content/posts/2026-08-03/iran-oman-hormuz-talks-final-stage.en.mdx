Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on August 2 that talks with Oman on a new route for ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz have reached their final stage.

## New Sea Route to Replace Old Corridors

Araghchi's Telegram channel said on August 2 that talks with Oman "are in the final stage." Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/iran-says-negotiations-with-oman-over-strait-of-hormuz-in-final-stages) the statement, citing his post.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei clarified what exactly the sides are negotiating. It concerns a new maritime route through the strait, separate from existing corridors — "not the northern or southern route, but one that respects the sovereign rights of both sides," Al Jazeera [quoted](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/iran-says-negotiations-with-oman-over-strait-of-hormuz-in-final-stages) him as saying.

Both Iran's and Oman's territorial waters border the strait, Al Jazeera noted in the same report.

## Blockade in Effect Since July 14

The blockade resumed on July 14 at 4 p.m. Eastern time by order of U.S. President Donald Trump, according to a [press release](https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/PUBLIC-RELEASES/Article/4542098/us-forces-to-resume-naval-blockade-against-iran/) from U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) dated July 13. The previous round of the blockade ran from April 13 to June 18: during that period the U.S. redirected more than 140 vessels, disabled nine and let through more than 50 ships carrying humanitarian cargo.

CENTCOM [said](https://x.com/CENTCOM/status/2083936817561080120) on August 2 in its X account that since the blockade resumed, 35 commercial vessels have been redirected, two disabled and two inspected. There is no press release with these figures on the agency's website, and the numbers are not independently confirmed.

## Saudi Arabia Asked Trump for a Pause

That same day, August 2, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called Trump and, according to Al Jazeera, stressed the need to "prioritize dialogue" to de-escalate the situation. The crown prince [feared](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/saudi-crown-prince-mbs-urges-trump-to-prioritise-dialogue-in-us-iran-war) that a strike on Iran's energy infrastructure would provoke retaliation against oil and gas facilities in Gulf states.

The call preceded Trump's statement postponing new strikes on condition of a quick deal over the strait — Trump [wrote](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/2/iran-war-live-tehran-warns-of-decisive-response-to-any-us-aggression) this on Truth Social that same morning.

## What This Means for Uzbekistan

About 20.9 million barrels of oil and oil products pass through the Strait of Hormuz per day — roughly 20% of global liquid fuel consumption and about a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade, according to [calculations](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/special_topics/World_Oil_Transit_Chokepoints) by the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA).

Uzbekistan does not receive oil through the strait directly but depends on global price benchmarks. In the first half of 2026, the country imported $1.11 billion worth of oil and oil products — up 8.1% year on year, Interfax [reported](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1106257). Imports of automotive gasoline rose 1.8-fold to $409.5 million, while diesel fuel imports fell 11.1% to $228.9 million.

## What's Next

The parameters of the new route have not been publicly disclosed: Baghaei described it only as different from the northern and southern corridors. Neither Tehran nor Muscat has given a signing date.
