The Senate of Uzbekistan's Oliy Majlis on August 7 [approved](https://uza.uz/ru/posts/informaciya-o-vosemnadcatom-plenarnom-zasedanii-senata-oliy-mazhlisa-respubliki-uzbekistan_892436) the Law on Market Surveillance — the document introduces a unified legal mechanism for controlling non-food products that could pose a risk to consumers' life and health.

## What Changes for Business and Consumers

The law [establishes](https://uza.uz/ru/posts/informaciya-o-vosemnadcatom-plenarnom-zasedanii-senata-oliy-mazhlisa-respubliki-uzbekistan_892436) a mechanism for organizing market surveillance based on risk assessment. It includes control purchases, laboratory testing of products, and the withdrawal and recall of dangerous goods from circulation. The report does not name the specific authorized government body that will carry out these measures — the document refers to "authorized government bodies" in general terms.

At the same time, the law establishes guarantees for economic operators: the right to participate in surveillance activities, review inspection materials and appeal inspectors' decisions.

## A Package of Six Laws

The Law on Market Surveillance is one of six laws [approved by the Senate](https://uza.uz/ru/posts/informaciya-o-vosemnadcatom-plenarnom-zasedanii-senata-oliy-mazhlisa-respubliki-uzbekistan_892436) on the first day of the eighteenth plenary session on August 7. The package also includes a law on general product safety, a law on state pension provision for military personnel, amendments to the law on citizens' pension provision, amendments to the Law on Advertising, and a constitutional law on the Presidential Administration. Senate Chairwoman Tanzila Narbaeva chaired the session.

## The Bill's Path

The Legislative Chamber passed the bill in its second reading and [sent it to the Senate](https://t.me/qonunchilikpalatasi/35835) back in April 2026. The document establishes measures against manufacturers and distributors of substandard products, defines the rights and duties of market surveillance bodies, and sets out mechanisms for suspending production, seizing and recalling dangerous products. The same wording is repeated in the final version approved by the Senate.

## What's Next

To take effect, the law approved by the Senate must be signed by the president of Uzbekistan. A signing date has not yet been announced, and the text of the law had not been posted on lex.uz as of publication.
