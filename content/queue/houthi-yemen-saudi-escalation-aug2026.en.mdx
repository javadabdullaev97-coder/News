Yemen's Houthi movement launched missile and drone strikes on government troop camps in the provinces of Marib and Hadramaut on August 6, killing at least 30 soldiers and wounding another 15, according to authorities.

## Marib and Hadramaut: Strike Hit Morning Formation

The Houthis fired at least eight missiles from Al-Jawf province at positions in Al-Sinnah and Al-Abr (Hadramaut) and the Al-Ruwaik camp (Marib), Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/houthis-claim-to-have-killed-45-in-attacks-on-yemeni-government-forces), citing military sources. The strike hit at 2 p.m. local time on Thursday, August 6 — while the troops were at morning formation, which accounts for the high death toll. According to the Yemeni government, as of August 6 at least 30 soldiers were killed and 15 wounded. Houthi armed forces spokesman Yahya Saree said strikes with "missiles and drones" caused "hundreds" of casualties among forces he described as Saudi proxies. Independent sources have not confirmed that estimate.

On the morning of August 7, the Houthis fired at least 12 more missiles at the same provinces — Hadramaut and Marib, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/houthi-strikes-hit-yemen-as-saudi-arabia-braces-for-attacks-what-we-know). The total casualty count from the second wave was not available at the time of publication.

## Najran: 11 Civilians Wounded, Including a Child

On the same Thursday, August 6, the Houthis shelled the Saudi border province of Najran. Eleven civilians were wounded, including a four-year-old child who suffered second-degree burns, said Major General Turki al-Malki, spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/houthi-strikes-hit-yemen-as-saudi-arabia-braces-for-attacks-what-we-know). Among those injured were seven Saudi nationals, one Yemeni, two Egyptians and one Pakistani. Al-Malki accused the Houthis of indiscriminately shelling residential areas in violation of international humanitarian law.

## Intelligence Warns of Threat to Saudi Energy Facilities

According to intelligence from Saudi Arabia, the United States and other countries in the region, the Houthis, together with Iraqi armed factions operating under the control of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, may soon carry out coordinated strikes on civilian sites in the kingdom. Energy infrastructure, ports and airports are under threat, a senior Saudi official said, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/houthi-strikes-hit-yemen-as-saudi-arabia-braces-for-attacks-what-we-know).

## End of a Four-Year Lull

The escalation breaks the relative lull that had held in Yemen since 2022, Al Jazeera [notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/houthis-claim-to-have-killed-45-in-attacks-on-yemeni-government-forces). At the time, the two sides reached an unofficial truce that halted major fighting between the Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition. Tensions began rising on July 13, when the Yemeni government struck Sanaa airport over suspected shipments of Iranian military equipment. On July 20, the Houthis declared a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia and attacked Aramco oil facilities, the outlet [writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/houthi-strikes-hit-yemen-as-saudi-arabia-braces-for-attacks-what-we-know). About 30% of global container traffic and 12–15% of global trade pass through the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait off Yemen's coast — the escalation directly threatens shipping through this corridor, Al Jazeera notes.

Bloomberg [describes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/houthis-attack-saudi-backed-forces-in-yemen-escalating-tensions) the developments as a sign that the conflict could widen across the region amid the ongoing war being waged by the United States and Israel against Iran.

## What's Next

As of the morning of August 7, the Houthis had not declared an end to the operation — a new wave of missile strikes on Hadramaut and Marib continued into a second day. Saudi Arabia has not yet announced a military response to the strike on Najran; there was no official reaction from Riyadh to the intelligence warning about threats to energy facilities at the time of publication.
