Common Sense Media and Fairplay [told](https://www.engadget.com/2241179/child-safety-experts-skeptical-of-openai-chatgpt-for-teens/) Engadget that after launching [ChatGPT for Teens](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/) on August 18, 2026, OpenAI has not disclosed data on the effectiveness of its age-detection system.

LEAP News [reported on August 18](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/18/openai-launches-chatgpt-for-teens) on the launch of ChatGPT for Teens — a chatbot mode with strengthened safety settings for users under 18. OpenAI turns it on automatically for accounts the system classifies as belonging to this age group.

## Experts Want Evidence, Not Promises

Robbie Torney, head of AI and emerging technology evaluation at the nonprofit Common Sense Media, [told](https://www.engadget.com/2241179/child-safety-experts-skeptical-of-openai-chatgpt-for-teens/) Engadget that the announcement contains genuinely important commitments. "But we need evidence that these commitments and safety features actually work," he added.

According to Torney, OpenAI does not publish data on the effectiveness of its age-detection system: "The automated system is nowhere close to detecting 100% of the teens who use ChatGPT."

Josh Golin, executive director of Fairplay, [told](https://www.engadget.com/2241179/child-safety-experts-skeptical-of-openai-chatgpt-for-teens/) Engadget that some of the announced measures look like a step in the right direction. On closer examination, they raise serious questions — "including the fact that there's no accountability mechanism."

The company has not yet published data on the accuracy of its age-detection system and has not said when it might do so.
