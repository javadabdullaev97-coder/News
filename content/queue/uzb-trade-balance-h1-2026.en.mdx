Uzbekistan's foreign trade turnover rose 7.4% to $41 billion in January-June 2026. Exports fell 8.8% and imports rose 21%, the [National Statistics Committee](https://stat.uz/img/press-relizlar/press_release_rus_p53810.pdf) said on July 28.

Exports totaled $15.9 billion and imports $25.1 billion. The trade deficit widened to $9.3 billion from $3.4 billion a year earlier.

The main reason for the export decline was a collapse in gold shipments. Exports of non-monetary gold fell 4.3-fold to $1.5 billion, and its share of total exports dropped from 37.3% to 9.5%. Excluding gold, merchandise exports rose 28.7% to $8.2 billion.

## Imports: Cars, Gasoline and Gas Grow Fastest

Imports of passenger cars rose 84.5% over the half-year to $778.3 million. Gasoline imports grew 78.5% to $409.5 million, and natural and manufactured gas imports rose 41.4% to $971.7 million. Machinery and transport equipment overall accounted for 32.8% of total imports — the largest import category.

China remains Uzbekistan's largest trading partner, accounting for 23.1% of foreign trade turnover. It is followed by Russia (17.1%), Kazakhstan (6.8%), Turkey (3.4%) and Afghanistan (2.6%).

## Dependence on Iranian Transit

In 2025, $3.9 billion worth of imported goods passed through Iranian territory — about 9% of Uzbekistan's total imports, according to the [Fiscal Strategy of Uzbekistan for 2027–2029](https://api.mf.uz/media/filestore/Fiskal_strategiya_2027-2029.pdf). The document was prepared by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Another $1.4 billion in exports moved via this route — about 10% of exports excluding gold.

The dependence is especially pronounced for technological equipment: $1 billion worth of equipment was imported via Iran, or 14.8% of total imports in that category.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance estimates that potential effects of Middle East tensions on Uzbekistan's trade and logistics could reach $1–1.5 billion, or 0.7–1% of GDP. The risk could materialize if transit corridors through Iran are disrupted and shipping costs rise.
