US President Donald Trump said on August 9 that his administration is betting on economic pressure on Iran instead of new military strikes, as Tehran simultaneously tightens control over the Strait of Hormuz.

## Betting on Economics Instead of New Strikes

In a phone interview with Axios on August 9, Trump [said](https://www.dw.com/ru/трамп-склоняется-к-усилению-давления-на-экономику-ирана-вместо-военных-ударов/a-78298086) he is counting on the effect of the US naval blockade: "We're just watching Iran, and their tremendous inflation, and the fact they have no money." He said the blockade had deepened the country's economic crisis and left the authorities without funds to pay military salaries.

The statement came after months of escalation. On June 17, the United States and Iran [signed](https://www.dw.com/ru/трамп-склоняется-к-усилению-давления-на-экономику-ирана-вместо-военных-ударов/a-78298086) a framework agreement to halt hostilities. On June 25, Iran broke the agreement: an Iranian drone attacked a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, after which the US carried out retaliatory strikes on Iranian targets. On July 8, Trump declared the agreement void and reinstated oil sanctions against Iran. On August 8, Tehran put forward counter-demands — lifting sanctions, paying compensation, withdrawing US troops from the region, and ending what Iran described as "aggression against its allies."

According to [The Wall Street Journal, cited by RBC](https://www.rbc.ru/politics/09/08/2026/6a788e149a7947575a8bb441), Trump told aides in private conversations that he is ready to end the conflict with Iran without a new nuclear deal. In exchange, he expects the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, which he is presenting as his own "victory." Trump's argument: Tehran is unlikely to rebuild its nuclear program before the end of his presidency, since the US destroyed three key Iranian nuclear facilities a year earlier. US gasoline prices stood at $4.02 per gallon on August 8, compared with $3.16 a year earlier. The newspaper described this factor as part of the White House's domestic political calculus.

## Iran Bars US and Israeli Ships From the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's parliamentary National Security Committee unanimously [approved](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/09/1219851-v-irane-odobrili-zapret) a bill "On Strategic Measures to Ensure the Security and Prosperity of the Strait of Hormuz." The bill restricts passage for vessels belonging to the United States, Israel and other countries the text labels "hostile." It also covers civilian and military cargo linked to Israel.

Violations carry fines of up to 20% of the value of the cargo transported. Iran may bar passage rights for countries and individuals or entities that have caused it damage until the damage is compensated. In parallel, Iran and Oman agreed on a new route through the strait, with service fees to depend on the volume of services provided.

Iran also demands a ban on US warships passing through the strait and a fee for commercial vessels. This is part of a six-point list of demands that, according to the WSJ, were put forward by Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr — who at the time held the post of coordinator of Iran's National Security Council — including the lifting of sanctions and the naval blockade, [RBC reports, citing the WSJ](https://www.rbc.ru/politics/09/08/2026/6a788e149a7947575a8bb441).

## Change of Coordinator at Iran's Security Council

On August 9, Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Khamenei [appointed](https://www.ft.com/content/ce343513-5514-4519-95c8-19832ed46d16) Mohsen Rezaei as his representative to the Supreme National Security Council, replacing Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr in the post. Rezaei is 71. From 1981 to 1997, he headed the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps — nearly the entire duration of the Iran-Iraq war. He later held a series of senior political posts and in recent years served as a military adviser to Khamenei.

The appointment came in the midst of negotiations over the terms for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. According to the Financial Times, Rezaei has previously called Iran's control over the strait "more important than dozens of atomic bombs" and said Iran "will never allow a second corridor" to open in the strait.

## No Direct Talks Yet

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on August 9 that no direct talks are currently underway between Tehran and Washington — the two sides are exchanging messages through intermediaries, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/wait-for-hormuz-deal-stretches-on-as-iran-says-terms-must-be-met). According to Araghchi, Iran will not return to direct talks until the US stops violating the previously agreed memorandum. He said the agreement with Oman on a mechanism for ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz is close to completion, but the reopening of the strait itself will depend on additional conditions.
