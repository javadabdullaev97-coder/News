Nvidia on August 14 [announced](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/nvidia-s-500-billion-plan-envelops-wall-street-in-its-ai-frenzy) it is ready to mobilize up to $500 billion from six Wall Street asset managers to finance data center customers' purchases of AI chips.

Alphabet, Google's parent company, on August 17 [hired banks](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/alphabet-hires-banks-for-inaugural-aussie-dollar-bond-sale) for its first-ever bond sale in Australian dollars — another way to raise money amid capital spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure. At the same time, Goldman Sachs [published an analysis](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/goldman-sachs-says-ai-spending-not-boosting-corporate-earnings/) showing that rising corporate AI spending has yet to produce a noticeable increase in profits.

## Nvidia Seeks Wall Street Partners for $500 Billion

Nvidia signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo Global Management, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield Asset Management, Goldman Sachs and KKR. The parties will build financing platforms for the company's customers — hyperscale cloud providers, artificial intelligence labs and corporations that need money to build data centers and buy Nvidia chips.

The scheme taps private credit markets and bonds issued by special purpose vehicles (SPVs), which lease chips to customers without adding to the buyers' own balance sheets. Lenders will vet each loan separately. Nvidia has committed to providing residual-value guarantees on the chips for roughly 25% of the deals — partially insuring lenders against the risk that the equipment loses value faster than expected.

According to Bloomberg, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang decided to announce the initiative publicly. He aimed to show the company's investors that there is enough capital to finance customers — including startups Anthropic and OpenAI. Demand, Bloomberg reported, is not limited by the capacity of cloud providers such as Microsoft and Amazon.

## Alphabet Enters a New Debt Market

Banks ANZ, Deutsche Bank, RBC Capital Markets and TD Securities have been named arrangers for Alphabet's debut bond sale in Australian dollars. The company is considering notes with maturities of 3, 5, 10 and 20 years. The size of the offering and the intended use of proceeds have not been disclosed.

The deal follows Alphabet's $25 billion dollar-denominated bond sale in early August. Earlier, in June, the company raised about $85 billion through a share sale. In the second quarter of 2026, the company posted negative free cash flow for the first time in its history. The industry as a whole, according to Bloomberg's estimate, will spend more than $730 billion in 2026, mostly on AI infrastructure. The market for so-called "kangaroo bonds" — Australian-dollar securities from foreign issuers — has reached a record 60 billion Australian dollars (about $42 billion) since the start of the year, roughly 40% more than in all of 2025.

## Goldman Sachs: Spending Is Up, Payoff Isn't

Goldman Sachs analysts led by Ben Snider examined the second-quarter 2026 earnings of S&P 500 companies. Profits at companies that benefit from AI infrastructure rose 54% year over year, accounting for roughly half of the index's total profit growth. Profits at companies that did not quantify the effect of AI grew 14% on average, versus 17% for those that did provide such an estimate.

Only 2% of S&P 500 companies quantified the effect of AI in their second-quarter 2026 earnings at all. Of those, just 11% pointed to a measurable productivity gain — in coding or customer support, for example.

Meanwhile, AI spending at the median company is rising fast: from $5 per employee per month in January to $12 in July 2026. At companies in the top decile of spending, the figure rose from $240 to $650 per employee per month.

All three storylines are part of the same picture: tech giants' spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure is growing faster than their own cash flows. Companies are increasingly borrowing on the markets, while analysts see no matching profit growth among most corporate users of AI.
