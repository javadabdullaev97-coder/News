Satellite internet service Starlink officially launched in Vietnam on August 13, opening orders through the Vietnamese version of its website, [Nikkei Asia reports](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/telecommunication/elon-musk-s-starlink-launches-satellite-internet-service-in-vietnam).

The 100 Mbps plan costs about $43 a month, and the 400 Mbps plan costs about $66, according to several outlets. Customers pay separately for the terminal — a kit of an antenna and receiver that picks up signals from satellites in low Earth orbit. According to industry publications, this is already SpaceX's sixth market in Southeast Asia: the service had previously launched in Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and East Timor.

Vietnam approved a trial run of Starlink in spring 2025, according to several outlets. Per the same reports, SpaceX did not need to form a joint venture with a local partner — a condition usually required of foreign telecom operators in the country. The radio frequency license that paved the way for the service's launch was issued by the regulator on February 18, 2026, [Nikkei Asia reported](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/telecommunication/vietnam-grants-elon-musk-s-spacex-license-for-internet). The pilot period is set for five years, during which the number of Starlink subscribers in Vietnam is capped at 600,000, according to several outlets.

## SpaceX Signs Contract to Launch VinSpace Satellites

Two days earlier, on August 11, SpaceX and Vietnamese company VinSpace [announced the signing](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vinspace-announces-launch-contract-with-spacex-marking-a-new-milestone-for-vietnams-space-industry-302847867.html) of a satellite launch contract. VinSpace's spacecraft will fly in the second quarter of 2027 aboard a Falcon 9 rocket as part of a Transporter mission. That is SpaceX's term for launches in which a single rocket carries satellites for multiple customers into orbit and splits the launch cost among them, [Al Jazeera notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/11/vietnams-vinspace-announces-deal-with-spacex-to-launch-first-satellite).

"Reliable access to space is the foundation without which satellite developments never become real missions," VinSpace CEO Thu Vu [said](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vinspace-announces-launch-contract-with-spacex-marking-a-new-milestone-for-vietnams-space-industry-302847867.html).

VinSpace was registered in November 2025 with a charter capital of 300 billion dong (about $11.5 million), [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/11/vietnams-vinspace-announces-deal-with-spacex-to-launch-first-satellite). The company is part of Vingroup, Vietnam's largest private conglomerate. Vingroup founder Pham Nhat Vuong owns 71% of VinSpace.

Vietnam put its first satellite, VINASAT-1, into orbit in 2008. In 2024, Vietnam's ruling Communist Party added space technology to the country's strategic priorities, alongside developing its maritime and mineral resources, according to Al Jazeera.
