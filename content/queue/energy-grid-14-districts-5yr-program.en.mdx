President Shavkat Mirziyoyev was [briefed](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9496) on August 13 that Uzbekistan had expanded the "Yangiyul experience" of uninterrupted energy supply to 14 districts across the country and approved a five-year program to upgrade electricity and gas networks there. The districts were selected one per each region of the country.

## How the shortfall was calculated by mahalla

Earlier, the 14 selected districts were tasked with creating a model energy supply system. To do this, work was organized at the mahalla level: officials studied problems in the energy sector and took targeted measures with on-site visits, according to a [statement](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9496) from the presidential press service.

The effort covered 916 mahallas, 832,000 households, 49,000 business entities and more than 2,200 social infrastructure facilities. An energy passport and an energy resource demand balance were compiled for each mahalla — for the first time, the gap between demand and existing capacity was precisely calculated at the mahalla level.

A power capacity shortfall of 60 megawatts was identified in 265 mahallas, and a natural gas shortfall of 47 million cubic meters a year in another 42 mahallas. At the same time, capacity reserves were found in 207 mahallas.

## What has already been repaired

In the 14 districts, 5,695 transformers, 6,900 kilometers of electricity networks and 242 kilometers of gas networks, as well as 404 gas distribution units, require repair. By the time of the briefing, 441 transformers, 1,134 kilometers of electricity networks, 88 kilometers of gas networks and 92 gas distribution units had been repaired. Some facilities launched in previous years under investment programs but left unfinished were also brought into operation.

## The five-year program: the numbers

According to official estimates, demand for energy resources in these 14 districts will grow by 49% over the next five years. Taking this forecast and planned projects into account, authorities drew up a five-year grid modernization program. It calls for replacing 2,261 transformers and 7,449 kilometers of electricity networks in 721 mahallas, as well as 313 gas distribution units and 327 kilometers of gas networks in 200 mahallas. The program is expected to add 1,300 megawatts of transmission capacity for the regions.

Energy consumption at social infrastructure facilities was studied separately: energy resource use was analyzed at 1,938 facilities, and 226 of them with low efficiency indicators were placed in the "red" category and included in a program to switch to energy-efficient systems. The statement also cites expected savings — 4.3 million kilowatt-hours of electricity and 2.1 million cubic meters of natural gas a year — but the presidential press service did not specify whether this figure applies to the entire five-year program or only to the conversion of these 226 facilities to energy-efficient systems.

The presidential press service did not name the specific 14 districts, the volume of investment in soums or foreign currency, or the officials responsible for implementing the program.

## What's next

The president instructed officials not to limit the approach applied in the 14 districts and to expand it to all districts of the country. They were ordered to organize work at the mahalla level, compile energy passports and energy consumption balances, conduct a targeted review of network conditions, and eliminate problems in all regions in stages.

The same meeting also discussed accelerating the project to transfer Samarkand region's regional electricity networks to foreign investor management, as well as the construction of the "Surkhan — Puli-Khumri" high-voltage transmission line.
