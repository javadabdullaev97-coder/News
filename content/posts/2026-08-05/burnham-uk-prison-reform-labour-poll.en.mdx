British Prime Minister Andy Burnham's government on August 4 [excluded](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/rape-serious-child-sex-and-grooming-offences-barred-from-sentencing-changes) people convicted of rape, serious child sex offenses and grooming from an early release program — the first course correction two weeks into his premiership.

## From Manchester Mayor to 10 Downing Street

Burnham took over the Labour Party and became prime minister on July 20 — without a general election. Keir Starmer announced his resignation in June after the party's heavy losses in May's local elections and a string of political missteps, [PBS NewsHour writes](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/why-britain-is-getting-a-new-prime-minister-without-a-general-election). Under the rules of Britain's parliamentary system, the ruling party can change leaders mid-term. The winner of the internal party race automatically becomes prime minister, while the next general election is not due until 2029 at the earliest.

Burnham, previously elected mayor of Greater Manchester, won the backing of 379 of the 401 Labour MPs in the House of Commons and was confirmed as party leader at a special conference on July 17. He took office as prime minister on July 20 after an audience with King Charles III.

## Out of Prison — and the First Criticism

Under Starmer, the government had already launched an early release program for prisoners amid overcrowded jails. The automatic release point for standard sentences was to drop from half to a third of the time served. For more serious offenses, the threshold was to drop from two-thirds to half. The program was scheduled to start in September.

Burnham suspended the launch and on August 4 [announced](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/rape-serious-child-sex-and-grooming-offences-barred-from-sentencing-changes) new exclusions: rapists, pedophiles and those involved in grooming schemes will not be eligible for early release. These categories have been added to the existing list of 18,000 offenders serving life and indeterminate sentences deemed most dangerous by the courts. Those who are released will be required to wear electronic tags; those convicted of rape and serious child sex offenses will face a year of GPS monitoring. The government has allocated £100 million for electronic monitoring, with another £10 million going to victim support services. The program's launch has been pushed back from September to October.

"Keeping the public safe is my top priority," Burnham said, explaining the decision to exclude those convicted of rape, serious child sex offenses and grooming. Justice Minister Alex Norris called the decisions difficult: "No minister would want to be taking these decisions, but it's right that these heinous crimes are excluded."

## Labour Overtakes Reform UK

Despite criticism over the prison reform, a [poll](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/labour-leads-reform-in-uk-poll-in-latest-sign-of-burnham-boost) by JL Partners cited by Bloomberg showed 27% of voters would back the Labour Party against 25% for Nigel Farage's Reform UK. It is Labour's first lead over Reform in this polling series since March 2025 and the party's best result since November 2024.

Labour's rising support has been linked to its by-election win in Makerfield in June and Burnham's first weeks at 10 Downing Street. Reform UK's support has declined following its defeat in the same constituency and scandals over undeclared gifts and donations to party leadership.

## What's Next

The expanded rollout of the early release program is set for October. The government has promised to launch a crime victim support hotline by that date.
