The Nevada Transportation Authority (NTA) granted Tesla a robotaxi permit for Las Vegas on July 27 but approved only 10 vehicles instead of the 5,000 requested.

The permit is an Autonomous Vehicle Network Company (AVNC) license for commercial operation of driverless taxis. It restricts the vehicles to a geofenced zone along the Las Vegas Strip, sets a speed cap of 45 miles per hour (about 72 km/h) and bans passenger drop-offs at airports. Rides must be accompanied by "appropriate human oversight," and every vehicle must be labeled "robotaxi," [according to documents cited by Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-nevada-robotaxi-permit-10-vehicles-las-vegas/).

Tesla filed the application (Docket 26-05015) on June 5, seeking Nevada's approval for 5,000 vehicles across all of Clark County, including Harry Reid and Henderson airports. The regulator has not publicly explained how a 5,000-vehicle application turned into a 10-vehicle permit, [Electrek notes](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-nevada-robotaxi-permit-10-vehicles-las-vegas/). The only other competitor with an active Nevada permit — Amazon's Zoox service — is capped at a maximum of 100 vehicles, [the outlet notes](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-nevada-robotaxi-permit-10-vehicles-las-vegas/).

Tesla's active fleet of driverless Robotaxis in the US — without a safety operator behind the wheel — numbers about 20 vehicles operating simultaneously, Electrek estimates. If Tesla deploys all 10 permitted vehicles in Las Vegas, the fleet would grow by roughly half, [the outlet calculated](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-nevada-robotaxi-permit-10-vehicles-las-vegas/). The company expanded its ride zone in Austin and launched commercial driverless rides in Miami, Orlando and Tampa. [Tesla disclosed this in its July 22 quarterly update for investors](https://assets-ir.tesla.com/tesla-contents/IR/TSLA-Q2-2026-Update.pdf), business outlets report.

## Tesla prepares to launch Cybercab

In Austin, Texas, Tesla is simultaneously preparing for the first public launch of Cybercab. It is a two-seat driverless taxi without a steering wheel or pedals, running on Full Self-Driving (FSD), Tesla's proprietary autonomous driving system. By July, more than 100 finished cabs were sitting at the Tesla Gigafactory Texas plant, [Electrek reports](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-cybercab-launch-least-exciting-vehicle-ever/).

The company completed briefing Austin's emergency services on how to remove driverless vehicles from the road. Since July, Tesla has been carrying employees on test rides along private roads around the plant's campus, [Electrek writes](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-cybercab-launch-least-exciting-vehicle-ever/).

On August 17, the official Tesla Robotaxi account [announced on X](https://x.com/robotaxi/status/2089512069519577544) (formerly Twitter) a giveaway of invitations to the Cybercab launch event. The more Robotaxi rides a user completes before August 23, the higher their chances of attending.

The company's internal target is to launch the service for the general public by the end of August, though the timeline could slip, [Electrek writes](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-cybercab-launch-least-exciting-vehicle-ever/), citing sources from The Information.

## What's next

The invitation giveaway for the Cybercab launch event runs through August 23. Tesla has not yet announced an official launch date for the passenger service.
