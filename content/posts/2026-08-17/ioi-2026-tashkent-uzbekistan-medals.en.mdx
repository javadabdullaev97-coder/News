Uzbekistan's national team won medals across its full roster for the first time in the country's history at the 38th International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI) in Tashkent on August 9-16, 2026 — one silver and three bronze.

This was Uzbekistan's first time hosting the Olympiad and the second in Central Asia after Kazakhstan hosted IOI in 2015, according to [data on participating countries](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/countries/). The competition drew 375 students from delegations representing more than 90 countries, according to the [list of delegations](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/delegations/2026).

The rounds were held at the Central Asian Expo exhibition center, spanning 23,000 square meters, while teams stayed at hotels in New Uzbekistan park, according to the organizers' [data on venues](https://ioi2026.uz/venues-and-hotels). The Olympiad was organized by the Ministry of Digital Technologies, the Ministry of Public Education, the Center for the Development of Digital Education and the IT Education Association of Uzbekistan, according to the [Olympiad's official website](https://ioi2026.uz/).

The competition format consists of two five-hour rounds, with participants solving algorithmic problems individually; the maximum score is about 600 points. Roughly half of participants won medals: 31 gold (361.12 points and above), 62 silver (303.28 points and above) and 94 bronze (228.80 points and above), according to the [official results table](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/results/2026). Qiwen Xu of China took first place with 498.27 points.

Pavel Durov's Telegram channel [says](https://t.me/durov/540) Tashkent was not chosen by chance: Uzbekistan considers itself the homeland of Muhammad al-Khwarizmi, the scholar whose name gave rise to the word "algorithm." Telegram [announced](https://t.me/durov/540) it would award 235 medalists digital collectible Algorithm Cups cards worth from $117,000.

## How Uzbekistan's team performed

Uzbekistan's four-member team won medals across its full roster for the first time in the country's history: one silver and three bronze. Jahonali Haydaraliyev of Tashkent won silver with 303.48 points, placing 93rd overall. Temur Kadirbergenov of Nukus won bronze with 295.79 points, placing 108th. Two more bronze medals went to Ulug'bek Rahmatullayev of Namangan (288.58 points, 118th place) and Jahongirshoh Avazxonov, also of Namangan (285.97 points, 120th place). Another team member, Sardorbek Salimov, received an honorable mention from the jury, according to the [same results table](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/results/2026).

A year earlier in Bolivia, Uzbekistan's team won one silver and one bronze medal, according to [IOI 2025 results](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/results/2025). The current result is the country's best in its entire history of participating in the Olympiad.

## How other regional teams performed

Kazakhstan won one gold and three silver medals: Maksim Tsoy placed 5th with 452.33 points, while silver also went to Aldiyar Seytbay, Mansur Mamadakhunov and Akezhan Askar. Ukraine won one gold and two silver medals — Vladislav Khalin placed 16th with 398.32 points, and silver went to Ilya Shevchenko and Daria Perekopska. The data is confirmed by the [official results table](https://stats.ioinformatics.org/results/2026).

Durov's Telegram channel [says](https://t.me/durov/542) Russia won 2 gold and 2 silver medals, while Belarus won gold, silver and two bronze. The official results table lists participants from these two countries without a country affiliation — unlike Ukraine and Kazakhstan, which are marked with the [UKR] and [KAZ] codes.

The 39th Olympiad will be held in Germany in 2027, according to [IOI data](https://ioinformatics.org/).
