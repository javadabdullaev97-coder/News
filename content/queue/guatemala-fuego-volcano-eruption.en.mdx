Guatemala's Fuego volcano intensified its eruption on the morning of August 3 — authorities declared an orange alert nationwide, and by August 4 more than 650 people had been evacuated from the danger zone.

## Timeline of the eruption

Activity at Fuego — one of the most active volcanoes in Central America, standing 3,763 meters tall and located roughly 40 kilometers from Guatemala's capital — began building on the morning of Monday, August 3, and intensified by evening, Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/guatemala-issues-second-highest-danger-alert-after-fuego-volcano-erupts). Guatemala's Institute of Volcanology, Seismology, Meteorology and Hydrology (Insivumeh) recorded lava flows and pyroclastic emissions of gas, ash and rock debris moving down the volcano's slopes. The ash column rose to about 6 kilometers, Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/guatemala-issues-second-highest-danger-alert-after-fuego-volcano-erupts).

Guatemala's National Coordinating Agency for Disaster Reduction (CONRED) [declared](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/guatemala-issues-second-highest-danger-alert-after-fuego-volcano-erupts) an orange alert nationwide — the second-highest of CONRED's four alert levels, below only red, the highest. The volcanology institute warned that the eruption is moving toward a more explosive phase.

Due to the threat of lahars and falling ash, a national highway running near the volcano has been closed, and schools in municipalities close to Fuego have suspended classes, the Associated Press [reported](https://www.ksat.com/news/2026/08/04/guatemala-evacuates-villages-due-to-an-eruption-of-the-fuego-volcano/).

## Evacuation continues

According to CONRED data as of Tuesday, August 4, more than 650 people have been evacuated from the danger zone from eight communities — residents have been placed in temporary shelters, the Associated Press [reported](https://www.ksat.com/news/2026/08/04/guatemala-evacuates-villages-due-to-an-eruption-of-the-fuego-volcano/). The village of El Porvenir and the hamlet of Las Lajitas were the first evacuated on Monday — at the time, about 250 residents were involved; over the course of Tuesday the list of communities being evacuated grew.

"About 10 more communities are expected to be evacuated during the day," CONRED executive secretary Claudine Ogaldez [said](https://www.ksat.com/news/2026/08/04/guatemala-evacuates-villages-due-to-an-eruption-of-the-fuego-volcano/). Agency spokesperson Valeria Urizar advised residents of the danger zone not to wait for an official order: "If an evacuation has been declared or conditions threaten your life, follow the principle of self-evacuation," Al Jazeera [quoted](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/guatemala-issues-second-highest-danger-alert-after-fuego-volcano-erupts) her as saying.

There are no official reports of deaths or injuries from the current eruption as of publication.

## Fuego and its previous eruptions

Fuego is one of Guatemala's three active volcanoes, erupting almost continuously since 2002 with minor ash emissions and periodic stronger bursts of activity. The most destructive occurred in June 2018: pyroclastic flows buried the village of San Miguel Los Lotes, killing 194 people, with another 234 listed as missing and presumed dead, Al Jazeera [recalled](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/guatemala-issues-second-highest-danger-alert-after-fuego-volcano-erupts). In 2023, another surge in activity forced the evacuation of about 1,200 people, and in June 2025, more than 500.
