Google launched a "Preferred Source" button on August 20 that publishers embed on their websites so readers can mark them as a priority source in Google Search and Google News, helping publications recover traffic lost to AI-driven search.

Previously, the preferred-source icon sent readers to a separate Google settings page. Now clicking the embedded button immediately adds the site to a reader's priority sources and returns the user to where they left off on the publisher's page, according to [the company's official blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/personalize-search-discover-news/).

Preferred sources appear more often in the Top Stories module — news cards at the top of search results. They can also receive a special label in AI Overviews and AI Mode, tools where Google generates an answer to a query using artificial intelligence instead of a plain link to a website, [Google said](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/personalize-search-discover-news/).

Since launch, users have selected more than 600,000 unique sources — up from 345,000 in May 2026, when Google expanded preferred sources to AI Overviews and AI Mode. The feature had already been available in Top Stories, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/google-gives-publishers-a-new-way-to-fight-ai-driven-traffic-losses/), citing Google data.

The answers Google generates in AI Overviews and AI Mode respond to a reader's query directly on the results page, without requiring a visit to the source website. As a result, publishers are seeing fewer click-throughs from search. Preferred-source status remains one of the few ways to earn a click even from inside an AI-generated answer.

Users click through to a preferred source twice as often as to an ordinary site in the same results, Google says in data cited by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/google-gives-publishers-a-new-way-to-fight-ai-driven-traffic-losses/).

Publishers can embed the button using code from [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources) documentation — either two lines of JavaScript or a direct link for sites without scripts. Only entire domains and subdomains can participate: a standalone blog on a site's subdirectory cannot be added to the list of sources, [the documentation states](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources).

Google also expanded personalization in Discover, the recommendation feed in its mobile app and on the search homepage. In the coming days, users will be able to use a menu to flag topics they want to see more or less of, [the company said](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/personalize-search-discover-news/).
