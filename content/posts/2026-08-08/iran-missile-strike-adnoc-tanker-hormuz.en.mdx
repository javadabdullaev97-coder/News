Iran fired a missile at a tanker operated by ADNOC, the UAE's state oil company, in the Strait of Hormuz early on August 8 — preliminary reports suggest a fire may have broken out on board. The UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs (UAE MoFA) says no casualties were reported.

## What is known about the attack

The UAE MoFA [called](https://www.ft.com/content/9a1ab09b-82d7-43c2-b44a-ebb41d352dab) the incident a "hostile Iranian attack that struck an ADNOC tanker with a missile while it was transiting the Strait of Hormuz." There were no casualties, according to WAM. WAM [did not disclose](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/08/1219737-raketa-v-ormuzskom-prolive) the vessel's name or the exact location of the strike — the UAE state news agency limited itself to a general statement on the incident and said the situation is under control.

The August 8 attack was not the first in the past week. According to WAM, an unidentified projectile struck a cargo vessel roughly 20 nautical miles off the port of Khasab on [August 3](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/08/1219737-raketa-v-ormuzskom-prolive), and the Liberian-flagged bulk carrier Minoan Pioneer on August 4. US President Donald Trump said the Strait of Hormuz "will open very soon — or Iran will pay the price."

## Iran offers to lift sanctions in exchange for passage guarantees

Alongside the string of attacks, Tehran [offered](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/08/1219737-raketa-v-ormuzskom-prolive) to discuss lifting US sanctions in exchange for guarantees of unimpeded passage for commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz.

## Talks with Oman: close, but with conditions

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said talks with Oman on a new maritime corridor through the Strait of Hormuz [are nearing their final stage](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-08/iran-says-hormuz-deal-close-but-that-its-conditions-must-be-met), but stressed this does not mean the Strait of Hormuz will open soon, according to Bloomberg.

According to Bloomberg, Iran is insisting on two conditions: a ban on US and Israeli vessels passing through the strait, and compensation from "hostile" countries for transit rights. Washington is seeking free navigation without Iranian fees and without the need to obtain Tehran's approval for each passage.

## What's next

None of the parties to the talks has yet given a date for a possible announcement of an interim agreement between the US, Iran and Oman.
