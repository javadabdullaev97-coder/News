Bloomberg reported on August 21 on a Counterpoint Research forecast: Samsung will [overtake](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/samsung-set-to-top-phone-market-in-downturn-counterpoint-says) Apple and reclaim the top spot in the smartphone market in 2026, helped by its resilience to rising memory chip prices.

According to Counterpoint Research, Samsung held [24% of the global smartphone market](https://www.androidauthority.com/counterpoint-research-q2-2026-smartphone-shipment-report-3686931/) in the second quarter of 2026. Apple had 20%, Xiaomi 12%, OPPO 11%, and vivo 8%. Global shipments fell 11% year over year, the worst second quarter since 2013. The cause is a shortage of DRAM and NAND chips: memory makers are prioritizing data-center orders for AI over consumer electronics.

According to [ibtimes.com.au](https://www.ibtimes.com.au/samsung-reclaims-top-spot-smartphone-market-2026-1872262), memory and storage account for more than 60% of the cost of budget smartphones and more than 30% for premium models. Counterpoint senior analyst Shilpi Jain said "the global memory crisis has overtaken every other factor in importance and become the biggest drag on the smartphone industry."

## Why Samsung is more resilient than its rivals

Samsung manufactures its own memory chips, so rising DRAM and NAND prices hit it less than vendors that buy memory from outside suppliers. ibtimes.com.au notes this, citing Counterpoint. According to Android Authority, Samsung benefited by raising prices less often than rivals in the Indian and Middle Eastern markets. Apple, meanwhile, did not raise prices anywhere in the world.

Counterpoint forecasts global shipments will fall by roughly 14% for all of 2026, and analysts expect the memory shortage to persist into 2027.
