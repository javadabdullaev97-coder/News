On July 31, 2026, Uzbekistan Airways told passengers about temporary US entry restrictions imposed by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) over an Ebola outbreak in Congo.

## Who Is Barred From Boarding

The restrictions don't originate with the airline — it merely [relayed to passengers](https://t.me/uzbekistanairways/8766) the US regulator's requirements. According to the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/returning-travelers.html), no one who has been in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) within 21 days before departure will be allowed to board a flight to the US. The rule applies to US citizens too — there are no exceptions for them.

Separately, the CDC suspended entry into the country for foreign nationals who have been in the DRC, Uganda or South Sudan in the past 21 days. The rule operates under an [order](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/evdorder.html) suspending the right of entry, issued under quarantinable-disease legislation.

## Who Must Fly Through Only Four Airports

US citizens who have been in Uganda or South Sudan, but not the DRC, within 21 days before arrival must enter the country only through four designated airports. These are [Washington Dulles](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/returning-travelers.html) (IAD), Hartsfield-Jackson in Atlanta (ATL), George Bush in Houston (IAH) and John F. Kennedy in New York (JFK). There, passengers are sent for additional health screening: a questionnaire on travel history and symptoms, contactless temperature checks, observation by CDC staff, and enrollment in a 21-day SMS-monitoring system.

According to Uzbekistan Airways, violating these requirements can result in deportation and fines — the airline does not back up this part of its notice with a link to a specific document from US authorities.

## Not New Rules, but Another Extension

The restrictions haven't been in effect only since July 31 — the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/port-health/legal-authorities/evdorder.html) introduced them on May 18, 2026, and amended them on May 22. Since then, the agency has extended them several times, on June 21 and July 13; the latest extension runs for 30 days, meaning it lasts roughly until mid-August. The date Uzbekistan Airways cited in its notice to passengers coincides with when the airline informed its customers of requirements that were already in force.

## The Outbreak in Congo

The restrictions stem from an outbreak of fever caused by the Bundibugyo virus, one of the species of ebolavirus. According to [CDC data](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/index.html) as of July 31, the outbreak is spreading faster than previous Ebola epidemics and has become the second-largest on record. Confirmed cases topped a thousand within the first 40 days alone, and more than 100 deaths were recorded in the first week of July alone. The CDC is tracking case clusters in the provinces of Haut-Uélé, Ituri, North Kivu, South Kivu and Tshopo.
