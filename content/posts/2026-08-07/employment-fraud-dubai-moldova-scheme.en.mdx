The Department for Combating Economic Crimes under the Prosecutor General's Office of Uzbekistan said on August 7 it had uncovered two overseas employment fraud schemes — in Karakalpakstan and in Tashkent.

## Karakalpakstan: victims promised jobs in Dubai and Moldova

According to [the Department's statement](https://t.me/Department_uz/12965), the agency's office in the Ellikkala district of the Republic of Karakalpakstan conducted a pre-investigation inquiry. It found that a man identified by the initials R.S. gained the trust of three local residents by promising to send them to work in Dubai (UAE) and Moldova. In return, he received $2,000 and 4 million soums from them (about $336 at the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-07/) for August 7 — 11,915.64 soums per dollar). According to the Department, he obtained the money fraudulently, and the promised jobs never materialized.

## Tashkent: Belarus job offered through migration agency contacts

The Department uncovered the second case in the Yangihayot district of Tashkent. According to the same [statement](https://t.me/Department_uz/12965), the agency's office carried out an operation together with other law enforcement agencies. Suspects were caught red-handed — at the moment money changed hands — including the head of LLC "E.W.," identified by the initials B.U., and several other individuals involved. The suspects convinced a city resident, identified by the initials Sh.L., that they would use contacts at a migration agency to send him to work in Belarus. In return, they received $500 and 1.2 million soums (about $101 at the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate for August 7).

The Department also published a [video report, "Vaziyat 24,"](https://t.me/Department_uz/12964) about the employment fraud case uncovered in Tashkent.

## Case status

In the Karakalpakstan case, the Department has so far reported only the results of the pre-investigation inquiry — its statement does not mention a decision to open a criminal case. In the Tashkent case, the suspects were detained with physical evidence at the scene where the money changed hands. Until a court verdict takes legal effect, everyone named in this report is presumed innocent.
