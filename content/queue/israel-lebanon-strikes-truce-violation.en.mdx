The Israeli military struck south Lebanon overnight into Saturday, August 15, killing at least 11 people — the deadliest escalation in the two months since the truce with Hezbollah, [Financial Times reported](https://www.ft.com/content/1bd69e67-266f-4a4a-9c49-eb3c83bcb295).

A strike on a house on the outskirts of Ansar village in Nabatieh district [killed](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/israeli-strike-kills-seven-in-south-lebanon-as-attacks-intensify) seven people, including three children. A second strike hit the village of Deir ez-Zahrani, killing four more. According to Al Jazeera, at least 19 people were wounded: two in Ansar and 17 in Deir ez-Zahrani.

The Israeli military said the strikes targeted Hezbollah infrastructure in the Ali Taher and Ansar areas "in response to actions against Israeli soldiers" in the area. The office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Hezbollah violated the ceasefire by attacking Israeli troops in the so-called security zone in south Lebanon.

## Lebanon's Response

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam called the escalation "extremely dangerous" and said the presence of Hezbollah's weapons in the country's south does not give Israel the right to violate Lebanese territory. Those killed in Ansar, he said, were not military infrastructure. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun called the strikes a violation of the ceasefire framework agreement and international law.

## Truce Context

The truce between Israel and Hezbollah took effect in June 2026. The parties later agreed on a framework deal: an Israeli troop withdrawal in exchange for Hezbollah's disarmament. Since June, the parties have held several rounds of talks. Hezbollah has refused to disarm or hold direct talks with Israel.
