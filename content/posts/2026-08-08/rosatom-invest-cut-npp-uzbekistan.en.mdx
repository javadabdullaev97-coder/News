Rosatom CEO Alexey Likhachev announced the state corporation's final 2026 investment program on July 16 — more than 900 billion rubles, almost half what it was a year earlier.

Three weeks later, neither Rosatom nor Uzbekistan's Uzatom Agency has publicly said whether the cut will affect the plant the Russian company is building in Uzbekistan's Jizzakh region.

## What Exactly Was Cut

At Director's Day for central office staff on July 16, Likhachev named the final investment program figure — [more than 900 billion rubles](https://strana-rosatom.ru/2026/07/16/aleksej-lihachev-neobhodim-rost-effe/) for 2026. A year earlier, in 2025, the program totaled [1.66 trillion rubles](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1103692); in 2024, it was 1.456 trillion — a cut of about 46% from last year.

At the Central Bank of Russia's exchange rate on July 16 (77.96 rubles per dollar), the new program amounts to roughly $11.5 billion, compared to about $21 billion a year earlier. The same rate was applied to both figures for comparability.

The CEO [explained the decision](https://strana-rosatom.ru/2026/07/16/aleksej-lihachev-neobhodim-rost-effe/) as a push for efficiency: "This year we plan to cut costs by 5%, and by 2028, on a cumulative basis, to cut costs by more than 10%." Likhachev did not specify which projects were being delayed — he said only that Rosatom had dropped a number of projects or pushed back their timelines. In the same remarks, he named Primorskaya NPP, Kola NPP-2, Beloyarsk NPP and Smolensk NPP as priority construction sites, by contrast. The company plans to pour first concrete at three of them in 2027. Neither the remarks nor the subsequent Interfax report mentioned foreign projects — including Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan — at all.

## What About Uzbekistan

Since July 16, Rosatom's official Telegram channel [@rosatomru](https://t.me/s/rosatomru) has not posted about the investment program or the Uzbekistan project. Uzatom's own channels — the agency's Telegram channel and its news section on gov.uz — have also issued no statements on how Rosatom's budget cut affects the Jizzakh plant. Checked August 8.

The only public assurance that the investment program cut will not affect the Uzbekistan project came from a local outlet, citing a comment it obtained directly from Rosatom's press service. The claim has not been repeated on the company's official channels — neither on Telegram nor on the rosatom.ru website.

## Construction Proceeds as Planned

There are no formal signs so far that the Uzbekistan project has been suspended. Construction of the first power unit of the integrated nuclear plant in Jizzakh region officially began on [June 4, 2026](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/06/04/1203382-rossiya-uzbekistan-aes). The ceremony was held via video link by Presidents Shavkat Mirziyoyev of Uzbekistan and Vladimir Putin of Russia.

On July 29, Rosatom and Uzatom [set up a joint working group](https://www.atomic-energy.ru/news/2026/07/29/167423) to coordinate the review of the plant's project documentation — after the investment program cut had already been announced. This does not directly confirm or refute the Uzbekistan claim, but it shows that operational cooperation between the parties on the project continues.
