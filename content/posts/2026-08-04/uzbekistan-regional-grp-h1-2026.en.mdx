The National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan reported on August 3 that gross regional product (GRP) in Tashkent reached 226 trillion soums in January–June 2026 — up 13.3%, the fastest growth among all regions.

At the [exchange rate of the Central Bank of Uzbekistan](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-07-31/) (CBU) for July 31 — 12,006.39 soums per dollar — Tashkent's figure is equivalent to roughly $18.82 billion. Uzbekistan's gross domestic product (GDP) for the same period was earlier [estimated](/article/uzbekistan-gdp-h1-2026-growth-85pct) by the National Statistics Committee at 1,072.7 trillion soums, or about $89.3 billion.

## Navoi and Tashkent Regions Rank Second and Third

Second by volume is Navoi region: 113.9 trillion soums (≈$9.49 billion). Its growth rate — 6.7% — is the slowest among all regions, despite ranking second by volume. Third is Tashkent region: 103.8 trillion soums (≈$8.65 billion), up 8.7%. The data were [published by the National Statistics Committee](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69605-ya-m-azhmi-b-jicha-ajsi-ududlar-etakchi-4) on August 3 for all 14 administrative-territorial units of the country — the Republic of Karakalpakstan, 12 regions, and the city of Tashkent.

Other regions by GRP volume for January–June 2026:

- Samarkand region — 67.4 trillion soums (≈$5.61 billion), up 9.7%;
- Fergana region — 65.2 trillion soums (≈$5.43 billion), up 9.1%;
- Andijan region — 59.6 trillion soums (≈$4.96 billion), up 8.8%;
- Kashkadarya region — 55.7 trillion soums (≈$4.64 billion), up 8.4%;
- Namangan region — 45.5 trillion soums (≈$3.79 billion), up 8.3%;
- Bukhara region — 44.8 trillion soums (≈$3.73 billion), up 8.8%;
- Surkhandarya region — 35.9 trillion soums (≈$2.99 billion), up 7.3%;
- Khorezm region — 32.9 trillion soums (≈$2.74 billion), up 9.1%;
- Republic of Karakalpakstan — 32.4 trillion soums (≈$2.70 billion), up 10.2% — the second-fastest growth rate after Tashkent;
- Jizzakh region — 29.7 trillion soums (≈$2.47 billion), up 8.6%;
- Syrdarya region — 19.1 trillion soums (≈$1.59 billion), up 8.4% — the smallest volume among the regions.

## Sum of Regional Figures Does Not Equal National GDP

Adding up the figures for all 14 regions gives roughly 931.9 trillion soums — 140.8 trillion soums (≈$11.73 billion) less than the reported 1,072.7 trillion. The gap is explained by the calculation methodology: some GDP components, including net taxes on products, are not allocated among regions because of insufficient regional-level data and are counted only in the nationwide figure — as the National Statistics Committee [explains](https://stat.uz/img/press-relizlar/analitika-vrp_rus_p27142.pdf) in its GRP calculation methodology. As with the overall GDP figure, the data remain preliminary.

## Tashkent's Share of the National Economy Grew for a Second Straight Year

Tashkent's 226 trillion soums account for about 21% of the country's total. A year earlier, in January–June 2025, the city's share stood at 19.6%, with GRP of 158.2 trillion soums and growth of 10.6%, according to the [National Statistics Committee's release](https://stat.uz/img/press-relizlar/analitika-vrp_rus_p27142.pdf) for that period. Over the year, the city not only expanded its output by nearly 68 trillion soums but also accelerated its growth rate — from 10.6% to 13.3%.
