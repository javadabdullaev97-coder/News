Chinese private company LandSpace landed the reusable first stage of its Zhuque-3 rocket on August 19 during the vehicle's second flight — the first time a Chinese private company has landed an orbital-class booster, [Spaceflight Now reports](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/19/landspace-becomes-first-commercial-chinese-company-to-land-an-orbital-class-booster/).

The rocket lifted off from the Dongfeng launch site at Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwestern China on August 19 at 07:35 Beijing time (04:35 Tashkent time), [Spaceflight Now reports](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/19/landspace-becomes-first-commercial-chinese-company-to-land-an-orbital-class-booster/). According to LandSpace and open sources, the rocket placed the Honghu-03 satellite into orbit. LandSpace's own account on X also reported the launch and landing — the time and location match Spaceflight Now's data.

The first stage decelerated using its own engines and touched down on deployable landing legs — the same technique used by the reusable first stage of SpaceX's Falcon 9 rocket. The stage landed in Minqin County, Gansu Province. A fire broke out in the tail section after landing, which didn't stop the company and the outlet from calling the mission a success, [Spaceflight Now notes](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/19/landspace-becomes-first-commercial-chinese-company-to-land-an-orbital-class-booster/). Zhuque-3 runs on a mixture of liquid methane and oxygen. This methane-oxygen propellant — known as "methalox" — is easier to refuel and service between reusable flights than traditional kerosene-oxygen fuel.

This is Zhuque-3's second orbital flight, [NASASpaceflight notes](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/08/zhuque-3-second-flight-successful-landing/). During the first flight, the rocket placed its payload into orbit, but the landing attempt failed. The current landing was LandSpace's first successful one, [according to Spaceflight Now](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/08/19/landspace-becomes-first-commercial-chinese-company-to-land-an-orbital-class-booster/).

LandSpace said it plans to return the stage to service as soon as possible, [Ars Technica reports](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/08/the-floodgates-are-open-after-another-chinese-company-lands-a-reusable-rocket/). The exact date of the next launch has not yet been announced.
