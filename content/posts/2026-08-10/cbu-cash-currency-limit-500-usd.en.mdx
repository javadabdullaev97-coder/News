Starting August 9, 2026, banks in Uzbekistan have been selling individuals up to $500 in cash without requiring a passport or other identification document — the previous threshold was $100.

## What changed

The new rule was introduced by a [joint resolution](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8184199) of the Board of the Central Bank of Uzbekistan (dated April 16, 2026, No. 343-В-14) and the Department for Combating Economic Crimes under the Prosecutor General's Office (dated April 15, 2026, No. 16). The document amends subparagraph "b" of paragraph 22 of the Internal Control Rules for Combating the Legalization of Proceeds from Criminal Activity, the Financing of Terrorism, and the Financing of the Proliferation of Weapons of Mass Destruction at commercial banks. The underlying act has been [in force since 2017](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/3212192) — registered by the Ministry of Justice under No. 2886 on May 23, 2017.

The resolution on the amendments was registered by the Ministry of Justice on May 8, 2026, under No. 2886-12. Under the general rule, such departmental acts take effect three months after registration — accordingly, the new threshold took effect on August 9, 2026.

The rules previously required a bank to identify an individual client purchasing cash foreign currency if the transaction amount exceeded $100. The resolution replaced that figure with $500: now presenting an identification document is mandatory only for transactions above the new threshold.

## Context

The same resolution changed another threshold within the same subparagraph: instead of being tied to "500 times the base calculation value," the rules now set a fixed amount — 175 million soums. The amended resolution's text does not specify which transaction this threshold applies to — that requires the full revised version of the rules incorporating the amendments.

The Central Bank did not publish a separate press release on the amendments taking effect on August 9 — the new version of the rules is established directly by the resolution registered with the Ministry of Justice.
