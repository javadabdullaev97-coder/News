The number of work entries by Uzbek citizens into Russia fell 13.2% in the first half of 2026, to more than 1.1 million, [Vedomosti reported](https://www.vedomosti.ru/society/articles/2026/08/06/1219138-chislo-rabochih-vezdov-v-rf-grazhdan-tsentralnoi-azii-sokratilos) on August 6, citing Russia's Border Service of the Federal Security Service (FSB).

## Border service figures: a decline across all three countries

The decline affected all three largest suppliers of labor migrants to Russia from Central Asia. Uzbekistan remains first by absolute number of entries — more than 1.1&nbsp;million from January to June 2026. But the trend is negative: down 13.2% from the first half of 2025. Tajikistan's drop is steeper — 494,500 entries, down 17.9%. Kyrgyzstan's is 289,100, down 16.7%.

Combined, citizens of the three countries entered Russia for work purposes about 1.9&nbsp;million times over the half-year — against more than 2.3&nbsp;million times in the same period of 2025. That amounts to an overall decline of about 15%. Vedomosti [obtained](https://www.vedomosti.ru/society/articles/2026/08/06/1219138-chislo-rabochih-vezdov-v-rf-grazhdan-tsentralnoi-azii-sokratilos) this data from the border service.

## Why the flow is shrinking: Central Bank of Uzbekistan data

The Central Bank of Uzbekistan, in its «[Labor Market Review](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/789/2p21t0slnrb1selfp0hrs1hmvv72n272/Mehnat-bozori-sharhi-2026_yil-I-chorak.pdf)» for the first quarter of 2026, cites its own data on the number of Uzbek labor migrants in Russia — based on Rosstat statistics. According to that data, the number of Uzbek citizens working in Russia under a work patent stood at 1.34&nbsp;million as of the end of the first quarter of 2026 — down 8.8% from the previous quarter and down 1.8% from a year earlier.

These two indicators are calculated using different methodologies and cover different periods, so they are not directly comparable: the border service records the number of work-purpose entries over a half-year, while the Central Bank of Uzbekistan counts active work patents at the end of a quarter.

The Central Bank of Uzbekistan attributes the decline to three factors: higher patent costs since the start of the year, the ruble's weakening in March, and the broadening geography of Uzbek labor migration. The same causes are visible in the structure of remittances: Russia's share of remittances to Uzbekistan fell from 77.6% in the first quarter of 2025 to 72.4% in the first quarter of 2026. At the same time, total remittances for the quarter rose 13% year-on-year, to $3.8&nbsp;billion.

## Where migrants are shifting to

The share of other destinations in remittances is growing. According to the Central Bank of Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan's share rose by 1 percentage point, to 4.1%, the share of European countries rose by 1 pp, to 3.3%, and South Korea's share rose by 0.6 pp, to 4.1%. The share of other countries rose by 2.6 pp, to 16.2%.

The number of labor migration permits for Turkey, cited by the Turkish migration agency in the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's report, stood at about 70,000 in the first quarter of 2026. That is 14% more than a year earlier. The number of Uzbek citizens in South Korea over the same period rose to 99,600.

Overall population mobility, meanwhile, is rising. In the first quarter of 2026, 1.63&nbsp;million Uzbek citizens traveled abroad for any purpose, not only work — 11.6% more than a year earlier. The number of air passengers rose 32%, to 2.25&nbsp;million.
