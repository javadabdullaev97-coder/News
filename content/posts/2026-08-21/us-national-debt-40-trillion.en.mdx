The gross U.S. national debt surpassed the $40 trillion mark for the first time on August 18, [according to data](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/debt-to-the-penny) from the U.S. Treasury Department — a day earlier, the figure was still below that threshold.

The exact figure at the close of trading on August 18 was $40.05 trillion ($40,047,425,768,420). On August 17, it stood at $39.99 trillion — below $40 trillion.

The debt grew by $3 trillion in just over a year: it [crossed the $37 trillion mark](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/debt-to-the-penny) by August 11, 2025. The $35 trillion threshold was crossed on [July 26, 2024](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/debt-to-the-penny) — meaning the debt grew by $5 trillion in two years and three weeks.

The debt is equivalent to 101% of U.S. gross domestic product in 2026, [Al Jazeera calculated](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/20/us-debt-hits-40-trillion-who-does-washington-owe-and-why-does-it-matter). If current trends continue, the figure could rise to 120% of GDP by 2036 — above the postwar-era record of 106%. Servicing the debt costs the federal budget about $1.1 trillion a year — the second-largest line item in the budget, according to the same data.

## Treasury Increases Bond Buybacks

The U.S. Treasury Department [announced](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0607) on August 19 that it is at least doubling the volume of buyback operations for long-term bonds — issues with 10–20 and 20–30 year maturities. The maximum size of a single operation will grow from $2 billion to at least $4 billion. The changes take effect September 9 and will remain in place through November 4 — the end of the current debt refinancing quarter.

The department attributed the decision to steady demand from market participants and a large volume of high-quality bids at previous buyback auctions.

## Who Holds the U.S. Debt

About $21 trillion of the debt is held domestically: the Federal Reserve ($4.528 trillion), mutual funds ($5.195 trillion), pension funds ($1.135 trillion) and commercial banks ($2.083 trillion). The rest is held by other domestic creditors, from state governments to private investors, [Al Jazeera calculated](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/20/us-debt-hits-40-trillion-who-does-washington-owe-and-why-does-it-matter). Another roughly $11 trillion is held by foreign holders, the largest of which are Japan ($1.203 trillion), the United Kingdom ($889 billion) and China ($683 billion). The share of foreign holders grew from 5% in 1970 to 32% in 2025.

Some economists call the rising cost of servicing the debt amid high interest rates a "doom loop" — a vicious cycle. The more expensive it becomes for the Treasury to borrow, the more it has to borrow just to pay interest on debt already accumulated. The debt first surpassed $30 trillion in January 2022, [South China Morning Post notes](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/3364613/us-debt-hits-us40-trillion-high-raising-doom-loop-risk).

"The federal debt is already raising the cost of living and crowding out other spending and investment, threatening the economy and Americans' long-term well-being," Bipartisan Policy Center President Margaret Spellings [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/19/us-national-debt-passes-record-40-trillion).
