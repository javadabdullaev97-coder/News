The US military has used up nearly 80% of THAAD missile defense interceptors and almost its entire reserve of long-range precision missiles over five months of war with Iran, CNN and Reuters reported on August 4–5. At the same time, the cargo ship Minoan Pioneer was shelled and caught fire off the coast of Oman.

## US: Missile Defense and Long-Range Missile Stocks Nearly Depleted

CNN and Reuters [reported](https://www.rbc.ru/politics/05/08/2026/6a7276d39a79473b96ac71c8) on August 4, citing sources familiar with internal military data. US weapons stockpiles have fallen to a dangerous level in two areas during the war with Iran.

The first is missile defense systems. According to CNN, the US military has used up about 80% of its prewar stock of THAAD interceptors and roughly half of its Patriot interceptors. Senior military commanders privately describe the Pentagon's overall munitions reserve as "dangerously low."

The second is long-range strike missiles. According to Reuters, the US Army has used up nearly its entire stock of ATACMS tactical missiles and PrSM precision-strike missiles, along with just under half of the global reserve of Tomahawk cruise missiles.

The White House and the Pentagon have denied claims of a shortage. The Trump administration said the US has "more munitions than anyone else in the world" and that the country's defense manufacturers are producing them "more than ever." A Pentagon spokesperson told CNN that the armed forces have "everything needed to act at the time and place the president chooses." Doubts about readiness for new strikes were among the reasons Trump called off another strike on Iran last week, RBC [reports](https://www.rbc.ru/politics/05/08/2026/6a7276d39a79473b96ac71c8), citing Reuters.

## Number of Wounded US Troops Exceeds 686

The Pentagon updated casualty figures on August 4–5: the number of wounded US service members since the start of the war with Iran has exceeded 686, Vedomosti [reports](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/05/1218901-ranenih-konflikte-iranom), citing Pentagon data.

Of these, 417 service members were wounded in Operation Epic Fury, which ended on May 5, and about 270 in combat operations that have been underway since July 7. The Pentagon lists 18 service members as killed: 14 during Epic Fury and another 4 since July 7.

## Cargo Ship Minoan Pioneer Shelled and Catches Fire Off Oman

The Liberian-flagged cargo ship Minoan Pioneer came under fire on August 4 in the Strait of Hormuz near the coast of Oman, Kommersant [reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8862193), citing data from the British maritime risk management company Vanguard.

An unidentified projectile struck the ship's engine room, after which the fire spread to the crew quarters, Interfax [reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107370). The crew abandoned the ship, and one sailor is listed as missing. Neither Vanguard nor Reuters has established who carried out the attack.

## US Says It Has Intercepted 45 Ships in Three Weeks of Blockade

US Central Command (CENTCOM) said that as of August 4, since the start of the renewed naval blockade of Iran, US forces have redirected 45 commercial vessels, disabled two, and inspected another two. Of these, 10 vessels were redirected over the past two days, Interfax [reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107467). There is no independent confirmation of these figures. The blockade has been in effect since July 14.

## Iran Threatens Strikes on US Ships

Mohsen Rezaei, senior adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader, said on August 4 in an interview with Iranian state media that Tehran will not allow the opening of a "second corridor" through the Strait of Hormuz and will strike US warships if the United States reroutes shipping or continues the blockade. "We will not allow a second corridor to open. Even if the US sends warships, we will strike them," Rezaei [said](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/04/iran-usa-military-ships-in-hormuz). He added that "American ships and bases will certainly face serious danger."

Alongside these reports, Iran and Oman continue negotiations on opening a separate maritime corridor through the strait — LEAP News [covered](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-progress) this track separately on August 5.

## Why This Matters for Uzbekistan

Uzbekistan does not receive oil through the Strait of Hormuz directly, but it depends on global oil prices for its imports of petroleum products. The shelling of the cargo ship off Oman and reports of depleted US military stockpiles are signals that markets typically interpret as rising escalation risk and a trigger for oil price spikes.

## What's Next

Neither the Pentagon nor the White House has given a timeline for replenishing missile defense and long-range missile stockpiles. CENTCOM has not said whether it plans to expand the list of intercepted vessels. Iran has not specified under what exact conditions its forces would open fire on US ships.
