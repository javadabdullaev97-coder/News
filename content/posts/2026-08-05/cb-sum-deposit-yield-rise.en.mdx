The weighted average rate on term deposits in sums in Uzbekistan rose to 17.6% annually in June 2026, up from 17.1% in May — the first increase after three months of decline, according to [data from the Central Bank of the Republic of Uzbekistan](https://cbu.uz/upload/medialibrary/342/i0el22ifurmxh1li50qtnct8wt4hpye3/depozit_uz.pdf) (CBU).

## What the CBU data shows

According to [CBU calculations](https://cbu.uz/upload/medialibrary/342/i0el22ifurmxh1li50qtnct8wt4hpye3/depozit_uz.pdf), the rate on sum deposits declined for three consecutive months: from 18.1% in March to 17.4% in April and 17.1% in May. In June it rose by 0.5 percentage points to 17.6% annually. Year on year, the rate is still lower: in June 2025 it stood at 19.7%.

The June increase was driven mainly by short-term deposits — those with terms of up to one year. Their rate rose from 14.7% in May to 15.4% in June. The yield on long-term deposits — over one year — held steady at 18.1% annually, unchanged from the previous month.

The dynamics differ for households and businesses. The average rate on deposits from individuals fell to 19.2% annually in June — down from 19.7% in May and 21.4% a year earlier. The rate on deposits from legal entities, by contrast, rose slightly, from 15.2% in May to 15.3% in June, though it was at 17.9% a year earlier.

## Foreign-currency deposits: a spike in May, a pullback in June

Rates on term deposits in foreign currency moved less steadily. The average rate fell to 3.5% annually in March, rose to 4.1% in April, and then jumped sharply to 5.5% annually in May. In June the increase reversed into a decline: the rate fell to 4.5% annually, according to [CBU statistics](https://cbu.uz/upload/medialibrary/342/i0el22ifurmxh1li50qtnct8wt4hpye3/depozit_uz.pdf).

The May spike and June pullback are almost entirely tied to deposits from legal entities: their average foreign-currency rate rose from 2.8% in April to 5.6% in May, then fell to 3.8% in June. The rate on foreign-currency deposits from households was steadier — 4.9% in April, 5.3% in May and 5.3% in June.

## Real yield: adjusted for inflation

The CBU also publishes rates on sum deposits from households and businesses net of inflation expectations. For individuals, the real yield in June was 8.3% annually, against 8.7% in May — a decline of 0.4 percentage points over the month, but 1.1 percentage points higher than a year earlier. For legal entities, the real rate rose slightly instead, to 4.9% from 4.7% in May, still below last year's 5.4%.

The CBU publishes monthly statistics on deposit and loan rates on a [dedicated page of its website](https://cbu.uz/ru/statistics/rates/) — but its figures for individual months diverge from the bank's PDF infographic data, which this article relies on.
