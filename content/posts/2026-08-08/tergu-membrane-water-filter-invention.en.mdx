The Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovations held the final of the national contest "Kelajak muhandislari" ("Engineers of the Future") on August 5–7, where teams defended their projects before a jury in three categories.

## TerSU's project: membrane filters from local raw materials

In a separate post, the Ministry of Higher Education [highlighted](https://t.me/eduuz/50240) a development by Termez State University (TerSU) — the UZECOMEM (Water Technology) project, presented during the "Kelajak muhandislari" final. The project's lead is Botir Jumaev, a lecturer at TerSU's Faculty of Chemistry.

The project's goal is to establish production of highly effective membrane filters for water purification based on local raw materials. According to the [ministry's description](https://t.me/eduuz/50240), the filters are resistant to chemical and mechanical stress and trap various substances and salts contained in water.

## How the final unfolded

The contest final [took place](https://t.me/eduuz/50262) on August 5–7: participants presented their developments and defended them before a jury in three categories — "Best Idea," "Best Project," and "Best Invention." The organizer is the Ministry of Higher Education, which published video recordings of all three days of the final on its Telegram channel.

The ministry has not yet published the full list of winners, the prize fund, or plans for implementing or scaling TerSU's development.
