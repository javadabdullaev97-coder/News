US President Donald Trump said on August 7 that the war with Iran "can't go much longer" — the same day the Financial Times reported that Iran's oil export terminal on Kharg Island had gone idle.

"I think it's going to end pretty soon. I don't think they can go much longer," Trump said, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/trump-says-war-cant-go-much-longer-whats-the-latest-on). According to him, talks with Tehran are going "very well," though Iran denies direct contacts with Washington and says it is only negotiating with Oman as a mediator.

## Kharg Terminal Not Receiving Tankers

The terminal on Kharg Island, which handles about 90% of Iran's oil exports, has not received tankers since July 31 — operations there halted after the US resumed its naval blockade, [the Financial Times reports](https://www.ft.com/content/6bc9c93d-1b75-4512-8bc2-ba33cc3c4a53), citing data from maritime intelligence firms Kpler, Energy Aspects and Windward.

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz overall remains low and uneven: according to PBS NewsHour, 84 vessels passed through the strait last week — down from 45 the week before, while weekly traffic before the war exceeded 700 vessels, [PBS reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/a-deal-with-iran-over-the-strait-of-hormuz-may-require-a-compromise-from-trump). Al Jazeera, citing Reuters data, cites a different slice of the same week — from Monday through Thursday traffic totaled 33 vessels, against 50 the previous week, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/trump-says-war-cant-go-much-longer-whats-the-latest-on).

## Iran Seeks Ban on US and Israeli Ships

On August 6, Bloomberg [reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/iran-seeks-bar-on-us-ships-in-hormuz-as-deal-with-oman-advances) that Iran intends to seek a ban on passage through the strait and the Persian Gulf for ships from the US, Israel and other countries Tehran considers hostile, and to demand compensation from them for "damage inflicted" before granting passage rights. The plans have not yet been formally adopted.

At the same time, talks with Oman on a framework agreement for the strait continue on the terms LEAP News [covered](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-agreed-in-principle) on August 7: a temporary 60-day route without mandatory transit fees, which still needs approval from Iran's Supreme National Security Council. Neither side has reported new details of the framework agreement since August 7.

## What's Next

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said "progress" has been made in talks over the strait but stressed there is "no finality yet," [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-us-denies-negotiations-begin-strikes-called-off/). Neither side has announced a formal agreement so far.

## How This Differs From LEAP News' Previous Report

LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-agreed-in-principle) on August 7 on an agreement in principle between Iran and Oman on a route through the strait — neither side has announced a final text since then. What's new here is Trump's statement about the war ending soon, the Financial Times data on the effective halt of exports through the Kharg terminal, and Iran's intent to seek a ban on US and Israeli ships. LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/us-iran-war-missile-depletion-tanker-attack-hormuz) data on US losses and munitions depletion on August 5 — neither side had published new updates on those as of August 7.
