The Hydrometeorological Service Agency under the National Committee for Ecology and Climate Change of the Republic of Uzbekistan (Uzhydromet) on August 6 launched, in test mode, an aerological balloon with a radiosonde in Bukhara Region for the first time in years of independence.

## What the probe recorded

Uzhydromet raised the balloon ahead of the 35th anniversary of Uzbekistan's independence, [the agency said](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21896) on its Telegram channel. The balloon carries a modern radiosonde — a device that records air temperature, relative humidity, atmospheric pressure, and wind speed and direction at different altitudes in real time. During the flight, the device transmits this data to a ground receiving station.

## Why such observations matter

Uzhydromet uses the aerological data it collects for synoptic analysis, improving weather forecast accuracy, and early detection of dangerous hydrometeorological phenomena. The agency also uses the data to provide aviation, agriculture, and other sectors with reliable hydrometeorological information, [the agency's post says](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21896). Uzhydromet itself called the launch the start of a new stage in the development of the country's hydrometeorological service and the study of the upper atmosphere.

## Who is responsible for the observations

Aerological measurements are carried out by Uzhydromet — the agency [responsible](https://gov.uz/ru/hydromet/pages/agentlik-haqida) for developing hydrometeorological observations and providing sectors of the economy with weather data. Uzhydromet also monitors the water regime of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya river basins and prepares short-term and long-term weather and climate forecasts for the entire country.

Uzhydromet's post describes the August 6 launch as a test — the agency has not yet given a schedule or said how regularly it plans to launch aerological balloons in the future.
