According to the Financial Times, cited by [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-dissolved-the-team-built-to-catch-catastrophic-ai-risks-reassigning-its-work-to-other-groups/) and [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2237916/openai-reportedly-disbanded-its-preparedness-team-as-part-of-streamlining-process/), OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team, which assessed catastrophic risks from AI models, in late July 2026.

OpenAI itself has made no official statement about the reasons for the dissolution — the information is based on the FT report and sources inside the company. Work on assessing biological and cyber risks was reassigned to staff in existing divisions: a separate Preparedness unit no longer exists, [The Decoder reports](https://the-decoder.com/openai-dissolved-the-team-built-to-catch-catastrophic-ai-risks-reassigning-its-work-to-other-groups/). Dylan Scandinaro, who previously led the team, now works on risks from recursively self-improving systems, but without the structure he previously had under him.

When reporters asked for comment, OpenAI called the move a "streamlining process" ahead of the IPO. CEO Sam Altman had earlier asked employees to drop "side projects" and focus on ChatGPT, [Engadget reports](https://www.engadget.com/2237916/openai-reportedly-disbanded-its-preparedness-team-as-part-of-streamlining-process/). The Preparedness team's dissolution came days after an incident involving the company's test models. [OpenAI said](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/open-ai-cyber-models-hack-hugging-face.html) that in July 2026 the models broke out of a controlled environment and hacked into Hugging Face's infrastructure. That allowed the models to obtain answers to a cybersecurity test on their own.

## Top Executives Depart

OpenAI is losing senior executives at the same time. On August 11, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/11/longtime-openai-executive-brad-lightcap-leaves-as-shakeup-at-ai-lab-continues.html) that Brad Lightcap is leaving the company after eight years to "start something new." He served as OpenAI's chief operating officer until April 2026, then moved into a "special projects" role. On August 13, OpenAI [announced](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/openai-denise-dresser-executive-exits.html) the departure of chief revenue officer Denise Dresser, who had held the position for eight months. She will be replaced by Dali Rajic, former president and chief operating officer of Wiz. CNBC called it the second major executive departure in a matter of days.

## Reaction and IPO Context

Kevin McCormick, founder of the startup SignAudit.AI, called the talent exodus a "huge red flag" ahead of the IPO — the company's initial public offering of shares. [CNBC cited](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/open-ai-ipo-red-flag.html) his assessment, posted on X, on August 14. OpenAI [confirmed](https://fortune.com/2026/06/09/openai-files-confidential-s-1-sec-ipo/) it confidentially filed an S-1 form with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on June 9, 2026, at an $852 billion valuation. The company said the exact timing of the offering has not yet been determined.

Employees are unhappy with frequent reorganizations, [The Decoder writes](https://the-decoder.com/openai-dissolved-the-team-built-to-catch-catastrophic-ai-risks-reassigning-its-work-to-other-groups/), citing sources inside the company. One of them described a "growing sense of responsibility and anxiety" over insufficient attention to safety following the Hugging Face incident.
