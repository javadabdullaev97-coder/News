The National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan [reported](https://t.me/uzstataxborot/69173) on August 5 that consumer prices fell 0.1% in July 2026 — the annual consumer price index stood at 106.4%.

## What Got Cheaper and What Got More Expensive During the Month

According to [the National Statistics Committee](https://t.me/uzstataxborot/69173), food prices fell an average of 0.91% in July compared with June. Non-food goods rose 0.3%, and paid services to the population rose 0.5%.

Within the food category, the trends diverged. Eggplants (−42.8%), bell peppers (−39.4%) and tomatoes (−24.0%) saw the sharpest price drops — a seasonal decline driven by the summer harvest. At the same time, carrots (+12.6%), long-grain rice (+6.4%), round-grain rice (+6.1%), mung beans (+4.2%) and onions (+1.7%) became more expensive.

Among non-food goods and services, the biggest increases were for AI-98 and AI-100 grade gasoline (+10.0%) and AI-95 (+8.5%). Household waste collection fees rose 2.7%, and mobile phone service prices rose 2.4%.

The agency [notes](https://t.me/uzstataxborot/69173) that the index is calculated using data from more than 10,000 retail outlets nationwide, which report monthly prices for 419 types of goods and 98 types of services.

## Annual Figure

On an annual basis, the consumer price index stood at 106.4% in July, meaning prices rose an average of 6.4% over the past 12 months. That is 2.5 percentage points below the July 2025 figure, when annual inflation stood at 8.9%, [according to National Statistics Committee data](https://t.me/uzstataxborot/69173).

The figure was almost unchanged from June, when annual inflation also stood at 6.4%, according to [the Central Bank of Uzbekistan](https://cbu.uz/ru/monetary-policy/publications/press-releases/4256870/).

## Context: Central Bank Rate and Inflation Target

The Central Bank of Uzbekistan's board [kept](https://cbu.uz/ru/monetary-policy/publications/press-releases/4256870/) the key rate unchanged at 14% per annum on July 29. The regulator forecasts overall inflation at 6.5% for 2026 and aims to bring price growth down to its medium-term target of 5% over time. The board's next rate meeting is scheduled for September 16, 2026.
