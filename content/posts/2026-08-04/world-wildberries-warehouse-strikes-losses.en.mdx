Losses of Wildberries sellers from Ukrainian strikes on warehouses in Russia could reach 215–280 billion rubles (≈2.3–3.0 billion euros), [Forbes](https://www.dw.com/ru/forbes-потери-селлеров-wildberries-могут-достигать-280-млрд-рублей/a-78214022) estimated on August 3 — following drone attacks on the company's warehouses on August 3–4.

## Forbes Estimate: Up to 280 Billion Rubles

According to [DW, citing Forbes](https://www.dw.com/ru/forbes-потери-селлеров-wildberries-могут-достигать-280-млрд-рублей/a-78214022), Sergei Semko, an analyst at the research agency Data Insight, estimated the potential losses of sellers trading through Wildberries at 215–280 billion rubles. The estimate does not account for the strike on the warehouse in Vladimir region on the morning of August 3. The methodology is based on a 2024 precedent: after a fire at a 112,000-square-meter warehouse in Shushary, the company compensated sellers nearly 35 billion rubles for inventory.

The warehouse near Vladimir became, according to DW, the 18th company facility attacked since the start of the campaign.

According to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/russia-weighs-aid-to-sellers-after-ukraine-hits-e-commerce-giant), since mid-July Ukraine has struck Wildberries logistics facilities around a dozen times, killing at least eight people. Wildberries accounts for 52% of all online orders in Russia, Bloomberg notes.

## New Strikes on August 3–4

On the morning of August 3, drones attacked a Wildberries warehouse complex in the Sobinsky district of Vladimir region — LEAP News [reported on the strike](/article/kyiv-zaporizhzhia-strikes-russia-refineries) on the day of the attack. According to updated data from [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1107203), citing regional governor Alexander Avdeev, four people were injured (two were initially reported). The fire covered about 100,000 square meters. Wildberries-Russ said part of the goods were saved while the fire was contained. The Investigative Committee opened a criminal case under the terrorism statute.

Overnight into August 4, a drone attacked a warehouse linked to Wildberries in the village of Novoselki near Chekhov in Moscow region — five people died and ten more were injured, said Moscow region governor Vorobyov, [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c151pkww79zo) writes.

On the morning of August 4, a drone attacked a Wildberries-Russ logistics facility in Krasny Bor, Tosno district, Leningrad region — a fire broke out there, employees had been evacuated in advance, and no casualties were reported, [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1107307) said, citing the company and regional governor Alexander Drozdenko.

At 08:47 on August 4, drone debris damaged the wall of a Wildberries-Russ warehouse in the village of Emmaus, Kalininsky district, Tver region, [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1107316) reported, citing acting regional governor Vitaly Korolyov. Warehouse employees were not injured.

## Russia Prepares Support Measures for Sellers

Authorities are considering a support package for sellers affected by the attacks, [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/russia-weighs-aid-to-sellers-after-ukraine-hits-e-commerce-giant) writes, citing sources: a deferral of loan payments for sellers of up to a year is under discussion, along with the possibility for banks to restructure such debts without forming additional reserves. State-owned bank VTB could play a key role in the scheme, while Sberbank has already developed its own restructuring program for Wildberries sellers. The Kremlin confirmed that discussions are underway at the government level, but a final decision has not yet been made.

Sellers, according to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-03/russia-weighs-aid-to-sellers-after-ukraine-hits-e-commerce-giant), warn that without substantial support, thousands of small businesses risk closing, and that tax holidays and loan deferrals matter more to them than the symbolic compensation the company has already offered.

## Logistics Relocates

Wildberries has redirected part of its deliveries to other facilities, [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/08/03/1218417-o-perestroike-logistiki) writes. Boxed and individual pallet deliveries are now accepted in Tula, Tver, Yekaterinburg and Krasny Bor, while mono-pallets have been redirected to Omsk, Yekaterinburg and Novosibirsk. A new facility has opened for FBS shipments, and order processing at the "Vladimir: Sukhodol" sorting center is temporarily free. Sellers have been allowed to cancel deliveries to the damaged warehouse or reschedule them without recalculating coefficients or incurring penalties for transit shipments.

## Regional Dimension

The attacks on Wildberries' logistics come as the company expands in Central Asia. In June 2026, at the Tashkent International Investment Forum, Robert Mirzoyan, CEO of the merged Wildberries & Russ company, announced investments of over $300 million in a Central Asian logistics hub based in Uzbekistan, [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/06/18/1206766-wildberries-v-uzbekistane) writes. The hub includes a sorting center and warehouse complex in Tashkent region covering about 170,000 square meters. The company entered the Uzbek market in 2022; according to figures cited by the outlet, the turnover of Uzbek goods through Wildberries exceeded $1.5 billion in 2025.

Wildberries is also looking for warehouse space in Kazakhstan in case part of its capacity needs to be relocated from the strike zone. Kazakhstan's Ministry of Trade and Integration said on July 29 that it had not received any official requests from the company on the matter. The ministry also noted that "it is fundamentally important that any projects by international companies be implemented on a mutually beneficial basis, taking into account the country's national interests," [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/07/29/1217345-kazahstana-wildberries) reported. Meanwhile, the RWB group is already building a warehouse of more than 100,000 square meters near Almaty and a 160,000-square-meter facility in Astana, due to be commissioned in 2027.
