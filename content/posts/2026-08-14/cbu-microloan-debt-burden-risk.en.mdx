The Central Bank of Uzbekistan on July 7, 2026, published its [Financial Stability Report for 2025](https://cbu.uz/en/press_center/releases/4124365/): the debt burden of the population on microloans (DSTI — the ratio of loan payments to income) rose from 37% to 40%.

## What the Central Bank review showed

Outstanding loans to individuals grew 24% in 2025 to reach [220.3 trillion soums](https://cbu.uz/upload/medialibrary/971/ex1bwz6uennm2rwvm1q8ekvp47d407re/2025_yil-uchun-moliyaviy-barqarorlik-sharhi.pdf) (about $18.3 billion at the Central Bank's exchange rate as of January 1, 2026). The share of retail loans in banks' aggregate portfolio rose from 33% to 36%, while the combined portfolio of microfinance organizations and pawnshops grew 45% over the year.

At the same time, the overall debt burden indicator for individuals with bank loans actually declined slightly — from 38% to 37%: the improvement came from mortgage and auto loans. The deterioration is concentrated specifically in the microloan segment, where the number of borrowers approached 2.7 million people, [the Central Bank said in a press release](https://cbu.uz/en/press_center/releases/4124365/). The regulator classified credit risks in this segment as factors requiring special attention.

The share of non-performing loans across the banking system as a whole declined by 1 percentage point over the year, to 3%. In other words, the regulator's concern is not deteriorating payment discipline as such, but the growing debt burden amid continued lending — debt service quality rather than loan issuance volume.

Household deposits grew 30% over the year to 169.1 trillion soums (about $14.1 billion) and continue to exceed the volume of retail loans.

The review also provides macroeconomic context: real GDP grew 7.7% in 2025, while annual inflation stood at 7.3%. The regulator assesses banking system stability indicators — 18.3% capital adequacy and a 208% liquidity coverage ratio — as comfortable.

## Senate requires credit ads to warn of risks

The Senate of the Oliy Majlis, at its eighteenth plenary session, [passed a law on August 7](https://uza.uz/ru/posts/informaciya-o-vosemnadcatom-plenarnom-zasedanii-senata-oliy-mazhlisa-respubliki-uzbekistan_892436) amending the Law "On Advertising": advertising of financial services must now include a warning that using these services and failing to meet debt obligations may entail financial risks.

At the same session, senators passed five more laws — including on market surveillance, general product safety, and raising the maximum pension for military personnel with 30 or more years of service from 75% to 100%.
