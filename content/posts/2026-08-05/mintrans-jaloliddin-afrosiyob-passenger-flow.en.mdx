Uzbekistan's Ministry of Transport [said in a Telegram post](https://t.me/Mintrans_uz/28552) on August 4 that passenger traffic on the Jaloliddin Manguberdi and Afrosiyob trains on the Tashkent-Samarkand-Bukhara-Khiva routes will double.

## What the Ministry of Transport posted

The ministry posted a video in its "Fakt 24" segment on its Telegram channel, titled "Passenger traffic on the joint Jaloliddin Manguberdi and Afrosiyob trains will double." The [post](https://t.me/Mintrans_uz/28552) contains a video 3 minutes and 17 seconds long. The ministry did not provide a written explanation alongside the video: the claim of a twofold increase in traffic is not yet backed by specific baseline and target figures in text form.

## Jaloliddin Manguberdi: the first three months

The Jaloliddin Manguberdi high-speed train entered service on the Tashkent-Khiva route on May 5, 2026. The train, built by South Korea's Hyundai Rotem, covers 1,220 km with stops in Samarkand, Navoi, Bukhara and Urgench in 7.5 hours — twice as fast as regular trains, which take about 14 hours for the same route, railway operator JSC O'zbekiston Temir Yo'llari [said](https://railway.uz/ru/informatsionnaya_sluzhba/novosti/37807/). The train has 389 seats across seven cars, with a top speed of 250 km/h.

The train departs from Tashkent on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays, and from Khiva on Wednesdays, Fridays and Sundays.

By July 29, the first train [had carried](https://t.me/uzrailways_uz/16600) about 30,000 passengers, according to data from a press tour that O'zbekiston Temir Yo'llari organized for journalists at the high-speed train service facility. Uzbekistan ordered six such trains from Hyundai Rotem in total: three had been delivered to the country by the end of July, with the third undergoing technical testing.

## Afrosiyob: on the Tashkent-Bukhara line since 2011

Afrosiyob trains (Spanish-built Talgo 250) have been running since 2011, when the first two trains were delivered for the Tashkent-Samarkand route. Two more trains arrived in 2017, and in 2019 a [contract](https://www.railway.uz/uz/informatsionnaya_sluzhba/novosti/24161/) was signed with the Spanish side for two additional trains worth 57.5 million euros. Afrosiyob's top speed is 230 km/h; the Tashkent-Samarkand trip takes 2 hours 8 minutes, Tashkent-Bukhara takes 3 hours 51 minutes, and Tashkent-Karshi takes 3 hours 21 minutes.

## State target: doubling traffic by 2030

The Ministry of Transport's announcement of a twofold rise in passenger traffic fits into a broader railway development program. Presidential Resolution No. PP-391 of December 27, 2025 [set a target](https://gov.uz/ru/mintrans/news/view/116202) to increase electric train passenger traffic on domestic routes from 10.5 million to 23.5 million people a year by 2030. The number of routes is to grow from 40 to 53. The document also calls for completing electrification of the Bukhara-Urgench-Khiva section (465 km), which has already opened the way for high-speed trains. The Miskin-Nukus section (196 km) is planned to be electrified by the end of 2026.
