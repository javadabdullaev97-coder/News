Yemen's Houthis on Sunday, August 9, attacked the Saudi Aramco oil refinery in Jizan, Saudi Arabia, with a drone — two days after Riyadh signed a defense pact with Turkey and Pakistan.

## What happened at the Jizan refinery

Early on August 9, a fire [broke out](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/saudi-arabia-says-fire-extinguished-at-aramco-facility-in-jizan) at the refinery in Jizan, in southwestern Saudi Arabia near the border with Yemen. Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy said the fire had been extinguished and there were no casualties; it did not specify the cause of the blaze. The Jizan refinery processes [400,000 barrels of oil per day](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3363465/yemens-houthis-attack-saudi-refinery-after-kingdom-signs-defence-pact).

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/saudi-arabia-says-fire-extinguished-at-aramco-facility-in-jizan) on the social media platform X that "Yemen's armed forces successfully struck the Aramco refinery in Jizan with a drone, and the strike was precise." According to him, the attack was a response to Saudi drones violating Yemeni airspace over the provinces of Saada and Hajjah. Official Saudi Arabia did not comment on this version.

## Strike on the port of Al-Mokha and casualties

The same day, the Houthis attacked the port city of Al-Mokha on Yemen's Red Sea coast. According to Saree, the strike was carried out with "a large number of ballistic missiles and drones." Yemen's Ministry of Transport [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/saudi-arabia-says-fire-extinguished-at-aramco-facility-in-jizan) the port infrastructure suffered serious damage.

According to the military spokesman, the attack on the port killed at least seven people — the figure is as of August 9 and may be revised.

## Yemen prepares retaliatory strikes

Yemen's internationally recognized government [announced](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/9/yemeni-army-warns-houthis-after-attacks-heighten-escalation-risk) it was preparing retaliatory military operations against Houthi positions, accusing the group of escalating strikes on military facilities. Yemen's army warned the Houthis amid a growing risk of the conflict spreading.

## Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad defense pact

The refinery attack came two days after Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan [signed](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it) a collective defense agreement in Mecca on August 7. The document was named the Mecca Joint Deterrence Agreement. It was signed by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif.

The agreement establishes the principle that "an armed attack on any of the three countries will be regarded as an attack on all three." A Turkish official described the pact as defensive and not directed against any specific actor; the agreement is open to other countries in the region joining, he told Reuters. Pakistan's Foreign Ministry [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it) the agreement promotes "peace, security and stability in the region and beyond." Iran has not issued an official statement on the pact; Iranian parliament member Ibrahim Rezaei called the document a "paper agreement" that would not provide security.

## Context: the Houthis and the US-Israel war against Iran

The Iran-backed Houthis declared a naval blockade of the Red Sea against Saudi Arabia in July, in response to actions by Saudi military forces that Riyadh denies. The escalation comes amid the ongoing US-Israel war against Iran and tension around the years-long truce between the Houthis and the Yemeni government.
