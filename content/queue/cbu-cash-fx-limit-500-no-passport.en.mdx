The Central Bank of Uzbekistan and the Department for Combating Economic Crimes under the Prosecutor General's Office on August 9 raised the threshold for simplified identification when banks sell cash currency from $100 to $500.

## What changed

The CBU and the department amended the [Internal Control Rules for Commercial Banks on Combating the Legalization of Proceeds from Criminal Activity, the Financing of Terrorism and the Financing of the Proliferation of Weapons of Mass Destruction](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/3212190) — the document has been in force since 2017.

The amendments were approved by [Resolution No. 343-B of the CBU Board dated April 16, 2026, and Resolution No. 16 of the Department dated April 15, 2026](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8184199). The Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Uzbekistan registered the document on May 8, 2026, under number 2886-12. The amendments took effect three months after registration — on August 9, 2026.

Clause 22 of the rules defines the cases when a bank may conduct simplified customer identification — without presenting a passport or other identity document. In the third paragraph of subclause "b," the figure "100" was replaced with "500." A customer can now buy up to $500 in cash foreign currency at a bank without presenting documents — the previous threshold was $100.

## Second threshold in the same clause

The same resolution changed another threshold — for transactions made without opening a bank account. The wording "500 times the base calculation value" was replaced with a fixed amount — 175 million soums (about $14,600 at the CBU exchange rate for August 10 — [11,952.10 soums per dollar](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/)). Unlike the threshold for cash currency, this amount is now fixed in soums and is no longer tied to the base calculation value, which is revised annually.
