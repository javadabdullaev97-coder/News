Nvidia struck a $6 billion deal with American AI startup Poolside on August 20: the company is getting a license for the Model Factory platform and offering jobs to 109 of the startup's employees, [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-is-acquiring-poolsides-model-factory-and-109-employees-for-6-billion/) reports.

Nvidia also [invested $1 billion in Poolside itself](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-is-acquiring-poolsides-model-factory-and-109-employees-for-6-billion/), valuing the company at $12 billion excluding the new investment. The Model Factory license is non-exclusive: Poolside retains the right to sell the technology to other buyers. Model Factory is Poolside's platform for building generative AI models for coding tasks; the startup used it to train its own Laguna model.

News of the deal first surfaced in a letter from Poolside's founders to investors, which [Newcomer obtained](https://www.newcomer.co/p/sources-poolside-strikes-6-billion) — an American outlet specializing in technology deals.

Poolside's three co-founders are staying with the company and will keep working on the startup independently, [The Decoder reports](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-is-acquiring-poolsides-model-factory-and-109-employees-for-6-billion/), citing the letter to investors. In it, the founders described what happened as neither an acquisition nor an acquihire — a term for deals in which an entire startup team joins the buyer's staff to hire specialists rather than to acquire the business. At Poolside, only 109 employees received offers from Nvidia, not the entire staff: the three founders are not among them.

As of August 21, neither Nvidia nor Poolside had published official press releases about the deal. [Nvidia's news archive](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news) and [Poolside's website](https://poolside.ai) contain no materials about it.

According to The Decoder, the deal with Poolside resembles Nvidia's earlier agreements with Groq (about $20 billion) and Enfabrica (about $900 million). In both cases, Nvidia gained access to the startups' technology and specialists without formally acquiring the companies.
