U.S. President Donald Trump signed the National Space Transportation Policy memorandum NSPM-17 on August 20, setting a goal of more than 1,000 rocket launches and landings in the United States annually by 2030.

## What's Changing

The memorandum [replaces](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-17/) the 2013 presidential directive (Presidential Policy Directive 26) and rescinds a number of requirements from the 2004 space transportation policy. The document states: "By 2030, our space transportation ranges must grow to support more than 1,000 launches and landings annually." The new policy [calls](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-17/) access to space "a vital national and economic security interest." Agencies are directed to encourage joint construction of launch infrastructure with private companies and to speed up permitting and environmental clearances. They are also directed to develop a transparent system for recovering the cost of shared space services. Specific companies — SpaceX, United Launch Alliance or Blue Origin — are not named in the text of the memorandum or the fact sheet: it addresses the industry as a whole.

## What Agencies Must Do

Within 180 days, the Secretary of War and the NASA Administrator must develop federal criteria for allocating launch range time to enable more efficient use of resources by commercial operators. The document refers to the defense secretary as the "Secretary of War" — the position's official title since 2025. Within the same timeframe, the Secretary of Transportation must identify sites for new launch complexes and incorporate space launch management into the airspace modernization program. Within 90 days, the Secretary of the Interior must identify federal lands for a separate landing site for returning rocket stages and capsules. On spectrum for space activities, the Department of Commerce and the Federal Communications Commission must report within 180 days, and every two years thereafter. The Department of Commerce itself must prepare a plan for developing a federal landing site within 240 days. The Assistant to the President for Science and Technology must coordinate a space industry development strategy within 180 days, and every two years thereafter. The State Department, together with the Department of Commerce, must update export controls within 120 days and every two years thereafter.

## Who Is Affected

The memorandum's direct implementers are the Department of Defense, NASA, the Departments of Transportation, the Interior and Commerce, the U.S. State Department, and the Federal Communications Commission. For commercial launch and landing operators in the United States, the document means faster approvals for sites and ranges and a new system for paying to use federal infrastructure. The memorandum itself does not specify which companies will fall under these rules first.

## When It Takes Effect

The memorandum has been in effect since it was signed on August 20, 2026. Agencies' first reports and plans are due in stages:

- land for the landing site — within 90 days;
- updated export controls — within 120 days;
- reports on ranges, safety, spectrum and the industrial base strategy — within 180 days;
- the Commerce Department's federal landing site plan — within 240 days.
