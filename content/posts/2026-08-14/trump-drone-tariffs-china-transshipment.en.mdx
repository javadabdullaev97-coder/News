US President Donald Trump on August 13 signed a proclamation imposing tariffs of up to 100% on drones and their components, while the White House accused more than 40 countries of helping China evade American tariffs.

## Drone Tariffs: Rates Depend on Size and Country of Origin

According to the [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-bolsters-national-security-and-strengthens-u-s-supply-chains-by-imposing-tariffs-on-drones-and-their-parts-and-components/), the 100% tariff applies to drones weighing more than 25 kilograms and to drones with features the administration considers sensitive to national security — such as thermal imaging and docking stations — as well as to critical components of such aircraft.

Smaller drones without such features, and some of their components, are subject to a 25% tariff. For drones and parts from the European Union, Japan, Liechtenstein, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan, the rate is reduced to 15%, and for products from the United Kingdom, to 10%, if production is predominantly localized in these countries or in the US.

Most of the tariffs will take effect 21 days after signing — that is, around [September 3](https://www.theverge.com/policy/979977/trump-declares-100-percent-tariffs-on-many-drones-and-all-aircraft-parts) — while tariffs on less sensitive components will take effect after 180 days. The proclamation also directs the Secretary of Commerce to create a program supporting the localization of drone production in the US.

The White House explains the decision by citing national security threats: the fact sheet calls drones "a key technology in modern military conflicts," and describes dependence on foreign — primarily Chinese — components of unmanned systems as a source of cyber threats.

## White House Accuses More Than 40 Countries of Helping China Evade Tariffs

That same day, the White House Office of Trade and Manufacturing Policy published the [report "The Great Transshipment Scam"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/08/the-great-transshipment-scam/) — according to [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/5ca83d6d-2c13-4d5e-b564-881f89189b3e), it names more than 40 US trade partners, including Mexico, Canada, European Union countries, India, Japan and South Korea, as links in a scheme through which Chinese exporters repackage and relabel goods to conceal their origin and evade tariffs imposed by Washington against China.

The report claims the scheme causes significant damage to the US federal budget; the exact figures could not be independently confirmed.

## Context: Regulatory Standoff Over Chinese Drones

The drone tariffs come amid a broader administration campaign against Chinese drone manufacturers. On [July 22, the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC)](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/22/us-targets-china-in-move-to-ban-military-grade-drone-imports) proposed rules restricting imports of advanced drones with swarming capabilities and high-resolution thermal imaging — measures aimed primarily at China's DJI and Autel Robotics; exemptions for components manufactured outside China have been extended to January 2028.

Beijing had already responded to Washington's previous restrictions: on August 5, [China imposed countermeasures](https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/international-relations/us-china-tensions/china-hits-back-at-us-fcc-bans-with-sanctions-and-drone-curbs), including sanctions against a US testing laboratory and tighter export controls on drones and related technologies, in response to the FCC's proposed restrictions on imports of Chinese drones. As of publication, Beijing had not issued an official reaction directly addressing the August 13 tariffs and the transshipment report.
