Tesla filed an application with the Texas Comptroller (the state's tax agency) on July 22 for a tax incentive for the $10.1 billion Project Crystal Sun solar plant in Fort Bend County. The document became publicly available on August 6, [according to the application page](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/development/prop-tax/jeti/application-details.php?id=J0050) on the agency's website.

The application envisions $1.5 billion in real estate investment and $8.6 billion in equipment. The company expects to create 9,712 permanent jobs and about 1,147 more during construction, [according to the filed document](https://assets.comptroller.texas.gov/open-data/jeti/J0050/J0050-tesla-lamar-app.pdf).

The site will span about 3,050 acres (more than 1,200 hectares) near the city of Richmond in Fort Bend County, [PV Magazine reports](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/12/tesla-files-tax-incentive-application-for-10-1-billion-texas-solar-cell-plant/). The land falls under the Lamar Consolidated ISD school district, through which Tesla is applying for the tax break.

According to the application, the plant is meant to become a fully vertically integrated production facility — from silicon ingots and wafers to finished modules — modeled on Chinese manufacturing complexes. Construction is planned for 2026-2028, with commercial production set to begin in the first quarter of 2029.

The application was signed on Tesla's behalf by the company's tax attorney, and the document was prepared by consulting firm Kroll. Tesla had not issued its own statement or press release about the project as of publication — all information comes from documents filed with the agency, [ABC13 Houston notes](https://abc13.com/post/tesla-wants-tax-breaks-build-solar-manufacturing-plant-fort-bend-county-documents-reveal/19679629/).

## What Tesla is asking for

The tax break is being processed under the JETI program (Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation Act), which caps the taxable value of a company's property for local school taxes for 10 years. In the application, Tesla states that without approval it will consider sites in other states for the project.

The company presents its own estimates in the document: over 38 years, the project is projected to add $107 billion to Texas's gross regional product. State and local budgets are additionally expected to receive $6.4 billion in tax revenue over the same period. These figures are Tesla's own forecast and have no independent confirmation.

## What happens next

Under JETI rules, the state comptroller's office must issue a recommendation — to approve or reject the application — within 60 days after the application is deemed complete. After that, the office of the Texas governor and the Lamar Consolidated ISD board of trustees each have 30 days to make their own decision. The agreement takes effect only if both parties agree, [according to JETI's process rules](https://comptroller.texas.gov/economy/development/prop-tax/jeti/process.php).
