US President Donald Trump said August 14 he will "soon" declare the Strait of Hormuz US territory after the "defeat" of Iran, speaking at a campaign rally in Garden City, New York.

## "Soon I'll declare it US territory"

"Once we finish crushing Iran, which is suffering a very serious defeat, pretty soon I'm going to declare the Strait of Hormuz territory of the United States," Trump said to laughter from the audience, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/trump-says-he-will-declare-strait-of-hormuz-a-us-territory). He spoke at the Nassau County police academy, where he traveled to campaign for Republican candidates ahead of the midterm elections to Congress. Trump also called the US naval blockade of Iran a "steel wall" and described it as "impenetrable."

The outlet notes it remains unclear how seriously Trump meant the statement and whether it signals a new policy line from Washington.

## A 20% tariff and "guardian" status for the strait

The "US territory" claim is not the first such statement from Trump about the strait. In July he proposed that the United States become the "guardian" of the Strait of Hormuz and collect a 20% tariff on the value of all goods passing through it, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/trump-says-he-will-declare-strait-of-hormuz-a-us-territory). International lawyers cited by the outlet say such a levy would violate international maritime law on freedom of passage for merchant vessels.

Iran's deputy foreign minister for legal and international affairs, Kazem Gharibabadi, responded to Trump's statement on social media: "The Strait of Hormuz cannot be seized by a tweet, an aircraft carrier, a decree, or a campaign speech. Iran does not fear threats and does not yield to shows of force," [Al Jazeera quotes him as saying](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/trump-says-he-will-declare-strait-of-hormuz-a-us-territory).

## An "indefinite" blockade and new sanctions

US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth said Thursday, August 13, during a visit to Panama that the US Navy is capable of sustaining the blockade of Iran "indefinitely": "We'll rotate ships in and out, as we've done before, and we'll continue to do so," [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/us-threatens-indefinite-blockade-against-iran-how-long-can-it-last). The blockade of Iranian ports has been in effect since April 13; according to the outlet, Iran has since lost at least $6 billion in oil revenue, though Tehran itself insists the strait remains blockaded primarily by its own forces, not by the United States.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent [said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week) the same day that Washington will announce measures of "economic isolation the world has not seen" next week: "Watch this space — there will be new announcements next week, because we're going to apply measures unprecedented in the history of a country's economic isolation." He said the new sanctions will be part of a "one-two punch" alongside the continued port blockade. President Trump described the current tactic as "restrained," saying the administration is "just watching Iran with its massive inflation and no money," [Bloomberg writes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week).

Brent crude fell August 14 amid the escalation, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-to-roll-out-economic-isolation-plan-for-iran-next-week).

## Control of the strait — a sticking point in negotiations

The United States and Iran signed a memorandum ending hostilities in June, under which Tehran pledged to "make every effort" to ensure the safe passage of merchant vessels. The agreement did not last a month: fighting resumed by late June, both sides accused each other of violations, and control of the strait remains the main sticking point in ongoing ceasefire negotiations, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/trump-says-he-will-declare-strait-of-hormuz-a-us-territory).

On August 14, the UAE accused Iran of a new attack on two tankers belonging to state company ADNOC in the strait, while Trump had again claimed, two days earlier on August 12, "full control" over the waterway for the United States — both episodes were [previously covered by LEAP News](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/14/world-hormuz-adnoc-tanker-attacks-us-iran-control-claims). On August 9, Iran put forward six conditions for fully reopening the strait, including the lifting of sanctions and the withdrawal of US naval forces from the region.

## Domestic political backdrop

Trump is waging the campaign amid rising political risk ahead of the November midterm elections: according to Bloomberg, the president said he does not care whether the conflict with Iran ends before the elections, even though oil and gasoline prices in the United States have risen because of the war, [Bloomberg writes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/us-readies-iran-penalties-as-trump-downplays-midterms-impact). According to a late-July Quinnipiac University poll cited by Al Jazeera, 60% of American voters oppose military action against Iran.

Washington has promised to unveil the new "economic isolation" measures against Iran next week.
