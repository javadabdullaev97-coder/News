US President Donald Trump said in a Fox News interview on Monday, August 17, that he would bomb Oman if it obstructed talks over the status of the Strait of Hormuz. The statement came on the day a 60-day memorandum between the United States and Iran on negotiations over the strait expired.

"If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the s--- out of them," Trump told Fox News correspondent Trey Yingst by phone, who relayed the quote on air. The president later spoke to reporters in the Oval Office. Responding to a question, he added: "I don't think they behaved very well, but we'd handle them very easily, just like we do other things" ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/us-iran-war-deal-expired-strait-of-hormuz/)). According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/trump-demands-iran-put-up-the-white-flag-of-surrender-as-mou-expires), Iran and Oman have spent weeks discussing terms for shipping through the strait.

The US-Iran memorandum, signed on June 18, gave the sides 60 days to negotiate and expired on Monday. Iran says the United States violated the terms of the agreement shortly after it was signed ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/us-iran-war-deal-expired-strait-of-hormuz/)).

In the same interview, Trump called on Iran to "put up the white flag of surrender." He also said he was not setting Tehran a new deadline: "I don't have a timetable, I'm not in a rush" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/trump-demands-iran-put-up-the-white-flag-of-surrender-as-mou-expires)). The president reiterated that the United States controls the Strait of Hormuz through a blockade and suggested he could declare it US territory (Al Jazeera). Iran responded that the strait "remains Iranian" (Al Jazeera).

## Strait Blocked, Oil Prices Rise

Maritime traffic through the strait has nearly stopped. According to CBS News, the number of daily vessel transits fell from 19 to three between August 11 and 16. Traffic through the strait dropped 19.5% over the week. Before the war, about 130 vessels passed through the strait per day — now movement is minimal, with most transits following a route designated by Iran ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/us-iran-war-deal-expired-strait-of-hormuz/)).

Oil prices rose in response to the threats: a barrel of Brent crude gained 2.7% to $90.87 (CBS News).

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran "has not yet decided to resume talks with the US" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/trump-demands-iran-put-up-the-white-flag-of-surrender-as-mou-expires)).
