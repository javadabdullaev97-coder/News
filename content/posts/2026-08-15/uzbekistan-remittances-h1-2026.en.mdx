Personal remittances to Uzbekistan rose 13% year-on-year to $9.28 billion in January-June 2026, the Central Bank of Uzbekistan said in a [review](https://cbu.uz/uz/press_center/reviews/4227079/) published July 23.

## Inflows and Outflows

According to [central bank data](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/7bb/22prqsb717ddmitk1ad7ms5qmnxy15kb/Ichki-valyuta-bozori-sharhi-_-I-yarim-yillik-_2_.pdf), $9.28 billion flowed into Uzbekistan through international money transfer systems in January-June 2026, compared with $8.22 billion a year earlier. Outflows from the country totaled $1.33 billion over the same period — up 8% from the first half of 2025.

The net remittance balance — the difference between inflows and outflows — reached nearly $8 billion. A year earlier the figure stood at about $7 billion, a roughly 14% increase.

Over a two-year horizon, the growth is more pronounced: in the first half of 2024, Uzbekistan received $6.46 billion in remittances. Inflows have grown 44% over two years.

## Currency Market Overview

The rise in remittances came amid broader growth in foreign currency supply on the domestic market. Total demand for foreign currency in the first half of 2026 reached nearly $32 billion — up 20% from a year earlier. Currency supply, excluding the central bank's own operations, grew even faster — up 31% to $27.8 billion.

Demand for foreign currency from legal entities rose 19%, while supply rose 29%. Export foreign currency proceeds of businesses increased 30% to $10.1 billion. Of that, companies sold $5.6 billion (55%) on the domestic market — $1.2 billion more than a year earlier.

The structure of import financing also shifted: the share of funds purchased on the domestic market (conversion) rose to 67.2% from 63.1% in the first half of 2025. The share of companies' own foreign currency funds fell to 22.6% from 23.7%, and the share of foreign currency loans dropped to 10.2% from 13.2%.

## Household Transactions and the Soum Exchange Rate

Households sold $12.3 billion to commercial banks — almost 1.4 times more than in the first half of 2025 ($9.1 billion). Households bought $6.8 billion — up 26% from a year earlier ($5.4 billion). As a result, household currency supply exceeded demand by $5.49 billion.

The soum's exchange rate fluctuated between 11,935 and 12,320 soums per dollar in the first half of 2026 and stood at 12,009 soums at the end of the period — virtually unchanged since the start of the year. Two-way daily exchange rate volatility, however, rose to 30.2 soums from 22.7 soums a year earlier.

The bank's [review](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/7bb/22prqsb717ddmitk1ad7ms5qmnxy15kb/Ichki-valyuta-bozori-sharhi-_-I-yarim-yillik-_2_.pdf) noted that the IMF, in its June Article IV report, reassessed the soum's exchange rate regime, changing its status from "crawling" to "floating." Thirty-three countries currently hold that status, including Kazakhstan, Turkey, South Korea, Malaysia, Thailand and Armenia.

## What's Next

The Central Bank of Uzbekistan announced plans to develop the domestic currency market through the end of 2026 and into 2027. The bank intends to develop a strategy for its own currency operations based on a risk-oriented approach and to build up market-making. Plans also include improving trading infrastructure — settlement, clearing and market monitoring systems. A separate task is to strengthen the legal and institutional framework for hedging currency risk, including derivative financial instruments.
