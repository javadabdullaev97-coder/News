U.S. Deputy Secretary of Defense Stephen Feinberg signed a memorandum on August 4 to allocate up to $243.9 million to Palantir Technologies without competitive bidding. According to several outlets, there was no official confirmation as of publication.

The document, titled "Funding Palantir," orders the company's services to be funded without a competition through March 31, 2027, [DefenseScoop reports](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/13/feinberg-funding-palantir-memo-pentagon-bond-program/). Feinberg justified the decision by saying Palantir's software "improves the efficiency of the defense industrial base" and helps identify delays in the production of munitions and equipment.

According to [Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/business/2026/08/pentagon-palantir-no-bid/415400/), the memorandum does not name a specific contract or a legal basis for waiving the competition. U.S. federal procurement law generally requires one of several justifications: urgency, a sole responsible source, or national security interests. Feinberg also directed the Pentagon's procurement and financial leadership to find additional funds for Palantir for the period from April 2027 to December 2028.

The agency is also budgeting more than $1.5 billion in fiscal year 2027 to expand access to Palantir's platform for Maven Smart System data analysis, DefenseScoop reports. Another $60 million is earmarked for the Virtual Joint Operations Center initiative. The outlets note it remains unclear whether the deal has been finalized.

## Pentagon and Palantir have not confirmed the sum

The U.S. Department of Defense (officially renamed the Department of War in 2025) has neither confirmed nor denied the $243.9 million figure. Palantir Technologies did not respond to outlets' requests for comment.

## Context: the BOND program

The memorandum relies on the Business Operators for National Defense (BOND) program, which the Pentagon launched in February 2026. It embeds private-sector executives in the agency's procurement processes to speed up production of weapons and equipment. Since 2024, Palantir has received $3.2 billion in contractual obligations from the Pentagon, about half of them without competition, according to data from analytics firm GovTribe cited by Defense One.

Funding under the memorandum runs through March 31, 2027. By that date, the Pentagon's procurement and financial leadership has been ordered to find sources of additional funds for Palantir for 2027-2028. A response will be added to this article once received.
