On August 21, Google added spending and access control tools for enterprise customers to Antigravity, its AI programming environment, the [Google Cloud blog reported](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/expanding-google-antigravity-for-enterprise-customers). AI agents in Antigravity are programs that write, test and fix code on their own, based on a developer's task, without step-by-step human involvement.

The new features are available to customers on Gemini Enterprise — Google's AI-powered enterprise services package — at the Standard, Plus and Standard Emerging Market tiers, [the post says](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/expanding-google-antigravity-for-enterprise-customers). Administrators can set monthly spending limits at the project level directly in the billing console. They can also pool purchased tokens — the unit that measures an agent's workload — across teams, and enable overage billing at standard rates once the limit is exceeded.

For security, companies got sandbox settings — an isolated workspace where an agent's actions do not affect the rest of the system. Google also added controls over agent access to the browser and to MCP servers — external modules that extend an agent's capabilities with additional data and services. A single toggle enables full auditing: a log of all agent requests and responses for checking compliance with a company's internal requirements. Customer data stays within the customer's own cloud environment under the terms of Google Cloud's data processing agreement, the company notes.

Antigravity's IDE extensions are available to users of Visual Studio Code, JetBrains — starting with version 2026.2.1 — and Zed, according to an [Antigravity blog post](https://antigravity.google/blog/antigravity-ide-extensions) published on August 20. Support for Visual Studio, as well as enterprise features for JetBrains and Zed, are still in testing.

Google says it plans to add separate spending limits for individual users and teams by the end of 2026, according to the company's blog.
