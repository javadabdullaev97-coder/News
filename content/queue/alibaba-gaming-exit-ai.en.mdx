Alibaba Group agreed on August 17 to sell gaming studio Lingxi Games to private equity fund Trustar Capital, continuing to shift its business focus toward artificial intelligence and cloud computing.

Lingxi CEO Zhou Bingshu told staff about the deal in an internal letter that did not disclose the deal's value or closing date. According to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/alibaba-to-sell-gaming-arm-for-1-5-billion-in-boost-to-ai-pivot), Lingxi's valuation in the deal will be at least $1.5 billion. Other sources cited by [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/alibaba-lingxi-china-ai-us/) put Alibaba's proceeds from the sale above $2 billion.

Alibaba will transfer its entire stake in Lingxi to Trustar Capital. Zhou Bingshu and the current management team will remain in charge of the studio, [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/alibaba-lingxi-china-ai-us/).

Lingxi Games developed the strategy game "Three Kingdoms: Strategy Edition" and was a wholly owned Alibaba gaming unit. Gaming was never a strength for the company compared with rival Tencent, [Fortune notes](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/alibaba-lingxi-china-ai-us/).

## A bet on artificial intelligence

The Lingxi sale extends the AI strategy Alibaba Group announced on February 24, 2025. At the time, the company said it planned to invest 380 billion yuan (about $53 billion) in cloud and AI infrastructure over three years — more than Alibaba spent on cloud and AI over the previous decade, according to [Alibaba Group's statement](https://home.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1830678592242057216).

Alibaba Group CEO Eddie Wu called artificial intelligence a once-in-a-generation opportunity, [according to Alibaba Group's statement](https://home.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-1830678592242057216). He also said the company's cloud division remains its main source of AI-related revenue.

Alibaba's exit from gaming shows where the company is directing capital amid intensifying US-China tech rivalry, [Fortune assesses](https://fortune.com/2026/08/18/alibaba-lingxi-china-ai-us/). Alibaba's Qwen series of cloud models have surpassed 3 billion downloads in six months — according to Fortune, ahead of Meta and Google on that measure.
