Iran and Oman made progress on August 4-5 in talks on a new shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz — a topic LEAP News has [covered](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-03/iran-oman-hormuz-talks-final-stage) since August 3 — regional sources told AP.

## Route: entry via Iran, exit via Oman

The emerging agreement envisions ships entering the Persian Gulf via a route controlled by Iran and exiting via a route controlled by Oman — with fees for security and environmental protection services, AP [reported](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/iran-and-oman-make-progress-on-deal-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz-officials-say), citing two regional officials.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said on August 5 that the talks aim to establish "safe entry and exit routes" that "respect the sovereign rights" of both sides while addressing security concerns, Interfax [reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107485). According to Baghaei, the talks are progressing positively at both the technical and political levels; the two sides will announce the final outcome only after the negotiations conclude. Al Jazeera [also reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/5/iran-war-live-tehran-oman-talks-on-hormuz-positive-ship-hit-in-red-sea) the same day on Iranian officials' remarks describing the talks with Oman as "positive."

## Dispute over fees: voluntary contributions versus a fixed charge

The two sides reached the current stage after exchanging counterproposals in late July, Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/29/iran-and-oman-swap-proposals-to-manage-strait-of-hormuz-what-we-know). Oman proposed a model modeled on the Strait of Malacca: separate routes with parity of participation for both sides and voluntary contributions from shipowners for navigation security and environmental protection. According to Paul Musgrave, a professor at Georgetown University in Qatar, the existing Malacca model generates about $70 million a year. Iran insisted on a route running wholly or partly through its territorial waters, and on a mandatory fee that, according to Al Jazeera, Tehran proposed at $1 million per vessel.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC on August 4 that he saw a chance for a deal "today or tomorrow," but described the desired outcome as "freedom of movement" — meaning without mandatory Iranian fees, AP [reported](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/iran-and-oman-make-progress-on-deal-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz-officials-say). That description conflicts with AP's own characterization of the scheme under discussion as involving "fees for security services" — it remains unclear what fee arrangement will make it into the final agreement.

## Washington: "there is progress, but no final deal yet"

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters on August 4 that "there is progress in the talks, but no final deal yet," and said he hoped for a swift conclusion, AP [reported](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/iran-and-oman-make-progress-on-deal-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz-officials-say). Rubio had previously called ceding control over the strait to Iran "a very dangerous precedent."

Tehran, meanwhile, continues to keep the two tracks separate: on August 3, the Iranian Foreign Ministry [denied](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-04/iran-denies-trump-hormuz-talks-claim) Trump's claims of direct talks with the United States, insisting that the dialogue on the route through the strait is being conducted only with Oman. Rubio's and Bessent's statements on August 4 describe specifically the Iran-Oman track — they do not separately address direct talks between Washington and Tehran.

## US position: more than a thousand ships in three months

US Central Command (CENTCOM) said on August 5 that over the past three months the US military has helped more than a thousand ships pass through the strait, and that the southern route remains open to commercial shipping, RIA Novosti [reported](https://ria.ru/20260805/ormuz-2109051557.html). This is the official US position; there is no independent confirmation of the figure.

## Why this matters for Uzbekistan

About 20% of global oil consumption passes through the Strait of Hormuz — LEAP News [wrote](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-03/iran-oman-hormuz-talks-final-stage) about this on August 3, citing the US Energy Information Administration. Brent crude recovered part of the previous day's losses on the morning of August 4 and traded at $84.65 a barrel, Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/4/us-stocks-near-record-high-oil-falls-as-trump-claims-iran-talks-underway) — markets react to every new signal of progress in the talks. Uzbekistan does not receive oil through the strait directly, but depends on global prices when importing petroleum products.

## What's next

Neither Tehran nor Muscat has named a date for signing the agreement. An interim memorandum that the United States and Iran signed on June 17 with Pakistan's mediation covered a general framework for reopening the strait but collapsed in July over disagreements on routes. The US naval blockade in place since July 14 remains in effect until the Iran-Oman talks are concluded.
