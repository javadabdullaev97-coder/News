The Board of Peace, established in January 2026, [signed its first contract](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864388) on August 6 for the construction of a military base for international forces in the Gaza Strip. The same day, Uganda's parliament [approved sending](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/uganda-approves-sending-troops-to-gaza-for-international-force-proposed-by-trumps-board-of-peace) its troops there.

## The base contract

The tender for the facility's construction was won by Arkel International, a U.S. company based in Louisiana, [Kommersant reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864388), citing The Guardian; the contract has not yet been finalized. The base, measuring 100 by 120 meters, is designed at the first stage for 150 Moroccan troops, with a planned expansion to 5,000 people. The facility will be located about 1.6 km from the border with Israel, in territory controlled by the Israeli army — according to Kommersant and Vedomosti, this is meant to allow for the rapid evacuation of personnel in the event of an attack.

According to [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/06/1219411-sovet-mira-ssha), the base will become part of the infrastructure of the International Stabilization Force (ISF), which is meant to monitor compliance with the terms of the ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hamas. The first group of Moroccan troops was originally supposed to arrive at the site in June, but the timeline was pushed back by several months.

## Uganda's decision

Uganda's parliament on Thursday, August 6, [approved sending](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/uganda-approves-sending-troops-to-gaza-for-international-force-proposed-by-trumps-board-of-peace) troops to join the International Stabilization Force for Gaza — the first public confirmation of the country's readiness to join the mission. According to PBS, Uganda's Defense Minister Kiryowa Kiwanuka justified the decision by citing the experience of Ugandan troops in peacekeeping operations in Somalia. The exact size of the Ugandan contingent has not been disclosed.

Some opposition lawmakers — including Hassan Kirumira — publicly questioned the government's motives, noting that Uganda is a member of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation; it remains unclear whether the decision is linked to any reciprocal arrangements.

## What is known about the international force

The International Stabilization Force was proposed by the Board of Peace in February 2026; under the plan, it is meant to number 20,000 troops, with command to be given to U.S. Major General Jasper Jeffers, who has been proposed for the post. Besides Uganda, Morocco has already confirmed it will contribute troops. Deployment of the force depends on the phased withdrawal of Israeli troops from Gaza under the ceasefire agreement reached in October 2025, and is set to begin after Hamas disarms.

## What's next

The contract for the base near Gaza has not yet been finalized — no timeline for completing the deal has been given. Deployment of the main contingent of the international force remains contingent on the terms of the truce being met: Hamas's disarmament and the withdrawal of Israeli troops from surrounding territory.
