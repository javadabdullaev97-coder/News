The Ministry of Justice registered Central Bank amendments to remote identification rules for fintech services on August 14 — registration is now possible using a phone number issued to a close relative, if the family relationship is confirmed.

This concerns [Resolution No. 21/2 of the Central Bank Board dated August 3](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8406083), which amends the regulation on minimum information security requirements for remote financial services. The document governs the operations of banks, payment organizations and payment system operators.

## What is changing

Before the amendments, the system checked whether the personal identification number (PINFL) of the SIM card owner matched the PINFL of the person registering in the app. If they did not match, the organization was required to deny registration and card linking, and the user received an SMS explaining why.

Now, if the numbers do not match, the organization must additionally check whether the number belongs to a close relative. If the relationship is confirmed, registration in the mobile app is allowed. The amendments do not specify how exactly banks and payment organizations will technically verify the relationship.

The same section of the document removes biometrics as a mandatory requirement for one more step in the registration and card-linking process. Now an OTP code is sufficient for it.

## Who counts as a close relative

The resolution introduces a closed list for the first time: parents, brothers and sisters, sons and daughters, spouses, as well as the spouse's parents, siblings and children. The list does not cover uncles, aunts or cousins.

Linking other people's bank cards and accounts to one's own account is still prohibited — even those of relatives. The amendment does not affect this rule: it applies only to initial registration by phone number.

## What else changed

For peer-to-peer (P2P) transfers between individuals, banks and payment organizations will set their own threshold amount below which an OTP code is not required. Transfers above the threshold still need to be confirmed with a code. The organization itself is liable for fraud in transactions made without an OTP. Transfers via websites are prohibited — only through the mobile app.

The app will partially hide the cardholder's first and last name — for the sender of a transfer, the recipient, and the owner of the number the card is linked to.

When logging in from a new device or resetting a password, all cards linked to the account are now automatically blocked — they will need to be reconfirmed with an OTP code. Biometrics remain mandatory for logging in from a new device and for password recovery themselves — the amendments did not affect this.

## When it takes effect and who it applies to

According to [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8406083), the resolution takes effect three months after official publication — approximately November 16, 2026.

The rule applies to all banks, payment organizations and payment system operators providing remote financial services to individuals in Uzbekistan — that is, all mobile banking and fintech app users in the country. The current [regulation](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8007760), which is now being revised, took effect only on April 22, 2026.
