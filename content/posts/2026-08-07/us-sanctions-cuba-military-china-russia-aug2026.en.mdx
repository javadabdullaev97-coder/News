The US Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) [imposed](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806) sanctions on August 6 against five Cuban companies and six individuals over arms purchases for the Cuban military from Russia and China.

## Who Was Added to the List

Among the new designees are [Mónica Milián Gómez](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/us-diplomat-rubio-targets-cuban-military-officials-companies-in-sanctions), a Cuban citizen residing in Moscow, according to OFAC, and [Waldo Pérez Cortés](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806), another designee, who lives in Beijing.

Also added to the list were Óscar Enrique Biosca Gallego, José Antonio Remón Rodríguez, Roberto Jesús Visiana Musset and Heriberto Sánchez Alleyne — the latter, [according to OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806), linked to the state company Tecnoimport. OFAC updated the biographical data on August 6 for two individuals already on the sanctions list — Minister of the Revolutionary Armed Forces Álvaro Victoriano López Miera and Chief of the General Staff Roberto Legrá Sotolongo — without adding them as new designees.

## Which Companies Are Targeted

OFAC added five state-owned enterprises to the list:

- **Tecnoimport** — a state company that has imported and exported technical products since 1995; according to the State Department, it [purchases military equipment](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/us-diplomat-rubio-targets-cuban-military-officials-companies-in-sanctions) for the Cuban army from Russia and China.
- **Military Industrial Union (UIM)** — a state enterprise that produces and repairs weapons.
- **Yuri Gagarin State Military-Industrial Enterprise** — repairs and services Cuba's fleet of Russian-made military aircraft.
- **Tecnotex** — a state wholesale trading enterprise operating since 1983.
- **Duna** — a diversified trading company registered in 2022.

The sanctions freeze the property and assets of all designees located in the United States or under the control of US persons. US citizens and companies are barred from doing business with them, [OFAC said in its release](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260806).

## Legal Basis

The sanctions were imposed under [US Presidential Executive Order 14404](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20260501_33), "Imposing Sanctions on Those Responsible for Repression in Cuba and Threats to the National Security and Foreign Policy of the United States," which Donald Trump signed on May 1, 2026. The State Department published a fact sheet on the decision, [Targeting Enablers of the Cuban Regime's Arms Imports and Foreign Military Cooperation](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/targeting-enablers-of-the-cuban-regimes-arms-imports-and-foreign-military-cooperation-fact-sheet/).

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the Cuban government "a state sponsor of terrorism that spies on America, arms leftist radicals... and serves as a platform for Russia, China and Iran 90 miles from US shores," [Al Jazeera quoted him as saying](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/us-diplomat-rubio-targets-cuban-military-officials-companies-in-sanctions).

## Context

In July, the US Treasury Department had already added nine organizations and two individuals linked to Cuba to the sanctions list — according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/us-diplomat-rubio-targets-cuban-military-officials-companies-in-sanctions), those sanctions targeted the energy and financial sectors and medical labor missions, not arms procurement. In January 2021, in the final month of his first term, Trump first designated Cuba a state sponsor of terrorism. After returning to power in 2025, he restored that designation.
