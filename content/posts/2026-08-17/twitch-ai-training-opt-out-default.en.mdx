Twitch has used users' broadcasts and chats to train Amazon's AI models by default since August 12, 2026, according to reports cited by several outlets; there has been no official confirmation as of publication.

The option is called "Training for Generative AI" and is located in the account's Security and Privacy section, at the bottom of the settings page. It is enabled by default on all channels: to exclude their content from future training of Amazon's AI models, a streamer has to go into settings and turn the toggle off manually.

The option covers broadcasts, video on demand (VOD), clips, channel chat messages, as well as images and text on the channel page. Turning the toggle off does not affect data already collected — it only stops the content from being used in future training cycles. Chat moderation (AutoMod), automatic captions and Twitch's recommendation algorithms keep working regardless of the setting — the option does not turn them off.

## Streamers' reaction and Twitch's position

The announcement sparked a wave of discontent within the Twitch community. A comment on the support forum demanding that participation in AI training be voluntary rather than automatic quickly gained supporters. According to several outlets, it received about 14,000 upvotes within a few hours.

Some users also reported that the toggle spontaneously reverted to "on" after they had turned it off — [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/videos/cwyq22g0ylxo) wrote about this.

Twitch Chief Product Officer Mike Minton explained the choice of the default format during the company's livestream. "If this was opt-in, no one would agree to it. That's honestly the whole answer" — several outlets quote him as saying.

A request has been prepared and handed to the owner for sending to Twitch's press office; a reply will be added to the article once received.
