U.S. District Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin has set the antitrust trial over the Paramount Skydance–Warner Bros. Discovery deal for March 2–19, 2027 — later than the date Paramount had hoped for.

## What the Court Decided

Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California ruled that the trial will run for 12 court days, starting on Tuesday, March 2, 2027, [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-warner-bros-antitrust-trial-date-march-2027-1236827843/) and [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-warner-bros-trial-march-2027-1236664236/) reported. Paramount had asked for a trial in November 2026, while the coalition of 12 state attorneys general and the Writers Guild of America (WGA), which are challenging the deal, pushed for April 2027 — the judge chose a date in between.

The judge scheduled the final pretrial conference for February 24, 2027. The parties must file a joint case management statement by noon on August 13, 2026 — ahead of the first case management conference on August 19, according to the schedule cited by [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-warner-bros-antitrust-trial-date-march-2027-1236827843/).

The date has a direct financial impact on the deal: starting October 1, 2026, Paramount will begin paying Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders a "delay penalty" of [$7 million a day](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-warner-bros-antitrust-trial-date-march-2027-1236827843/), according to Variety, until the deal closes. With the trial starting in March 2027, that sum will exceed $1 billion before a ruling is even reached.

## Why the 12 States and Writers Are Suing

The lawsuit was filed by a coalition of attorneys general from 12 states, led by California, together with the Writers Guild of America. The plaintiffs cite Section 7 of the Clayton Act, which bars mergers that substantially lessen competition: they argue that combining two of the world's five largest film studios would harm competition in the film and television industry, [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-warner-bros-antitrust-trial-date-march-2027-1236827843/) reports.

In the summer of 2026, the judge already [blocked](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-warner-bros-antitrust-trial-date-march-2027-1236827843/) the deal's closing with a temporary restraining order, and Paramount agreed to postpone the merger until at least June 2027 — pending the outcome of the trial.

Paramount announced the deal on [February 27, 2026](https://ir.paramount.com/news-releases/news-release-details/paramount-acquire-warner-bros-discovery-form-next-generation/): the company offered $31 per share for Warner Bros. Discovery, valuing it at $81 billion by market capitalization and $110 billion including debt. The U.S. Department of Justice and Warner Bros. Discovery shareholders approved the deal in spring and summer 2026.

## Ellison's Response: It's Not About Market Share, It's About CNN

Paramount CEO David Ellison published an op-ed in The New York Times on Tuesday, August 4, arguing that opposition to the deal has less to do with market share than with whether he can "be trusted as a steward" of CNN after the merger — [Variety quotes him as saying](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/david-ellison-cnn-paramount-warner-bros-trusted-steward-1236827405/).

"There has been speculation about my political views, my loyalties, my intentions," Ellison wrote, adding: "I have voted regularly for candidates of both parties; I hold views that would be called conservative and views that would be called liberal, like most Americans do; and when it comes to our news organizations, I have no intention of subjecting the newsrooms to my views" — [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/david-ellison-cnn-paramount-warner-bros-trusted-steward-1236827405/) quotes him as saying.

Ellison pledged to preserve CNN's editorial independence: the network's journalists "will remain accountable to the facts and to those they serve — not to any political party or movement," the op-ed states. [Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/08/05/paramount-ceo-says-politics-fueling-warner-bros-merger-battle_6756171_19.html) notes that the statement came amid Paramount's effort to ease political concerns around the deal.

## Paramount's Mixed Quarter

The same day, August 4, Paramount Skydance reported its second-quarter 2026 earnings. Net income attributable to the company fell to $41 million (4 cents per share) from $57 million (8 cents per share) a year earlier, [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-reports-mixed-earnings-results-1236663819/) reports.

The streaming division posted its best results ever: Paramount+ added 2 million subscribers, bringing its total base to 81.6 million, with the quarter marking the service's best-ever subscriber retention and lowest-ever churn — driven by the "Yellowstone" spin-off "Dutton Ranch," UFC broadcasts, and coverage of some World Cup soccer matches in Latin America, [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-q2-earnings-profit-declines-tv-revenue-1236827854/) writes. Direct-to-consumer subscription revenue rose 9% to $2.5 billion, while the segment's pre-tax profit climbed 44% to $366 million.

Streaming growth partly offset a decline in traditional television: TV Media segment revenue fell 9% to $3.13 billion, driven by a 14% drop in advertising revenue and a 6% decline in distribution fees amid ongoing cable subscriber losses. The company's total revenue was [$6.9 billion](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/paramount-q2-earnings-profit-declines-tv-revenue-1236827854/).

## What's Next

The parties must agree on a trial plan by August 19, 2026. Before the trial begins on March 2, 2027, a final pretrial conference will take place on February 24, 2027. Until then, Paramount will continue paying Warner Bros. Discovery $7 million a day for delaying the deal's closing.
