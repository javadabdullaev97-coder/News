The hyperspectral satellite Samarkand-2028 reached orbit on August 5 from a sea platform off Haiyang, China, and several hours later sent its first image to Earth — of Athens, the Uzbekcosmos agency [said](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2682).

The satellite conducts remote sensing of Earth — imaging the planet's surface for scientific and applied purposes.

## Who built what

The satellite was built by the Chinese company STAR.VISION, while Uzbekcosmos engineers [developed a separate artificial intelligence module for it](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2670) that processes part of the hyperspectral data directly in orbit — before it is transmitted to Earth. This reduces the volume of data transmitted and speeds up its analysis.

The agency separately stressed that Samarkand-2028 is not a national Uzbek satellite but a joint international project with China. Uzbekcosmos [clarified](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2672) that this is the first time Uzbek engineers' AI module has been built into one of STAR.VISION's international satellite projects. The country acted not as a customer of ready-made data, but as a creator of part of the technology.

The satellite is named after the fact that Samarkand will host the 79th International Astronautical Congress in 2028.

## What the data will be used for

The satellite's data is expected to be used in agriculture, water resource management, environmental monitoring, emergency response and urban planning, [according to the launch announcement](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2670). According to China's state broadcaster CGTN, for Uzbekistan the satellite is intended primarily for [end-to-end monitoring of the cotton industry](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-05/China-launches-hyperspectral-satellites-serving-global-partners-1PmkKoWmmXK/share_amp.html).

A second hyperspectral satellite was launched alongside it — for agricultural monitoring in Indonesia — on the same Smart Dragon-3 carrier rocket, CGTN [reported](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-05/China-launches-hyperspectral-satellites-serving-global-partners-1PmkKoWmmXK/share_amp.html).

## What's next

The partnership between Uzbekcosmos and STAR.VISION is formalized through a memorandum between the two parties. The agency has not disclosed a schedule for the further transmission of data from the satellite and has not named specific Uzbek agencies as recipients of the imagery.
