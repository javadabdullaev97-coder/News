The Tourism Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan on August 4 [announced the drafting](https://gov.uz/ru/uzbektourism/news/view/201920) of a presidential resolution on developing adventure tourism — the document provides for leasing unused aerodromes to businesses and a regulatory system for light aviation.

## Aerodromes for adventure tourism

The draft [envisages](https://gov.uz/ru/uzbektourism/news/view/201920) restoring decommissioned runways and aerodromes in the regions. After renovation, they are planned to be leased to entrepreneurs and grassroots projects. Training facilities on aerodrome grounds are also proposed to be leased out for courses in adventure tourism disciplines.

The Tourism Committee drafted the document together with a working group of representatives from relevant ministries and agencies. The draft does not name the specific aerodromes that will be restored first.

## A regulator for light aviation

The draft proposes designating the Civil Aviation Agency under the Ministry of Transport as the state regulator for light and amateur aviation. The agency would receive additional powers across several areas — aircraft registration, certification, maintenance, personnel training, flight safety oversight and drafting aviation rules for light aircraft.

The rules would cover aircraft weighing up to 5,700 kg and helicopters up to 2,250 kg — limits proposed in line with International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) principles. Aircraft weighing up to 115 kg would be fully exempt from registration.

Once the resolution is approved, the Ministry of Transport would have three months to develop a simplified procedure for registering, certifying and inspecting light aircraft. Six months are allotted to prepare a bill on mandatory insurance in this area.

## Customs exemptions for equipment

Equipment used in adventure tourism would be exempt from import customs duties — from January 1, 2027, to January 1, 2030. The exemption covers around 40 categories: quad bikes, buggies, camper vans, personal watercraft, parachutes, paragliders and mountaineering gear.

## Where adventure tourism development is proposed

While preparing the draft, officials identified 162 natural sites with potential for adventure tourism across five regions: 88 mountains, 21 lakes, 20 waterfalls, 18 caves and grottoes, and 15 mountain peaks. Most of these sites — 77 — are in Tashkent region, with another 30 in Surkhandarya, 22 in Samarkand, 20 in Kashkadarya and 13 in Jizzakh region.

The draft also provides for training mountain guides to the standards of the international associations UIAA, UIMLA and IFMGA — specialized courses are planned in eight regions. Permits to visit border and mountain areas would be issued electronically, within 10 days, through the national portal.

The Tourism Committee was [established](https://lex.uz/docs/-7851788) by Resolution PP-348 of November 19, 2025, as successor to the tourism committee under the Ministry of Ecology, Environmental Protection and Climate Change. The same document set a target of raising tourism's share of GDP from 3.5% to 7% and increasing the flow of foreign tourists from 10 million to 20 million a year by 2030.

## What's next

The draft resolution has been submitted for public discussion, and its text has not yet been signed by the president. The Tourism Committee's release does not specify when the resolution itself would take effect — only deadlines for implementing regulations after its approval: three months for a simplified light-aviation registration procedure and six months for the mandatory insurance bill.
