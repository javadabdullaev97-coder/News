ByteDance, the developer of TikTok, drew more than $30 billion in orders for a syndicated bank loan on August 18, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-18/bytedance-draws-over-30-billion-in-orders-for-jumbo-bank-loan), citing people familiar with the matter.

According to the agency's sources, the volume of orders reflects strong lender interest amid the company's growing investment in artificial intelligence. Bloomberg did not cite comments from ByteDance or the arranging banks.

Bloomberg [first reported on talks over this loan on June 24](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/bytedance-seeks-20-billion-in-its-largest-ever-offshore-loan): at the time, the deal was for $20 billion — the largest offshore loan in the company's history. Citigroup and JPMorgan are arranging the deal; according to trade publications, the loan term is three years with an option to extend to five.

Orders exceeding $30 billion are roughly one and a half times the original $20 billion target. That points to an oversubscribed order book, but ByteDance has not yet disclosed the final size or exact terms of the loan.

## AI capital spending

The company is ramping up spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure. [According to Bloomberg data from May 27](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-27/bytedance-weighs-capex-of-as-much-as-70-billion-in-ai-push), ByteDance is weighing capital expenditure of up to $70 billion on data centers and AI infrastructure in 2026. Under favorable conditions, that figure could rise to $100 billion in 2027.

## A similar loan in 2024

ByteDance's previous foray into the syndicated loan market was also oversubscribed. In September 2024, the company [raised $10.8 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-09-27/bytedance-to-sign-10-8-billion-corporate-loan-in-asian-record) — a record loan at the time for a technology, media and telecommunications company in Asia. Citigroup, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan arranged that deal, and the order book exceeded the initial $9.5 billion target.
