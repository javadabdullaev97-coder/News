The Senate of Uzbekistan, at its 18th plenary session on August 7, 2026, [approved](https://senat.uz/ru/plenary-sessions/post-5865) a law on consumer protection in credit and finance matters — part of a package of measures tightening requirements for advertising of loans and installment plans.

## What the Presidential Resolution Already Established

Three weeks before the Senate's decision, on July 15, 2026, President Shavkat Mirziyoyev [signed](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8329412) Resolution PP-265, "On measures to improve public services provided to the population and create additional conveniences for citizens." Section IV of the document addresses regulation of financial services advertising.

According to the [resolution](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8329412), the Competition and Consumer Rights Protection Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan, together with the Central Bank, must submit a draft regulatory act to the Cabinet of Ministers by September 1, 2026, that will require:

- disclosing, in any advertising of loans, installment plans and credit sales of goods to individuals, the amount of overpayment relative to the current price of the good, work or service, along with its final (total) cost;
- not accompanying advertising of financial services with guarantees or promises of future returns if these cannot be determined at the time the contract is concluded.

## What the Senate Approved

The news item about the August 7 session published on the Senate's website is titled "Consumer rights in credit and finance matters are being improved" — by its title, it belongs to the same group of borrower-protection measures referenced in Resolution PP-265. The full text of the approved law was not publicly available on the Senate's website or lex.uz at the time of publication; once signed by the president, it will be published on lex.uz under a separate number.

Exactly what advertising requirements the Senate-approved law introduces, and what they entail, is not disclosed in the documents published so far — the full text will appear on lex.uz after the president signs it.

## What's Next

The Cabinet of Ministers is due to receive, from the Competition and Consumer Rights Protection Committee and the Central Bank, a draft act on disclosing loan overpayments and total loan costs — by September 1, 2026. The law approved by the Senate will take effect after being signed by the president and officially published.
