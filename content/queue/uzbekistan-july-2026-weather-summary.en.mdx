Uzhydromet said on August 6 that Uzbekistan's average monthly air temperature in July was 1.3–2.3 degrees above the climatic norm, with 16 weather stations across the country recording new absolute temperature records.

## New Heat Records

The agency's full official name is the Agency for Hydrometeorological Service under the National Committee for Ecology and Climate Change of the Republic of Uzbekistan (Uzhydromet).

The norm was exceeded the most in the north and in desert regions, where the average monthly temperature was 2.5–2.8 degrees above the climatic norm, [Uzhydromet said](https://gov.uz/ru/hydromet/news/view/202947).

Uchquduq in the Kyzylkum Desert set a new absolute record for its station, with a reading of +49.7 degrees. That is 3.5 degrees above the previous maximum of +46.2, set in 2021. Other new records include:

- Bukhara — +46.9 (previous maximum +46.2, 2005);
- Navoi — +46.5 (previous +45.9, 2021);
- Arnasay — +46.5 (previous +44.0, 2021);
- Shahrisabz — +44.9 (previous +44.6, 2021);
- Dustlik — +44.8 (previous +44.5, set in 1984 and matched in 2021);
- Namangan — +44.2 (previous +42.5, 2007);
- Fergana — +43.9 (previous +42.2, 1944).

In total, Uzhydromet's list includes 16 stations with new record highs, from Bukhara and Uchquduq in the west to Sokh in the Fergana Valley in the east.

## How Temperatures Changed During the Month

July started cooler than usual — in the first days of the month, temperatures stayed 3–5 degrees below the norm, with daytime air warming to 27–32 degrees, [the agency's summary said](https://gov.uz/ru/hydromet/news/view/202947). From the second five-day period, the heat rose sharply.

The peak came on July 18–19 (in northwestern regions, two days earlier, on July 16–17). Temperatures rose to +43…+45 degrees across most of the country, and to +46…+50 degrees in the south and in the desert. At night, the air cooled to 25–28 degrees, and to 30–33 degrees in the south and desert regions. In the early days of the month's third ten-day period, the heat temporarily eased to normal levels, but by the end of July temperatures again exceeded the norm by 2–3 degrees.

## Tashkent Did Not Break Its Absolute Record

In Tashkent, the heat peaked on July 19, when the air warmed to +44.3 degrees. That is 0.3 degrees below the capital's absolute maximum of +44.6 degrees, recorded on July 18, 1997, [Uzhydromet said](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21895).
