The Ministry of Justice of Uzbekistan on August 1 registered amendments to the regulation on the procedure for transforming credit institutions — the document defines how banks and microfinance organizations switch to Islamic banking principles.

## What the Document Changes

The amendments were registered under No. 3656-1 and are incorporated into the current regulation on the procedure for transforming credit institutions, which describes the reorganization process for banks and microfinance organizations (MFOs). The basis is [Law No. ZRU-1126](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8099403) of March 27, 2026, on introducing Islamic banking activity in Uzbekistan, which took effect on June 29, 2026.

According to the [Ministry of Justice statement](https://t.me/huquqiyaxborot/20221), the amendments establish how credit institutions can switch to Islamic banking principles. This is financing without loan interest, based on partnership, profit-and-loss sharing, and transactions compliant with Sharia norms. The options provided for include converting a conventional MFO into an Islamic microfinance bank and transforming an Islamic microfinance bank into a full-fledged Islamic bank.

A separate requirement concerns accessibility. To obtain state registration and a license, an organization switching to Islamic banking must confirm with an inspection report that its buildings comply with construction standards for accessibility for people with disabilities.

## How Licensing Works

Under the [law](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8099403), banking activity in Uzbekistan is now conducted on the basis of one of two licenses — a conventional banking license or a license for Islamic banking activity. Both are issued by the Central Bank without a limited term of validity, and neither is transferable to other persons. Both full-fledged banks and microfinance organizations can engage in Islamic banking, provided they hold the relevant license.

## Demand Is Already Growing

According to a [Central Bank press release](https://cbu.uz/uz/press_center/releases/3916363/), microfinance organizations provided Islamic financing services worth 11.2 billion soums (≈$933,000 at the Central Bank's exchange rate for July 31) in the first quarter of 2026. That is 8 times more than in the same period of 2025. The largest share (7.7 billion soums, or 69%) came from murabaha — cost-plus financing with deferred payment. Another 2.1 billion soums came from mudaraba — profit-sharing partnership.

## What's Next

The amendments to the regulation on transforming credit institutions were registered by the Ministry of Justice on August 1, 2026. The law on Islamic banking, on the basis of which they were adopted, has been in effect since June 29, 2026.
