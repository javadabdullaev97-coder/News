On Saturday, August 8, 2026, a car bomb exploded at a toll booth on the Pan-American Highway in Colombia's Cauca department — a day after President Abelardo de la Espriella's inauguration in the city of Cali.

## A Change of Power in Colombia

De la Espriella, a millionaire lawyer and political newcomer, [was sworn in as Colombia's president on Friday](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/08/colombia-de-la-espriella-inauguration), succeeding leftist Gustavo Petro; his candidacy in the election was backed by US President Donald Trump, and on the day of the inauguration the United States [announced it was ready to allocate](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/de-la-espriella-sworn-in-as-colombia-leader-vows-crackdown-on-armed-groups) $1 billion to Colombia for security — a decision that still requires approval by Congress.

## Bombing on the Pan-American Highway

On Saturday, August 8, an explosive device detonated in a parked car at a toll booth on the Pan-American Highway in the southwestern department of Cauca, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/car-bomb-attack-rattles-colombia-after-inauguration-of-hardline-president). Two security guards suffered minor injuries. According to a witness, the person who left the car fled on a motorcycle.

The Colombian army blamed two dissident factions of the former Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (FARC) that reject the decade-old peace agreement. The agencies cited no official confirmation of involvement from the groups themselves.

Colombian Transport Minister Elsa Noguera said, "Colombia will not kneel to violence," adding that the country would not allow its infrastructure to be destroyed. According to Al Jazeera, the minister was directly referencing de la Espriella's inaugural speech, in which he vowed to "crush narco-terrorism," and confirmed the authorities' commitment to a hardline response.
