Nvidia is in early talks about a possible partnership, investment or acquisition involving South Korean AI chipmaker Rebellions, Bloomberg [reported on August 21](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/nvidia-in-talks-with-chip-startup-rebellions-for-potential-deal), citing people familiar with the matter.

According to Bloomberg, the parties are discussing several options: a technical partnership, a direct investment by Nvidia in Rebellions, or an acquisition of the company. The talks are at an early stage and no final decision has been made. The agency did not disclose the names of the people involved. Neither Nvidia nor Rebellions has publicly confirmed the outcome of the discussions.

Rebellions is a South Korean company that develops chips for AI inference — the stage at which an already trained model responds to user queries, as opposed to the more resource-intensive training process. The company's flagship product is the Rebel100 chip, rated at 2 petaflops and manufactured on Samsung's 4-nanometer process. It underpins the RebelServer, RebelRack and RebelPOD server systems.

On December 2, 2024, Rebellions [completed a merger](https://rebellions.ai/newsroom/rebellions-and-sapeon-korea-complete-merger-launching-koreas-first-ai-chip-unicorn/) with South Korean chipmaker SAPEON Korea. According to the company, the combined entity became South Korea's first chip "unicorn" — a company valued at more than $1 billion. The merged company is backed by SK Group entities: SK Telecom, SK hynix and SK Square. "Amid rapid shifts in the global AI chip market, cross-industry collaboration has become the foundation of success," Rebellions CEO Sunghyun Park said at the time.

In March 2026, Rebellions [raised](https://rebellions.ai/newsroom/rebellions-closes-400-million-pre-ipo-and-launches-rebelrack-and-rebelpod-to-accelerate-global-expansion/) $400 million at a $2.34 billion valuation. The round was led by Mirae Asset Financial Group and the state-backed Korea National Growth Fund. According to Park, the company's chip delivers "higher power efficiency at comparable performance" for inference tasks. Rebellions positions itself as an Nvidia competitor specifically in this market segment and is running pilot tests with U.S. customers.
