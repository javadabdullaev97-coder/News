A joint expedition by Fergana State University and China's Luoyang Institute of Archaeology has found the remains of a fortress wall dating to the 3rd century BC–10th century AD at the Kuva ancient settlement (Fergana region); the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation [said](https://t.me/eduuz/50139) on its Telegram channel August 6.

The work is being carried out under a joint Uzbek-Chinese research center established at Fergana State University — named in the ministry's Uzbek-language post as the "Fargʻona-Shanshi arxeologik tadqiqotlar markazi." China's Luoyang Institute of Archaeology (Henan Province) is the Chinese partner in the project, the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation channel [said](https://t.me/eduuz/50139). The post describes the find as "rare physical evidence" of East-West cultural ties along the Great Silk Road and an example of Uzbek-Chinese scientific cooperation. The ministry's post does not give the exact dimensions of the wall section found, its depth, or the location of the excavation within the settlement.

The Kuva ancient settlement arose in the 4th–3rd centuries BC and developed as a trade and craft center with pottery, jewelry and metalworking production, according to [data](https://meros.uz/ru/object/quba-budda-ibodatxonasi) from Uzbekistan's National Register of Cultural Heritage Objects. The town existed until the 13th century, when it fell to Genghis Khan's forces. Archaeologists had previously found the remains of a Buddhist temple from the second half of the 7th century at the site, with a statue of Buddha 2.5 times human height.
