Applicants seeking a second or subsequent higher education degree at Uzbekistan's universities can submit
documents through the `my.edu.uz` portal through August 10, 2026, inclusive — registration opened
on July 10.

## Who can apply, and how

The right to pursue a second or subsequent bachelor's degree belongs to those who already hold
a bachelor's diploma or a certified specialist diploma. This is established by the
[Regulation approved by Resolution No. 606 of the Cabinet of Ministers of the Republic of
Uzbekistan, dated September 24, 2021](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/-5657199).

Registration takes place electronically through the `my.edu.uz` portal: the applicant fills out
a form and specifies the university and field of study.

Second-degree studies are conducted exclusively on a contract (paid) basis — a state grant cannot
cover a repeat degree. Each university's council sets its own tuition fee based on the cost of
educating one student.

An example of how admissions work on the ground comes from a
[post by the Ministry of Higher Education's Telegram channel](https://t.me/eduuz/50026) about
Namangan State Pedagogical Institute. The university has allocated 525 second-degree slots for
full-time study and 200 for evening study; document submission there also closes on August 10.

## Timeline and what happens after applying

Registration for second and subsequent higher education runs annually from July 10 through
August 10, inclusive. Universities are required to announce admission results no later than
August 30. Those enrolled begin studies directly in the 2nd year, and the program is one year
shorter than a standard bachelor's degree.

## One university per application — what is known

Each registration in the `my.edu.uz` system is tied to one specific university and one field of
study. That is how the procedure is structured under the
[Regulation on the Procedure for Obtaining Second and Subsequent Higher Education](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/-5657199).

In early August, social media and Telegram channels discussed a claim that applicants could
submit documents to three universities at once for a second degree. No official statement from
the Ministry of Higher Education or its Telegram channel directly confirming or denying this
claim could be found — the Regulation contains no separate provision on the number of
universities, only a description of the procedure for selecting one institution when applying.
