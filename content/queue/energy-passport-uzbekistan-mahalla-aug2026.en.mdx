Uzbekistan's Ministry of Energy will draw up an energy passport for every mahalla in the country — the document will record data on local consumers and the condition of electricity and gas supply networks. This was [stated](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20692) in an interview published this week on the ministry's official Telegram channel.

## What the Passport Will Record

The energy passport will combine data on the number of consumers and their categories, the condition of electricity and gas distribution networks, and the volumes and timeframes needed for repairs or infrastructure modernization. Separately, the document will show the possibilities for connecting new consumers, [the publication says](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20692). The passport is also meant to show in which mahallas, and to what extent, capacity needs to be expanded where it is currently insufficient.

The initiative is part of a broader package of the ministry's digital projects: online platforms linking energy companies with consumers and mobile apps for notifying people about outages. The ministry is also using artificial intelligence to monitor and forecast network failures, [according to](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20692) the Ministry of Energy channel. Other priorities include reducing energy resource losses and expanding competition in the liquefied gas market.

## Context: The Ministry's Updated Leadership Team

The ministry has also [formed](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20679) an expert working group tasked with preparing proposals on reforming the supply of liquefied gas to the population, the electricity benefits system, and simplifying gas prepayment for entrepreneurs, on the instruction of the head of state. [The ministry's current leadership](https://gov.uz/ru/minenergy/guides) is headed by Sherzod Khodjaev.

## What's Next

The ministry has not yet named specific deadlines for completing work on the energy passports. Nor have pilot regions been specified where the formation of passports might begin — judging by the publication, this concerns a phased rollout covering all mahallas in the country.
