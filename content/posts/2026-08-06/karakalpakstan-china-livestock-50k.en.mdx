Karakalpakstan announced on August 6 that it has signed a memorandum with China on the supply of 50,000 head of pedigree cattle over three years — the first 200 head have already been delivered to the republic.

## What was signed, and by whom

The agreement [was signed](https://joqargikenes.uz/ru/posts/3067/) during a visit by a Karakalpakstan delegation to China, led by F. Nurmukhammadov, first deputy chairman of the republic's Council of Ministers. The memorandum of cooperation was signed by the Chinese company Hualing Yecheng Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Development Co., Ltd. and the Karakalpakstan-based LLC O'rta qala chorva mollari.

Under the terms of the memorandum, 50,000 head of pedigree cattle are to be brought into Karakalpakstan over three years. At the initial stage of the project, 200 head have already been delivered — they have been placed for keeping and breeding.

## Link to the livestock resolution

The [Jokargi Kenes of the Republic of Karakalpakstan](https://joqargikenes.uz/ru/posts/3067/) states that the project implements the objectives of Uzbekistan President's Resolution No. PP-179. On the same day, [May 12, 2026](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8214086), Decree No. UP-84 established the Agency for Livestock and Pasture Development under the Ministry of Agriculture — Resolution No. PP-179 defines its powers, including over the sector's investment and credit projects.

[LEAP News reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-05/uzbekistan-livestock-poultry-895m-2028) on August 5 that President Shavkat Mirziyoyev reviewed a national livestock and poultry farming development plan for 2026-2028 worth $895 million. Among other things, the plan called for importing 100,000 head of pedigree cattle from China and European countries nationwide. The Jokargi Kenes does not specify whether the announced supply of 50,000 head to Karakalpakstan is part of this nationwide quota or in addition to it.

## What's next

The Jokargi Kenes does not give a timeline for the next shipments — it is only known that the entire volume of 50,000 head is planned over three years from the date the memorandum was signed. The agency states that the project is meant to strengthen the sector's breeding stock, increase meat and milk production, and introduce modern livestock-keeping technologies in the region.
