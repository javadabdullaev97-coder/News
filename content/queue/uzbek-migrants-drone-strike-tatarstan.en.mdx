Uzbekistan's Ministry of Emergency Situations (MES) confirmed that eight of the country's citizens died when Ukrainian drones struck the Taneko oil refinery and residential buildings in Nizhnekamsk, Tatarstan, on August 10, [Fergana.agency](https://fergana.agency/news/148549/) reports.

The attack took place on Monday morning: drones hit industrial and residential targets in the city, located roughly 800 kilometers east of Moscow. Ukraine's military said the strike hit the Taneko refinery — one of Russia's largest oil refineries — sparking a fire there. Raids on this industrial district had also occurred in June and July 2026, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/10/at-least-13-killed-in-ukrainian-drone-attack-on-russias-nizhnekamsk) reports. The republic declared mourning for the victims.

Some of the drones hit a dormitory in Nizhnekamsk's industrial zone that housed labor migrants from Uzbekistan and Tajikistan — nine of the 13 people killed died there, [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/central-asian-migrant-workers-killed-in-central-russia-drone-raid) reports.

Tatarstan officials' first reports on the morning of the attack put the toll at 12 dead and 39 injured. By 2:04 p.m. Moscow time, the figures had risen to 13 dead, including a child, and 78 people seeking medical help — 25 of whom were hospitalized, [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8876089) reports. Russia's Investigative Committee opened a criminal case over the attack as an act of terrorism, noting that the strike hit civilian sites and people with no connection to the fighting.

Uzbekistan's Consulate General in Kazan initially reported seven citizens killed, but the country's MES later revised the toll to eight, [Fergana.agency](https://fergana.agency/news/148549/) reports. All eight victims worked at a construction site in Nizhnekamsk, and several more Uzbek nationals were hospitalized with injuries of varying severity, [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/central-asian-migrant-workers-killed-in-central-russia-drone-raid) reports. According to several sources, a Tajik citizen was also among those killed.

## Repatriation and Tashkent's response

President Shavkat Mirziyoyev ordered the creation of a government commission headed by Deputy Prime Minister Achilbay Ramatov, with representatives from the MES, the Foreign Ministry, the Ministry of Health and the Migration Agency, [Fergana.agency](https://fergana.agency/news/148549/) reports. Uzbekistan's ambassador to Russia, Botirjon Asadov, and the consul general in Kazan, Nodirjon Kasimov, traveled to the site and met with local authorities, [Dunyo.info](https://dunyo.info/en/trudovaya-migraciya-/tela-sootechestvennikov-pogibshih-v-rezultate-ataki-dronov-v-tatarstane) reports.

The bodies of the eight victims were flown back to Uzbekistan on a special flight on August 12, [Dunyo.info](https://dunyo.info/en/trudovaya-migraciya-/tela-sootechestvennikov-pogibshih-v-rezultate-ataki-dronov-v-tatarstane) reports. "Uzbekistan's Foreign Ministry, working with the Russian side, has taken control of all matters related to the situation," the ministry said in a statement cited by [Dunyo.info](https://dunyo.info/en/trudovaya-migraciya-/tela-sootechestvennikov-pogibshih-v-rezultate-ataki-dronov-v-tatarstane).

Nizhnekamsk is one of Tatarstan's petrochemical industry hubs, where thousands of migrants from Central Asia work at construction sites tied to production expansion, [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/central-asian-migrant-workers-killed-in-central-russia-drone-raid) notes. Russia is home to between two and three million Uzbek citizens, according to the same source.
