Tashkent City Prosecutor's Office has opened a criminal case against a state executor of the Bektemir branch of the Bureau of Compulsory Enforcement — the Prosecutor General's Office reported on August 6, saying he is suspected of receiving 6.1 million soums.

## What the investigation found

According to a [statement from the Prosecutor General's Office](https://t.me/uzbprokuratura/87782) on Telegram, the suspect — a state executor identified by the initials B.N. — was handling enforcement proceedings against LLC "Uzmaxsus-Suvqurilish". According to investigators, he told the chairman of the company's rehabilitation commission about 12 enforcement proceedings. He demanded money for not executing the documents and temporarily suspending them.

B.N. was caught red-handed while receiving 6.1 million soums — about $513 at the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-06/) for August 6 (11,886.72 soums per dollar). Tashkent City Prosecutor's Office opened a criminal case against him under Articles 25 and 168 (Part 4, Point "g") of the Criminal Code of the Republic of Uzbekistan.

The Prosecutor General's Office did not specify the exact time or location of the detention, nor did it say whether B.N. acted alone or with accomplices. He is presumed innocent until a court verdict takes legal effect.

## Bureau of Compulsory Enforcement

The Bureau of Compulsory Enforcement was established under the Prosecutor General's Office of the Republic of Uzbekistan by [Presidential Resolution PP-3016](https://lex.uz/docs/3220006) dated May 30, 2017, and handles the enforcement of court rulings and other enforcement documents nationwide through district and city branches, including the Bektemir branch.

## What's next

The Prosecutor General's Office urged citizens to report corruption via the hotline 1161. The agency did not specify a timeline for completing the investigation into B.N.'s case.
