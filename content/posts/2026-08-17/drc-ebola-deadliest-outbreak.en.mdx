The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has become the deadliest in the country's history: as of August 16, the death toll — according to DR Congo authorities, cited by [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/congo-ebola-outbreak-becomes-deadliest-with-2-325-deaths-almost-5-000-cases) — surpassed 2,300.

The previous record — 2,299 deaths during the 2018–2020 epidemic in North Kivu province, according to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/congo-ebola-outbreak-becomes-deadliest-with-2-325-deaths-almost-5-000-cases) — had until now been considered the deadliest Ebola outbreak in DR Congo's history. The total number of confirmed cases since the current outbreak began in May has approached 5,000, Bloomberg reports.

According to data cited at the briefing on August 12 by World Health Organization (WHO) Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, confirmed cases stood at 4,449 and deaths at 2,061 across five provinces and 53 health zones. "This is already the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record, and it's spreading faster than any previous one," [Tedros said](https://www.who.int/news-room/speeches/item/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-media-briefing---12-august-2026).

The outbreak is caused by a rare strain of Ebola virus — Bundibugyo — for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment, [WHO's outbreak summary says](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON614). Only the 2014–2016 West Africa epidemic remains larger than the current one — 28,616 cases and 11,310 deaths in Guinea, Liberia and Sierra Leone. But back then, it took nearly five months from the declaration of the epidemic to the first thousand deaths. The current outbreak reached two thousand deaths in under three months, Bloomberg notes.

The outbreak was declared in mid-May in Ituri province in northeastern DR Congo, and WHO declared it a public health emergency of international concern on May 17. Ituri accounts for about 90% of cases and 80% of deaths, Tedros said. By August 13 the epidemic had spread to a sixth province, Bas-Uélé, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/ebola-outbreak-spreads-to-sixth-congo-province-africa-cdc-says), citing the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC).

The outbreak's case-fatality rate — the share of deaths among confirmed infections — rose from about 20% in early June to 46% by August. That's according to DR Congo authorities' data cited by [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgll74vvydo). Experts interviewed by the outlet say the rise reflects not an increase in the virus's danger but failures in case detection and patients' access to medical care.

## What Authorities and WHO Are Doing

Africa CDC estimated that containing the outbreak would require $1.4 billion, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/dr-congo-ebola-epidemic-becomes-worlds-second-largest-outbreak). Carl Skau, acting head of the UN World Food Programme, called the outbreak the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic he had seen, the outlet reports.

Two candidate vaccines against the Bundibugyo virus have entered Phase 1 human trials for the first time, while animal studies showed cross-protection from the vaccine against the Zaire Ebola virus, Tedros said at the August 12 briefing.

## What's Next

WHO and DR Congo authorities plan to triple the bed capacity of treatment centers to 3,000 beds within 12 weeks of the August 12 briefing and raise contact-tracing coverage to 95% from the current 80%, Tedros said. WHO has not yet announced an exact start date for Phase 3 vaccine trials.
