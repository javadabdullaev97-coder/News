The Pentagon on August 3 signed two seven-year framework agreements worth more than $3 billion with Northrop Grumman to boost output of components for Patriot PAC-3 and THAAD interceptor missiles.

## What the agreements cover

According to [Northrop Grumman's press release](https://investor.northropgrumman.com/news-releases/news-release-details/northrop-grumman-enters-3-billion-landmark-agreements-accelerate), the first agreement, worth $2 billion, covers production of solid rocket motors and ignition safety devices for PAC-3 MSE missiles — Northrop Grumman will become a second supplier of such motors for Patriot. The second agreement, worth $1 billion, provides for increased deliveries of THAAD components — interceptor bodies, aft bulkheads and heat shields.

The company plans to double solid rocket motor manufacturing capacity at its facilities in Utah, nearly triple capacity at the Allegany Ballistics Laboratory in Rocket Center, West Virginia, by 2027, and increase output by 25% at its plant in Elkton, Maryland. The San Diego facility will expand THAAD component production. PAC-3 MSE output is expected to grow from about 600 missiles a year to several thousand, the release said. Northrop Grumman Vice President Ben Davis said "long-term investments in breakthrough manufacturing technologies and resilient supply chains are enabling the shift from steady-state production to surge production in record time" — the quote is cited in the company's press release.

According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/3/pentagon-boosts-patriot-thaad-interceptor-output-amid-stockpile-strain), Under Secretary of War for Acquisition and Sustainment Michael Duffy said building an "arsenal of freedom" requires "resilient, dynamic supply chains...". The agreements are meant to triple PAC-3 production and quadruple THAAD production.

## A separate contract with Lockheed Martin

According to [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/03/1218525-patriot-i-thaad), on July 31 the Pentagon and Lockheed Martin signed a modification to an existing annual contract for PAC-3 missile production, converting it into a seven-year contract worth up to $58.6 billion — a figure also cited by [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/3362841/us-signs-deal-boost-patriot-and-thaad-missile-parts-production). [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/3/pentagon-boosts-patriot-thaad-interceptor-output-amid-stockpile-strain) says the agreement was signed "on Wednesday" without naming a specific date — sources differ on this point. According to Al Jazeera, the contract had previously been worth $4.7 billion a year. The Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman agreements are separate contracts but were announced as part of the same Pentagon initiative to expand the production base for air and missile defense systems.

## Why now — an interceptor shortage

Pentagon officials attribute the accelerated production push to depleted interceptor stockpiles caused by conflicts in the Middle East and continued military aid to Ukraine, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/3/pentagon-boosts-patriot-thaad-interceptor-output-amid-stockpile-strain) and [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/3362841/us-signs-deal-boost-patriot-and-thaad-missile-parts-production) report. According to an estimate by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) cited by SCMP, the U.S. military has fewer than 1,000 Patriot interceptors and fewer than 250 THAAD interceptors in stock.

Al Jazeera calls Patriot "Ukraine's only weapon capable of intercepting ballistic missiles," noting that Kyiv's stocks of these interceptors are limited amid continued Russian strikes.

According to [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8861630), in late July the Pentagon requested $18.2 billion in additional funding from Congress to replenish missile stockpiles — part of a broader $67 billion supplemental funding package tied to the fallout from the confrontation with Iran.

## What's next

Northrop Grumman is to ramp up monthly THAAD component deliveries and reach increased PAC-3 MSE motor production volumes over the seven-year life of the agreements, with the main capacity expansion at the West Virginia facility planned by 2027. The $18.2 billion in additional funding the Pentagon requested to replenish missile stockpiles has not yet been approved by the U.S. Congress.
