US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) told the Court of International Trade on August 4 that it had refunded importers $100 billion — 60% of $165 billion in "Liberation Day" tariffs ruled illegal in February 2026.

## What the Court Filing Showed

According to [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/b0f728aa-1e71-4bb0-b50f-2e0818712734) and [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/05/trump-administration-has-paid-back-100bn-of-165bn-tariff-refunds), customs officials filed the document on Tuesday, August 4. The refunds are processed through CAPE, a system CBP launched specifically to handle payments on the voided tariffs. Tens of thousands of refund declarations are going through it. Customs is still paying out the remaining roughly $65 billion — 40% of the court-ordered amount — and has not given a date for when the payments will be complete.

## How the Tariffs Became Illegal

The US Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026, that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not give the president authority to impose tariffs unilaterally. The ruling struck down the so-called "Liberation Day" tariffs, which Donald Trump introduced in April 2025: a baseline 10% rate for most exporting countries and higher "reciprocal" rates for roughly 60 trade partners the US accused of maintaining their own barriers against American goods. The court ordered the administration to refund all $165 billion collected under the scheme. LEAP News has [previously covered](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/04/world-us-states-tariff-lawsuit) the arguments in this dispute in the context of the 25 states' lawsuit against the tariffs' successor.

## Who Gets the Money — and Why the Treasury Is Unhappy

The refunds go not to consumers but to importers — companies that officially paid the tariff at the border. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, speaking at a Dallas Economic Club event in late February, called the anticipated payouts the "ultimate corporate welfare" and said: "My sense is Americans won't see this," [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/bessent-says-tariff-revenue-to-be-virtually-unchanged-in-2026). Bessent's logic: some importers have already passed the cost of the tariffs on to retail prices, so the money from the court ruling will go to the companies that paid the tariff, not to the buyers who paid for the goods at the final price.

## Tariffs Refunded, New Ones Imposed in Their Place

The reversal of the IEEPA tariffs doesn't mean the administration is abandoning its tariff policy — the White House is using other legal grounds the court's ruling did not touch. On August 3, attorneys general and governors of 25 states [filed a lawsuit](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/04/world-us-states-tariff-lawsuit) against new tariffs on goods from the same 60 trade partners — this time imposed under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 on the pretext of combating forced labor. The plaintiffs call it an attempt to recreate the restrictions already ruled illegal under a different pretext.

## What's Next

Customs will continue payments through CAPE — according to [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/b0f728aa-1e71-4bb0-b50f-2e0818712734) and [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/05/trump-administration-has-paid-back-100bn-of-165bn-tariff-refunds), about 40% of the court-ordered amount is still being processed. In parallel, the Court of International Trade is hearing the case over the new Section 301 tariffs, filed by the 25 states on August 3.
