US President Donald Trump on August 17 threatened to bomb Oman if it interferes with the blockade of Iranian shipping in the Strait of Hormuz — the day a 60-day memorandum with Tehran on ship passage formally expired.

Trump said this in a phone interview with Fox News: if "Oman gets in the way," the United States would "bomb the hell out of them." [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-17/trump-threatens-to-bomb-oman-if-it-gets-in-way-of-us-fox-news-msx5rdoc) reported this, citing Fox News. According to Trump, the US has no timeline for resolving the conflict, and a direct communication channel is in place with officers of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

The memorandum lifting the blockade, which the US and Iran signed on June 17, promised ships free passage for 60 days — that period expired on August 17.

Transit through the strait had nearly stopped by that date. According to Kpler data cited by [Reuters](https://lufkindailynews.com/news_reuters/business/oil-hangs-onto-gains-as-us-iran-peace-talks-stall-hormuz-shipping-slows/article_97661cea-dba6-5afb-9237-3d672c925317.html), five ships crossed the strait on Saturday, August 15, and none on Sunday, August 16. A week earlier, 31 ships passed over the same two days. Before the US and Israeli war against Iran that began in February 2026, the strait handled about 130 ships a day. That is a fifth of global oil supplies. The slowdown began after the UAE reported an attack on three tankers belonging to state-owned Abu Dhabi National Oil Company.

Oil is getting more expensive against this backdrop: according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/12/oil-prices-rise-as-attacks-dent-hopes-for-strait-of-hormuz-reopening), Brent futures rose to $89.53 a barrel on August 12. That is 24% above the level seen in late February, when the conflict began. On August 17, contracts traded near $89.28, Reuters reported.

## Oman and Iran Discuss Temporary Corridor

Oman is negotiating with Tehran over a temporary shipping scheme through the strait, the [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/d4d07198-498d-48d1-9e54-85f1febcd070) writes. The parties have already agreed on route coordinates and are discussing technical, legal and environmental details, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/hormuz-deal-close-whats-the-latest-on-each-sides-positions) reports. Iran is insisting on joint management and port fees, and is also demanding that the naval blockade of Iranian ports be lifted before the strait reopens. Washington rejects any scheme that would give Tehran control over the route and is demanding full freedom of passage without fees or permits.

## Tehran Positions Security Officials for a Protracted War

On August 10, Iran's supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei appointed six new commanders across the security services, [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/17/world/middleeast/iran-khamenei-military-leaders-war.html) reports. Mojtaba Khamenei took over leadership of the country in March 2026 after his father, Ali Khamenei, was killed in an Israeli strike in late February.

Ahmad Vahidi was named commander-in-chief of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, with Mostafa Izadi as his deputy. Ali Abdollahi was appointed chief of staff of the Armed Forces, with Kioumars Heidari as his deputy. Ali Ozmaei became commander of the IRGC Navy, and Hossein Taeb was named head of the Basij militia. In addition, former IRGC commander Mohsen Rezaei was appointed secretary of the Supreme National Security Council.

The reshuffle strengthens Mojtaba Khamenei's authority while addressing vulnerabilities exposed by two wars, Ali Vaez, a senior analyst at the International Crisis Group, told [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/11/whats-behind-irans-new-military-appointments-and-what-do-they-indicate). Analyst Sina Toossi noted that the new commanders have been tasked with preparing "powerful offensive operations" — a signal of a shift from deterrence to readiness to strike first.

Talks on the strait continue with no announced deadline, and the naval blockade remains in place.
