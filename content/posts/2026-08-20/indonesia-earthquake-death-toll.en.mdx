A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck Indonesia's Flores Island early on August 15. According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/indonesia-earthquake-death-toll-flores-island-independence-day/), as of the evening of August 17 the death toll had risen to at least 68, with more than 200 injured.

The epicenter was located 68 kilometers north-northwest of the city of Ende, in East Nusa Tenggara province, at a depth of 10 kilometers, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/at-least-two-killed-as-magnitude-7-7-quake-hits-indonesia) reports. Authorities issued a tsunami warning and lifted it a few hours later — no major wave formed.

The death toll grew as rescuers reached villages cut off by landslides. On August 15, Al Jazeera reported 47 deaths. On the morning of August 17, Indonesia's National Disaster Mitigation Agency (BNPB) put the toll at 53 — LEAP News [reported earlier](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/17/world-indonesia-earthquake-toll-53). By that evening the count had risen to at least 68, CBS News reports.

According to BNPB data cited by CBS News, more than 1,300 homes were damaged on Flores, with nearly 250 destroyed entirely. About 5,000 people have moved into temporary tent camps. Some areas remain cut off by landslides and fallen trees — rescuers reached the remote island of Palue only late Sunday evening, CBS News reports.

## Aid on Independence Day

Indonesia marked the 81st anniversary of its independence on Monday, August 17, but thousands of Flores residents were still waiting for help, CBS News reports. BNPB deployed nearly 1,500 military and police personnel to the disaster zone and delivered 275 tons of supplies on 10 aircraft. Two field hospitals were set up in the city of Ruteng.

"My house is now just a pile of rubble. We urgently need food, medicine and clean water," clothing vendor Rashid Jafar from the village of Reo told CBS News.

BNPB deputy chief Abdul Muhari announced the deployment of rescue units in a video statement cited by CBS News.

## What's next

BNPB declared a 14-day state of emergency starting Sunday, August 16, CBS News reports. More than 1,500 aftershocks have been recorded since the disaster began, and rescuers continue working to reach villages cut off by landslides.

The 1992 Flores earthquake and tsunami killed about 2,500 people, [Euronews](https://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/17/deadly-quake-killed-at-least-53-people-in-indonesia) notes.
