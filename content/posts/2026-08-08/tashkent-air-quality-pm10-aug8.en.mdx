Uzgidromet recorded PM10 in Tashkent's air at 86 µg/m³ as of 9 a.m. on August 8 — the third straight daily exceedance of the standard and the highest reading for the monitored period.

## What the Instruments Showed on the Morning of August 8

According to a [Uzgidromet bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21915) published on the morning of August 8, automated monitoring stations recorded a PM10 concentration of 86 µg/m³. The maximum permissible concentration (MPC) is 75 µg/m³, meaning the exceedance was 1.14 times the standard. The finer PM2.5 particulate concentration was 31 µg/m³, staying within the MPC of 60 µg/m³.

Uzgidromet's full official name is the Hydrometeorological Service Agency under the National Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan for Ecology and Climate Change. The agency publishes air quality bulletins from Tashkent's automated monitoring stations on its Telegram channel several times a day.

## Exceedance Persists for a Second Day

The PM10 deviation from the norm did not start on August 8. [LEAP News wrote](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/tashkent-air-quality-pm10-aug7-morning) about the first exceedance in several days, recorded on the morning of August 7. At the time, [Uzgidromet's 9 a.m. bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21898) showed PM10 at 83 µg/m³ — 1.10 times above the MPC of 75 µg/m³.

The deviation did not disappear that evening. [The 6 p.m. bulletin on August 7](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21904) recorded the same PM10 level — 83 µg/m³, an exceedance of 1.10 times. The 86 µg/m³ recorded on the morning of August 8 marks the third straight above-standard reading in a day and the highest value for the period.

The PM2.5 reading, while staying within the MPC across all three measurements, moved unevenly: 27 µg/m³ on the morning of August 7, 22 µg/m³ that evening, and 31 µg/m³ on the morning of August 8 — a peak for the monitored period.

## Pollution Also Noted in Six Other Cities

Besides Tashkent, [Uzgidromet's environmental bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21903) as of 5 p.m. on August 7 recorded atmospheric pollution in Angren, Jizzakh, Qarshi, Qibray, Navoi and Akhangaran. The agency did not provide specific pollutant concentrations for these cities — only the fact of observed pollution.

## Gap With WHO Recommendations

In its [guidelines revised in 2021](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034433), the World Health Organization recommends not exceeding 45 µg/m³ of PM10 per day. The 86 µg/m³ recorded by Uzgidromet on the morning of August 8 is nearly double that reference — even though Uzbekistan's own MPC was exceeded by only 1.14 times. For PM2.5, the WHO's daily reference is 15 µg/m³: the measured 31 µg/m³ is more than double that level, though still well below Uzbekistan's standard of 60 µg/m³. Uzgidromet did not accompany its recent bulletins with recommendations for the public.
