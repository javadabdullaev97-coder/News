Apple on August 19 filed a court opposition to OpenAI's motion to dismiss the trade secret theft case, reaffirming its allegations against the company, its io Products division and two former employees, [9to5Mac reports](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/19/apple-hits-back-at-openais-bid-to-dismiss-lawsuit-reaffirms-pervasive-trade-secret-misappropriation/).

In the filing, Apple's lawyers wrote that the defendants "do not grapple with the shocking allegations at the core" of the case. The lawsuit was filed in July 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California — case No. 5:26-cv-07078, [Apple Inc. v. Liu et al](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/19/apple-hits-back-at-openais-bid-to-dismiss-lawsuit-reaffirms-pervasive-trade-secret-misappropriation/). The defendants are former Apple engineers Chang Liu and Tang Tan, as well as OpenAI and its hardware division, io Products. The company set up the division together with designer Jony Ive to develop its own devices, [Claims Journal reports, citing AP](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/07/339348.htm).

## Allegations and the Parties' Defense

According to Apple, Liu left the company in January 2026 and did not return his company-issued laptop. He later exploited a previously unknown vulnerability in the authentication system to access Apple's internal file storage after leaving the company. Apple claims Liu downloaded confidential materials about unreleased devices and "was excited about the find" — writing "LOL… so funny" in a message, [according to Apple's court filing](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/19/apple-hits-back-at-openais-bid-to-dismiss-lawsuit-reaffirms-pervasive-trade-secret-misappropriation/). Tan now leads OpenAI's hardware division. Apple accuses him of asking a then-current Apple employee to bring "some of the parts [she] had been working on."

The lawsuit concerns trade secrets tied to Apple's unreleased products: manufacturing technologies, components and supplier data. Apple claims OpenAI gathered this information while building its own consumer device and hired more than 400 former Apple employees, [Claims Journal reports](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/07/339348.htm).

OpenAI filed a motion to dismiss the lawsuit in early August, calling Apple's claims "rotten to its core." The company maintains that its employees' actions were "harmless and lawful." OpenAI also said former employees helped their former colleagues at their request, and that hiring specialists from Apple is standard industry practice.

## What's Next

A hearing on Apple's request for a preliminary injunction is scheduled for October 1, 2026. This temporary measure could restrict the defendants' further actions until a final ruling in the case. The court has not yet ruled on OpenAI's motion to dismiss the lawsuit entirely.
