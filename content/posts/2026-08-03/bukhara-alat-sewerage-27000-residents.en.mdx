About 27,000 residents of Bukhara Region's Alat district will get access to centralized sewerage under a $385.12 million project by the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB), JSC [O'zsuvta'minot](https://t.me/uzsuv/45308) said on August 3.

## What is being built in Alat district

The company did not disclose in its post either the cost of the work specifically in Alat district or the timeline for connecting homes — for details, O'zsuvta'minot refers readers to a [separate publication](https://uzsuv.uz/ru/posts/20598) on its website.

Part of the answer comes from AIIB documentation. The bank finances the Bukhara Region water supply and sewerage development program. According to [the bank's data](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2026/_download/Uzbekistan/Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-ProjectBWSP-S_RFB_04_Contract_AwardGoods_Works_NCS.pdf), the contract for building the sewerage network in Alat district was awarded to a joint venture of Uzbekistan's Obidjon Akmalxon Hamkor and Turkey's Atilla Insaat Turizm ve Ticaret — the contract price was $23.3 million ($23,295,455.78). According to [AIIB's project implementation report](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2026/_download/Uzbekistan/PIMR_SBF_Uzbekistan_P000314_Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project-Phase-1_No12_April_2026_Public-Version.pdf) for January–March 2026, this component of the project includes laying collectors, sewage pumping stations, and treatment facilities.

## Part of a $385 million project

Sewerage construction in Alat is one component of the Bukhara Region Water Supply and Sewerage Project, which AIIB [approved](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2020/approved/Uzbekistan-Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project.html) on April 3, 2020. The bank's loan totals $385.12 million, the agreement was signed on September 30, 2020, the borrower is Uzbekistan's Ministry of Economy and Finance, and the implementing agency is JSC O'zsuvta'minot.

Besides sewerage in Alat, the program envisages building and reconstructing water supply networks in Bukhara, Romitan, Peshkun, Jondor, and Karaulbazar districts and in Kagan, as well as centralized sewerage systems in the district centers of Karakul, Alat, Karaulbazar, and Romitan districts and in Gijduvan. According to [AIIB data](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2020/_download/_pimr_December-2023/Uzbekistan_P000314_Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project.PDF), the entire project is meant to provide 1.15 million residents of Bukhara Region with access to safe drinking water and sewerage. The loan closing date was initially set for December 30, 2025, but on December 12, 2025, the government of Uzbekistan and AIIB signed an amendment to the loan agreement, extending it by 36 months — to December 30, 2028, according to [AIIB's report](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2026/_download/Uzbekistan/PIMR_SBF_Uzbekistan_P000314_Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project-Phase-1_No12_April_2026_Public-Version.pdf) for January–March 2026.

As of December 2023, according to [an earlier AIIB report](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2020/_download/_pimr_December-2023/Uzbekistan_P000314_Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project.PDF), construction of the sewerage network in Alat had not yet begun: the detailed design was still being finalized, and the site for the treatment facilities had not been finally agreed with authorities.

By April 2026, the picture had changed. Contracts for building the sewerage network and treatment facilities in Alat and Karakul were signed on March 24, 2026 — the network cost $49.5 million, and the treatment facilities cost $19.4 million, [AIIB said](https://www.aiib.org/en/projects/details/2026/_download/Uzbekistan/PIMR_SBF_Uzbekistan_P000314_Bukhara-Region-Water-Supply-and-Sewerage-Project-Phase-1_No12_April_2026_Public-Version.pdf) in its project implementation report (PIMR No. 12) for January–March 2026. The contractor has begun mobilization: a 20% advance payment has been made under the network contract in Alat, and 10% under the network contract in Karakul (both payments relate to the networks, not the treatment facilities). At the same time, the same report describes the treatment facilities lot in Alat (Alat WWTP) as having been re-tendered due to a lack of qualified bidders in the first round — which contradicts the statement about a treatment facilities contract signed on March 24. AIIB does not provide separate contract numbers for the treatment facilities in Alat and Karakul, and it is unclear from the report whether the Alat portion of this contract was actually signed.

The project as a whole is under AIIB's enhanced monitoring — Enhanced Monitoring, Level I — due to two "red flags": slow disbursement of funds and a slow pace of construction, according to the same report.

## What's next

O'zsuvta'minot did not give a completion date for construction or a schedule for connecting specific homes and mahallas to the new network in Alat district. These details, if they exist, are provided on a separate page of the company's website, which did not open via a direct link at the time of publication.
