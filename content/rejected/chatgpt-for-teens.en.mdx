OpenAI [launched](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/) ChatGPT for Teens on August 18, a separate version of the chatbot for users aged 13 to 17 with stricter content restrictions and new tools for parents.

Users are automatically switched to the teen version if the system itself estimates their age to be between 13 and 17. The same switch happens if a user manually enters that age, [the company's blog says](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/), a point also confirmed by [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/18/chatgpt-for-teens-openai/).

The version restricts content that OpenAI describes as potentially harmful to a teenager's development or not age-appropriate; the company does not disclose a specific list of such topics in the blog post. The blog separately notes notifications triggered by signs of eating disorders, a detail reported by [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/18/chatgpt-for-teens-openai/).

The company also added a Study Mode: instead of giving ready-made answers to homework, the chatbot asks guiding questions and works through material step by step. The mode can turn on automatically during designated study hours, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-launches-a-safer-chatgpt-for-teens-years-after-teens-started-using-it/), citing an OpenAI post.

Parents have gained access to part of a teen's account settings and can set "Quiet Hours" that restrict access to the chatbot. They can also receive notifications about risky situations, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-launches-a-safer-chatgpt-for-teens-years-after-teens-started-using-it/).

OpenAI is rolling out the teen version amid lawsuits in which families of deceased teenagers link their deaths to a lack of safeguards in the chatbot, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/openai-launches-a-safer-chatgpt-for-teens-years-after-teens-started-using-it/).
