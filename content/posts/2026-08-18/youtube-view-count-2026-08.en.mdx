YouTube will start counting a public view of a video from the moment it starts playing — from the very first frame — beginning August 24, 2026, the same way for regular videos, Shorts, podcasts and livestreams, YouTube said on its [support forum](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/433409976?hl=en).

"Starting August 24, 2026, a view will be counted the moment a video starts playing — from the very first frame," the post says. Previously, the company used different counting systems for different video formats. Creators complained about confusion in the metrics and asked for an accurate picture of content reach — that is how YouTube explained the reason for the change.

Public counters will rise for most channels after the update, because some views that previously weren't counted will now be counted immediately, [Engadget predicts](https://www.engadget.com/2238611/youtube-public-view-counts-will-likely-get-higher-starting-next-week/).

The new principle has already been in effect for Shorts for more than a year. Since March 31, 2025, YouTube has counted a view of a video in this format as soon as it starts playing or replaying. Previously this required a few seconds of viewing, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted). Now the same model is being extended to regular videos, podcasts and livestreams — YouTube calls this aligning all formats under a single standard, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/youtube-will-now-count-a-view-as-soon-as-a-video-starts-playing/).

The company never officially disclosed the exact previous threshold for regular videos. It was believed that a view was counted after at least 30 seconds of playback, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/youtube-will-now-count-a-view-as-soon-as-a-video-starts-playing/).

The previous counting method won't disappear: it will remain in creator analytics under the name Engaged views (literally, "views with engagement"). YouTube will continue to use it to determine engagement and eligibility for the Partner Program — the change in methodology won't affect creator earnings, [TechCrunch writes](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/youtube-will-now-count-a-view-as-soon-as-a-video-starts-playing/). The public counter under a video becomes a reach metric rather than an engagement metric.

Views of videos already published won't be recalculated retroactively: their current public counters will remain unchanged. Under the new rules, only views gained after August 24 will be counted, [Dexerto notes](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/youtube-is-making-a-major-change-to-how-long-form-video-views-are-counted-3399287/).
