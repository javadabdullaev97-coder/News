The Competition Development and Consumer Rights Protection Committee [said](https://raqobat.gov.uz/raqobat-qomitasi-talim-hizmatlari-sohasida-murojaatlarning-qarijb-96-foizi-nodavlat-talim-hizmatlariga-togri-kelib-ularning-soni-ortib-borayotganligi-sababli-sohada-mavjud-muammolarni-hal-etish/) on August 5, 2026, that nearly 96% of 2,070 education-related complaints filed over the past three years involved private institutions. During that period, the agency received 103,500 consumer complaints across all sectors.

## What Complaints Cite Most Often

According to the [committee's analysis](https://raqobat.gov.uz/raqobat-qomitasi-talim-hizmatlari-sohasida-murojaatlarning-qarijb-96-foizi-nodavlat-talim-hizmatlariga-togri-kelib-ularning-soni-ortib-borayotganligi-sababli-sohada-mavjud-muammolarni-hal-etish/), consumers most often report:

- education quality lower than promised in the contract;
- being charged for services that were not actually provided;
- incomplete disclosure of contract terms — particularly refund procedures;
- difficulties terminating installment payment contracts;
- a mismatch between advertised services and what the organization actually provides.

Following the review of complaints, consumers were refunded about 1 billion soums (about $84,000 at the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's August 5 rate of 11,942.21 soums per dollar), the committee [said](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12539) on its Telegram channel.

## What the Committee Is Proposing

The agency sent three proposals to relevant government bodies for regulating the private education services market:

- introduce a voluntary certification system for private educational institutions;
- develop and approve uniform rules for paid education services provided by non-state organizations;
- set minimum requirements for consumer interactions — from disclosing contract terms to refund procedures.

As benchmarks, the committee cites foreign private-education certification models — Qualiopi in France, NRTO in the Netherlands and EduQua in Switzerland.

## What's Next

The committee said it is ready to cooperate with the National Agency for Quality Assurance in Education, the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation, and the Ministry of Preschool and School Education in finalizing rules for the private sector. The publication did not specify a timeline for reviewing the proposals or adopting new rules.
