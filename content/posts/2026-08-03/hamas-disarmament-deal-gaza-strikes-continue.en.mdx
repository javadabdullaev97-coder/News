Hamas and other Palestinian factions agreed on a Gaza Strip disarmament plan with mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the United States on July 30, but Israeli strikes on the enclave did not stop.

## Terms of the Agreement

The agreement was announced by US President Donald Trump — according to him, the plan was drafted by the US-led Board of Peace and the International Stabilization Force for Gaza. The document, [a copy of which Al Jazeera obtained](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/gaza-board-of-peace-announces-hamas-disarmament-agreement-what-we-know), envisions the National Committee and the stabilization force entering Gaza. The committee will carry out an "inventory and storage" of weapons under the supervision of an international verification commission. The parties are required to prepare a schedule for the inventory of Hamas weapons within 14 days — the deadline can be extended with the consent of the commission and all parties involved. Weapons will not be handed over to Israel or any non-Palestinian body — the National Committee is designated as the sole custodian. Disarmament is synchronized with a phased withdrawal of Israeli forces.

Ghazi Hamad, a member of Hamas's negotiating delegation, [told Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/gaza-board-of-peace-announces-hamas-disarmament-agreement-what-we-know) that the movement "will not implement a single item of the agreement if Israeli forces do not fulfill their obligations."

Israel has not officially confirmed acceptance of the roadmap, [Al Jazeera notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/world-reacts-with-cautious-optimism-to-hamas-disarmament-agreement). National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called the agreement unacceptable: "The eliminations in Gaza must continue, [Palestinian] emigration must be encouraged, Israel must win."

## Response from Mediators and the UN

Board of Peace director Nikolay Mladenov wrote that "there were several moments when I did not believe we would make it," and that what matters now is not the document itself but its implementation, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/world-reacts-with-cautious-optimism-to-hamas-disarmament-agreement). EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs Kaja Kallas called the agreement "a constructive step toward peace" provided it is fully implemented, adding: "A lot has to come together for this to work."

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said on July 31 that the fighting "must stop on all fronts" in the Middle East, and that the region and the world "cannot afford another war," [the UN report says](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1168062). The same report cites UNRWA figures: from the ceasefire taking effect in October 2025 through July 22, 2026, 1,180 Palestinians were killed in Gaza and 3,810 were injured.

## Strikes on Gaza After the Deal Was Announced

A day after the agreement was announced, on August 1, the Israeli military [carried out strikes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/israeli-strikes-kill-four-across-gaza-destroy-medical-supply-warehouse) on several areas of Gaza — Sheikh Radwan, Deir al-Balah and Al-Mawasi. According to Gaza's Ministry of Health, at least seven people were killed and 76 injured over 48 hours. One strike hit a medical supply warehouse near Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital, said Gaza Health Minister Munir al-Bursh. The Israeli military and Shin Bet said they had destroyed five Hamas weapons warehouses overnight, one of them near that hospital. Al Jazeera does not specify whether this refers to the same site.

On August 2, the death toll rose: [according to medical sources in Gaza](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/israel-kills-five-in-gaza-despite-trumps-hamas-disarmament-plan), at least 17 people were killed on Sunday — the strikes hit residential buildings in Gaza City, Khan Younis and Deir al-Balah. Gaza's Ministry of Health reported that as of August 2, the total death toll in the strip since the ceasefire began in October 2025 had reached 1,222, with 4,053 injured.

That same day, August 2, the Israeli military [said](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8860987) it had killed three militants in Gaza City and the northern part of the strip. According to the Israeli military, they belonged to the Army of Islam and Islamic Jihad groups, as well as a Hamas battalion, and were preparing attacks on Israeli soldiers.

Casualty figures for Gaza come only from the strip's Ministry of Health and medical sources at hospitals — there is no independent confirmation of the numbers. The Israeli military, for its part, does not comment on the overall death toll and describes individual strikes as targeted operations against armed militants.

## What's Next

The nearest deadline under the agreement is 14 days from the signing of the document: by that point, the parties are only required to prepare a schedule for the inventory of Hamas weapons. US and Board of Peace officials estimate the disarmament itself will take 200–300 days, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/gaza-board-of-peace-announces-hamas-disarmament-agreement-what-we-know). Under the document, the withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza runs parallel to disarmament. Neither Israel nor Hamas has yet confirmed the practical implementation of these provisions has begun.
