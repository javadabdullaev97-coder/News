The United Arab Emirates on August 14 accused Iran of attacking two ADNOC tankers in the Strait of Hormuz the previous evening, amid an ongoing dispute between Washington and Tehran over control of the strait.

## Attack on ADNOC tankers

ADNOC confirmed that two of its vessels came under attack on the evening of Thursday, August 13, while transiting the strait; the situation was "brought under control" and there were no reports of casualties, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/uae-accuses-iran-of-attacks-on-two-adnoc-vessels-in-strait-of-hormuz) reported, citing the UAE's state news agency WAM. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) [reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) on Friday that two drones had struck tankers in the strait the previous day, causing minor damage with no casualties; it was not clear whether these incidents matched the attack on the ADNOC vessels.

The United Arab Emirates' Ministry of Foreign Affairs (UAE Foreign Ministry) [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/uae-accuses-iran-of-attacks-on-two-adnoc-vessels-in-strait-of-hormuz) early Friday that the country "strongly condemns the hostile Iranian attack targeting two ADNOC-affiliated vessels during their passage through the Strait of Hormuz." The ministry called Iran's attempts to use the strait as a tool of economic pressure "piracy" and "a direct threat to regional stability, its peoples and global energy supplies." Iran did not immediately comment on the attack or the UAE's statements.

Saudi Arabia also condemned the attack on Friday. The kingdom's Foreign Ministry [said on X](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) that it "condemns and denounces in the strongest terms" the strike on the two ADNOC tankers, calling it "an unacceptable repetition of Iranian attacks on commercial vessels and tankers."

This is the second attack on ADNOC vessels in a week: on [August 8](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/08/iran-missile-strike-adnoc-tanker-hormuz), the UAE accused Iran of a missile strike on one of the company's tankers, which also caused no casualties. According to ADNOC data as of August 14, a total of 15 of the company's vessels have come under attack in the strait since the war began in late February, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/uae-accuses-iran-of-attacks-on-two-adnoc-vessels-in-strait-of-hormuz).

## Trump claims "full control," Iran responds in kind

A Truth Social post published under Trump's name on August 12 said: "The United States has full control of the Strait of Hormuz. I think we're going to keep it!" The same post described the US naval blockade of Iran as a "steel wall" and said Iran had "nothing to counter it with," [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/12/as-strait-of-hormuz-transit-drops-trump-again-says-us-has-control). On August 10, he told reporters at the White House that the US Navy had "swept the entire strait of mines," [Deutsche Welle reported](https://www.dw.com/en/gulf-states-scramble-for-strait-of-hormuz-alternatives/a-78336679).

Iran responded the next day, August 13, with a series of statements from various officials. Basij commander Hojjat al-Islam Hossein Taeb [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/iran-says-strait-of-hormuz-under-its-control-rejects-trump-claims) "today the Strait of Hormuz is under the administration and control of the Islamic Republic, and our country continues on its path in complete safety," adding: "The Americans have seen the strength of the Islamic revolution." Rear Admiral Ali Azmaei, the recently appointed commander of the naval forces of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), [said](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/): "We have full and decisive control over movements in the strait. No activity there escapes the observation of IRGC naval forces," adding that "reality should be assessed on the ground, not through statements by American officials."

Iran's joint military headquarters, Khatam al-Anbia, [called](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/iran-says-strait-of-hormuz-under-its-control-rejects-trump-claims) US statements about the free passage of vessels through the strait "lies and fabrication" and stressed that no vessel would pass through the strait without Tehran's permission. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in a post on X that the US "has long been making miscalculations due to intelligence failures," calling Washington's actions around the Strait of Hormuz "an even bigger miscalculation": "Worse than fake news is only fake intelligence."

Senior military-political official Rasoul Sanaei-Rad [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/iran-says-strait-of-hormuz-under-its-control-rejects-trump-claims) Iran would not open the strait until the US fulfills its obligations under the temporary ceasefire agreement, and that opening the strait is not something Washington can achieve unilaterally. He warned that in a possible new war, Iran would act "harder and more aggressively."

## Traffic through the strait plunges

According to the Kpler analytics platform, only 8 vessels passed through the strait on Tuesday, August 11 — compared with 130–140 vessels a day before the war; the average over the past ten days was about 12 vessels a day, [Al Jazeera reported, citing Reuters data](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/12/as-strait-of-hormuz-transit-drops-trump-again-says-us-has-control).

A statement by US Energy Secretary Chris Wright that "the seven-day average of oil coming out of the Strait of Hormuz is now nearly 9 million barrels per day" was [disputed](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/12/as-strait-of-hormuz-transit-drops-trump-again-says-us-has-control) by Brett Erickson, an analyst at consulting firm Obsidian Risk Advisors: "There is zero evidence that 9 million barrels a day is coming out of the strait. Zero. Not 'maybe,' not 'potentially.'" Dennis Citrinowicz, a senior researcher at the Institute for National Security Studies (INSS), told Al Jazeera: "The US does not control the Strait of Hormuz. Despite everything that has happened, Iran retains sufficient capacity to disrupt shipping through it."

The drop in traffic is forcing Gulf states to look for alternative routes. According to the IMF PortWatch platform, Saudi Arabia's shipments from its Gulf coast fell from 47.5 million tons a year earlier to 6.3 million tons in April–May, while deliveries via the Red Sea along the East-West pipeline to the port of Yanbu rose from 29.6 million to 54.8 million tons — offsetting about 61% of the lost volume, [Deutsche Welle reported](https://www.dw.com/en/gulf-states-scramble-for-strait-of-hormuz-alternatives/a-78336679). The UAE has seen no similar success: traffic through its Gulf coast fell from 68.5 million to 12 million tons over the same period, while shipments through the country's alternative ports, Fujairah and Khor Fakkan, also declined, from 13.7 million to 6.3 million tons, instead of absorbing the redirected cargo. Economist Hassan Mansour told DW that discussed new pipeline projects — such as the Basra-Aqaba pipeline, estimated at $8-10 billion and requiring five to seven years to build — would not solve the oil market's problem "here and now."

## Crisis timeline since early August

The crisis over the Strait of Hormuz has been unfolding since late February, when the US and Israel launched a military operation against Iran. In June, the parties signed a ceasefire memorandum, but the agreement did not last a month. On August 9, Iran set out six conditions for fully opening the strait — from lifting sanctions to withdrawing US naval forces from the region — while Trump said he was betting on economically exhausting Tehran instead of launching new strikes, [LEAP News reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/10/iran-hormuz-strait-crisis-aug2026). Iran simultaneously held talks with Oman on a temporary maritime corridor and approved a bill restricting the passage of US and Israeli vessels through the strait, [LEAP News reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/10/iran-hormuz-economic-pressure-aug2026).

US Vice President JD Vance [told](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) Fox News on August 13 that the Trump administration's "goal number one" is "keeping cheap oil and gas prices for Americans," ranking preventing Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons second. The same day, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent [threatened](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) Iran with "economic isolation the world has never seen" and announced new measures for the following week. Earlier, on August 4, Bessent [told](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) CNBC that a deal to open the strait could be reached "within days."

West Texas Intermediate crude traded at around $81 a barrel on the evening of August 13 — down from less than $70 in February but up from more than $110 in April. Gasoline in the US [averaged](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-strait-of-hormuz-uae-accuses-attacks-vance-oil-gas/) $4.07 a gallon, compared with $3.16 a year earlier, according to the American Automobile Association (AAA).
