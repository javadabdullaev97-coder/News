The Central Bank of the Republic of Uzbekistan (CBU) has tightened requirements for how banks handle customer complaints — the resolution was [registered with the Ministry of Justice of Uzbekistan](https://t.me/centralbankuzbekistan/13395) on August 1, 2026, under No. 3030-12. It takes effect one month after official publication.

## What's Changing

The document — [Resolution of the CBU Board No. 19/16 of July 15, 2026](https://cbu.uz/upload/medialibrary/660/gxmd5djd01h64hn3i7yfhwgff28ege23/3030_12.pdf) — introduces additions and amendments to the Regulation on Minimum Requirements for Commercial Banks in Their Relations With Consumers of Banking Services. The regulation's base version was approved back in 2018 by CBU Board Resolution No. 16/18 of April 28, 2018 ([reg. No. 3030 of July 2, 2018](https://lex.uz/docs/3819160)).

The regulation introduces a legal definition of the term "systemic deficiency" for the first time. It refers to a deficiency that is not limited to a single case. It manifests itself in a specific financial product, business process, network of bank service branches, information system or the bank's internal control system, as well as in contracts, operations or service processes of similar content. Such a deficiency leads to violations of consumer rights.

Banks are now explicitly required, when handling customer complaints, to eliminate not only the violations and negative situations specified in the complaint itself but also the systemic deficiencies — that is, the underlying causes of those violations.

## New Obligations for Banks

The resolution rewrites clause 58<sup>17</sup> of the regulation. The key new requirements:

- The bank must summarize and analyze complaints received on a quarterly and annual basis, preparing analytical reports with data on the types, number and causes of complaints, the most frequent customer grievances, and which banking products and regions they relate to.
- The bank's management board must review the state of complaint handling at its quarterly and annual meetings and approve action plans to eliminate systemic deficiencies, using a new standard form (Annex No. 5 to the regulation).
- Board decisions and analytical reports on complaints are forwarded to the bank's internal audit service, which uses them to plan inspections and monitoring.
- The bank must publish quarterly reports on its official website no later than the 20th day of the month following the reporting quarter, and annual reports no later than February 15.
- The bank must include information on complaint handling in its annual report, approved at the general shareholders' meeting.
- The bank must send copies of board decisions on complaints to the CBU within three business days of their approval, and information on the elimination of each systemic deficiency within five business days of its resolution.

The resolution separately defines five situations in which measures to eliminate a systemic deficiency are considered not to have been taken. These include failure to approve an action plan, missing the deadlines it sets, the deficiency persisting despite measures taken, and the absence of an assessment by the internal audit service or the board's failure to act on audit findings within a month.

## Context

This is already the second amendment to the regulation on banks' work with consumers this year. The previous change — [resolution reg. No. 3030-11 of October 28, 2025](https://cbu.uz/ru/press_center/news/2964671/) — introduced a mandatory risk checklist for loan issuance, requirements for restructuring the debt of customers in financial difficulty, and a ban on a number of unfair bank practices. The rule took effect on January 29, 2026.

The regulation has been regularly revised since its approval in 2018 — over the past year alone, it was amended in October 2025 and July 2026.
