The Central Bank of Uzbekistan said $9.3 billion in international remittances from individuals flowed into the country in January-June 2026, up 13% from the first half of 2025.

$1.3 billion left the country over the same period, up 8% year-on-year. The positive balance of cross-border transfers reached $8 billion, according to [a review](https://cbu.uz/uz/press_center/reviews/4227079/) of the domestic foreign exchange market.

A separate [Central Bank review of individuals' foreign exchange transactions](https://cbu.uz/uz/press_center/releases/4359622/), published August 14, recorded a sharp increase in remittances from several countries. Transfers from the United Kingdom rose 62%, from European Union countries 27%, and from the United States 19%. The bank linked this to the ongoing diversification of labor migration destinations.

More than half of inflows — $4.8 billion, or 51.7% — came through direct bank card transfers (P2P), which grew 32% in volume. Traditional money transfer systems processed $4.3 billion (46.7% of total inflows), and SWIFT transfers accounted for $142 million (1.6%).

## Remittances' Share of the Economy

Remittances remain one of Uzbekistan's main sources of foreign currency. In 2025, $18.9 billion in international remittances flowed into the country — up 28% from 2024, the [Central Bank reported earlier in a review of international migration](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/5b2/5d64ar05r47x0pk5egxjwouhlcj8ge9v/1.-Sharh-migratsiya-UZB.pdf). By LEAP News' calculations, this equals about 13% of the country's GDP. The National Statistics Committee estimated GDP at 1,849.7 trillion soums (≈$147 billion at the average 2025 exchange rate) — up 7.7% for the year.

Russia remains the main source of remittances, though its share is declining. In the first quarter of 2026, it fell to 72.4% from 77.6% a year earlier, the Central Bank noted in a labor market review. The shares of Kazakhstan and South Korea each rose to 4.1%, while the share of European countries rose to 3.3%.
