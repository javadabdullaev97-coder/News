The death toll from the mass border crossing from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta [passed 80](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/spanish-pm-sanchez-faces-criticism-as-eu-meets-over-ceuta-crossings) by August 4, according to Spanish authorities, while an independent count by the organization Caminando Fronteras was higher.

LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-05/ceuta-migrant-crisis-death-toll-75-eu-schengen) on the Ceuta crisis when Spanish authorities put the death toll at 75 on August 4. At the time, the EU Council declined to back a suspension of Spain's participation in Schengen proposed by Italy and Denmark, and at least three countries did not sign a letter criticizing Madrid. Since then the death toll has risen further, and the list of countries that declined to sign the letter has grown to four.

## Independent Count Higher Than Official Figure

Spanish authorities [gave](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/spanish-pm-sanchez-faces-criticism-as-eu-meets-over-ceuta-crossings) an updated figure at a meeting of EU interior ministers on August 4: the death toll from attempts to cross the border passed 80. People drowned at sea or sustained fatal injuries trying to climb over the breakwater and the border fence.

The nongovernmental organization Caminando Fronteras, which has documented migrant deaths on the Spanish-Moroccan border for years, cited a higher figure in a [report](https://caminandofronteras.org/dolor-en-la-frontera-de-ceuta/) dated August 3 — 84 confirmed deaths as of August 1: 67 bodies were found in Ceuta and 17 in the Moroccan town of Fnideq, on the other side of the border. The organization notes that dozens of people remain missing and that some bodies remain unidentified — most of the dead were young people.

The discrepancy between the figures from Madrid and independent observers remains unresolved: counting methods differ, and some bodies are found after authorities stop reporting on them officially.

## The Split in the EU Deepens

On August 1, Ireland, France, Portugal and Luxembourg [were asked to sign](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8862187) a letter to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen demanding an immediate response from Spain to the migration crisis. This is the same letter LEAP News previously reported on, when at least three countries — France, Portugal and Luxembourg — were known to have declined. According to Politico, cited by Kommersant, the document was ultimately signed by 22 of the European Union's 27 member states, with Ireland joining the group of four that declined. All of them argued that the letter failed to demonstrate solidarity with Spain and only escalated the situation.

Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni [delivered](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/spanish-pm-sanchez-faces-criticism-as-eu-meets-over-ceuta-crossings) some of the sharpest criticism directed at Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez. Since July 30, Italy [has imposed](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/italy-suspends-free-movement-with-spain-is-schengen-in-crisis-after-ceuta) selective document checks on passengers arriving from Spain by air and sea — a measure that remains in force. Sánchez called Italy's position contrary to EU law, humanitarian law and the principles of solidarity.

## Most Migrants Already Back in Morocco

Of the 72,000 people who illegally entered Ceuta since July 30, about 70,000 have already [returned](http://ru.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/04/interior-ministry-of-spain-70-thousands-migrants-returned-home) to Morocco, Spain's Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska said. According to him, about 2,000 people [remain](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8862402) in Ceuta. The head of Ceuta's administration cites a higher figure — 3,000 to 5,000 [people](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/italy-suspends-free-movement-with-spain-is-schengen-in-crisis-after-ceuta), saying only that "the vast majority" have already left the enclave.

Following the video conference of interior ministers, EU member states [pledged](http://ru.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/04/interior-ministry-of-spain-70-thousands-migrants-returned-home) to strengthen the early-warning system for similar migrant surges.

## A Longstanding Dispute Over the Enclaves

Ceuta and Melilla remain at the center of a long-running sovereignty dispute between Spain and Morocco: Rabat considers the enclaves occupied territory, while Madrid points to their status as Spanish cities dating back to the 15th–16th centuries. The current crisis has [been linked](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/ceuta-and-melilla-why-europes-african-border-remains-a-flashpoint), among other things, to a ruling by Spain's Supreme Court that changed the procedure for so-called "hot returns" of migrants.

## What's Next

Spanish authorities do not rule out that the final death toll may still change: the search for possible victims at sea and along the coast continues, and the figures from Madrid, Rabat and Caminando Fronteras still diverge.
