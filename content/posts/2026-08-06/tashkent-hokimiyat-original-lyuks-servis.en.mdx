The Tashkent city hokimiyat on August 5 responded to appeals from citizens who bought apartments from developer ORIGINAL LYUKS SERVIS LLC in Sergeli district, where construction is estimated to be 53% complete.

## What the Internal Inspection Found

On the instruction of the khokim of Tashkent, an [internal inspection](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51273) was conducted of the documents and construction progress of the residential complex at Sergeli district, Kushkurgan mahalla, 46a Bishkent Street. The inspection found that the 13,051-square-meter land plot was leased to the developer in 2021. The construction permit was obtained in October 2022. The opinion of the working body of the Architecture and Urban Planning Council was issued on September 22, 2023, under number 97277837. The working design documentation was registered with the territorial construction oversight authorities on July 29, 2024, under number 138348266.

The hokimiyat said that, based on the review, construction work at the site is estimated to be 53% complete.

## The Hokimiyat's Legal Position

The Tashkent city hokimiyat [said](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51273) it is not a party to the contracts concluded between citizens and the developer. The hokimiyat classified questions of meeting construction and apartment-handover deadlines, refunds, compensation for possible losses and fulfillment of other obligations as civil-law relations. Such disputes are resolved under the procedure established by law, including in court.

At the same time, the hokimiyat said it understands the concerns of citizens who invested money in buying housing. All appeals and substantiated demands from residents are a priority in decision-making.

## What Happens Next

A representative of the hokimiyat held a meeting with the homebuyers, the construction company's management, representatives of the prosecutor's office, state construction oversight bodies and other relevant agencies. At the meeting, residents raised questions about the deadlines for completing construction and handing over apartments, sources of further financing, the company's credit and collateral obligations, and existing restrictions.

Following the meeting, the developer pledged to resolve the issues raised by residents in the near future. The company also pledged to provide a schedule for fulfilling its obligations — completing construction stages, processing documents and handing over apartments. The schedule will be posted at the site in a location accessible to residents.

The hokimiyat announced a follow-up meeting involving homebuyers, the developer, a bank and law enforcement agencies. The meeting will present the schedule, information on financing sources and measures to fulfill obligations to residents. The hokimiyat did not name a date for the next meeting. According to the hokimiyat, the situation is under the personal oversight of the khokim of Tashkent.

The [statement](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51273) makes no mention of a criminal case or specific individuals at fault — the hokimiyat describes the situation as a civil-law dispute between buyers and the developer.

## The Hokimiyat's Warning to Future Buyers

The hokimiyat separately noted that a developer having permit and design documentation does not by itself mean it will fulfill its contractual obligations to citizens. Before signing a deal, the hokimiyat recommended studying the terms and deadlines for fulfilling obligations, as well as the developer's financial standing and business reputation. It also specifically recommended taking into account the developer's history of previous projects and its ability to meet the obligations it undertakes.
