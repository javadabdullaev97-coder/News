VK filed a lawsuit against Apple Inc, Apple Distribution International Ltd and Apple Rus LLC in the Moscow Arbitration Court on August 19. The suit concerns the removal of VK's apps from the App Store.

The arbitration court hears disputes between companies and entrepreneurs, not criminal cases. The case is before a court of first instance, according to [RBC](https://news.mail.ru/society/72068111/) and [Komsomolskaya Pravda](https://news.mail.ru/society/72068056/), which cite the arbitration case database; LEAP News could not confirm the case number directly. LEAP News was unable to open the case file directly — the [kad.arbitr.ru](https://kad.arbitr.ru/) website blocked access; there is no official confirmation from the court as of publication.

According to VK's press service, the company is demanding that its apps be returned to the App Store, that push notifications be restored for users, and that Apple pay a penalty if it fails to comply with the court's ruling. "Apple removed VK's apps from the App Store unilaterally and without warning," the press service said in a statement cited by [RBC](https://news.mail.ru/society/72068111/) and Komsomolskaya Pravda.

## What led to the lawsuit

Apple removed more than a dozen VK apps from the Russian App Store on June 25, [The Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/06/25/vk-accuses-apple-of-removing-apps-from-app-store-a93094) reported. They included VKontakte, VK Music, VK Messenger, VK Video, Odnoklassniki, Dzen and Skillbox.

Earlier in June, Apple had already removed the messenger app MAX from the store, citing the need to comply with sanctions requirements but without naming a specific sanction, the outlet wrote. Apple did not respond to the outlet's request for comment on the reasons for removing VK's other apps.

VK says the company has never been under sanctions and has never appeared on any sanctions list, The Moscow Times reported. Russian presidential press secretary Dmitry Peskov said the government expects an explanation from Apple and advised Russians to switch to other operating systems.

Russia's Ministry of Digital Development said the removal had no technical or legal grounds and called Apple's actions politically motivated, according to [Komsomolskaya Pravda](https://news.mail.ru/society/72068056/).

As of publication, there have been no appeals and no official response from Apple on the merits of the lawsuit.
