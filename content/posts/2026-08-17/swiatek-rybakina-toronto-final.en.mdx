Polish tennis player Iga Swiatek beat Kazakhstan's Elena Rybakina 6-2, 6-3 in the final of the WTA 1000 Canadian Open in Toronto on August 13.

The win [ended](https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/aug/14/iga-swiatek-takes-swing-at-critics-after-ending-title-drought-in-cincinnati) Swiatek's title drought, which had lasted about 11 months. The trophy was her first of the 2026 season.

In the semifinal, Swiatek [beat](https://www.ubitennis.net/2026/08/iga-swiatek-battles-into-first-final-of-2026-faces-no-1-chasing-rybakina-in-toronto/) Ukraine's Elina Svitolina 6-3, 1-6, 6-3, rallying back after a lopsided second set. Rybakina reached the final by beating American Coco Gauff 5-7, 6-2, 6-2.

Ahead of the final, organizers noted that a win would put Rybakina within four ranking points of world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka, UbiTennis [reported](https://www.ubitennis.net/2026/08/iga-swiatek-battles-into-first-final-of-2026-faces-no-1-chasing-rybakina-in-toronto/). The final loss left the WTA rankings unchanged.

"I want to dedicate this title to everyone who faces unfair criticism and hatred. Keep fighting, keep growing, focus on yourselves — you know best what's good for you," Swiatek [said](https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2026/aug/14/iga-swiatek-takes-swing-at-critics-after-ending-title-drought-in-cincinnati) at the award ceremony.

According to the Guardian, her comments were a response to criticism directed at her — mostly from Polish media and fans amid an uneven season. Before Toronto, Swiatek had not gone past the quarterfinals at Grand Slam tournaments and had changed coaches.

Coach Francisco Roig, who previously worked with Rafael Nadal, helped her prepare for the final, UbiTennis [reported](https://www.ubitennis.net/2026/08/iga-swiatek-battles-into-first-final-of-2026-faces-no-1-chasing-rybakina-in-toronto/) ahead of the match.
