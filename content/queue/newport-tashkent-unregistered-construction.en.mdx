The Inspectorate for Construction and Housing and Utilities Oversight under the Cabinet of Ministers said August 5 that six of nine blocks of the Newport residential complex in Tashkent were built without registration with the agency.

## What the inspection found

Mega Logistics Service LLC is building the Newport complex in Tashkent's Yakkasaray district. Of the project's nine blocks, the architectural and urban planning council approved three — the developer holds a positive opinion from the council's working body and positive expert reviews for these. Construction and installation work on the remaining six blocks proceeded without registration under the procedure established by the inspectorate — this was revealed during oversight measures, [the agency said](https://t.me/OZR_QSN/18261) on its official Telegram channel.

According to the inspectorate, the developer was issued mandatory written orders several times. The developer did not comply. The agency held the customer's officials administratively liable — the fine amount was not disclosed in the publication. The inspectorate referred the collected materials to law enforcement agencies "for an appropriate legal assessment," without specifying which article the responsible individuals could be charged under.

The inspectorate published the clarification in response to social media posts about insufficient oversight of the Mega Logistics Service construction site by regulatory agencies, including the inspectorate itself. That is how the post frames the reason for the publication.

## Prosecutor's office opens criminal case over demolition of building on the same site

Even before the inspectorate's statement, another dispute was unfolding around the Newport site. [The Tashkent City Prosecutor's Office said](https://t.me/toshkentprokuraturasi/132693) August 2 that it had opened a criminal case against officials of "Mega Logistics Service" and other individuals. According to the prosecutor's office, the company demolished residential building No. 19 on A. Kahhar Street in the Damarik mahalla of Yakkasaray district. Relocation of the previous residents had not yet been completed at that point, and they had not been paid compensation in full.

In the same statement, the prosecutor's office said the developer had built five apartment buildings on the site without permitting documentation — a 20-story block, two 19-story blocks, a 12-story block and a 2-story block. This figure does not match the inspectorate's count of six blocks without registration — the sources do not explain the discrepancy.

## What remains unknown

Neither the inspectorate nor the prosecutor's office named an exact fine amount for the developer or the article of the Criminal Code under which responsible individuals could be charged, in their publications. The inspectorate and the prosecutor's office also did not say by when Mega Logistics Service must legalize the remaining blocks of the residential complex.
