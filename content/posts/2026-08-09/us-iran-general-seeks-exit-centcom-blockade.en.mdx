Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff General Dan Caine is seeking an "off-ramp" from the war with Iran, fearing escalation, CNN reported August 7, citing White House sources.

## General Caine: 'Air Superiority Has Its Limits'

Caine discussed the risks of escalation with Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) Director John Ratcliffe, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance, [CNN reports](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/07/politics/general-dan-caine-off-ramp-iran-war). According to the network, the general believes US air superiority may not deliver the political goals the White House has set: further strikes could damage Tehran but would not create conditions for capitulation or a durable agreement — and risk provoking an Iranian response that would widen the conflict.

At a White House meeting in late July, Caine raised the issue of depleted US ammunition stockpiles — a subject the Trump administration publicly denies. According to CNN, the general believes there is almost nothing left worth hitting: "There's essentially not much left to hit — it's whack-a-mole with drone and missile sites." At the same time, according to sources, Caine assured Trump that the military could carry out a larger operation if needed: "We can definitely defeat them."

Caine's spokesman Joseph Holstead declined to comment on the substance of the private conversations, saying the chairman's office "does not comment on anonymous, agenda-driven characterizations" of discussions with senior leadership, CNN reports.

## NYT: Strikes Have Not Halted Iran's Nuclear Program

More than five months after the war began, US and Israeli strikes have slowed but not halted Iran's nuclear program, [The New York Times](https://gvwire.com/2026/08/07/trump-vowed-to-thwart-irans-nuclear-program-heres-where-that-stands/) reports (article by Lara Jakes, August 7). According to an International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) estimate from February 2026, Iran retained about 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60% — enough to quickly produce 9-10 nuclear weapons if enrichment were pushed to weapons grade.

Strikes damaged facilities in Isfahan, Natanz and Fordow but did not destroy them completely. The exact location of the enriched uranium stockpile is unknown: Iran has denied UN inspectors access to its most sensitive nuclear sites, the NYT writes.

Arms Control Association Executive Director Daryl Kimball told the newspaper that the "maximum pressure" policy and "military adventurism have not destroyed and cannot destroy" Iran's nuclear capability. Physicist James Acton of the Carnegie Endowment estimated that, absent further constraints, Iran could build a bomb "in less than a year, possibly much faster."

## Vance: 'We're Still in the Middle of the Game'

US Vice President JD Vance said in a Fox News interview aired August 8 that talks with Iran had "advanced" over the past week but that the conflict was far from over: "I want to remind people that we're still in the middle of the game," [Vance said](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-08/vance-says-us-in-middle-of-the-game-in-iran-conflict), according to Bloomberg. He said Washington is simultaneously using "diplomatic, economic and military tools" to secure the best outcome for Americans and that he is "pretty confident" the goal will be achieved.

Vance said the US expects oil and gas exports from the Persian Gulf to return to pre-war levels, in line with Iran's assurances. Washington intends to verify those assurances independently: "We don't trust — we verify," he said.

## CENTCOM: 51 Vessels Redirected

As of August 7, US Central Command (CENTCOM) forces had redirected 51 merchant vessels, disabled two and boarded two more as part of the naval blockade of Iran in effect since July 14, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108169), citing CENTCOM. That is 6 more than the 45 redirected vessels CENTCOM reported as of August 4, figures [LEAP News](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/us-iran-war-missile-depletion-tanker-attack-hormuz) cited on August 5. There is still no independent confirmation of these numbers.

## Iran Sets Six Conditions on Hormuz, Warns Neighbors

Secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr set out six conditions on August 8 for a settlement over the Strait of Hormuz, [Euronews's Russian edition reports](https://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/08/iran-ne-otkryvaet-ormuz-tegeran-vydvinul-ssha-shest-uslovij): an end to threats and insults against Iran's religious shrines; an end to military action against Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq; lifting the naval blockade and withdrawing US forces from the region; compensation for damage from "two aggressive wars"; lifting sanctions; and unfreezing and returning blocked Iranian assets.

Separately, the conflict over the strait has been compounded by a defense pact signed in Mecca on August 7 between Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye — the parties agreed to treat an attack on any of the three countries as an attack on all of them, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/saudi-arabia-pakistan-and-turkiye-sign-defence-deal-amid-regional-turmoil). Iranian lawmaker Ebrahim Rezaei responded that "a paper agreement with Turkiye and Pakistan will not bring the Saudis security" and called on Riyadh to "reconsider its policy," according to Al Jazeera.

## What's Next

Neither the White House nor the Pentagon has given a timeline for a final decision on Iran — the Trump administration publicly denies both the ammunition shortage and internal disagreements over further escalation. Iran's Supreme National Security Council has not said whether it is prepared to soften any of its six conditions in exchange for an interim deal with Oman over the strait.
