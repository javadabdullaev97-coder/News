Kyrgyz President Sadyr Japarov said on August 13 in an [interview with journalist Ali Toktakunov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m8xcakFR0Ak) that he had earlier planned to hand the presidency to Kamchybek Tashiev, but changed his mind and confirmed he will run in the 2027 election.

## The Succession Plan That Never Happened

"I thought that after my six-year presidential term ends in 2027, if Kamchy is ready, I won't run for president again," [Japarov said](https://gazeta.kg/exclusive/213546-tashiev-mog-stat-prezidentom-v-2027-godu-zhaparov-raskryl-svoj-plan.html) in the interview. He said that in that scenario, Tashiev could have become president while he himself would have left politics.

Japarov has held the presidency since January 2021, and his current six-year term expires in 2027.

Until February 2026, Tashiev held the posts of deputy chairman of the Cabinet of Ministers and chairman of the State Committee for National Security (SCNS). Japarov dismissed him on [February 10, 2026](https://timesca.com/japarov-says-tashiyev-was-his-planned-successor-as-prosecutors-seek-nine-year-terms/).

## Why Japarov Changed His Mind

In the interview, Japarov attributed the decision to problems within Tashiev's circle and questions over the valuation of assets seized under a campaign to recover state property from former officials and businesspeople — known in Kyrgyzstan as "kusturization." The president said some of the seized property had been valued above its real worth, creating reputational risks for the campaign, he [told K-News](https://knews.kg/2026/08/14/sadyr-zhaparov-rasskazal-o-razgovorah-s-kamchybekom-tashievym-posle-ego-otstavki/).

Japarov's remarks in the interview made no mention of the criminal case against Tashiev. In early February 2026, shortly before his dismissal, an open letter signed by 75 people calling for early presidential elections was published. Japarov characterized the letter as an attempt to seize power, after which the case was sent to court. In early July 2026, a court of first instance [found Tashiev guilty](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/former-kyrgyz-security-chief-sentenced-for-trying-to-violently-seize-power) in the case along with seven other defendants, including former Prosecutor General Kurmankul Zulushev and former parliament speaker Nurlanbek Turgunbek uulu, sentencing him to a suspended four-year prison term with a three-year probation period.

Tashiev's defense has appealed the verdict, while prosecutors are [seeking a tougher sentence](https://timesca.com/japarov-says-tashiyev-was-his-planned-successor-as-prosecutors-seek-nine-year-terms/) of nine years in prison on appeal. The verdict has not taken effect — the appellate court has not yet issued a ruling.

## A Second Term and the January 27, 2027 Election

Commenting on his own participation in the 2027 campaign, Japarov said the path to the election is open to anyone who wants to run and that the vote will be fair. According to [Gazeta.kg](https://gazeta.kg/exclusive/213615-prezident-rasskazal-pojdet-li-on-na-vybory-v-2027-godu.html), in the same interview he confirmed his intention to run for a new term.

Kyrgyzstan's next presidential election [is scheduled for January 27, 2027](https://24.kg/vlast/383453_sleduyuschie_vyiboryi_prezidenta_proydut_27yanvarya_2027_goda/) — the fourth Wednesday of January, as set by new election legislation.

According to Japarov, his personal relationship with Tashiev remains friendly, but Tashiev will not return to a government post.
