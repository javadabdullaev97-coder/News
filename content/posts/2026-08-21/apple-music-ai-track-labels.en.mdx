Apple Music plans to make AI-track labeling mandatory by the end of 2026, according to Variety — [Engadget reported](https://www.engadget.com/2241260/apple-music-will-reportedly-label-any-ai-made-tracks-later-this-year/), citing the outlet. As Engadget notes, the expansion of the [Transparency Tags system](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/apple-music-to-label-ai-made-tracks-1236839371/) is tied to a flood of low-quality AI content on streaming platforms — the industry calls such tracks "slop."

This concerns the expansion of a system Apple Music launched in March 2026. Labeling is currently voluntary: labels and distributors decide for themselves whether to flag the use of artificial intelligence in a release's metadata. The system covers four categories — cover art, audio track, composition and video clip — but applies only at the copyright holder's discretion, as [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/04/apple-music-to-add-transparency-tags-to-distinguish-ai-music-says-report/) at launch.

According to Variety, the new "Made With AI" label will become mandatory for tracks where AI generated a "significant portion" of the content. Apple has not yet disclosed the exact threshold for the share of AI content or the launch date for mandatory labeling — it is only known that this could happen later in 2026.

Apple did not respond to Engadget's request for comment, and the company has not officially confirmed any of these plans.
