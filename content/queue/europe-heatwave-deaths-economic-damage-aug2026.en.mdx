Italy on August 6 declared a "red" danger level for the first time in all 27 cities of its heat-monitoring system, a day after Austria recorded an absolute national temperature record of 41.2°C.

## 'Red' Alert Across All Major Italian Cities

All 27 cities in Italy's heat-health monitoring network — from Trieste in the northeast to Palermo in Sicily — simultaneously received the maximum "red" warning level for the first time in the system's history. [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/europe-heat-wave-red-alert-italy-austria-record/). The "red" level signals health risks not only for elderly and chronically ill people but also for healthy, active adults.

## Record Heat in Austria, Wildfires in France

In the village of Bad Deutsch-Altenburg near the border with Slovakia, temperatures reached 41.2°C on August 5 — the [highest ever recorded in Austria](http://ru.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/06/zhara-v-evrope-krasnyj-uroven) by the GeoSphere Austria weather service.

In southeastern France's Var department, [the region's largest wildfire in decades](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/europe-heat-wave-red-alert-italy-austria-record/) has been burning since July 20, scorching more than 23 square miles (about 60 km²). The fire's spread has been stopped, but according to CBS News it retains the ability to reignite. By the end of July, wildfires across Europe as a whole had [destroyed more than 430,000 hectares of forest](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864090), according to Kommersant. Financial Times estimates the damage from this season's fires at €3.1 billion.

## Energy and Transport Hit by Drought

Drought and record-low water levels on the Danube have [disrupted operations at Hungary's only nuclear power plant](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/europe-heat-wave-red-alert-italy-austria-record/), which cools its reactors with river water. The operator has cut output to about 10% of normal levels, even though the plant typically supplies roughly half the country's electricity. Authorities have introduced conservation measures for industrial consumers and households during peak-demand hours. Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar said the coming days will determine whether a full shutdown of the plant can be avoided.

In Germany, drought is lowering water levels on the Rhine and other rivers, complicating river shipping and disrupting industries that depend on it.

## Official Heat Mortality Data in Germany

According to a weekly report from the [Robert Koch Institute (RKI)](https://www.rki.de/DE/Themen/Gesundheit-und-Gesellschaft/Gesundheitliche-Einflussfaktoren-A-Z/H/Hitze/Bericht_Hitzemortalitaet.html), published on August 6, heat-related deaths in Germany from mid-April to July 26 are estimated at 11,900. The 95% confidence interval ranges from 10,700 to 13,100. That exceeds the previous record years of 2018 and 2019, when heat-related deaths, according to RKI, also totaled around 7,000 per year. RKI attributes about 9,600 of these deaths to the extremely hot 26th calendar week — the end of June, when temperatures in some regions exceeded 40°C. People over 85 were hit hardest.

RKI emphasizes that the figure is a statistical estimate of excess mortality, not a direct count of deaths officially attributed to heat.

## EU Heat Damage Estimated at €800 Billion

Analysts at Allianz, cited by the EUobserver portal, estimated the potential damage to the EU economy from heat at €800 billion by 2030, [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864090) and [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/economics/news/2026/08/06/1219334-anomalnaya-zhara) report. The estimate is based on a study of heat waves in more than 40 countries from 2014 to 2024. Analysts project the largest losses for France — about €211 billion — followed by Italy, Germany, and Spain.

According to Vedomosti, wildfires in Europe have forced more than 300,000 people to leave their homes since the start of the season. Kommersant describes June 2026 as the hottest month on record, with an average temperature of 20.74°C.
