A grid failure in a neighboring country on August 14 disrupted the operation of Central Asia's unified power system — Uzbekistan's Surkhandarya region, parts of Almaty, Bishkek and cities in Tajikistan were left without power for several hours.

## What happened in Uzbekistan

The Ministry of Energy of Uzbekistan [reported](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20737) the accident. According to the ministry, a grid failure in a neighboring country affected Central Asia's unified power system. This caused power outages in Surkhandarya region and other regions of the republic. The ministry did not specify in which country's power grid the failure occurred.

By midday, power engineers were gradually restoring electricity supply. According to [the Ministry of Energy](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20738), power supply in Surkhandarya region was restored to 80%, with work continuing in the remaining districts.

According to the [ministry's latest update at the time of publication](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20739), the grid failure in the neighboring country occurred at 14:38 Tashkent time. By the evening of August 14, power supply was fully restored in all affected regions of Uzbekistan except Surkhandarya region, where full restoration was promised soon.

## Power went out in four countries almost simultaneously

Besides Uzbekistan, the outages affected southern regions of Kazakhstan, part of Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan.

In Kazakhstan, according to [data](https://www.inform.kz/ru/minenergo-obyasnilo-pereboi-selektrichestvom-valmati-idrugih-regionah-kazahstana-7bac51d7) from Kazakhstan's Ministry of Energy, a power surge occurred on August 14 due to a sharp change in electricity flow from Central Asia's power system. Emergency automatic protection was triggered, leaving four districts of Almaty and ten districts of Almaty region without power. Restoration work was carried out by KEGOC JSC.

According to [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1109344), in Kyrgyzstan the outages affected Bishkek and several other regions, while in Tajikistan they hit a number of cities. Power supply in both countries also began to be restored in stages.

## What is known about the cause

The Ministry of Energy of Uzbekistan attributed the cause to an accident "in the power grid of a neighboring country," without specifying which one. According to [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1109344), some regional media linked the failure to Tajikistan's power grid — the Tajik side later denied reports that the accident occurred at the Norak hydropower plant.

[Kazakhstan's Ministry of Energy](https://www.inform.kz/ru/minenergo-obyasnilo-pereboi-selektrichestvom-valmati-idrugih-regionah-kazahstana-7bac51d7) offered its own version of the cause: the ministry also does not consider the accident to be strictly Kazakhstani, explaining the failure as a sharp change in electricity flow from Central Asia's power system. As of the evening of August 14, no final official conclusion had been published on which country the primary fault originated in.

## What's next

The Ministry of Energy of Uzbekistan promised to provide additional information as restoration work in Surkhandarya region is completed.
