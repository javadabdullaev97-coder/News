Turkey, Qatar and Egypt issued a joint statement on August 3, accusing Israel of violating the Gaza ceasefire agreement and creating a risk of derailing the second phase of the peace plan.

## What the joint statement says

The three countries mediating the Gaza talks [said](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/joint-statement-issued-by-the-mediators--the-republic-of-turkiye--the-arab-republic-of-egypt-and-the-state-of-qatar-on-the-ongoing-israeli-violations-in-gaza.en.mfa) they strongly condemn Israel's continued violations — above all, strikes on healthcare facilities and medical infrastructure. These strikes have caused civilian casualties, including women and children. The document describes such actions as "a flagrant violation of international law and international humanitarian law."

Turkey, Qatar and Egypt stressed that continued violations undermine efforts to implement the second phase of the agreement. Hamas and other Palestinian factions have already announced their agreement to the "roadmap," including the provision on disarmament. The mediating countries called on Israel to fulfill its obligations under international law and the terms of the ceasefire, ensure the protection of civilians, medical workers and healthcare facilities, and allow unimpeded delivery of humanitarian and medical aid to the strip. In their assessment, the international community must exert the necessary pressure on Israel to see through US President Donald Trump's peace plan.

## Strikes on medical infrastructure

One of the episodes preceding the statement was the August 1 strike on the medical supply warehouses of Al-Aqsa Hospital in Deir al-Balah. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/israeli-strikes-kill-four-across-gaza-destroy-medical-supply-warehouse), at least seven people were killed in that strike. Of the four warehouses storing medicine, two were completely destroyed and two more damaged — intravenous solutions, medications, and dialysis and resuscitation equipment were destroyed. Gaza Ministry of Health Director-General Munir al-Bursh said that destroying medicine and blocking its delivery "turns medicine and illness itself into weapons of war."

On the evening of August 2, an Israeli drone strike on a vehicle near the Sharia court building in the Remal neighborhood of Gaza City killed four more people, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/palestine-weekly-israel-kills-dozens-in-gaza-after-hamas-disarmament-deal).

## Scale of casualties since the ceasefire

According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/palestine-weekly-israel-kills-dozens-in-gaza-after-hamas-disarmament-deal), citing Gaza authorities, at least 19 people were killed in the strip on August 2 alone, bringing the death toll over the August 1-2 weekend to 26. July 2026 was the deadliest month since the start of the year, with 152 people killed. Since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025, the death toll in Gaza has exceeded 1,250, with more than 4,100 wounded (as of early August 2026).

## What the second phase of the plan means

The second phase envisions the withdrawal of Israeli troops beyond the so-called yellow line only after Hamas completes disarmament, the Board of Peace [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms). It is a body established in January 2026 at the initiative of US President Donald Trump to govern the strip after the war. Disarmament is to cover light and heavy weapons, as well as tunnels. Hamas said it is ready to fulfill this part of the agreement provided Israel meets its own obligations.

Israeli authorities had not officially responded to the joint statement by Turkey, Qatar and Egypt as of publication. Earlier, the office of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms) that the published text of the roadmap for the second phase of the plan "does not reflect Israel's position." Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called the document "completely different" from what the government had approved and demanded the cabinet revise it. These comments concerned the text of the agreement itself, not the strike on the hospital or the statement by the three mediating countries.
