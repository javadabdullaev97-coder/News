Uzbekistan's Business Ombudsman this week asked the Constitutional Court to review an article of the Code on Administrative Liability (KoAO) that bars review of Supreme Court rulings issued in revision proceedings even when new evidence of innocence emerges.

## What Happened to the Entrepreneur

The Office of the Ombudsman for Protection of the Rights and Legal Interests of Business Entities under the President of the Republic of Uzbekistan [said](https://t.me/biznes_ombudsman/12824) it had reviewed a complaint from an individual entrepreneur identified as Sh.R. According to the office, a fire broke out on May 28, 2025, at a furniture-manufacturing facility he owned. Authorities declined to open a criminal case over the incident. However, the entrepreneur was held liable under Part 3 of Article 211 of the Code on Administrative Liability for violating fire-safety rules and fined [750,000 soums](https://www.facebook.com/biznes.ombudsman/posts/pfbid0B6k5BBasyzN96mXCkLnj52vxAS2Uy4voodCoNAyUmZuVrJu5o8gR82RgU5HLDQkAl). That is about $63 at the exchange rate of the Central Bank of Uzbekistan on August 7, 2026.

The fine was upheld by the cassation court and later by the Supreme Court's revision (supervisory) instance. Under [Article 324-39 of the KoAO](https://t.me/biznes_ombudsman/12824), a ruling issued in revision proceedings is final and cannot be reviewed by any other court.

## No Court Reviewed the New Evidence

After the revision instance's ruling had already taken effect, a separate inquiry into the same incident was carried out. In June 2026, it produced a forensic examination report and a final investigative decision. They [establish](https://www.facebook.com/biznes.ombudsman/posts/pfbid0B6k5BBasyzN96mXCkLnj52vxAS2Uy4voodCoNAyUmZuVrJu5o8gR82RgU5HLDQkAl) that the fire was caused by an accident in the furniture facility's electrical wiring, not by the entrepreneur's fault or deliberate actions.

Because of the rule making the revision instance's ruling final, no court has reviewed this new evidence of innocence. The ruling imposing the 750,000-soum fine remains in force, according to the office.

## What the Ombudsman Is Challenging

The Business Ombudsman's office has sent a formal request to the Constitutional Court asking it to review Article 324-39 of the KoAO for compliance with three articles of the Constitution:

- Article 28, the presumption of innocence;
- Article 54, which makes ensuring human rights and freedoms the state's highest goal;
- Article 55, the right of everyone to have their rights defended by an impartial court and to have violated rights restored.

The Constitutional Court has not yet announced whether it will accept the request for review or set a hearing date.
