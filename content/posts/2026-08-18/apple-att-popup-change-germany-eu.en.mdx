Germany's Federal Cartel Office (Bundeskartellamt) on August 17 [ordered Apple](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html) to redesign the pop-up windows that ask users for permission to be tracked across apps — the App Tracking Transparency (ATT) system.

The agency had been examining complaints about the ATT design since June 2022. [The regulator found](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html) that the wording and symbols in the pop-ups discouraged users from consenting to tracking by third-party apps, while Apple's own services faced no such pressure.

"It is not our goal to achieve the highest possible consent rate for personalized advertising. We want users to make their decision freely and consciously," Bundeskartellamt President Andreas Mundt said.

Apple pledged to remove from the pop-ups the elements the agency deemed discouraging to users' consent — described in the press release as "potentially discouraging symbols and wording." The design of the request will become neutral in content, wording and appearance — the same for Apple's own apps and third-party ones. Developers will get more room to explain why personalized advertising is needed and will be able to combine the ATT request with their own data-protection notice instead of two separate screens.

There is no fine: the proceedings ended without a formal finding of infringement. Apple itself proposed the commitments, and the agency tested them with a market survey in December 2025 before declaring them binding. France and Italy separately fined Apple in 2025 over similar complaints about ATT — 150 million euros and 98.6 million euros, respectively.

According to [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/980977/apple-app-tracking-transparency-settlement-germany), Apple said it would extend the changes to almost every European Union country, without specifying which ones.

Apple launched ATT in April 2021 alongside [iOS 14.5](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/17/apple-app-tracking-transparency-changes-germany/). Since then, apps have been required to ask permission before tracking users across other apps and websites to show personalized ads. [According to The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/980977/apple-app-tracking-transparency-settlement-germany), social media companies — including Meta and Snap — lost about $10 billion in advertising revenue in the second half of 2021 because of ATT.

## Apple's position

Apple said the current ATT pop-up "gives users a clear, simple and effective way to control their data." Even so, [the company agreed](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/17/apple-app-tracking-transparency-changes-germany/) to change the request's text and design at the agency's demand.

## What's next

Apple has four months from the official delivery of the decision to test the changes with app developers and roll them out. [The commitments](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html) will remain in force for seven years under the oversight of an independent trustee.
