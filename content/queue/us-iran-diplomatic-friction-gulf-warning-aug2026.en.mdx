Iran warned Persian Gulf states through diplomatic channels on August 5-6 that a new US attack on its territory would trigger retaliatory strikes on the region's energy infrastructure, Reuters [reports](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/exclusiveiran-threatens-to-hit-gulf-states-if-us-launches-new-strikes-4839049).

The warning came as Iranian officials publicly rejected statements by US President Donald Trump that Washington and Tehran are close to a comprehensive settlement of the conflict.

## Iran Rejects Trump's Claims of a Deal

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said on August 3 that Iran is not holding direct talks with the US but is discussing de-escalation with Oman and other countries in the region, Al Jazeera [reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/trump-blasts-duplicitous-iran-after-tehran-denies-it-is-in-talks-with-the-us). In response, Trump called Iran's leadership "incredibly duplicitous."

On August 6, Iranian parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Baqer Ghalibaf accused Trump of running "show diplomacy on loop": according to him, the US president cyclically threatens massive strikes and then calls them off, announcing a nonexistent agreement, Al Jazeera [writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/irans-ghalibaf-accuses-trump-of-conducting-show-diplomacy-on-loop). Ghalibaf confirmed that a separate agreement with Oman to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is "on the verge of being finalized" — LEAP News [covered](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/iran-oman-hormuz-deal-agreed-in-principle) that deal separately on August 7.

## Iran Warns Gulf of Response to New US Strikes

At risk, according to Reuters citing five sources, are energy facilities, oil refineries, power grids, water and transport infrastructure, and oil fields in Gulf states.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi held a series of talks with counterparts from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Qatar, as well as with Pakistan's army chief. According to an Iranian source cited by Reuters, the message was consistent: "We are ready for retaliation, but a diplomatic solution is the best way to avoid a wider escalation." Another Iranian official told Reuters: "The enemy continues to threaten Iranian infrastructure. If Iran is harmed, it will inflict far greater harm in response."

The warning came after Trump threatened on July 28 to strike Iran's power grids and infrastructure, then said on August 2 that he had agreed to call off the strike "on condition a deal could be reached quickly," Reuters [notes](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/exclusiveiran-threatens-to-hit-gulf-states-if-us-launches-new-strikes-4839049). Qatar, Oman and Saudi Arabia have publicly called on Washington to resume talks with Tehran and prevent a new escalation.

## Context: Fifth Month of War and US Missile Shortage

The war between the US and Iran has been under way since February 28, 2026. By early August, the US military, according to CNN and Reuters, had expended nearly 80% of its THAAD missile defense interceptors and most of its stock of long-range precision missiles — LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/us-iran-war-missile-depletion-tanker-attack-hormuz) on August 5. Around the same time, the cargo ship Minoan Pioneer was shelled off the coast of Oman, and an adviser to Iran's supreme leader threatened strikes on US ships in the Strait of Hormuz.

## What's Next

Neither the White House nor Iran's Foreign Ministry has given a timeline or format for a possible signing of a broader settlement agreement — unlike the separate deal with Oman on the Strait of Hormuz, which is awaiting approval from Iran's Supreme National Security Council. Iran has not specified what US actions it would consider grounds for striking Gulf states' infrastructure.
