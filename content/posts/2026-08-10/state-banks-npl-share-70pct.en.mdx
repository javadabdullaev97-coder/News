The Central Bank of Uzbekistan (CBU) published data on June 25: banks' non-performing loans reached [23.6 trillion soums](https://cbu.uz/uz/statistics/bankstats/4066771/) as of June 1, 2026, or about $1.97 billion at the CBU exchange rate. Of that, 70.7% was held by banks with state ownership.

## How the bad debt is distributed

According to the CBU, commercial banks' total loan portfolio stood at [635.98 trillion soums](https://cbu.uz/uz/statistics/bankstats/4066771/) as of June 1 (about $52.99 billion). Overdue and non-performing loans made up 3.7% of that portfolio.

Of the total non-performing loans, banks with state ownership account for 16,696 billion soums and private and other banks for 6,914 billion soums. State banks' loan portfolio (420.44 trillion soums) makes up 66.1% of the entire market. That means their share of bad debt (70.7%) exceeds their share of the portfolio itself. Loan quality at state banks is below average: overdue loans make up 4.0% of their portfolio, versus 3.2% at other banks.

## Growth over two months

As of [April 1, 2026](https://cbu.uz/uz/statistics/bankstats/3791161/), non-performing loans in the banking system totaled 19,861 billion soums. Of that, 14,296 billion soums, or 72.0%, belonged to state banks. Over two months, total bad debt grew by 3,748 billion soums (18.9%), and state banks' bad debt grew by 2,400 billion soums (16.8%). The share of non-performing loans in the banking system's total portfolio rose from 3.2% to 3.7% over the same period.
