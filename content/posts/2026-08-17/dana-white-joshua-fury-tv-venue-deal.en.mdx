Zuffa Boxing head Dana White on August 17 for the first time discussed his involvement in negotiations over an Anthony Joshua-Tyson Fury fight, saying he closed a television deal and agreed on a venue, [BoxingScene reports](https://www.boxingscene.com/articles/weary-dana-white-on-anthony-joshua-tyson-fury-it-should-have-happened-10-years-ago).

"We closed the TV deal and the venue deal," White said, commenting on the state of negotiations after the UFC 330 event. The former UFC president called the situation drawn-out: "This fight should have happened 10 years ago. Will it happen now? We'll see."

Joshua's contract requires the fight to take place in the United Kingdom and expressly bars White from taking part in organizing it, [Bloody Elbow reports](https://bloodyelbow.com/2026/08/16/dana-white-bemoans-lack-of-progress-on-anthony-joshua-vs-tyson-fury-super-fight/) — a condition the British fighter's promoter, Eddie Hearn, insists on. Still, according to BoxingScene, White continues to negotiate moving the fight to the United States. Options under discussion include Madison Square Garden in New York and a broadcast on Netflix.

White himself indicated he does not object to a UK venue, saying the only thing that matters is whether the fighters want to get in the ring: "For the fight to happen, both guys have to want it. Period," he said, adding: "Either they want to fight or they don't. I don't know."

Earlier today, LEAP News reported that Hearn and Joshua [are insisting the fight take place in the United Kingdom](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/17/fury-joshua-uk-showdown-talks) — now White says talks on moving the fight to the US are progressing. Negotiations over a Fury-Joshua superfight have dragged on for years: both boxers rank among the most recognizable heavyweights of the past decade. There is still no official confirmation of a date or venue for the fight.
