Google [won](https://ppc.land/google-wins-bankrupt-spirit-airlines-data-for-10-million/) an auction for bankrupt Spirit Airlines' corporate data for $10 million on August 14, but a bankruptcy court in New York [postponed](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/20/googles-attempt-to-buy-spirit-airlines-data-might-come-unstuck/) the hearing to approve the deal to September 9 amid objections.

The data was sold under Section 363 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code, a mechanism that allows a debtor's assets to be sold at a court-supervised public auction. The auction took place in the [bankruptcy case](https://ppc.land/google-wins-bankrupt-spirit-airlines-data-for-10-million/) of Spirit Aviation Holdings (case No. 25-11897) before the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, presided over by Judge Sean Lane. Mercor.io Corp served as the backup bidder at the auction with an offer of $7.5 million. The hearing to approve the deal had been [scheduled](https://ppc.land/google-wins-bankrupt-spirit-airlines-data-for-10-million/) for August 19, but the court postponed it.

The package [includes](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/google-aims-to-boost-ai-with-purchase-of-spirit-airlines-data) about 100 million emails from 80,000 Microsoft 365 corporate accounts and 500 million messages and files from Microsoft Teams. It also includes 30 million lines of code from 516 repositories and 175,658 employee records dating back to 1986. Separately sold were pricing data on 7.2 billion competitor flights and 7.5 billion revenue transactions since 2008. [Excluded](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/google-aims-to-boost-ai-with-purchase-of-spirit-airlines-data) from the deal are personal data, about 97.5 million passenger profiles and 50.2 million Free Spirit loyalty program records. Google [said](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/google-aims-to-boost-ai-with-purchase-of-spirit-airlines-data) it had "acquired a portion of Spirit Airlines' corporate dataset that could be useful for improving our products and artificial intelligence models."

## Union challenges sale of employee data

The Association of Flight Attendants-CWA (AFA-CWA), a union representing former Spirit Airlines employees, filed an objection in court against the deal. Union President Sara Nelson [said](https://finance.biggo.com/news/1dcd410e-ed0b-42a7-a748-253c0750fde9): "This is outrageous. We objected in court to Google's attempt to buy data that should never have been for sale in the first place." In its court filing, the union [said](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/union-challenges-google-spirit-airlines-data-purchase-1815216): "The privacy architecture of this deal is consumer-facing, but its substance is disproportionately employee-facing. As a result, employee data is far more sensitive than customer data, yet receives far less protection."

The union [asked](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/union-challenges-google-spirit-airlines-data-purchase-1815216) the court to either fully exclude flight attendant data from the deal or conduct an independent review of personnel and disciplinary records before any transfer. The union also demands that Google be barred from profiling specific employees using this data and that clear rules be set for any further transfer of the data to third parties. The union also challenges the data anonymization procedure itself, arguing that former employees can still be identified when the data is cross-referenced with other sources. Following AFA-CWA's objections, Judge Sean Lane [agreed](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/20/googles-attempt-to-buy-spirit-airlines-data-might-come-unstuck/) to pause approval of the deal.

According to SiliconANGLE, Micro1 Inc founder Ali Ansari sent Spirit Airlines' legal team a counteroffer to buy the data, which he told Business Insider. Unlike AFA-CWA, Micro1 did not file a formal objection with the court. The company [offered](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/20/googles-attempt-to-buy-spirit-airlines-data-might-come-unstuck/) $12.5 million for the data package — $2.5 million more than Google's bid — saying Google's winning bid was undervalued.

## What's next

The bankruptcy court will consider AFA-CWA's objections and Micro1's competing bid at the September 9 hearing. Google's deal will not take effect before that date.
