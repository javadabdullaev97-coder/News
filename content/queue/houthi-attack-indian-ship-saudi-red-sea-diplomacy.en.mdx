The Indian vessel Faize Noore Oliya sank on August 4 in the Red Sea off the coast of Yemen after being hit by an unidentified projectile; all 14 crew members — 13 Indian nationals and one other crew member — were rescued.

## Attack Off Hodeidah

The vessel was sailing about 13 nautical miles south of the Houthi-controlled port of Hodeidah when it was struck by a projectile and sank, [Reuters reports](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/08/04/projectile-sinks-indian-ship-near-yemeni-waters-but-all-seafarers-safe-indian-minister-says). Yemeni rescuers pulled the crew aboard and brought them to the port of Mokha.

India's Minister of Ports, Shipping and Waterways Sarbananda Sonowal [said](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/08/04/projectile-sinks-indian-ship-near-yemeni-waters-but-all-seafarers-safe-indian-minister-says) that "India strongly condemns this unprovoked attack on a defenseless sailing vessel," and called the crew's safety the top priority.

## Who Is Being Blamed

The Ministry of Transport of Yemen's internationally recognized government, which is backed by Saudi Arabia, blamed the Iran-aligned Houthis for the attack and said it was carried out with an explosive-laden unmanned boat, [Reuters reports](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/08/04/projectile-sinks-indian-ship-near-yemeni-waters-but-all-seafarers-safe-indian-minister-says). The Houthis themselves had not claimed responsibility for the attack or commented on it as of publication.

The incident occurred against the backdrop of a naval blockade the Houthis declared against Saudi Arabia on July 20, accusing its authorities of an "unjust and brutal siege" of Yemen lasting nearly 12 years. The blockade has been accompanied by attacks on ships in the Red Sea, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/29/saudi-arabia-seeks-international-coalition-against-houthis-in-red-sea).

## Saudi Arabia Bets on Diplomacy

Saudi Arabia is trying to contain the escalation of its conflict with the Houthis through diplomatic means and is negotiating with the Yemeni group through Omani mediators, while simultaneously preparing a military option in case the talks collapse, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/saudi-arabia-tries-diplomacy-to-stop-houthi-attacks-escalating). In late July, the kingdom carried out limited airstrikes on Houthi positions for two consecutive days, but has refrained from further strikes since then while pursuing a diplomatic solution.

The United States is discouraging Saudi Arabia through diplomatic channels from a large-scale military response to Houthi attacks on ships off its Red Sea coast — Washington fears that further escalation of the Saudi-Houthi confrontation would further disrupt global shipping and energy markets, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/saudi-arabia-tries-diplomacy-to-stop-houthi-attacks-escalating). Riyadh has held Oman-mediated talks intermittently since the 2022 truce.

## Coalition to Protect Shipping and Blockade-Running

Alongside the negotiations, Saudi Arabia is building an international coalition to protect shipping in the Red Sea: by July 30, representatives of 13 out of 51 invited countries had joined — 43 states sent delegations to the founding meeting, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/30/why-is-saudi-arabia-forming-a-coalition-to-protect-the-red-sea). The list includes Turkey, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Jordan, Egypt, Pakistan, Djibouti, Somalia, Bangladesh, Yemen, Sudan and the Comoros. The UAE and Oman did not join the coalition, and the United States had not formally confirmed its participation as of publication.

Despite the blockade, some vessels continue to pass through the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait: two Pakistan-flagged tankers left the Saudi port of Yanbu and crossed the Red Sea and the strait without incident, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107508), citing data from Windward, a company that tracks cargo flows. This happened a few days after Pakistan's Foreign Ministry warned the Houthis that any attack on ships under the Pakistani flag would be treated as a threat to national security.

## Aramco: July Strikes Did Not Affect Output

Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser said Houthi strikes on the company's facilities in July did not have a significant impact on operational or financial performance: "Certain company facilities were attacked, but the impact was not material, either operationally or financially," Bloomberg [quoted him](https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/ceo_plays_down_impact_of_attacks_on_aramco-04-aug-2026-184285-article/) as saying. Nasser said the company is able to reach its full production capacity of 12 million barrels per day within three weeks if needed. Bloomberg notes that part of Aramco's exports has already been rerouted through the western port of Yanbu — a measure linked to shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, not to the July Houthi attacks on the company's facilities.

## What's Next

Saudi Arabia's Oman-mediated talks with the Houthis continue with no announced deadline for completion. The Houthis have not claimed responsibility for the attack on the Faize Noore Oliya or commented on the Yemeni government's accusations. The conflict in the Red Sea and the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait is unfolding alongside a separate crisis in the Strait of Hormuz — these are two distinct conflicts with different parties involved.
