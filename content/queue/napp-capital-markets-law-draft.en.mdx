The National Agency for Perspective Projects (NAPP) on August 6 [detailed](https://t.me/napp_uz/2352) the draft law "On the Capital Market," which brings regulation in line with international standards and has already passed its first reading in the Legislative Chamber of the Oliy Majlis.

## What the Draft Law Changes

In its publication, NAPP [lists](https://t.me/napp_uz/2352) four main areas of the document: protecting investor rights, introducing new financial instruments, streamlining market infrastructure and expanding international cooperation.

Among the new instruments introduced by the draft law are Islamic bonds (sukuk), sustainability bonds, and covered and securitized bonds, the Legislative Chamber press service [said](https://t.me/qonunchilikpalatasi/36966) following consideration of the document. According to [the president's website](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9339), the draft also provides for derivative financial instruments — options, futures, forwards and swaps. The document expands the powers of the Central Securities Depository and simplifies foreign investors' access to the market.

## Status and Developers

The Legislative Chamber [adopted](https://t.me/qonunchilikpalatasi/36966) the draft law in its first reading on July 28. Lawmakers discussed attracting investors, the quality of corporate governance and requirements for companies' information disclosure. The document, according to [the president's website](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9339), consists of 16 chapters and 123 articles and was drafted by NAPP together with international financial organizations.

NAPP acts as the regulator of Uzbekistan's capital market and also maintains the registry of participants in the special legal regime known as the Regulatory Sandbox in this market.

## Context

On June 22, President Shavkat Mirziyoyev [reviewed](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9339) a presentation of the new version of the capital market law and instructed officials to discuss the document with international partners. The meeting addressed harmonizing regulatory requirements with international standards and creating conditions for the development of Islamic financing, derivatives and mortgage bonds.
