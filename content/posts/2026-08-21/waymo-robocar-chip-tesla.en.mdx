Waymo said on August 20 that it had developed its own chip for self-driving cars — built on a 5-nanometer process with claimed performance exceeding 1,000 TOPS, [according to the company's blog](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/08/look-under-our-trunk/).

TOPS (trillion operations per second) is a unit measuring chip performance for neural-network computing. A 5-nanometer process means a more compact, energy-efficient chip compared with earlier solutions. Waymo calls the new chip an AI accelerator — a specialized chip that processes sensor data and runs neural networks in real time.

According to [the company](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/08/look-under-our-trunk/), the chip simultaneously processes streams from 13 high-resolution cameras as well as lidar and radar, combining them into a single picture of the surroundings. Waymo said it has increased the chip's computing power 20-fold over eight years, letting the system make decisions with minimal latency. The system is built on two independent compute cores for redundancy and is liquid-cooled. There are no independent measurements of these figures — they are the company's own numbers.

Tesla has also been developing its own chips for autonomous driving for years, [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/edge-and-iot/2026/08/20/waymo-has-designed-a-robocar-chip-to-stay-ahead-of-tesla/5290592) notes. At the same time, Waymo continues to list Nvidia among its hardware partners — alongside AMD, Micron, Samsung, TSMC and other companies, [according to the blog](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/08/look-under-our-trunk/).

Waymo says it will reveal more technical details about the chip at the Hot Chips conference in Stanford next week, [according to the company's blog](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/08/look-under-our-trunk/).
