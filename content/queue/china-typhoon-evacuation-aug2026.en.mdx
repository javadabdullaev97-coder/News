Typhoon Dolphin — the most powerful tropical cyclone to hit China this year — made landfall in Zhejiang province on the evening of August 9, forcing authorities to evacuate more than a million people. That is according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/flight-cancellations-evacuations-as-china-braces-for-typhoon-dolphin) and [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/natural-disasters/typhoon-dolphin-strikes-eastern-china-forcing-mass-evacuations).

## Landfall in Zhejiang

The typhoon made landfall near the city of Yuhuan in eastern Zhejiang province at around 17:30 local time (09:30 UTC) on Sunday, August 9 — [Nikkei Asia reported](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/natural-disasters/typhoon-dolphin-strikes-eastern-china-forcing-mass-evacuations), citing Chinese meteorological authorities.

Maximum wind speed near the typhoon's center at landfall reached 151 km/h (42 m/s) — equivalent to a Category 1 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson scale, [Al Jazeera noted](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/flight-cancellations-evacuations-as-china-braces-for-typhoon-dolphin).

## Evacuations and Canceled Flights

According to Al Jazeera and Nikkei Asia, as of the day the typhoon made landfall, authorities had evacuated:

- in Shanghai — at least 30,300 people from risk zones;
- in the city of Wenzhou (Zhejiang) — more than 900,000 residents, with more than 1,000 additional temporary shelters opened;
- in the city of Taizhou (Zhejiang) — about 390,000 people, with the evacuation completed by Saturday evening, August 8;
- in Fujian province — 98,900 people from high-risk areas, after authorities raised the typhoon response level to the third tier — [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/flight-cancellations-evacuations-as-china-braces-for-typhoon-dolphin), citing the state news agency Xinhua.

In total, according to data published as of August 9, more than 1 million people were moved out of the storm zone.

Shanghai's two airports canceled around 1,400–1,500 flights, according to flight tracker VariFlight as of August 9. The figures from [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/flight-cancellations-evacuations-as-china-braces-for-typhoon-dolphin) and [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/natural-disasters/typhoon-dolphin-strikes-eastern-china-forcing-mass-evacuations) differ slightly, as the number rose throughout the day.

## Path Through Japan

Before reaching China, the typhoon passed through southern Japan. Near Kume Island, part of Okinawa prefecture, sustained wind speeds reached 162 km/h (100 mph), with gusts of up to 216 km/h (134 mph) on Saturday, August 8, [Al Jazeera wrote](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/typhoon-dolphin-hits-japan-heads-towards-china). As of August 9, six people were affected and more than 50,000 buildings were left without power, [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/flight-cancellations-evacuations-as-china-braces-for-typhoon-dolphin) and [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/natural-disasters/typhoon-dolphin-strikes-eastern-china-forcing-mass-evacuations) reported. Airlines canceled flights to and from Okinawa, as well as flights to China and Taiwan.

## What's Next

After making landfall, the typhoon is forecast by meteorologists to continue moving westward into China's central and southwestern regions, prolonging the risk of flash floods and landslides in mountainous areas. Central and eastern Zhejiang are expected to see 250 to 500 mm of rainfall by August 10, [Nikkei Asia noted](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/natural-disasters/typhoon-dolphin-strikes-eastern-china-forcing-mass-evacuations).
