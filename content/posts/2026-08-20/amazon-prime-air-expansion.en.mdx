Amazon announced on August 19 that it will expand its Prime Air drone delivery service to nearly 500 US cities and towns by the end of 2026 — six times its current coverage, [the company said](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-prime-air-drone-delivery-expansion).

Prime Air currently operates in cities across seven states — Arizona, Florida, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Nebraska and Texas, [according to the announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-prime-air-drone-delivery-expansion). Per [AVweb](https://avweb.com/aviation-news/amazon-prime-air-drone-delivery-expansion/), that amounts to 11 delivery hubs across 10 metropolitan areas, each covering about 453 km². Amazon plans to launch the service in Georgia, Ohio, Illinois, Idaho and New York, including the suburbs of Chicago and Atlanta, as well as Cleveland, Syracuse and Boise.

"We've already delivered hundreds of thousands of packages by drone this year," Amazon Prime Air Vice President David Carbon [said](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/amazon-prime-air-drone-delivery-expansion). He said the company aims to reach customers in nearly 500 cities and towns by the end of 2026. The service operates under a Part 135 certificate from the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) — the same category used by commercial airlines, the company said.

Amazon's MK30 drone delivers packages weighing up to 2.3 kilograms — more than 60% of the items customers order most often — within 30 to 60 minutes. The aircraft is equipped with an obstacle-detection system that monitors its surroundings and makes decisions in real time, according to AVweb.
