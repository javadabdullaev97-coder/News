High-scoring applicants who did not qualify for any of the five bachelor's degree programs they chose will get the right to pick a sixth, according to information cited by several media outlets; there has been no official confirmation as of publication. The reports concern the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovation of Uzbekistan, which has not yet commented on the information.

According to outlets citing the ministry, applicants who did not qualify for any of their five originally chosen programs will be able to use the sixth option. One outlet, citing the ministry's channel, specifies that this concerns applicants who did not gain admission either on a grant or on a contract basis — other sources do not provide this detail. The additional program must match the set of subjects the applicant took in entrance exams and have vacant places left after the main placement round.

The outlets do not give the exact threshold above which a score counts as "high" — the ministry has not yet named this figure. According to one outlet's estimate, more than 20% of applicants who failed to gain admission on their first attempt could expect the additional option — this estimate does not match the 37.54% figure for those who missed the passing score in 2025, which does not apply to the current admissions campaign.

According to media reports, the new procedure is planned to launch during the week following August 19, 2026 — the exact date for submitting applications for the sixth program has not yet been announced.
