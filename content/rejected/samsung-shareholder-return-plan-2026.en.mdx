Samsung Electronics' board of directors approved a shareholder return program worth 90–110 trillion won for 2026 on August 21, according to several South Korean business outlets. That translates to roughly $65–79 billion — a calculation based on the exchange rate implied by Bloomberg's own estimate; no separately published rate for the date of the event was found. A press release with the results of the August 21 board meeting had not been published on the company's official website at the time of publication.

The company will allocate about 30 trillion won to third-quarter dividends, including the regular quarterly payout, [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026082154366) reports. The board will determine the remaining part of the program — additional dividends, share buybacks or cancellations — in January 2027, the outlet writes. Separately, the board approved about 15 trillion won for share buybacks tied to employee compensation programs.

Following reports of the decision, Samsung's preferred shares rose about 8%. [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026082154366) devoted a separate headline to the question: «우선주만 8% 급등 왜?» ("why did preferred shares alone jump — by 8%?"). The board's August 21 decision was also confirmed by [MoneyToday](https://www.mt.co.kr/industry/2026/08/21/2026082117113559181) and [Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10848466), which cited a record program total of up to 110 trillion won.

Earlier, on August 20, [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-20/samsung-plans-as-much-as-79-billion-in-shareholder-returns), citing an anonymous source, reported a possible sum of up to 110 trillion won (about $79 billion by the agency's own calculation).

## What's next

Samsung Electronics' board of directors will meet in January 2027 to determine the remaining part of the program — the specific structure of additional dividends, buybacks and share cancellations, Hankyung reports.
