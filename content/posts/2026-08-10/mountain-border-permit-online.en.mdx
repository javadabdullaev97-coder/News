The Tourism Committee on August 4 published a draft presidential resolution on developing adventure tourism — the document moves the issuance of permits for mountain and border areas online via [my.gov.uz](https://gov.uz/ru/uzbektourism/news/view/201920) within 10 days.

## Who gets a permit, and how

Under the [draft document](https://gov.uz/ru/uzbektourism/news/view/201920), electronic issuance of special permits to visit mountain and border areas will be rolled out by the Border Troops of the State Security Service (SGB). The agency will have two months from the resolution's adoption to launch the system. Both local residents and foreign tourists will be able to apply, and review will take no more than 10 days.

Currently, obtaining passes to Uzbekistan's border and mountain zones requires an in-person application and takes longer — the draft is meant to replace this procedure with an online service on the single public-services portal.

## Special rules for buses in the mountains

Under the same draft, special rules are being introduced for buses and minibuses on tourist routes in mountain areas — Charvak, Zaamin and the Kamchik Pass. The measure is aimed at passenger safety on mountain roads, according to the [text of the document](https://gov.uz/oz/uzbektourism/news/view/201920).

## 162 natural sites for adventure tourism

The Tourism Committee included in the draft a list of 162 natural sites with potential for adventure tourism across five regions: Tashkent region (77 sites), Surkhandarya (30), Samarkand (22), Kashkadarya (20) and Jizzakh (13). The list includes mountain ranges, lakes, waterfalls and caves.

## Incentives for adventure tourism operators

The draft provides for exemption from customs duties on imports of about 40 types of equipment and gear for adventure tourism — from January 1, 2027, to January 1, 2030. Separately, training of guides and instructors under UIAA, UIMLA and IFMGA standards is planned — courses will open in eight regions: Karakalpakstan, and Jizzakh, Kashkadarya, Namangan, Samarkand, Syrdarya, Surkhandarya and Tashkent regions.

## What's next

The document remains at the draft stage — it is still being prepared, and no final date for adopting the resolution has been named. After adoption, the Tourism Committee will draft, within a month, a regulation governing the practice of adventure tourism, including the creation of extreme-tourism facilities — ziplines, rope jumping, rope parks and suspension bridges.
