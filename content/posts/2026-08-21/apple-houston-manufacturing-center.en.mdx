Apple opened its Advanced Manufacturing Center in Houston, Texas, on August 13 — a 20,000-square-foot facility offering free training in modern manufacturing methods for small and medium business owners.

Apple CEO Tim Cook and US Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick attended the ceremony. Texas Senator Ted Cruz and Houston Mayor John Whitmire were also present, [the company said](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-opens-advanced-manufacturing-center-in-houston/). "We believe in American workers and American ingenuity, and we're moving with incredible speed," Cook said. Lutnick called the center's opening a step toward fulfilling Apple's pledge to bring manufacturing back to the US.

The training center sits on the same site where Apple produces advanced AI servers. The first batch of servers left the assembly line less than a year after the site was chosen for the plant, the company said. Later in 2026, the facility will begin assembling Mac mini — the first time the computer will be [made in the US](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/02/apple-accelerates-us-manufacturing-with-mac-mini-production/).

Training is free for small and medium business owners. Participants take classes on quality control using machine learning, work with a holographic demo table, and learn laser engraving and assembly, according to Apple.

Apple invested hundreds of millions of dollars in the Houston facility in less than nine months, the company said. The center is part of Apple's $600 billion US manufacturing investment program, announced in 2025.

The Houston opening was one of Tim Cook's last public events as Apple CEO: starting September 1, 2026, he will hand the role to John Ternus and become executive chairman of the board — LEAP News [reported on that decision](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/17/apple-cook-steps-down-ternus-ceo) earlier.
