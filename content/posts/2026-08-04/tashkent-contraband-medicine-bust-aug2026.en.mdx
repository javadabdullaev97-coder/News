Authorities in Tashkent shut down a channel supplying and selling counterfeit medicines from Russia on August 3, seizing 1,330 packages of 29 types of drugs from three households. This was reported by the Telegram channel "Xavfsizlik," which is linked to the State Security Service.

## What Was Found and How It Was Smuggled In

According to the channel, the operation was carried out jointly by officers of the State Security Service and the State Customs Committee. Searches were conducted at three households in Tashkent and Tashkent region linked to a resident of Tashkent's Yangihayot district and his two children.

The seized batch comprised 1,330 packages of 29 types of medicines, including Buserelin, Volini, Bikana and Tirzetta. Their total value was estimated at about 600 million soums — around [$50,200](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-03/) at the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate for August 3 (11,960.18 soums per dollar).

According to investigators, the family brought the medicines into the country from Russia in small batches via railway and airline passengers, then sold them in Tashkent. [The seized drugs have been sent for forensic examination](https://t.me/xavfsizlik_uz/2448), and the inquiry into illegal trafficking continues.

## Why This Is a Violation

Importing and selling medicines in Uzbekistan requires state registration and licensing — functions assigned to the [Agency for Development of the Medical and Pharmaceutical Industry](https://gov.uz/ru/uzpharmagency/pages/about) under the Ministry of Health. The agency licenses pharmaceutical activity and maintains an information system tracking the movement of medicinal products — it was precisely this system, according to the channel, that participants in the exposed scheme bypassed by bringing in drugs through flight and rail passengers instead of official supply and registration channels.
