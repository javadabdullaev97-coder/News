FIFA President Gianni Infantino held a crisis meeting with senior management in Salé on August 5, after the collapse of a plan to sell tournament rights and UEFA's loss of confidence in FIFA's leadership.

## What Collapsed: FIFA's "Subsidiary" Plan

The crisis began with Infantino's initiative to create FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), a separate commercial entity to manage rights to the World Cup and other FIFA tournaments. The plan envisaged selling about 20% of the new entity's shares to private investors for $4.2 billion; the investment group was set to include a company with family ties to US President Donald Trump, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/5/fifas-infantino-set-for-crunch-meeting-after-world-cup-selloff-crisis).

The plan was opposed by UEFA (Europe), CONCACAF (North and Central America) and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC), as well as a number of national federations within FIFA, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/5/fifas-infantino-set-for-crunch-meeting-after-world-cup-selloff-crisis). Infantino's senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro resigned in protest, while former Arsenal manager Arsène Wenger, who oversees global football development at FIFA, publicly distanced himself from the initiative — saying he had not been involved in drafting it and calling the plan's withdrawal "absolutely necessary," [Sky Sports reports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/12040/13569849/gianni-infantino-fifa-president-asks-senior-staff-to-attend-crisis-meeting-in-morocco-on-wednesday).

FIFA officially withdrew the plan on Friday, July 31. Secretary General Mattias Grafström, in an internal memo to staff, called the episode a "sad and regrettable chain of events" after which the project was "definitively closed," [Sky Sports reports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/12040/13569849/gianni-infantino-fifa-president-asks-senior-staff-to-attend-crisis-meeting-in-morocco-on-wednesday).

## UEFA Loses Confidence in Infantino

Despite the plan's withdrawal, UEFA, headed by Aleksander Ceferin, said on August 1 that it had lost confidence in FIFA's leadership — shortly after UEFA members voted on Thursday to potentially boycott FIFA tournaments if the plan were not withdrawn, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/1/uefa-welcomes-fifa-world-cup-standdown-after-voting-to-boycott). UEFA's statement said the organization "can no longer tolerate secretive schemes on accelerated timelines devised by unnamed individuals," and that FIFA's current leadership "has lost the confidence not only of UEFA but of many other members."

According to BBC Sport, UEFA is considering options for pressuring Infantino, including a vote of no confidence, [the BBC reports](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/c62vd95ljp0o). FIFA's presidential election is set for March 2027; Infantino had expected to win a fourth term with little competition. Convening an extraordinary FIFA congress that could address a leadership change would require the support of at least 43 of the 211 national associations that make up the organization, [Al Jazeera notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/5/fifas-infantino-set-for-crunch-meeting-after-world-cup-selloff-crisis).

## Jordan Accuses FIFA of "Blackmail"

Jordan Football Association President Prince Ali bin Al Hussein, a former FIFA vice-president, accused the organization of blackmail on August 4. According to him, FIFA offered to help Jordan resolve its accumulated grievances in exchange for his endorsement of Infantino's re-election, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/4/jordan-fa-chief-accuses-fifas-infantino-of-blackmail-over-endorsement). "We in Jordan pride ourselves on adhering to ethical principles," Prince Ali said, declining the offer.

Jordan's grievances include high World Cup ticket prices in the United States and visa denials for the team's fans, player bonuses for the Arab Cup final that have gone unpaid since December, and, according to Prince Ali, less favorable tax terms for his team from the United States compared with teams from Canada and Mexico, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/4/jordan-fa-chief-accuses-fifas-infantino-of-blackmail-over-endorsement).

## Crisis Meeting in Morocco

The meeting with FIFA's senior management took place on August 5 at the organization's Africa division office in Salé, across the Bou Regreg river from the Moroccan capital, Rabat, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/8/5/fifas-infantino-set-for-crunch-meeting-after-world-cup-selloff-crisis). The meeting's main goal, according to the outlet, is to ensure Infantino remains in office until the presidential election in March 2027.

FIFA had not disclosed the outcome of the meeting as of publication.

## What's Next

According to BBC Sport, UEFA is considering options for pressuring Infantino, including a vote of no confidence at a FIFA congress. Infantino has not yet officially responded to the crisis. FIFA's presidential election will take place in March 2027.
