Epic Games on August 17 [responded](https://9to5mac.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/gov.uscourts.cand_.364265.1713.0.pdf) to Apple's motion for talks in the App Store case — the company is ready to discuss a settlement directly but does not see a need for a court settlement conference right now.

Apple filed an [administrative motion](https://9to5mac.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2026/08/gov.uscourts.cand_.364265.1710.0.pdf) on August 13 — Document 1710 in Epic Games v. Apple, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR. The company asked Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers to refer the parties to a settlement conference — judge-mediated negotiations — before Magistrate Judge Joseph Spero. Apple's attorneys discussed the motion with Epic's attorneys on August 11, but Epic did not agree to grant it, according to the filing. Apple said that discussing a settlement in a closed, non-adversarial format would improve the chances of finding a practical solution and remove the need for protracted litigation.

In its response filed August 17 (Document 1713), Epic said it is ready to consider a direct proposal from Apple. "Epic has always been and remains willing to consider any direct proposal from Apple that includes a serious settlement offer," the filing states. The condition is that it must "introduce competition and benefit all developers." At the same time, Epic does not believe referring the case to a settlement conference now would advance a settlement, the response states. If the court orders such a conference anyway, Epic said it would participate in good faith, according to the filing.

## The App Store commission dispute

Epic Games has been suing Apple since 2020, when Fortnite was removed from the App Store for circumventing the commission on in-app purchases. The dispute centers on whether Apple can restrict developers from promoting payment methods outside the app. In 2025, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals urged the parties to agree on a commission for clicks on external payment links — a ruling Apple cited in its motion.

On the same day, August 13, Apple proposed a new commission structure for U.S. purchases to the court in a separate filing — 15% for regular apps, 10% for participants in the Video, News and Mini Apps Partner programs and for subscription renewals, 5% for Small Business Program participants. LEAP News wrote about this proposal in more detail on the day it was filed.
