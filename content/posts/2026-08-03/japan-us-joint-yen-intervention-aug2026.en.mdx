The Federal Reserve Bank of New York on July 31 sold euros and bought yen on behalf of the US Department of the Treasury for the first time since 2011, acting jointly with Japan, whose currency had plunged to a 40-year low.

## How the Intervention Unfolded

The yen weakened throughout the previous week and on Thursday, July 30, approached 164 yen per dollar — the lowest level since 1986, [Nikkei Asia reports](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/currencies/us-treasury-intervenes-to-support-the-yen-after-japan-steps-in-ft-reports). On Friday, the US Treasury warned a number of banks that it might intervene in trading. Later that day, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, on behalf of the Treasury, sold euros and bought yen through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley — the dollar weakened from about 158.9 to 157.6 yen within half an hour before the close of the New York session.

By Monday, August 3, when both sides officially confirmed the operation, the yen continued to strengthen. The dollar weakened by another 1.4% to 155.20 yen, hitting a three-month high, while the yen gained 3.8% over the two preceding trading days, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/3/japan-and-us-confirm-rare-joint-intervention-to-prop-up-yen).

## Official Confirmation and the Sides' Positions

Japan's Ministry of Finance on Monday published a [statement by Minister Satsuki Katayama](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/public_relations/statement/others/20260803073000.html): the ministry bought yen on July 31 (US Eastern Time) "in coordination with the US Department of the Treasury" — the operation was meant to stop "the excessive volatility and disorderly moves in the yen of recent months." The legal basis for the intervention was cited as the joint statement by the finance ministers of Japan and the United States from September 2025.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said: "We fully support Japan's decisive steps in the currency and money markets aimed at correcting the significant undervaluation of the yen," — [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/3/japan-and-us-confirm-rare-joint-intervention-to-prop-up-yen). US President Donald Trump, speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One on Sunday, called the support for the yen a signal of friendship: "It was mostly a signal of friendship. They have a weakening yen, and they wanted a little help. And we're always there for Japan," — [Bloomberg quotes him as saying](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-02/trump-says-us-intervention-in-yen-was-a-signal-of-friendship).

## Why the Intervention Is Rare

This is the first joint currency intervention by the United States and Japan since 2011 — back then, by contrast, the countries sold yen to weaken it after the earthquake and tsunami in Japan and to ease the repatriation of funds by Japanese insurers, [Nikkei Asia writes](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/currencies/us-japan-intervention-helps-lift-yen-to-155-but-concerns-remain).

The current decline of the yen stems from the gap between the Bank of Japan's ultra-low rates and the Federal Reserve's high rates, which is pushing capital out of the yen and into the dollar, as well as from rising import-driven inflation in Japan. In response, the US Department of the Treasury joined the operation.

## What's Next

Japan's Ministry of Finance said it was ready for further joint operations should volatility resume. Analysts surveyed by Nikkei Asia note that the yen's structural weakness has not gone away: yields on Japanese government bonds continue to rise, and the country's stock indexes have responded to the intervention with declines — [the outlet writes](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/markets/currencies/us-japan-intervention-helps-lift-yen-to-155-but-concerns-remain).
