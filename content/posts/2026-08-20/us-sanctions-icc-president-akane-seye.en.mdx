The US State Department on August 18 imposed sanctions on International Criminal Court (ICC) President Tomoko Akane and senior ICC lawyer Abdoulaye Seye, who took part in preparing the arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The sanctions [will freeze the assets](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) of Akane and Seye in the United States and bar American individuals and companies from doing business with them. The measures take effect on September 17, 2026.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said both officials ["were directly engaged in the ICC's efforts to investigate, arrest, detain or prosecute officials whose governments do not recognize the court's jurisdiction"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/icc-condemns-us-sanctions-as-flagrant-attack-on-courts-independence).

Akane is a Japanese judge who serves as ICC president. Seye, a citizen of Senegal, was part of the prosecution team that secured the arrest warrant for Netanyahu and former Israeli Defense Minister Yoav Gallant. The ICC [issued that warrant in 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) over war crimes in Gaza.

## Reactions

The ICC called the sanctions ["a flagrant attack on the independence of an impartial judicial institution"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/icc-condemns-us-sanctions-as-flagrant-attack-on-courts-independence) and said such measures against judges, prosecutors and staff "undermine the rule of law." In a statement translated into Russian by [Euronews](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/18/icc-hits-back-after-us-announces-fresh-sanctions-on-senior-court-figures), the court added: "When members of the judiciary are threatened for enforcing the law, the international legal system itself comes under attack."

Netanyahu welcomed the sanctions and [called the ICC](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) a court that "cloaks the abuse of power in the language of international law" — a "kangaroo court."

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa said they [firmly support](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/icc-condemns-us-sanctions-as-flagrant-attack-on-courts-independence) Akane and other ICC officials, who "must be able to act independently and without external pressure."

Japan's Foreign Ministry called the decision ["very regrettable"](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) and said Tokyo [consistently supports the ICC](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/icc-condemns-us-sanctions-as-flagrant-attack-on-courts-independence). The ministry added that this support concerns the ICC's efforts "to prosecute and punish the most serious crimes of concern to the international community and to uphold the rule of law."

The Netherlands, where the ICC is headquartered, did not endorse the sanctions. Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen [wrote on X](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) that he had invited Akane to discuss "our unwavering support." He added: "International courts and tribunals must be able to freely carry out their mandates."

This is not the first round of US sanctions against the ICC: [Washington has previously imposed restrictions](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/18/us-sanctions-international-criminal-court-president-and-trial-lawyer) on more than ten of the court's judges and prosecutors. Those measures cut the individuals off from a significant part of the international banking system. In June, three ICC judges filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration over the sanctions.

## What's Next

The sanctions against Akane and Seye are officially set to take effect on September 17, 2026.
