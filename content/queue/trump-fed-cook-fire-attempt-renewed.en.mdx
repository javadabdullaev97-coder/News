US President Donald Trump on August 5 renewed his attempt to fire Federal Reserve (Fed) Governor Lisa Cook — two months after the Supreme Court blocked his first attempt.

## New Letter: Mortgage Fraud Accusations

On August 5, White House Deputy Chief of Staff Daniel Scavino sent Cook a [letter](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/donald-trump-renews-effort-to-fire-federal-reserve-governor-lisa-cook) demanding she respond to accusations of mortgage fraud. According to the White House, Cook listed two different residential properties — in Michigan and in Georgia — as her primary residence, which allowed her to obtain more favorable mortgage terms. The letter noted that the maximum penalty for such an offense in the US is up to 30 years in prison. Cook was given three weeks to respond, until August 26.

According to Bloomberg and Financial Times, the accusations were first raised in August 2025 by Trump ally William Pulte, director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), which oversees mortgage giants Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Trump made his first attempt to fire Cook at that time.

## Supreme Court Left Trump a Loophole

In June 2026, the US Supreme Court, according to Bloomberg, [ruled 5-4](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/trump-again-moves-to-fire-cook-reviving-fed-independence-fight) to let Cook remain in office while her lawsuit was pending. According to the agency, the court found that Trump had not given her notice and an opportunity to be heard before attempting to fire her — under the Federal Reserve Act, governors can only be removed for cause.

According to Bloomberg, Chief Justice John Roberts noted in a footnote to the ruling that nothing prevents Trump from trying to fire Cook again — provided he gives her proper notice and an opportunity to contest the accusations. The new letter from the White House staff is effectively that second attempt.

## Cook's Position and Her Defense

Cook's attorney Abbe Lowell [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/donald-trump-renews-effort-to-fire-federal-reserve-governor-lisa-cook): "As we have done before, we will challenge this new ploy and preserve her position and the Fed's historic role." According to him, the accusations are as baseless now as they were a year ago, when Trump first tried to remove Cook and interfere with the Fed's independence.

Cook joined the Fed's Board of Governors in 2022, appointed by President Joe Biden. In debates over monetary policy, she has favored keeping interest rates high to fight inflation, while Trump has pushed for cuts. The US president has also previously publicly threatened to fire Fed Chair Jerome Powell.

## Why It Matters Beyond the US

Central bank independence from the executive branch in setting interest rates is a principle that most countries have enshrined in law in recent decades as protection against politically motivated monetary policy. The conflict over Cook is a stress test for that very protection in the US: the Fed manages the world's largest reserve currency, and any pressure on its independence affects the stability of global financial markets, including the exchange rates of developing economies.

## What's Next

Cook has until August 26 to formally respond to the accusations and contest them. Meanwhile, her lawsuit against the first attempt to fire her remains ongoing — the outcome of that case will determine whether she keeps her seat as a Fed governor.
