Anthropic filed a confidential draft Form S-1 with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on June 1, 2026 — a first step toward a possible IPO, the company said in an [official statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec).

The SEC does not publish confidential filings until the review is complete. The filing "gives the company the option to go public after the SEC completes its review," Anthropic said in its statement. The offering itself, the company said, "will depend on market conditions and other factors."

The number of shares to be offered and their price have not yet been determined, the company said. Anthropic also did not name a listing date, exchange or underwriters.

In late May 2026, Anthropic raised $65 billion in a funding round that valued the company at $965 billion, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-files-confidential-1-joins-161008569.html) reported. That is nearly 2.5 times higher than its February 2026 round, when the company was valued at $380 billion, [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/anthropic/266640) reported. The company's annualized revenue run-rate exceeded $47 billion, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-files-confidential-1-joins-161008569.html) reported. The growth is attributed to rising corporate demand for Claude models for code development and task automation.

## Risks for private investors

Anticipation of the offering has fueled interest in buying Anthropic shares even before the company goes public. Financial advisers warn that access to such deals carries risks that are not always clear to retail buyers, [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/equities/anthropic/266640) writes.

"Investors need to understand: they're being paid to take on complexity," Seth Hickle, chief investment officer at Mindset Wealth Management, told the publication. He said such private investments can be illiquid and difficult to value.

Haley Shaffer, founder of consulting firm Waypoint West, said genuine access to such deals requires vetted partners: "There are no shortcuts in private markets. Real access requires real relationships," she told the publication.

Anthropic separately warned that it does not recognize share transfers made without board approval and considers such deals invalid, InvestmentNews notes. This includes sales through third-party secondary market platforms not coordinated with the company.

Analysts cited by business publications expect Anthropic to clear SEC review and hold a roadshow in the fourth quarter of 2026, [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-files-confidential-1-joins-161008569.html) reported. The company has not named an exact offering date.
