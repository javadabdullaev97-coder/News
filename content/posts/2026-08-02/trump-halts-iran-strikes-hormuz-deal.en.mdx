President of the United States Donald Trump said on the morning of August 2 that he is cancelling planned strikes on Iran — on condition that the sides quickly reach a deal to open the Strait of Hormuz.

## What Was Said

Trump wrote that Iran and other Middle Eastern countries asked him to hold off on the strikes, and that the framework of an agreement has already been settled. Among the parameters, he named [opening the Strait of Hormuz](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/2/iran-war-live-tehran-warns-of-decisive-response-to-any-us-aggression).

[BBC dates the report](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjwx74qgld2o) to August 2, 03:05 GMT, and notes that the message came amid reports that new intensive strikes on Iran were being prepared for the weekend.

No signed document exists, and no deadline for the deal has been named. Al Jazeera's live blog contains no reports of Tehran agreeing to these terms.

Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs warned of a decisive response to any aggressive actions by the US and Israel. This warning [is recorded in the same Al Jazeera live blog](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/2/iran-war-live-tehran-warns-of-decisive-response-to-any-us-aggression) that published the American president's statement.

## What Preceded the Statement

A day before the cancellation, Trump [authorized a new strike plan](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106884), presented to him at the Camp David residence. According to American officials cited by The Wall Street Journal, the strikes could have begun over the weekend and lasted several days. "We are going to hit them very hard," Trump said at the time. White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said Iran would not possess nuclear weapons.

After that, US embassies in Israel and other Middle Eastern countries [issued alerts](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/2/us-embassies-issue-alerts-after-trump-vows-to-hit-iran) for their citizens. Americans in the region [were advised](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106937) to "avoid travel throughout the region and be prepared to depart due to the potential for unforeseen escalation"; citizens outside the Middle East were advised to seriously reconsider plans to travel there. Al Jazeera correspondent Mike Hanna noted, however, that there were no clear signs of an imminent strike.

## Kuwait Says It Intercepted Drones

On the morning of August 1, Kuwait's armed forces [intercepted drones](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106919) in the country's airspace, a representative of Kuwait's Ministry of Defense said. The attacks struck a government facility in the north of the country and civilian vehicles, Interfax reported, citing CNN. There were no casualties; material damage from falling debris was recorded, according to a statement by Kuwait's Ministry of Defense. Kuwaiti officials called the incident "Iranian aggression."

Later on August 1, Kuwait's Ministry of Foreign Affairs [said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106933) it reserves the right to "take all necessary measures to protect its security, interests and resources."

## How It Got to This Point

The war began on February 28, 2026, with US and Israeli strikes on Tehran, after which Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping. On June 17, the sides signed a memorandum of understanding: the strait was to open for at least 60 days during negotiations. The memorandum collapsed in July over a dispute about ship transit routes.

According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/why-has-the-us-halted-its-bombing-of-iran), the US Department of Defense lowered the death toll among American service members from 18 to 14; 482 people were wounded. When fighting resumed in July, oil prices rose more than 20% and topped $100 a barrel, and after the pause in the exchange of strikes, prices fell by about 4%.

## Uzbekistan's Position

Uzbekistan has no direct military connection to the conflict. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Republic of Uzbekistan [issued a statement on February 28](https://gov.uz/ru/mfa/news/view/137818), the day the strikes began. The ministry expressed concern over the escalation and called on the sides to "exercise restraint and refrain from further steps that could lead to dangerous escalation."

## What's Next

Trump tied the cancellation of the strikes to the condition of quickly reaching a deal. He named neither a deadline nor a format for negotiations.
