The Central Bank of Uzbekistan on August 14 [published a review](https://cbu.uz/uz/press_center/reviews/4342919/) of individuals' foreign currency operations for the first half of 2026. The share of the country's labor migrants in the CIS fell to 29%, while it rose to 42% in the European region.

East Asia's share rose to 26%, [the review says](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/02b/n4p9nyn7idoap0d08g8rre91i096qec2/sharh.pdf), citing data from the Migration Agency under the Cabinet of Ministers.

In total, about 1.4 million Uzbekistan citizens work abroad — in nearly 40 countries. The largest destination remains unchanged: 834,200 people work in Russia.

The review doesn't explain the basis for the 29%, 42% and 26% figures. Calculated from the country breakdown, Russia and Kazakhstan together account for more than 65% of all 1.4 million migrants, not 29%. These percentages most likely describe the structure of new organized job placements in 2026, rather than the entire migrant population, but the Central Bank's review doesn't state this explicitly.

## Distribution geography

After Russia, the largest numbers are in Kazakhstan — 84,400 people, Turkey — 72,100, and South Korea — 46,400. European countries employ 258,000 Uzbekistan nationals, with another 97,700 in other countries.

Among all those working abroad, 398,900 people (28.6%) are women, and 402,100 (28.9%) are youth.

## Why demand is growing in new markets

According to the Migration Agency, 124,500 citizens have been placed in jobs abroad since the start of 2026. In 2025-2026, 18 intergovernmental and interagency agreements were signed with 14 countries. The review lists Germany, Italy, Spain, the United States and Japan among the promising partners.

Individuals' remittances to Uzbekistan from the United Kingdom grew by 62%, and from European Union countries by 27% (Ireland — 86%, Lithuania — 18%, the Netherlands — 7%). Remittances from the United States grew by 19%, [the review shows](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/02b/n4p9nyn7idoap0d08g8rre91i096qec2/sharh.pdf).

The Institute for Employment Research at Germany's Federal Employment Agency and the European Commission estimate Europe's labor shortage will reach 7.5-10.5 million people by 2030. This estimate is cited in the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's review. In total, Uzbekistan received $9.3 billion in remittances in January-June 2026 — 13% more than a year earlier.
