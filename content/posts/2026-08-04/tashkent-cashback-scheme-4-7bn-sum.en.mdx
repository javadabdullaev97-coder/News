The Department for Combating Economic Crimes under the Prosecutor General's Office of the Republic of Uzbekistan reported on August 4 the theft of 4.7 billion soums in budget funds in Tashkent through fake receipts and tax cashback.

## What the Department Found

According to [the Department's statement](https://t.me/Department_uz/12946), it uncovered two fraud schemes exploiting cashback in the Soliq Mobile app.

In the first scheme, according to investigators, officials at one company — identified in the Department's statement only as "G.M." — used computer equipment to produce fake documents for the sale of goods worth 76.2 billion soums. These fictitious sales were processed through cash registers, and the fake receipts were sold via Telegram for 70,000–150,000 soums apiece to 2,475 citizens listed in the unified social protection registry. These receipts generated about 7.5 billion soums in cashback, of which, according to the Department, 4.1 billion soums in budget funds were actually stolen.

In the second scheme, employees at two more companies — identified by the Department as "E.G." and "I.F." — processed fictitious sales worth 10 billion soums through cash registers. They sold the fake receipts to 1,812 citizens in the same category for 100,000–150,000 soums apiece. Of the 1 billion soums in cashback generated, 640.8 million soums were stolen.

Combined, the 4.1 billion and 640.8 million soums total 4.74 billion — the Department rounded this figure to 4.7 billion soums in the headline of its statement. At the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-04/) for August 4 — 11,989.46 soums per dollar — that is about $392,000.

Criminal cases have been opened under the relevant articles of the Criminal Code covering embezzlement of budget funds and forgery of documents. The Department did not name the suspects or their positions, and did not specify what stage the investigation into each of the two schemes has reached.

## How the Cashback Program the Fraudsters Exploited Works

The State Tax Committee monthly refunds part of the cost of purchases to program participants who register their receipt in the tax authorities' mobile app. As a general rule, this is [1% of the receipt amount](https://t.me/soliqnews/19428), capped at 247,200 soums per person per month. The cashback is intended for the receipt's owner, who made the purchase themselves. Registering someone else's receipt, obtained from an intermediary or a social media mailing list, is [prohibited](https://t.me/soliqnews/19336) — the Tax Committee had warned about this as early as May 25.

The schemes described by the Department were built precisely around circumventing this rule. Fake receipts for purchases that never happened were registered in the app not by buyers but by scheme participants, who collected cashback for goods that no one actually bought.

## What's Next

The investigation is ongoing, and the Department did not give a timeline for its completion. Until a court verdict takes legal effect, individuals connected to the schemes described are presumed innocent.
