Road Safety Service (SBDD) chief Murodbek Sherliyev told a session of the Legislative Chamber of the Oliy Majlis on August 4 that Uzbekistan recorded about 27 million traffic violations in 2025 — 77% of them severe.

## More Than 2,000 Killed in a Year

According to [SBDD data](https://yhxx.uz/uz/news/yurtimizda-2025-yilda-sodir-bolgan-yol-transport-hodisalari-natijasida-2-188-nafar-inson-vafot-etdi) published on January 14, 2026, Uzbekistan registered 9,226 road accidents in 2025. Of these, 1,832 were fatal and 7,394 involved injuries. The accidents killed 2,188 people, while another 8,901 suffered injuries of varying severity. That is 0.5% and 0.34% lower than a year earlier, respectively.

The number of accidents involving children fell by 171 cases (-9.6%), and the number of children killed dropped by 36 (-12.8%).

The SBDD also provided five-year trends: the death rate per 100,000 population fell from 6.9 in 2021 to 5.7 in 2025 — a 17.4% decrease. The number of accidents per 10,000 vehicles dropped from 6.3 to 4 — a 36.5% decrease. The agency attributes the more pronounced decline in relative indicators to population growth — from 35.3 million in 2021 to 38.2 million in 2025. The growing vehicle fleet has the same effect — up from 3.8 million to 5.5 million over the same period.

## Which Violations Kill Most Often

At the Legislative Chamber of the Oliy Majlis session on August 4, 2026, SBDD chief Murodbek Sherliyev presented a breakdown of 2025 traffic violations to justify a bill toughening liability for violations that endanger life on the road:

- speeding by 40 km/h or more — 156,300 cases, 1,625 killed and 606 injured;
- letting a minor aged 14–16 drive — about 2,500 cases, 44 killed and 101 injured;
- driving under the influence — 92 killed and 46 injured;
- trucks left on the roadside at night without lights — more than 2,000 cases, 84 killed and 126 injured.

Lawmakers approved the bill in its first reading. The document amends the Criminal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure, the Code of Administrative Liability and the law "On Road Traffic Safety."

## What the Bill Proposes

The proposed measures include a new type of administrative penalty — temporary vehicle seizure for six months to one year for violations that pose a high risk to life, administrative liability for organizing illegal street racing, the elimination of the fine discount for repeat offenders, and criminal liability for letting a minor drive if it leads to serious consequences.

Sherliyev stressed that the goal of the amendments is not to raise fines but to prevent actions that pose an extremely high risk to the lives of road users. A date for the bill's second reading has not yet been set.
