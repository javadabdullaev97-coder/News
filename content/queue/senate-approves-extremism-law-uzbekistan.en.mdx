The Senate of Uzbekistan's Oliy Majlis on August 8 [approved a law](https://senat.uz/ru/plenary-sessions/post-5872) that replaces the term "religious extremism" with "extremism" and reclassifies a first offense of producing, possessing or distributing extremist materials as an administrative violation.

## What the Law Changes

The document is the Law "On Amendments and Additions to Certain Legislative Acts of the Republic of Uzbekistan Aimed at Increasing the Effectiveness of Combating Extremism." It amends the Criminal Code and the Code of Administrative Liability. As [stated in the Senate's announcement](https://senat.uz/ru/plenary-sessions/post-5872), "direct criminal liability for committing such acts for the first time has been abolished, and administrative liability is introduced instead, with criminal liability arising only upon repeated commission."

At the same time, the underlying Law "On Countering Extremism" — [ZRU-489](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/3841963), adopted in 2018 — is being revised: according to the Senate's wording, it is given "a clear definition of the concept of extremism." In the legislation, the term "religious extremism" is being replaced with "extremism" — a change meant to ensure a unified legal approach in law enforcement practice.

The law also provides for defining a specific range of extremism-related crimes and implementing state programs to prevent extremism. It further provides for maintaining an electronic registry of extremist organizations and materials and strengthening the legal basis for monitoring and risk assessment.

## Additional Mechanisms

Other provisions include support for scientific research on countering extremism and protection and encouragement for individuals who report extremist activity. The law also introduces measures against the spread of extremist ideas online and in the media. In addition, it improves the procedure for designating organizations as extremist and the mechanisms for resocializing individuals convicted of extremism-related crimes.

Senators, according to the Senate, stressed that the law serves to "strengthen the legal balance between reliable protection of human rights and ensuring public safety in combating extremism." They also noted that the document should improve the effectiveness of preventive work.

## Background and What's Next

The amendments were discussed in the Senate's Defense and Security Committee back in early June. At the time, [UzA reported](https://uza.uz/en/posts/senat-zhamoat-tartibiga-tahdid-soluvchi-materiallar-tarqalishining-oldi-olinadi_866755) that the document would affect the Criminal Code, the Code of Administrative Liability, as well as legislation on youth policy, education and information. On August 8, the law passed plenary approval by senators.

The exact wording of the amendments to Criminal Code articles and the final text of the law will appear on lex.uz once the document is signed by the president.
