Kazakhstan will hold elections on August 23 to the Qurultay, a new single-chamber parliament that replaced the former Mazhilis and Senate starting July 1, 2026, following the March constitutional reform.

Kazakhstan's Central Election Commission approved the voting calendar. Candidate nomination [ran](https://qazinform.com/news/kazakhstans-cec-approves-qurultay-election-calendar-28e45b/amp) from July 2 to July 13, and registration of party lists was completed on July 23. The official election campaign [ran](https://qazinform.com/news/kazakhstans-cec-approves-qurultay-election-calendar-28e45b/amp) until 12:00 p.m. on August 22, the date that marked the day of silence before the vote.

Kazakhstan's new constitution [took effect](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/02/kazakhstan-starts-living-by-new-constitution-sets-date-for-parliamentary-elections) on July 1, 2026. It replaced the previous 1995 basic law and ended the work of the bicameral parliament — the Mazhilis and the Senate — which had operated for three decades. A referendum on the new constitution [was held](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/02/kazakhstan-starts-living-by-new-constitution-sets-date-for-parliamentary-elections) on March 15, 2026.

President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev called the former parliament's final session historic and said the country had launched an irreversible transformation process, Euronews [reports](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/02/kazakhstan-starts-living-by-new-constitution-sets-date-for-parliamentary-elections).

Ahead of the vote, DW [says it remains an open question](https://www.dw.com/ru/выборы-в-казахстане-зачем-токаеву-однопалатный-парламент/a-78447354) which parties will enter the Qurultay and what role the new legislative body will play.

Bloomberg [characterizes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-21/oil-rich-kazakhstan-set-for-elections-as-tokayev-bolsters-power) the elections as part of a political restructuring in which Tokayev continues to consolidate power. The agency notes that this keeps open the prospect of extending his rule in Central Asia's largest energy producer.
