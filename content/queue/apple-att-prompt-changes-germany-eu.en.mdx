The German Federal Cartel Office [announced](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html)
on August 17, 2026, that it had concluded a years-long investigation into
Apple: the company committed to redesigning the tracking consent prompt
for users.

This concerns App Tracking Transparency (ATT) — a pop-up window on iPhone
and iPad. It asks the user whether they allow an app to track their
activity across other apps and websites for personalized advertising.

The regulator found that in Apple's system, consent windows for the
company's own services looked more user-friendly than those for
third-party apps. "Apple is entitled to give its users a level of data
protection above the legal minimum. But if the company introduces
additional data-use rules within its ecosystem, those rules must not put
its own offerings at an advantage over competitors' offerings," [said
Bundeskartellamt president Andreas
Mundt](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

Bundeskartellamt opened the investigation in June 2022. The regulator
issued a preliminary assessment in February 2025, and market testing of
Apple's proposed changes took place in December of that year, [according
to the regulator's
materials](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

## What Changes

Under the terms of the agreement, Apple will align the design of consent
windows for its own services and third-party apps — they will become
identical in content, wording and appearance, [Bundeskartellamt
says](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

The window for third-party developers will lose "potentially off-putting"
symbols and wording — a characterization the regulator itself gave to the
current design, [the decision
states](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

Developers will gain the right to combine the ATT prompt with a separate
consent request under the European General Data Protection Regulation
(GDPR). Previously, ATT and GDPR prompts were shown separately, [the
regulator
notes](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

Bundeskartellamt also demanded that the consent-request architecture
itself be simplified — the process for third-party apps is currently more
complex than for Apple's own services, [the decision
shows](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).

## When It Takes Effect

Apple has four months from the official delivery of the decision to make
changes to the iOS and iPadOS interface, [the regulator
says](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).
The commitments will remain in force for seven years under the oversight
of an independent trustee.

## Who Is Affected

Formally, the decision covers devices with a German billing address in
the App Store. But Apple said the new rules will extend to nearly all
European Union countries, [Reuters
reports](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/17/apple-to-change-app-data-consent-rules-german-regulator-says).

Apple called the changes voluntary. "We believe the current ATT prompt is
clear and effectively protects users' control over their data," the
company said. Apple added that it agreed to change the prompt's text and
design at the regulator's request, [Reuters
reports](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/17/apple-to-change-app-data-consent-rules-german-regulator-says).

This is not the first monetary fine in the European Union tied to ATT.
France previously fined Apple 150 million euros, and Italy fined it 98.6
million euros, over similar complaints about the system, [Reuters
reports](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/17/apple-to-change-app-data-consent-rules-german-regulator-says).

"We are not seeking to achieve the maximum possible level of consent for
personalized advertising. We want users to be able to make a free and
informed decision," [Mundt
said](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2026/08_17_2026_Apple_ATTF.html).
