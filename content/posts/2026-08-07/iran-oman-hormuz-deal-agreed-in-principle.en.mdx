Iran said on August 6 it had reached an agreement in principle with Oman on terms for temporarily reopening shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/iran-says-deal-with-oman-on-strait-of-hormuz-agreed-in-principle) — the final text still needs approval from Iran's Supreme National Security Council.

## The terms: a 60-day route with no transit fees

The framework agreement runs for 60 days with an option to extend, [Kommersant reported](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8863861). Ships entering the strait will use a route closer to the Iranian coast, while those exiting will hug the Omani side; the agreement provides for neither transit fees nor vessel service charges, [Interfax specified](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107850). Earlier, in late July, the two sides had disagreed specifically over the size of port fees — now, according to this data, the issue has been resolved. At the same time, according to sources as of August 4, Iran plans to introduce a separate "service fee" to cover security costs, with revenue to be shared with Oman — this detail does not yet appear in the text of the framework agreement itself, [Kommersant wrote](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8863861).

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the new route would remain in effect "for two to four months — or longer if necessary," [Interfax reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107850).

Later on August 6, a clarification emerged: access to the new route will be closed to vessels from countries Tehran considers hostile — without specifying which ones, [Interfax reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107850).

## Tehran: the agreement with Oman does not guarantee safety

An official spokesperson for Iran's Foreign Ministry stressed that the understanding reached with Oman does not by itself make the strait safe, [Interfax reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107850). The risk of destabilizing actions by the United States remains — a condition Tehran has been repeating for several weeks now, tying the strait's reopening to the lifting of the US naval blockade.

## The US blockade continues

The second wave of the naval blockade of Iranian ports has been in effect since July 14, 2026. According to CENTCOM, US warships have redirected 48 commercial vessels, detained two more and disabled two others, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/will-the-us-iran-war-and-hormuz-deadlock-last-for-months), citing the US Central Command (CENTCOM). The US side insists on free passage for ships without Iranian permits or fees — a position Tehran opposes, [Al Jazeera noted](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/hormuz-deal-close-whats-the-latest-on-each-sides-positions).

The war between the United States and Iran has been underway since February 28, 2026. A memorandum of understanding dated June 17, 2026, which had opened the strait for the duration of negotiations, collapsed in July amid a dispute over shipping routes.

## Why this matters for global trade

Under normal conditions, about a fifth of global oil and gas consumption passes through the Strait of Hormuz, [Al Jazeera noted](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/hormuz-deal-close-whats-the-latest-on-each-sides-positions). Asia's dependence is higher: in February 2026, nearly 85% of its imported oil and about 28% of its liquefied natural gas moved through the strait, [Nikkei Asia calculated](https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/iran-tensions/iran-war/how-the-us-iran-war-has-redrawn-asia-s-energy-map). Some of the lost supply was offset by increased purchases from Russia and the United States — they became the main beneficiaries of the rerouting, [Nikkei Asia wrote](https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/iran-tensions/iran-war/how-the-us-iran-war-has-redrawn-asia-s-energy-map).

## What's next

Analysts polled by Al Jazeera do not rule out that the deadlock over the Strait of Hormuz could drag on for months — the two sides remain divided on the fundamental question of sovereignty over ship passage, [the outlet wrote](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/will-the-us-iran-war-and-hormuz-deadlock-last-for-months). Sources have not yet given a date for approval of the agreement by Iran's Supreme National Security Council or specified the official reactions from Oman and the United States.
