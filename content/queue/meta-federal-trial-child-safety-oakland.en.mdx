A federal court in Oakland opened proceedings on August 18 in a lawsuit filed by US state attorneys general against Meta, which is accused of deliberately fostering addiction to Facebook and Instagram among children.

The case is presided over by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers in the [Northern District of California](https://cand.uscourts.gov/cases-e-filing/cases/422-md-03047-ygr/re-social-media-adolescent-addictionpersonal-injury-products). It is part of the consolidated federal proceeding No. 4:22-md-03047, which includes lawsuits from individuals, school districts and state attorneys general. California, Colorado, Kentucky and New Jersey were the first to bring claims on behalf of the 29-state coalition, [Reuters reports](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-29-states-head-to-court-in-biggest-test-yet-of-youth-social-media-litigation-4854182). It remains unclear how the claims from the coalition's remaining states will be handled.

The lawsuit was filed in 2023. According to the plaintiffs, Meta designed "likes," the endless feed and recommendation algorithms to keep minors on its platforms as long as possible. The company also concealed the health risks to children from the public, the attorneys general allege. The attorneys general also allege that the company unlawfully collected data from users under 13, in violation of the federal law protecting children's online privacy.

## Positions of the parties

California Attorney General Rob Bonta [said](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/ahead-opening-statements-attorney-general-bonta-lays-out-case-against-meta-over): "Meta designed Facebook and Instagram to keep kids on the platforms longer and longer — to the point of physical and mental harm." Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser said the company "knew its platforms were harmful to young people but continued practices that kept them hooked." Kentucky Attorney General Russell Coleman called the trial "the largest consumer protection case in US history." New Jersey Attorney General Jennifer Davenport [said](https://www.njoag.gov/attorney-general-davenport-presses-case-against-meta-as-historic-trial-begins/) Meta "put an entire generation of children's mental health at risk."

Meta laid out its assessment in a [court filing dated July 6, 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/meta-youth-safety-trial-product-remedies/). The company estimates the lawsuit's potential damages could reach $1.4 trillion — comparable to its $1.5 trillion market capitalization. The company called a penalty of that size "unprecedented in the history of consumer protection enforcement" and "untethered to any proven violation."

## Parallel proceeding in New Mexico

A day before the Oakland hearings began, [according to Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/17/new-mexico-attorney-general-social-media-safety-laws-states-federal-trial-meta-facebook-instagram/), New Mexico Attorney General Raul Torres told The Guardian about two bills being prepared for the state legislature. One would remove the cap on fines under the state's consumer protection law. The other would extend social media safety legislation to cover AI systems and chatbots.

Torres cites the outcome of a separate case in Santa Fe court. In March 2026, a jury ruled against Meta on 75,000 counts of violating the state's unfair practices law and ordered it to pay $375 million. In early August, the court ordered the company to pay another $567 million into a damages fund and to change protections for underage users.

According to a [Reuters/Ipsos poll](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/meta-29-states-head-to-court-in-biggest-test-yet-of-youth-social-media-litigation-4854182), 85% of Americans believe social media can be addictive for children, and 61% support stricter oversight of such companies.
