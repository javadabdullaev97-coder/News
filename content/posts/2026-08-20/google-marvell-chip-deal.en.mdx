Marvell Technology issued a warrant to Google on August 18 for the purchase of up to 58,970,907 of its shares — up to $12.2 billion — under an expanded partnership to develop AI chips.

The warrant gives Google the right, but not the obligation, to buy the shares at a fixed price of $206.58 apiece. That is according to the [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001835632/000119312526356217/d412696d8k.htm) that Marvell filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on August 18.

The first tranche of the warrant — 1,360,867 shares — will vest for Google in equal quarterly installments over the first year after the agreement is signed. The remaining shares will vest as Google purchases Marvell products: one tranche for every $500 million in such revenue, for a total of 240 tranches through the end of Marvell's fiscal 2033. [The warrant can be exercised](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001835632/000119312526356217/d412696d8k.htm) until August 18, 2033.

The expanded partnership covers the development of custom chips for the TPU ecosystem — Google's name for its AI computing processors. It includes inference accelerators — for running already-trained AI models — as well as storage controllers and network interface controllers. It also includes memory controllers and near-memory computing modules, [according to the Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001835632/000119312526356217/d412696d8k.htm).

Marvell shares rose almost 8% by the close of trading on Wednesday, [Reuters reports](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/marvell-grants-google-12-2-123812695.html). Shares of Broadcom, which had until now been Google's main partner in TPU development, fell more than 5%.

## Why Google Wanted a Second Chip Supplier

Broadcom has long been Google's main partner in TPU development, [The Register writes](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/08/19/google-pits-marvell-against-broadcom-as-it-chases-ai-crown/5289902). The deal with Marvell gives Google a second custom chip supplier and leverage in price and performance negotiations with Broadcom, the outlet writes.

Reuters calls the deal part of a broader trend: companies are looking for cheaper alternatives to Nvidia's expensive graphics processors, which currently underpin most AI model training and operation.

"This is a big win for Marvell," said Morningstar analyst William Kerwin, adding that the deal expands Google's roster of suppliers rather than displacing Broadcom.
