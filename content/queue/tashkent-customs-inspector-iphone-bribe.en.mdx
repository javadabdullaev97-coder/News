The Department for Combating Economic Crimes under the Prosecutor General's Office of Uzbekistan reported on August 5 the detention of an inspector at the Keles customs checkpoint in Tashkent for accepting a $3,000 bribe to process smuggled iPhones.

## How the inspector was detained

The Tashkent city department of the agency carried out the operation jointly with the State Customs Committee of Uzbekistan, the Department itself [said](https://t.me/Department_uz/12954) on August 5. According to the investigation, the inspector at the Keles customs checkpoint of the Tashkent city customs department, identified in the report by the initials O.S., received $3,000 from a citizen identified as A.Z. The amount is equivalent to approximately 35.8 million soums at the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-05/) for August 5 — 11,942.21 soums per dollar.

The bribe, according to the investigation, was intended for processing customs documents for 10 iPhone mobile phones brought into Uzbekistan in violation of established procedure. The inspector, according to the Department, was to register the devices under the names of other citizens — using their passport data — and register the IMEI codes. The inspector was caught red-handed at the moment the money changed hands.

## Criminal case opened, investigation continues

A criminal case has been opened under Article 210 of the Criminal Code of Uzbekistan ("accepting a bribe"), the Department said in its statement. Investigative actions are continuing. The agency did not disclose the full names of either the inspector or the citizen who handed over the money, and did not report the pretrial restriction imposed or the timeline for referring the case to court. Both individuals are presumed innocent until a court verdict enters into legal force.

## What's next

The Department has not yet said whether other Keles checkpoint employees are being investigated or whether this case is linked to other shipments of smuggled phones. LEAP News will follow developments in the case.
