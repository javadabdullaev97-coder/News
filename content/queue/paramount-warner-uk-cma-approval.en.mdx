The UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) on August 6, 2026, approved the acquisition of Warner Bros Discovery by American corporation Paramount Skydance — a deal valued at $110 billion, according to Bloomberg. The regulator did not refer it for an in-depth Phase 2 investigation.

## What the CMA decided

The CMA reviewed the deal as part of a Phase 1 investigation that formally launched on June 9, 2026. On August 6, the regulator [announced the completion of its investigation](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/paramount-slash-warner-bros-discovery-merger-inquiry) without referring the case to Phase 2. According to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/paramount-warner-deal-cleared-by-uk-antitrust-watchdog), the regulator concluded that the merger does not create competition risks in the UK. This covers film distribution, the children's television market and streaming. The CMA's decision was, in Bloomberg's assessment, one of the last major foreign regulatory hurdles for the deal. It has already been approved or gone unchallenged in 66 jurisdictions worldwide.

## Conditions for Channel 5 and other UK assets

Alongside the CMA's decision, the UK's Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) [announced](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/public-statement-proposed-acquisition-of-warner-bros-discovery-by-paramount-decision-of-no-intervention-on-public-interest-intervention-or-foreign-sta) that it would not issue a public interest intervention notice. The department had considered doing so but decided against it after Paramount provided a package of legally binding undertakings.

According to DCMS, Paramount committed to:

- maintain the public service broadcaster status of its UK terrestrial channel Channel 5 and comply with its licence obligations through December 31, 2034;
- increase funding for news, children's programming and UK-produced drama;
- not merge linear channels with on-demand services in the UK;
- preserve the editorial independence of Channel 5 News from CBS News and CNN International;
- maintain the availability of CNN International to UK viewers;
- report annually to the department on compliance with these conditions.

[Variety notes](https://variety.com/2026/film/global/uk-paramount-warner-bros-merger-1236828958/), citing DCMS, that the company's children's channels — including Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network — will also retain separate editorial identities.

## Deal not yet closed in the US

The CMA's approval removes one of the last foreign regulatory hurdles, but it does not mean the deal is fully closed. According to the [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-warner-bros-discovery-deal-uk-review-expanded-1236650446/), the U.S. Department of Justice had earlier approved the deal at the federal antitrust level. However, a federal judge in California issued a temporary restraining order halting its closing. The lawsuit was filed by a group of attorneys general from several U.S. states and the Writers Guild of America. The plaintiffs argue the merger would harm competition in film distribution and cable licensing.

Paramount [agreed](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/paramount-warner-bros-discovery-deal-uk-review-expanded-1236650446/) not to close the transaction before June 2027. According to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/paramount-warner-deal-cleared-by-uk-antitrust-watchdog), a trial on the merits is scheduled for March 2027. This means the deal has been approved by regulators worldwide but is not yet legally finalized — its outcome will be determined by a U.S. court.

## What companies are merging

According to public information, Paramount Skydance Corporation was formed in 2025 when Skydance Media, controlled by the Ellison family, acquired Paramount Global. Paramount Global is the parent company of the CBS television network, Paramount Pictures studio, the Nickelodeon and MTV channels, and the Paramount+ streaming service. Warner Bros Discovery is the result of the 2022 merger of WarnerMedia and Discovery Inc. Its portfolio includes the Warner Bros Pictures studio, the HBO television network, CNN, the Discovery Channel network, the Max streaming platform and DC Studios.

If the deal closes, the combined company would rank alongside Disney, Comcast/NBCUniversal and Netflix in the scale of its film, television and streaming assets.
