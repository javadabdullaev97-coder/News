Tashkent Hokimiyat announced on August 1 a working meeting on creating a specialized shelter for stray dogs in the capital and a system for humane capture, veterinary examination, sterilization and finding new owners.

## What Was Discussed at the Meeting

The meeting itself [took place](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51207) earlier: the Russian-language version of the announcement dates it with the words "the other day," while the [Uzbek-language version](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51206) uses "kuni kecha" — meaning the day before, July 31. The press service did not give an exact date.

The meeting was attended by Sh. Mamadjanov, head of the Tashkent hokim's office staff; I. Matkarimova, a deputy and founder of the "Hayot" stray dog shelter; and heads of the city improvement department and the stray animal capture service.

As Tashkent hokim Shavkat Umurzakov previously reported, work on creating a "modern and humane system for handling stray animals is moving into the practical phase." The announcement specifically notes: "This is not just about building a shelter." The system is to include "safe and humane capture, veterinary examination, vaccination, sterilization, treatment, temporary keeping of animals, and finding new owners for them."

The hokim, [the announcement states](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/51207), "expresses serious concern about cases of cruel and irresponsible treatment of animals." A separate paragraph of the press release is devoted to ethics: "Kindness, care for the weak and compassion have always been characteristic of our people. Cruelty toward animals is alien to our traditions and values. The treatment of those who cannot defend themselves is an indicator of the morality and maturity of society as a whole."

Following the meeting, the hokimiyat said "specific further steps have been determined." Relevant specialists, veterinarians, and representatives of the public and volunteer community will be brought into the project. The announcement gives no construction timeline, site, shelter capacity, or the amount and source of funding. The Uzbek-language version of the statement was published under the headline "Muhokamalardan amaliy harakatlar sari" — "From Discussions to Practical Action" — and contains no additional figures.

## How the System Works Now

The current system for handling stray animals is set out in [Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 202](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/1833370) of July 8, 2011. The document mandated the creation, starting August 1, 2011, of stray animal capture units under city and district improvement departments, based on the existing dog-catching teams.

The same resolution instructed the Council of Ministers of Karakalpakstan and the hokimiyats of the regions and Tashkent to allocate land plots for crematoria and to oversee the transfer by citizen self-governance bodies of information about dog owners. The veterinary service is responsible for the annual rabies vaccination of dogs, while the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MIA) is responsible for enforcing rules on keeping dogs and cats. The resolution also provides for temporary holding facilities and quarantine for animals that have bitten people.

The same document permits the shooting of stray and sick animals in rabies zones, carried out by the Ministry of Internal Affairs on the recommendation of the veterinary service.

On April 29, 2025, the city administration [signed a memorandum](https://t.me/poytaxt_uz/38336) of cooperation with the "Mushukkent" project on joint work to promote responsible treatment of animals in the urban environment.

## Liability for Cruelty

Punishment for cruelty to animals was established by [Law No. ZRU-711](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/5601207) of August 25, 2021. It added Article 202-1 to the Criminal Code. Under this law, torturing animals or causing them suffering out of mercenary or other base motives, resulting in death or injury and committed after an administrative penalty, carries a fine of 25 to 50 base calculation units, up to 240 hours of community service, up to one year of correctional labor, or up to six months of restriction or deprivation of liberty.

The same law also amended Article 111 of the Code of Administrative Responsibility. Under ZRU-711, the deliberate torture of animals out of mercenary or other base motives carries a fine of 3 to 5 base calculation units. For a repeat violation within a year, an offense committed in the presence of a minor, or the death or injury of the animal, the fine is 5 to 10 base calculation units or up to 15 days of arrest. The sanctions are given as set out in Law ZRU-711 of August 25, 2021.

## What's Next

The hokimiyat did not give a date for the project's next stage or specify the future shelter's organizational form — whether it will be a city institution or run jointly with private and volunteer organizations. The press release mentions participation by the volunteer community and the public but without specific roles or commitments. No publication had appeared on the official tashkent.uz website at the time this article was published — the statement was released only on the press service's Telegram channel.
