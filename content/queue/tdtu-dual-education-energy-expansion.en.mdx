Tashkent State Technical University named after Islam Karimov will move all fourth-year students and half of third-year students in energy programs to dual education at production facilities starting from the 2026/2027 academic year, the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovations [said](https://t.me/eduuz/50066) on August 5.

## What Changes in the Academic Process

According to the [Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovations](https://t.me/eduuz/50066) (Ministry of Higher Education), TSTU students in energy programs will combine studies with hands-on training at partner production enterprises: 100% of the fourth-year cohort and 50% of the third-year cohort will move to the dual model. The ministry's statement does not name the specific partner enterprises, the number of students covered, or the program's funding source.

The ministry links the move to an instruction from President Shavkat Mirziyoyev to update curricula for energy programs, expand student involvement in dual education, and train specialists in solar and wind energy.

## Context: Fuel and Energy Sector Meeting

The instruction was given at the president's video conference on priority tasks for the fuel and energy sector on July 27, 2026. Following that meeting, Minister of Energy Zhurabek Mirzamakhmudov and the head of JSC Regional Electric Networks were [dismissed](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1105870), while the heads of four other major energy companies were put on probation until the end of the year. Mirziyoyev said at the time that the sector's problem was not funding but management of the system.

## What Remains Unknown

The Ministry of Higher Education has not disclosed whether the dual model will extend to other technical universities besides TSTU, how many students and which specific fuel and energy enterprises will be involved, or who is funding the transition. It is also not entirely clear from the ministry's wording whether the new rule is limited to TSTU's energy programs or applies to all of the university's specialties — the headline of the announcement refers to energy, but the sentence with the figures mentions "all of the university's programs." These details should be clarified as the ministry or the university release additional statements.
