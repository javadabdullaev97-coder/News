J.P. Morgan [announced](https://t.me/minecofinuz/16224) on August 8 that Uzbekistan's sovereign soum bonds will be added to the GBI-EM index starting September 30, 2026, the country's Ministry of Economy and Finance reported.

## What's Being Added to the Index

The bonds in question are three-year sovereign soum bonds worth $1 billion (12.2 trillion soums), which Uzbekistan [placed](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/203864) on the international market in 2026 at a rate of 12.25% per annum. According to the Ministry of Economy and Finance, it was the largest local-currency deal among countries in Central and Eastern Europe, the Middle East and Africa over the past 15 years.

GBI-EM (Government Bond Index – Emerging Markets) is J.P. Morgan's flagship index tracking the returns of emerging-market government bonds denominated in local currencies. It is tracked by asset managers with over $300 billion in assets, and currently includes bonds from 20 countries, 13 of which hold investment-grade ratings. Uzbekistan, [according to the release](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/203864) on the gov.uz portal, will become the only CIS country whose sovereign local-currency bonds are included in the index.

## Why It Matters for Uzbekistan

Inclusion in the index means the bonds automatically enter the portfolios of funds that benchmark their holdings to the GBI-EM composition — this broadens the pool of foreign investors and typically lowers borrowing costs for the issuer. The Ministry of Economy and Finance [cites](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/203864) Paraguay as an example: after its local-currency bonds were added to the GBI-EM index, borrowing rates for issuers in the country fell by up to 1 percentage point.

## Context: The Road to the Index

The decision followed several years of work developing the government bond market and improving the sovereign credit rating. According to the Ministry of Economy and Finance, the weighted average rate on government securities fell from 17.1% in 2022 to 12.6% in the first half of 2026, while the average maturity of the securities rose from 1.5 to 2.35 years.

At the same time, international rating agencies have been upgrading Uzbekistan's credit rating. Fitch Ratings raised the rating from "BB-" to "BB" on June 26, 2025, and improved the outlook from "Stable" to "Positive" on June 3, 2026. S&P Global Ratings improved the outlook on May 23, 2025, and raised the rating from "BB-" to "BB" on November 21, 2025. Moody's Ratings improved the outlook on June 13, 2025, and raised the rating from "Ba3" to "Ba2" on June 25, 2026. All data is cited in the [release](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/203864) on gov.uz.
