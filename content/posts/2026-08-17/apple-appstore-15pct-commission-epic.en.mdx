Apple on August 13 [proposed](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/app-store-fees-apple-link-outs/) to the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California a commission of up to 15% on purchases made through external links outside the App Store in the U.S. — half the standard 30%.

This is Apple's proposal within the Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc. case (No. 4:20-cv-05640), not a court ruling. The rate must still be approved by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers. Apple proposed splitting the commission into three tiers. The first is 15% for regular apps. The second is 10% for participants in the Video Partner, News Partner and Mini Apps Partner programs, as well as for subscription renewals. The third is 5% for developers in the Small Business Program with annual revenue of [up to $1 million](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/13/apple-proposes-commissions-of-up-to-15-for-off-app-store-purchases-in-the-us/).

The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals had earlier ruled that Apple may charge only for the "necessary expenses" of enabling external payments. According to Epic Games, such costs, by Apple's own admission, amount to 0%. Epic Games said it disagrees with the proposed fee structure and intends to challenge it with the involvement of experts.

## Legal background

Judge Gonzalez Rogers in 2021 ordered Apple to let developers direct users to alternative payment methods outside the App Store. In April 2025, the court found Apple in contempt of court for charging a 12–27% commission on such purchases. The court temporarily barred the company from charging any fee on them at all.

Apple appealed the ruling. The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the contempt finding but stated that the company is entitled to compensation for necessary expenses. It sent the case back to the district court to determine the rate.

## What's next

Epic Games must file an objection to the proposed rates, backed by expert opinions, within the deadline set by the court. Judge Gonzalez Rogers will determine the final commission rate after reviewing Epic's objections.
