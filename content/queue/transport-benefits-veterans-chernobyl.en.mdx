President Shavkat Mirziyoyev on August 3, 2026, approved a new procedure for discounted transit for war veterans and Chernobyl cleanup workers — paper coupons will be replaced with an [ID card or biometric passport](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9483).

## What's Changing

More than [48,400 citizens](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9483) currently hold the right to discounted transit in Uzbekistan. They are disabled people and participants of the 1941-1945 war, people with equivalent status, and those who took part in the cleanup after the Chernobyl nuclear plant disaster. In 2025, they used transit services 1.7 million times. The benefit is currently provided through paper coupons.

Under the new procedure, citizens in [11 categories](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9483) — including disabled people and war veterans, people with equivalent status, and Chernobyl cleanup workers — will retain free transit or a 50% discount on air, rail, and intercity transport. The way benefit eligibility is confirmed will change: instead of a paper coupon, presenting an ID card or biometric passport at the ticket counter will be sufficient. Eligibility will be verified through the "Unified National Social Protection" information system.

## Who Gets the Benefits

The categories covered by the new procedure are disabled people and participants of the Great Patriotic War, people with equivalent status, and citizens who took part in the cleanup after the Chernobyl nuclear plant disaster. The full list of all 11 categories and the breakdown by benefit type (who qualifies for free transit and who gets a 50% discount) is not disclosed in the [president's meeting protocol](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9483) — those details are expected to appear in secondary legislation that Mirziyoyev has instructed relevant agencies to draft.

## What's Next

Starting January 1, 2027, discounted air, rail, and intercity bus tickets can be purchased online through the "Baraka" mobile app of the social card. This is the only date specified precisely in the meeting protocol; the [source](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9483) does not specify the date on which the new procedure itself (abolishing paper coupons and switching to ID cards) will take effect — the head of state instructed agencies to "thoroughly work out the relevant documents" and set up service delivery on the ground.

The measure was among proposals to improve the social protection system — the same meeting separately addressed education coverage for children with disabilities, support for people without a fixed place of residence, and assistance for victims of human trafficking.
