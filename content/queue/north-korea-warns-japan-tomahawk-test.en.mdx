Kim Yo Jong, sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, said on Wednesday, August 5, that Pyongyang would consider additional military measures in response to Japan's test of a Tomahawk cruise missile.

## What Kim Yo Jong said

In a statement carried by North Korea's state news agency KCNA, Kim Yo Jong accused Japan's armed forces of abandoning their defensive doctrine. She said Japan's military is turning into a force capable of launching pre-emptive strikes, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/north-koreas-kim-yo-jong-issues-military-threat-over-japan-missile-test). Pyongyang intends to adopt "additional military options" that will make Japan feel its security has come under greater threat.

"We should make her [Japan] regret it," Kim Yo Jong said. Al Jazeera assessed this as one of the sharpest formulations she has directed at Tokyo in recent months, [the outlet writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/north-koreas-kim-yo-jong-issues-military-threat-over-japan-missile-test).

Kim Yo Jong also accused Tokyo of reviving the ambitions of a "war criminal state," linking this to the expansion of the Japanese military's offensive capabilities, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/north-korea-warns-of-more-military-steps-over-japan-s-rearmament). Separately, she accused the United States of contributing to Japan's military buildup. She said Washington is supplying Tomahawk missiles, modifying ships to use them, and supporting the tests, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/north-koreas-kim-yo-jong-issues-military-threat-over-japan-missile-test).

## The Tomahawk missile test

Japan test-fired the US-made Tomahawk cruise missile on July 29, launching it from the destroyer Chokai in the Pacific Ocean, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/5/japan-tests-us-tomahawk-missile-as-it-moves-away-from-post-wwii-status). The outlet calls the test another step in Tokyo's gradual departure from its post-war pacifist status.

Kim Yo Jong also cited other Japanese missile tests and Japan's participation in joint military drills with the United States in the Philippines in May. She called these additional signs of Tokyo's military buildup, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/north-koreas-kim-yo-jong-issues-military-threat-over-japan-missile-test).

Pyongyang has not yet disclosed which "additional military options" it is considering, [Bloomberg notes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/north-korea-warns-of-more-military-steps-over-japan-s-rearmament).
