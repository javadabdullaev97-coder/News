The U.S. State Department imposed sanctions on International Criminal Court (ICC) President Tomoko Akane of Japan and senior ICC prosecution lawyer Abdulaye Seye of Senegal — [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/19/us-sanctions-international-criminal-courts-president-and-top-lawyer) on August 19.

According to the outlet, [Seye was part](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/19/us-sanctions-international-criminal-courts-president-and-top-lawyer) of a team of ICC prosecution lawyers investigating alleged Israeli war crimes in the Gaza Strip.

## ICC Reaction

The International Criminal Court [called the sanctions](https://news.un.org/feed/view/ru/story/2026/08/1468515) "a flagrant attack on the independence of an impartial judicial body." The court warned that such measures "undermine the rule of law" and threaten "the international legal order itself."

In a statement relayed by the UN, the ICC said: "The Court does not intend to waver from its principles and stands firmly behind its staff." It also said it would continue to fully carry out its mandate.

According to the UN, following this decision, [nine of the ICC's 18 judges](https://news.un.org/feed/view/ru/story/2026/08/1468515) are now under U.S. sanctions, along with both deputy prosecutors and the court's former prosecutor. The sanctions also apply to one member of the court's registry staff.
