Uzgidromet recorded a concentration of fine particulate matter PM10 in Tashkent's ambient air at 83 µg/m³ as of 9 a.m. on August 7 — 1.10 times the maximum permissible concentration (MPC).

## What the Instruments Showed in the Morning

According to a [Uzgidromet bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21898) published on the morning of August 7, automated monitoring stations recorded a PM10 concentration of 83 µg/m³ against an MPC of 75 µg/m³ — an excess of 1.10 times. The finer PM2.5 particulate reading stayed within the norm: 27 µg/m³ against a permissible level of 60 µg/m³.

Uzgidromet's full official name is the Hydrometeorological Service Agency under the National Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan for Ecology and Climate Change. The agency publishes air quality bulletins from Tashkent's automated monitoring stations on its Telegram channel several times a day.

## Levels Were Normal a Day Earlier

On the evening of August 6, according to [the previous bulletin](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21893) from the same agency, the situation was different: PM10 stood at 60 µg/m³ and PM2.5 at 17 µg/m³, both within the MPC. [LEAP News wrote about this](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/tashkent-air-quality-aug6-uzgidromet) in a piece on the discrepancy between Uzbekistan's MPC and international air quality indexes.

The morning excess on August 7 is the first PM10 deviation from the norm over the past day recorded by the agency itself, rather than only by outside indexes. Uzgidromet did not accompany the bulletin with recommendations for the public.

## Different MPC Thresholds for the Same Indicator

In its bulletin at 9 a.m. on August 7, Uzgidromet listed the MPC threshold for PM10 at 75 µg/m³ — a standard lower than the 300 µg/m³ the same agency cited for PM10 in its bulletin at 6 p.m. on August 6. The discrepancy may be explained by the fact that Uzbekistan applies different thresholds for PM10 for single readings and daily averages, but the bulletin itself does not clarify this.

In its [guidelines revised in 2021](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034433), the World Health Organization recommends not exceeding 45 µg/m³ of PM10 per day — meaning even the level Uzgidromet called "within the norm" on August 6 (60 µg/m³) already exceeded the WHO reference, and the 83 µg/m³ recorded on the morning of August 7 exceeds it by nearly double.
