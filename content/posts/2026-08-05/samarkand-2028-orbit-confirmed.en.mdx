The Samarkand-2028 satellite successfully reached orbit on the morning of August 5, the Uzhydromet Telegram channel [said](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21872), confirming the launch from a sea platform off the coast of China's Shandong province.

## What is known about the launch

The launch vehicle carrying the Samarkand-2028 satellite and Indonesia's Lampung-1 was launched by the Chinese company STAR.VISION — a partner of Uzbekistan's Agency for Space Research and Technologies ("Uzbekcosmos") in the joint development of hyperspectral satellites with onboard AI modules. A day earlier, on August 4, Uzbekcosmos [said](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2670) the launch window was 07:00–09:00 Tashkent time and named the Haiyang spaceport (Eastern Spaceport) on the Shandong coast as the launch site. From there, vessels carrying the rocket travel out to the sea launch platform, from which the rocket actually lifted off.

The satellite carries an artificial intelligence module developed by Uzbekcosmos specialists to process hyperspectral data directly in orbit — LEAP News [published](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-04/samarkand-2028-satellite-launch) the project's parameters and the history of the memorandum with STAR.VISION the day before the launch.

## No confirmation from Uzbekcosmos yet

As of this article's publication, Uzbekcosmos's own Telegram channel had not posted a message about Samarkand-2028's successful orbital insertion — the agency's latest [post](https://t.me/uzcosmos_official/2670), published on August 4, contains only a briefing on the upcoming launch. So far, the successful launch has been reported by state Telegram channels not directly tied to the space sector — among them Uzhydromet, the hydrometeorological service under Uzbekistan's National Committee for Ecology and Climate Change. The Uzhydromet post itself, however, is not the weather service's own report but a repost from a third-party Telegram channel, Pressuzb.

## What's next

Uzbekcosmos earlier [said](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-04/samarkand-2028-satellite-launch) it would announce the exact launch time and separately confirm the satellite's orbital insertion once the mission was complete. As of publication, the agency has issued no statement of its own on the mission's results.
