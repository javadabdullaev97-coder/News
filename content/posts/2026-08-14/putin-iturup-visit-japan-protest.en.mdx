Russian President Vladimir Putin on August 13 visited Iturup Island in the southern Kuril Islands, which Japan claims, for the first time in his capacity as head of state — Tokyo urgently summoned the Russian ambassador and lodged a protest.

## What Putin did on Iturup

Putin toured the Yasny fish processing plant, a central hospital and a school on the island, and spoke with plant employees and local residents, Kommersant [reported](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8879070). The visit was preceded by Pacific Fleet exercises on August 12, which Putin observed from aboard the cruiser Varyag, followed by a meeting on the security of Russia's eastern borders, the outlet [writes](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8879070).

This is Putin's first visit to the archipelago as president, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/japan-protests-as-russias-putin-visits-disputed-kuril-islands). In 2010, Dmitry Medvedev, who was president at the time, visited Kunashir — one of the neighboring disputed islands, [Nikkei Asia notes](https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/international-relations/putin-visits-kuril-islands-claimed-by-japan-drawing-tokyo-s-ire).

## Tokyo's protest and the ambassador summons

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi called Putin's visit "absolutely unacceptable" and said it "hurts the feelings of the Japanese people," [Nikkei Asia reports](https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/international-relations/putin-visits-kuril-islands-claimed-by-japan-drawing-tokyo-s-ire). According to her, the trip "will complicate the restoration of Japan-Russia relations in the medium and long term."

Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi summoned Russia's ambassador to Tokyo, Nikolai Nozdrev, and lodged a "resolute protest," noting that the islands "are, historically and under international law, an integral territory of Japan," [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/japan-protests-as-russias-putin-visits-disputed-kuril-islands).

According to a report by NHK television, cited by [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/14/1221089-yaponiya-rassmotrit), the Japanese government is considering options for tightening existing sanctions against Russia imposed after the start of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The source did not specify a list of new restrictions — for now, discussion is limited to considering options.

## Russia's position: the Kurils are "Russian territory"

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said "Iturup Island and the Kuril Islands are officially Russian territory" and called Takaichi's and Motegi's statements "unacceptable," [Vedomosti reports](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/13/1221040-zaharova-vizit-putina-iturup). According to her, the most "notorious and dangerous" element of the Japanese statements is "revanchism."

Russia's embassy in Japan called the summoning of the ambassador an "unfounded demarche" and said that "the president's trips across Russian territory are exclusively sovereign decisions of the Russian leadership and cannot be a subject for discussion with foreign states," [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1109138). Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Alexander Grushko said "this diplomatic activity will lead nowhere," adding that "Russian land will remain Russian land in full accordance with international law."

## The dispute over four islands

The southern Kuril Islands — Iturup, Kunashir, Shikotan and the Habomai group — came under Soviet control in the days after Japan's surrender in 1945. Tokyo calls them the "Northern Territories" and demands their return, considering it a condition for concluding a peace treaty that the two countries have never signed since World War II, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/13/japan-protests-as-russias-putin-visits-disputed-kuril-islands). After 1945, about 17,000 Japanese residents were deported from the islands; today, according to the outlet, about 20,000 Russian citizens live there.

Relations between Moscow and Tokyo sharply deteriorated after Russia launched its invasion of Ukraine in 2022: Japan joined international sanctions against Russia and has since introduced them in several packages, [Nikkei Asia notes](https://asia.nikkei.com/politics/international-relations/putin-visits-kuril-islands-claimed-by-japan-drawing-tokyo-s-ire).
