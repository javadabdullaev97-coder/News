In early August, Uzbekistan returned two citizens who had been wanted internationally through Interpol: on August 4, an extremism suspect was extradited from Poland, while a fraud suspect who had been sought for 1.5 years was handed over from Turkey.

## Extradition from Poland: extremism case

The Republic of Poland [extradited](https://gov.uz/ru/iiv/news/view/201705) a man suspected under Part 1 of Article 244-2 of the Criminal Code — creating, leading or participating in religious extremist, separatist, fundamentalist or other banned organizations. This was [reported](https://t.me/IIVuz/150813) by the Ministry of Interior of the Republic of Uzbekistan.

According to investigators, after the criminal case was opened the suspect left Uzbekistan and spent a long time hiding abroad, where he was placed on Interpol's international wanted list. His whereabouts were established through cooperation between the Interpol national central bureaus of Tashkent and Warsaw, with assistance from the Uzbekistan embassy in Poland. The Interior Ministry has not disclosed the detainee's name.

## Extradition from Turkey: fraud case

The second man is an Uzbek citizen identified in the official statement by the initials A.A., suspected of fraud (Article 168 of the Criminal Code), who had been wanted for 1.5 years. He was detained in Turkey and handed over to the Uzbek side, the Uzbekistan Consulate General in Istanbul [said](https://t.me/istanbul2020Boshkonsulxona/2914).

According to the consulate, A.A. had been hiding in Turkey the entire time until local law enforcement located him. The extradition was carried out through cooperation between the law enforcement agencies of Uzbekistan and Turkey.

## What's next

The two cases are separate episodes unrelated in substance: one involves suspected extremist activity, the other suspected fraud. The fate of both men now depends on the course of the investigation and trial; officials have not yet disclosed further details about the identities of the extradited men.
