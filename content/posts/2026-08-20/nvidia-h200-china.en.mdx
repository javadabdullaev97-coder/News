ByteDance and Tencent each received roughly 10,000 Nvidia H200 chips in mainland China over the past few weeks, [the Financial Times reported on August 19](https://www.ft.com/content/6c5650fb-969d-4d4e-80d6-8d11002a8cf7), citing industry sources.

The Nvidia H200 is a chip used to train and run artificial intelligence models. According to the newspaper, this is the first concrete confirmation that chip shipments to mainland China have actually begun. Earlier reports on this had remained unconfirmed amid months of regulatory uncertainty. According to the Financial Times, this time the restriction was lifted by the Chinese side rather than the American one — Beijing cleared the chips through its own import review, [the paper writes](https://www.ft.com/content/6c5650fb-969d-4d4e-80d6-8d11002a8cf7).

Both companies are entitled to buy up to 100,000 such processors each from Nvidia, [Engadget notes](https://www.engadget.com/2239738/china-reportedly-allows-bytedance-tencent-import-10000-h200-chips/). The current shipment amounts to about a tenth of the approved volume. DigiTimes also [cites the figure](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260819VL212/nvidia-bytedance-chips-tencent-beijing.html) of 10,000 chips per company, referencing the Financial Times.

For comparison, Nvidia itself holds roughly half a million H200 chips in reserve, [The Decoder writes](https://the-decoder.com/china-lets-nvidias-h200-chips-trickle-onto-the-mainland-to-help-its-ai-firms-keep-pace-with-the-us/). The H200 is at least two generations behind Nvidia's most powerful chips, which China is still barred from buying under U.S. export controls, the outlet notes.

Nvidia chip shipments to China now run along two parallel tracks. One is direct sales of approved models like the H200; the other consists of attempts to bypass restrictions by renting access to more powerful H100 and A100 chips through foreign cloud providers.

## How the companies got approval to buy

The United States has barred Nvidia from selling powerful artificial intelligence chips to China over concerns about their military use. In December 2025, Washington allowed Nvidia to sell the H200 to approved Chinese buyers. In May 2026, ten companies, including ByteDance and Tencent, received that approval, [Engadget notes](https://www.engadget.com/2239738/china-reportedly-allows-bytedance-tencent-import-10000-h200-chips/).

According to DigiTimes, U.S. approval alone was not enough — additional clearance was required from the Chinese side, [the outlet writes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260819VL212/nvidia-bytedance-chips-tencent-beijing.html). Beijing had previously routed most purchased chips to Hong Kong rather than the mainland, [Engadget notes](https://www.engadget.com/2239738/china-reportedly-allows-bytedance-tencent-import-10000-h200-chips/). The outlet also points to the risk that H200 availability could reduce demand for processors from domestic manufacturer Huawei. The current mainland shipment signals that the previous approach may be changing.

## A loophole through the cloud

Alongside direct shipments, the United States is trying to close another channel that gives Chinese companies access to Nvidia chips — cloud services, [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/china-ai-nvidia-chips-us-export-controls.html). This concerns Nvidia H100 and A100 chips, which remain banned from direct sale to China. Chinese companies rent remote access to them through data centers in Singapore and Gulf countries instead of buying physical hardware, [TechBuzz.ai notes](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/china-s-ai-firms-bypass-nvidia-chip-ban-via-cloud-loophole).

A former U.S. Department of Commerce official told the outlet: "We built a wall around the hardware and forgot about the front door," [TechBuzz.ai reports](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/china-s-ai-firms-bypass-nvidia-chip-ban-via-cloud-loophole). The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security has begun reviewing the scale of such access. U.S. lawmakers are considering regulating remote access to chips through foreign cloud platforms, but there is no set timeline for a decision, [TechBuzz.ai notes](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/china-s-ai-firms-bypass-nvidia-chip-ban-via-cloud-loophole).
