Startup Callosum announced on August 20 that it had raised $100 million in a seed round led by Atomico, with participation from Plural, DCVC and the UK's state-backed Sovereign AI Fund, [the company said on its blog](https://www.callosum.com/blog/seed-round).

Callosum is building a platform that routes artificial intelligence tasks between different models and computing chips rather than processing all requests on a single type of processor. The company calls the approach Tailored Inference: inference is the stage at which an already trained model produces a response to a specific query. Access to the system is provided through an API — a programming interface through which third-party services connect to the platform, [the company said on its website](https://www.callosum.com/blog/seed-round).

Cerebras, a developer of specialized AI chips, became the flagship launch partner. "By integrating Cerebras into the Callosum platform, we're making ultra-fast inference available exactly where it has the greatest impact," said Cerebras Chief Strategy Officer Andy Hock, [the company quoted him as saying](https://www.callosum.com/blog/seed-round). Among its partners, the company also named Rebellions and a number of infrastructure providers and hardware manufacturers.

## State Fund's Participation

The round included Sovereign AI Fund, the UK's state investment fund that backs AI infrastructure within the country. UK AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said: "Artificial intelligence is nothing without the chips that power it — and demand for them will only grow," [Callosum said in its post](https://www.callosum.com/blog/seed-round).

On February 26, 2026, the company made its first public statement, announcing a pre-seed funding round, according to the archive of posts on Callosum's website, [list of blog entries](https://www.callosum.com/blog). The company has not disclosed the exact amount of that round in public materials.
