The Ministry of Economy and Finance of Uzbekistan on August 14 [reported](https://gov.uz/ru/imv/news/view/206361) preliminary results of the mass valuation of real estate in Tashkent: the market value of apartments exceeded the cadastral value by 7.4 times, and of household plots — by more than 14 times.

## What the valuation showed

According to the [ministry's data](https://gov.uz/ru/imv/news/view/206361), the cadastral value of more than 600,000 apartments in Tashkent currently stands at 57.8 trillion soums (≈$4.84 billion at the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-14/) for August 14 — 11,937.89 soums per dollar). The mass valuation put their combined market value at 426.7 trillion soums (≈$35.7 billion) — 7.4 times more.

For more than 213,000 household plots, the cadastral value is 39.1 trillion soums (≈$3.3 billion), while the market value is 548.4 trillion soums (≈$45.9 billion) — more than 14 times higher.

The results were discussed at an open dialogue at the ministry attended by media, city government representatives and industry specialists, where officials also explained the goals of the valuation, its methodology and the data it was based on.

## Price variation across the city

The valuation showed significant differences between districts of Tashkent. The price per square meter of apartments ranged from 4.6 million to 35.8 million soums (≈$385–3,000), and land plots from 115 million to 890 million soums per 100 square meters (≈$9,600–74,600).

Owners were given 60 days to review the results: during this period they can file an appeal to revise the valuation, request clarifications or commission an independent expert review, the ministry [said](https://gov.uz/ru/imv/news/view/206361).

## Why the valuation matters — and how it connects to taxes

The legal basis for the mass valuation is [Presidential Decree UP-43](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7417793) of March 5, 2025, "On introducing a system of mass valuation of real estate." Among the decree's goals are ensuring a balanced valuation of real estate for taxation, sale and purchase, collateral and other transactions, as well as creating a unified real estate market database.

To implement the decree, the National Center for Mass Valuation of Real Estate, with a staff of 30, was established under the Cadastre Agency. The Ministry of Economy and Finance shapes policy in this area, the Cadastre Agency coordinates the process, and the center carries out the valuation itself.

The decree provides for a phased rollout of the system nationwide: 2025–2026 — pilot project in Tashkent; 2026–2027 — Nukus and regional centers; from 2027 — the remaining regions. Once fully implemented, the plan is to establish the value of more than 8 million residential and non-residential premises and about 7 million land plots across Uzbekistan.

The publication of the valuation results by itself does not change current tax assessments — the ministry did not specify a timeline for when the new market value data will form the basis for calculating property tax and land tax.

## What's next

The mass valuation in Tashkent continues in test mode through the end of 2026. The next stages are Nukus and regional centers in 2026–2027. Owners of Tashkent real estate can appeal the valuation results within 60 days of their publication.
