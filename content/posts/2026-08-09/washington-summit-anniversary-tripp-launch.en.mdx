Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev and U.S. President Donald Trump discussed by phone on August 8 the anniversary of the Washington peace summit held a year ago at the White House, and the launch of the TRIPP transport corridor.

## Aliyev and Trump: Infrastructure Nearly Complete

In the August 8 phone call, the two leaders [confirmed](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108268) that work to build TRIPP transport infrastructure on Azerbaijani territory is close to completion. They also expressed hope that similar work would begin soon in Armenia. The leaders noted that peace and stability had held in the region over the past year, with no incidents capable of undermining them.

The sides also mentioned the year's economic results: exports of Azerbaijani oil products to Armenia and the launch of trade ties between the two countries. Trump invited Aliyev to the G20 summit, to be held in Miami in December 2026.

The same day, Aliyev [sent](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108278) Trump a letter marking the anniversary. In it, he described Azerbaijan-U.S. relations as being "at a historic high over 34 years" and listed the year's achievements: the trilateral declaration signed at the White House, the peace agreement with Armenia, the dissolution of the OSCE Minsk Group, the launch of the TRIPP project, and the signing of a strategic partnership charter during the visit of U.S. Vice President JD Vance.

## Pashinyan and Trump Discuss TRIPP Launch

Separately, Trump [held a call](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8875586) with Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan on August 8. The two sides confirmed their intention to begin practical implementation of TRIPP — the construction of road, rail and oil-and-gas infrastructure.

The route will run through Armenian territory and connect mainland Azerbaijan with Nakhchivan, and from there with Turkey. Pashinyan thanked Trump for organizing the peace summit in Washington on August 8, 2025. He also noted Trump's role in preparing the peace declaration between Armenia and Azerbaijan.

## Trans-Caspian Enterprise Fund — $201 Million

The same day, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio [announced](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/08/1219758-investitsii-v-zakavkaze) that the U.S. government had allocated $201 million to launch the Trans-Caspian Enterprise Fund. The fund will encourage private investment in the South Caucasus and Central Asia.

The initiative is part of a package of agreements around TRIPP — a project to modernize rail, road, energy and digital infrastructure that has become informally known as the "Trump Route." According to the U.S. Department of State, [the fund has already begun operating](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/08/the-anniversary-of-a-historic-breakthrough-and-the-trump-route-for-international-peace-and-prosperity-tripp): a board of directors has been elected, and engineering surveys for the railway are under way in Armenia. In Azerbaijan, construction of the highway and railway spur to the border with Armenia is close to completion.

## A Year After Washington

On August 8, 2025, Aliyev and Pashinyan [signed](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108278) a trilateral declaration establishing peace between Azerbaijan and Armenia at the White House, brokered by Trump. Following the meeting, the sides agreed to launch TRIPP — a corridor meant to connect mainland Azerbaijan with Nakhchivan through Armenian territory.

Over the past year, the sides [have not reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1108268) any incidents capable of derailing normalization.

## What's Next

The exact date for the start of construction on the Armenian section of TRIPP has not yet been announced — following the August 8 calls, the sides said they were ready to begin work "in the near future." The Azerbaijani section of the highway and railway spur is close to completion, according to the sides.
