The second phase of the Qosh-Tepa Canal in northern Afghanistan is 98% complete in earthworks and 90.5% complete in bank reinforcement, the [Telegram channel of the state company Ozsuvtaminot](https://t.me/uzsuv/45766) reported this month. The post cites data from Afghanistan's National Development Corporation (NDC).

Qosh-Tepa is a 285-kilometer trunk canal that diverts water from the Amu Darya to irrigate arid land in northern Afghanistan. The first 108-kilometer section opened in Balkh province on October 12, 2023 — eight months ahead of schedule, [Fergana.agency reported](https://fergana.agency/news/131657/). According to [NDC](https://www.ndc.gov.af/en/archives/7883), the canal is meant to supply water to land in Balkh, Jowzjan and Faryab provinces.

## How much water the canal will divert

According to the [Scientific-Information Center of the Interstate Commission for Water Coordination of Central Asia](https://www.cawater-info.net/afghanistan/qosh-tepa_e.htm), Afghanistan currently draws 3-5 cubic kilometers of water a year from the Amu Darya basin. Once the canal is complete, the same estimate puts potential intake at 9-11 cubic kilometers a year.

## Uzbekistan's position

President Shavkat Mirziyoyev first publicly commented on the project on September 15, 2023, at a meeting of the Council of Heads of State founders of the International Fund for Saving the Aral Sea in Dushanbe. A statement on the president's website said: "The problem of water scarcity in Central Asia has become acute and irreversible, and will only worsen." The region, in the press service's wording, has seen "a new participant appear in the water use process," [the statement said](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/6661). Mirziyoyev proposed forming a joint working group to study the canal's effect on the Amu Darya's water regime.

Minister of Water Resources Shavkat Khamrayev said in March 2025 that Afghanistan also has a right to water from the Amu Darya and proposed resolving the issue through negotiations. "Should we take up arms and go to war? No. We are building relations, holding a dialogue," [Fergana.agency quoted the minister as saying](https://fergana.agency/news/137071/). According to the same outlet, once all sections are operational, Qosh-Tepa will bring Afghanistan about $500 million a year and irrigate 555,000 hectares.
