On July 30, an institute under Uzbekistan's Ministry of Economy and Finance proposed shifting from universal heating subsidies to a social norm — 60 square meters per household or 16 square meters per person.

## What exactly the institute is proposing

The Institute for Shadow Economy Reduction and Fiscal Analysis under the Ministry of Economy and Finance of the Republic of Uzbekistan [presented](https://ifs.imv.uz/ru/press/news/toshkentda-ekspertlar-va-oav-vakillari-ishtirokida-fiskal-muloqot-bolib-otdi) the idea at the "Fiscal Dialogue" event in Tashkent. The event brought together representatives of government agencies, experts and journalists. Currently, the heating subsidy is provided to all residents in the same amount regardless of a family's income or property status. The institute [proposes](https://ifs.imv.uz/ru/press/news/issiqlik-taminoti-tariflari-va-ijtimoiy-meyor-taklifi-xususida) keeping the subsidized rate only within the social norm — 60 square meters per household or 16 square meters for each registered resident. For a family of four, this means a norm of 64 square meters. At the same time, the institute proposes strengthening support for families that genuinely need help.

According to the institute, under this approach, payment terms would not change at all for 45.4% of apartments in Tashkent with an area of up to 60 square meters. The institute did not specify in its materials by what factor payments would increase for area exceeding the norm.

## The institute: not a decision, but an idea for discussion

On July 31 — the day after the presentation — the institute [published a clarification](https://ifs.imv.uz/ru/press/news/issiqlik-taminoti-tariflari-va-ijtimoiy-meyor-taklifi-xususida). The trigger was media and social media reports interpreting the proposal as a "fivefold increase in tariffs." The institute called this interpretation one-sided, neither confirming nor denying the figure itself. At the same time, it stressed: "the essence of the proposal is not a mass increase in tariffs, but ensuring that state support is targeted."

In the clarification, the institute stated that the proposal "does not have the status of an official decision, document or draft regulatory legal act." No changes have yet been made to tariffs or payment procedures — residents continue to pay for heating under the current rules. In the medium term, the institute proposes providing needy families with targeted cash assistance through the [Unified Social Protection Registry](https://gov.uz/uz/advice/64/document/2279) instead of subsidizing tariffs for everyone.

## Context: the institute has put ideas up for public discussion before

The heating proposal is not the only initiative the institute presented at the "Fiscal Dialogue" on July 30. On the same day, the institute put forward other ideas in tax and social policy for discussion. The institute emphasizes that such materials are the [result](https://ifs.imv.uz/ru/press/news/issiqlik-taminoti-tariflari-va-ijtimoiy-meyor-taklifi-xususida) of its staff's own research rather than draft regulatory acts, and that "science develops through open discussion."

## What's next

The institute has not specified a timeline by which the proposal for a social norm on heating might gain the status of an official document. In the institute's own words, any proposal at this stage is "not a finished decision, but a matter put forward for open discussion."
