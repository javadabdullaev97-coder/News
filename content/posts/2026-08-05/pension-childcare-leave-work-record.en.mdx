The childcare leave period that counts toward mothers' work record for pension purposes has been increased from three to six years — the rule took effect on February 10, 2022. The [Telegram channel of Uzbekistan's Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://t.me/minecofinuz/16178) issued the reminder on August 4.

## What changed

The rule was introduced by [Law No. ZRU-752 of February 9, 2022](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/5852618), "On Amendments and Additions to Certain Legislative Acts of the Republic of Uzbekistan in Connection With the Further Strengthening of Guarantees of the Rights of Citizens and Business Entities." The document amended clause "g" of Article 37 of the [Law "On State Pension Provision for Citizens"](https://lex.uz/acts/112312) of September 3, 1993, No. 938-XII, replacing the wording "but no more than three years" with "but no more than six years."

This concerns the childcare leave period counted toward the work record used to calculate the old-age pension; the exact calculation method — whether combined across all children or counted separately for each child — is not specified in the text of the amendment. Before the amendment, a maximum of three years of such leave counted toward the record; now it is up to six years. The law has been in force since February 10, 2022.

## How this affects mothers' pension record

The procedure for assigning and paying state pensions is governed by the [Regulation approved by Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 592 of October 13, 2022](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/6236070). The old-age retirement age in Uzbekistan is 60 for men with 25 years of work record and 55 for women with 20 years of work record, according to [materials on the unified portal of interactive public services](https://gov.uz/ru/advice/NaN/document/205).

Extending the countable childcare leave period from three to six years gives women who spent several years out of work caring for a child more room to accumulate the work record needed for a pension.

## What's next

The rule has been in force since February 2022 — the Ministry of Economy and Finance published the clarification retroactively on August 4 as a reminder for future pension recipients. No separate new resolution or decree taking effect at a later date was mentioned in the publicly available part of the ministry's post.
