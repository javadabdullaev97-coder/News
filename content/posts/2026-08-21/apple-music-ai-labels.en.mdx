Apple told labels and distributors on August 20 that by the end of 2026, Apple Music will begin showing listeners visible "Made With AI" labels on content created using artificial intelligence (AI). The labels will appear on tracks, cover art and video clips — according to a letter from the company cited by [9to5mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/20/apple-music-will-soon-get-visible-labels-for-ai-generated-content/).

The label will apply to content that is "predominantly created using a generative AI service." That is how Apple defines material subject to disclosure in the industry letter cited by the outlet. The system is still based on self-declaration: labels and distributors assign the tag themselves when submitting a release. Apple does not independently verify the origin of content.

## Apple introduced the technical tags back in March

Apple first announced the requirement to disclose AI use in early March 2026. The company added an `<ai_transparencies>` field to the [Apple Music metadata specification](https://help.apple.com/itc/musicspec/en.lproj/static.html) for content providers. The tag can be applied across four categories: album cover art, track (sound recording), composition (including song lyrics) and music video. The label is applied any time AI was used for a "substantial portion" of the relevant element.

For now, tagging remains voluntary: if a content provider does not specify the tag, Apple Music assumes AI was not used, [MacRumors reports](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/05/apple-music-ai-transparency-tags/), citing Apple's announcement to the industry. A specification update to version 5.3.26 in April 2026 extended the `<ai_transparencies>` tag to music videos released as standalone singles as well.

Until now, this metadata was not shown to listeners directly. With the rollout of visible "Made With AI" labels by the end of the year, users will see the mark for the first time. It will appear directly on the track or album page.
