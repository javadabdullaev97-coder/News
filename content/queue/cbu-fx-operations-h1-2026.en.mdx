The Central Bank of Uzbekistan on August 12 published an [analytical review, "Foreign Exchange Operations of Individuals,"](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/02b/n4p9nyn7idoap0d08g8rre91i096qec2/sharh.pdf) covering January–June 2026: banks bought $12.3 billion in foreign currency from the public and sold $6.8 billion.

## Turnover rose to $19.1 billion

Total turnover of currency exchange operations between banks and the public over the six months reached [$19.1 billion](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/02b/n4p9nyn7idoap0d08g8rre91i096qec2/sharh.pdf) — up 31%, or $4.5 billion, from January–June 2025.

Banks bought $12.3 billion from the public — up 35% year-on-year. They sold the public $6.8 billion — up 26%. The difference between the currency banks bought and sold (net supply from the public) rose to $5.5 billion, compared with $3.7 billion in the first half of 2025.

The share of this net supply in total currency exchange turnover rose from 26% in the first half of 2025 to 29% this year, the Central Bank said. The regulator interprets this as a growing role for the public in shaping currency supply on the domestic market.

By channel, operations broke down as follows: $11.2 billion (59% of turnover) went through currency exchange offices — up 33%. Remote (online) service accounted for $7.5 billion (39% of turnover) — up 27%. Currency ATMs operating around the clock processed $404 million — up 61%.

## Remittances: $9.3 billion in inflows

In January–June 2026, Uzbekistan received [$9.3 billion](https://cbu.uz/upload/iblock/02b/n4p9nyn7idoap0d08g8rre91i096qec2/sharh.pdf) in international remittances — up 13%, or $1.1 billion, from the same period of 2025. Outbound transfers abroad totaled $1.3 billion — up 8%.

Most of the growth in inflows came from P2P transfers made directly to individuals' bank cards from abroad: their volume rose 32% to $4.8 billion (51.7% of all inflows). Transfers through traditional money transfer systems grew just 0.3%, to $4.3 billion (46.7% of inflows). SWIFT transfer volume fell 43%, to $142 million.

Among countries where remittances to Uzbekistan grew fastest, the Central Bank highlighted the United Kingdom (+62%), European Union countries (+27%, including Ireland at +86%) and the United States (+19%).

## 1.4 million Uzbeks work abroad

Citing data from the Agency for External Labor Migration, the Central Bank presents the structure of labor emigration: about 1.4 million Uzbekistan citizens work in nearly 40 countries. Of these, 834,200 are in Russia, 84,400 in Kazakhstan, 72,100 in Turkey, 46,400 in South Korea, 258,000 in European countries, and 97,700 in other countries. Since the start of the year, 124,500 citizens have been placed in high-paying jobs abroad.

According to the Central Bank, women make up 28.6% of labor migrants, and young people 28.9%.

The review also provides global context: according to the World Bank, India remained the world's largest recipient of remittances in 2025, at $151 billion.
