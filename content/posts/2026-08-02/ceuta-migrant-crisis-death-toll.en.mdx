At least [67 people died](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died) in a mass crossing of migrants from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta on July 30–31, according to figures given by Spanish authorities as of August 1.

## What Happened at the Border

Estimates of how many people crossed vary: [between 50,000 and 60,000](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106918), with Spain's Interior Ministry citing [around 50,000](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died). People made their way from Morocco on foot and by swimming starting Thursday, July 30, and reached Spanish territory by sea, going around the border barriers. Some of the dead drowned; others died in a crush at the breakwater on Tarajal beach.

By Friday evening, July 31, 48,300 people had returned to Morocco. On Saturday, Spain installed a 500-meter floating barrier at the Tarajal breakwater.

Ceuta authorities linked the influx to a ruling by Spain's Supreme Court that bars the immediate return of people arriving by sea without a review process. Human rights advocates question whether migrants were aware of the ruling.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez visited Ceuta on Friday and called the breach a violation of the country's territorial sovereignty. He said talks with Moroccan authorities indicated that the Supreme Court ruling had been exploited by [human-trafficking gangs](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/spain-pm-to-visit-ceuta-after-19-migrants-die-breaching-border-from-morocco). Spain [sent](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/7/31/spain-deploys-military-to-ceuta-after-migrant-surge-what-we-know) military personnel and police reinforcements to the enclave, while Morocco deployed security forces and water cannon at the border.

## How the Death Toll Changed

The death toll changed three times over two days. On Friday, the head of Ceuta, Juan Jesús Vivas, reported [34 deaths](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/spain-pm-to-visit-ceuta-after-19-migrants-die-breaching-border-from-morocco); that evening, the Spanish government raised the figure to 57. By August 1, Spanish authorities [reported 67 deaths](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died).

The figure is not final: Spanish officials [acknowledge](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died) that there may also be victims on the Moroccan side of the border. No official update to the data had been found as of publication.

## What the EU Is Demanding

Twenty-two EU governments [demanded](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died) urgent measures. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called the footage from Ceuta unacceptable. France announced tighter controls at its border with Spain.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said on July 31 that [protecting the EU's external border](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/newsroom/2780810-2780810) was of paramount importance for the bloc in order to "keep illegal migration under control." He said he had been in contact with his Spanish counterpart José Manuel Albares, Moroccan Minister Nasser Bourita, and EU colleagues. Wadephul called for "swift and joint action" to protect the Schengen area and keep it open. Quotes are based on an official statement from the German Foreign Ministry, translated from German.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz [said](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/bundeskanzler-friedrich-merz-zur-lage-in-ceuta--2448818) the same day that he expected all EU states to fulfill their duty to protect external borders, and Morocco to immediately take back irregular migrants. Merz welcomed the support of the European Commission and Commissioner Magnus Brunner and demanded the immediate application of the return regulation adopted in June.

Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani announced a temporary suspension of the Schengen agreement with Spain and called it a ["necessary choice"](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106864) to protect the safety of Italian citizens. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni had earlier [named](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2026/7/31/spain-deploys-military-to-ceuta-after-migrant-surge-what-we-know) a Schengen suspension with Spain among possible measures.

Other capitals also criticized Madrid. Finnish Interior Minister Mari Rantanen [said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1106791) Spain had "completely failed" to protect the Schengen area's borders. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson called on Spain to quickly bring the situation under control, while Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen expressed hope that Spain would do everything possible to protect the EU's borders.

## In Ceuta Itself, a Far-Right Rally Was Called Off

Hundreds of Ceuta residents [turned out against](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/2/2-08-sv-ceuta-cancel-farright-protests-es) a planned far-right anti-migration rally on August 2. Organizers canceled the event. It has not been disclosed who was organizing the rally.

## What's Next

The EU's Justice and Home Affairs Council will [hold a videoconference](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/spain-says-migrants-leaving-ceuta-after-mass-influx-in-which-67-died) on Tuesday, August 4, chaired by Irish Minister for Justice, Home Affairs and Migration Jim O'Callaghan. The stated goal of the meeting is to prevent further uncontrolled border crossings.
