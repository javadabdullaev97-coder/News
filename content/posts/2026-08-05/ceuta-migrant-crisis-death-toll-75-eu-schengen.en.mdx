The death toll from the mass border crossing from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta rose to [75 people](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80n3j55g0do), according to figures cited by the Spanish government delegation as of August 4, four days after the crisis began.

## The toll keeps rising

According to Spanish authorities, as of August 4 at least 75 people had died — almost ten more than the [67 confirmed as of August 1](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/02/ceuta-migrant-crisis-death-toll) that LEAP News reported earlier. Moroccan authorities give their own count — [11 deaths](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80n3j55g0do) — and do not recognize Spain's counting methodology. The figure remains provisional: some bodies may still be unidentified, and the search for possible victims at sea continues.

Estimates of how many people crossed the border have also risen. On August 1, the figure stood at [50,000–60,000](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/02/ceuta-migrant-crisis-death-toll) people; by August 4, Spain's interior minister, at a meeting of the EU Council, [put the number at 72,000](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80n3j55g0do), of whom about 70,000 had already returned to Morocco.

## EU Council declines to back Schengen suspension

The EU's Justice and Home Affairs Council held a [videoconference](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80n3j55g0do) on August 4 chaired by Irish Justice Minister Jim O'Callaghan — Ireland holds the rotating presidency of the Council of the EU this half-year.

The proposal by Italy and Denmark to temporarily suspend Spain's participation in Schengen failed to win support at the meeting. Former EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy Josep Borrell had already [written](https://x.com/JosepBorrellF/status/2083118059309473999) on his X account on July 31 that under EU law no country can unilaterally suspend another's participation in the Schengen area.

The suspension itself, announced by Italy on July 31, remains in effect: Italy's Interior Ministry [formally approved](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/31/italy-suspends-schengen-with-spain-over-ceuta-migrant-crisis-shuts-air-and-sea-borders) it on the morning of August 1 and introduced selective document checks for passengers arriving from Spain by air and sea. Italian Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani called the measure "a necessary choice to protect citizens' security and defend Europe's borders."

## Not everyone signed the solidarity letter

A letter initiated by Italy and Denmark [calling](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/01/22-eu-leaders-call-for-emergency-talks-over-ceuta-migrant-crisis) for a coordinated response to "uncontrolled mass border crossings" and not ruling out temporary tightening of internal Schengen checks was backed by 22 of the EU's 27 member states. At least three countries — France, Portugal and Luxembourg — declined to sign it. They have not publicly disclosed their reasons.

## Morocco asks EU not to hinder its development

Moroccan authorities [accused](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/le-monde-africa/article/2026/08/04/ceuta-crisis-morocco-urges-european-union-not-to-hinder-its-development_6756162_124.html) Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez of failing to warn Rabat about the consequences of a ruling by Spain's Supreme Court that, according to Spanish authorities, triggered the mass border crossing. Morocco also disputes Spain's death toll figures and has asked the European Union not to create obstacles to the country's economic development amid the diplomatic crisis.

## What's next

Spanish authorities continue searching for possible victims along the coast and at sea near Ceuta — they [do not rule out](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80n3j55g0do) that the death toll could rise further.
