The Interagency Tariff Commission under the Cabinet of Ministers of Uzbekistan approved regulated tariffs between energy sector enterprises — for electricity and natural gas — on July 15, the Ministry of Economy and Finance reported on August 6.

## What Exactly the Commission Approved

The commission made the decision at its [37th meeting](https://t.me/minecofinuz/16202) on July 15, 2026. It concerns tariffs for the generation, transmission, distribution and supply of electricity, as well as the transportation and supply of natural gas — but only in settlements between energy sector enterprises themselves.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance's Telegram channel [explicitly states](https://t.me/minecofinuz/16202): "These tariffs apply only to settlements between energy sector enterprises. This decision does not change the current retail tariffs for electricity and natural gas for households, business entities and other end consumers." Retail prices for households and businesses are not affected by this decision.

## What RAB Is and Why It Is Being Introduced

The new system is [another stage](https://t.me/minecofinuz/16213) of the transition to the **RAB** (Regulatory Asset Base) methodology, which the government announced earlier as part of the energy sector reform program. RAB ties tariffs not only to a company's current expenses but also to the value of its regulated assets — networks, substations, gas pipelines and equipment needed to provide services.

The legal basis for the decision is [Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 243](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/-8195731) of May 15, 2026, "On Setting Prices for Fuel and Energy Resources," which regulates, among other things, the procedure for approving tariffs in the energy sector.

## Projected Scale of Changes in the Sector

According to the [Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/159309) as of April 29, 2026, at the initial stage of the transition to the RAB methodology, the cost of electricity generation may rise by about 9%, transmission by 30%, distribution by 10%, and natural gas distribution services by about 23%. This stage is planned to be completed no later than September 1, 2026. These are cost estimates for individual links in the energy chain, not direct tariffs for end consumers.

The portion of costs for the generation, transmission and distribution of electricity and natural gas that exceeds the tariffs set for end consumers will be covered from the state budget, the [Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://gov.uz/en/imv/news/view/159309) said.

## What's Next

The Ministry of Economy and Finance calls the July 15 decision "another stage" of the phased transition to RAB. This means further revisions of tariffs between energy enterprises are expected as the reform moves toward the September 1, 2026 deadline. The ministry has not yet published official data on whether the methodology will change after that date or how it will affect end tariffs in the future.
