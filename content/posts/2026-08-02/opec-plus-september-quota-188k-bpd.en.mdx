Seven OPEC+ countries — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — raised September quotas by 188,000 barrels per day on August 2, completing the return of the second phase of voluntary production cuts.

## What Was Decided

The meeting was held by videoconference. The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) [calls the increase](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/611-2-august-2026.html) part of the return of additional voluntary cuts announced in April 2023.

A country-by-country breakdown [was published by Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107034): Russia's quota will rise by 62,000 barrels per day to 9.949 million, and Kazakhstan's by 10,000 to 1.628 million. The alliance's total permitted output level, according to the same data, will be 36.206 million barrels per day.

The September increase [completes the return](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107034) of the second phase of voluntary cuts — 1.65 million barrels per day. The first phase, a cut of 2.2 million barrels per day, was returned to the market by eight group members in September 2025. Using the same 188,000-barrel-per-day step, the seven countries [raised August quotas](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/1835609-5-july-2026.html) on July 5.

## Why Seven Countries, Not Eight

Until May 2026, the group unwinding voluntary cuts consisted of eight countries. The United Arab Emirates, according to [Vedomosti](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/08/02/1218207-opek-planiruet-kvotu), left the organization in May over disagreement with the size of its quota. The UAE is also absent from [the monitoring committee's membership](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/612-2-august-2026.html) published by OPEC on August 2.

## Monitoring Committee on Sea Routes

The same day saw the 67th meeting of the Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee (JMMC). According to [OPEC](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/612-2-august-2026.html), its members are Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Nigeria, Algeria and Venezuela.

The committee [reviewed](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/612-2-august-2026.html) production data for May and June 2026 and found that the deal's participants — OPEC countries and those that joined the Declaration of Cooperation — are broadly complying with its terms. The committee did not recommend changing the agreement's parameters, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107039).

In a separate point, the committee noted the "exceptional importance of protecting international sea routes to ensure uninterrupted energy supplies" and [expressed concern](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107039) about attacks on energy infrastructure, saying that restoring damaged facilities to full capacity is costly and time-consuming. Actions that undermine the security of energy supply increase market volatility, the statement says. They also weaken joint efforts under the Declaration of Cooperation to maintain market stability in the interests of producers, consumers and the global economy.

The Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea are not named in the text of the statement. The next JMMC meeting is on October 4.

## Quotas Rise, Output Doesn't

Since March, OPEC+ member countries in the Persian Gulf [have been unable to raise output](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107022) to the level of their quotas because of Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. Russia is also producing less than its permitted volume.

IG (Sydney) analyst Fabien Yip [told Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/6/opec-countries-say-they-will-expand-monthly-oil-production) on July 6, after a similar decision on August quotas: "Real barrels have been constrained by the Strait of Hormuz blockade for months and are falling noticeably short of quota."

In the same article, Sparta Commodities (Singapore) oil market analyst Neil Crosby [called the quotas](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/6/opec-countries-say-they-will-expand-monthly-oil-production) "essentially meaningless" in the short term. He spoke separately about a more distant horizon: "Perhaps in the medium term, if and when the Hormuz issue is sustainably resolved, we can look more closely at what the group needs and wants to supply."

## What's Next

After the September increase, the alliance intends to pause further output growth until the end of the year, [Vedomosti reports](https://www.vedomosti.ru/business/news/2026/08/02/1218207-opek-planiruet-kvotu), citing Reuters sources. Cuts of roughly 2 million barrels per day, in place since 2022, remain in effect. The parties will discuss 2027 quotas based on a revised assessment of members' production capacities.

The seven countries will meet again on [September 6](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/611-2-august-2026.html). A full-format OPEC+ ministerial meeting [is scheduled for November 29](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1107022).
