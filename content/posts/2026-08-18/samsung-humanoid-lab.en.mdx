South Korean outlet Edaily [reported](https://edaily.co.kr/News/Read?mediaCodeNo=257&newsId=01708886645548632) on August 18 that Samsung Electronics has opened a physical AI lab. The industry uses that term for technologies that teach robots to move in real, rather than virtual, space. Trade outlet [DigiTimes](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260818VL210/samsung-robotics-robot-manufacturing-data.html) carried the same news; Samsung did not publish its own press release about the new lab.

The lab operates within Samsung's RX (Robotics eXperience) robotics division. It replaced the Future Robot AI group, which was previously part of the company's Advanced Robotics Center, Edaily [writes](https://edaily.co.kr/News/Read?mediaCodeNo=257&newsId=01708886645548632). It is led by senior researcher Koo Dong-han, who holds a PhD in mechanical engineering from KAIST. He previously worked in Hyundai Motor's robotics department and moved to Samsung Research in 2020.

According to the outlet, the team handles full control of the humanoid robot's body: walking, object manipulation, reinforcement learning and imitation learning. Specific tasks include executing whole-body movements from voice commands and adjusting the robot's posture to the weight of an object it is carrying. Engineers test some of the developments on humanoids made by Chinese company Unitree.

## The lab's place in Samsung's structure

Samsung Electronics [launched](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202607211603001) the RX division on July 21, 2026. It reports directly to CEO and head of the Device eXperience (DX) Division Roh Tae-moon and consolidated the company's scattered robotics development efforts. The main site is an R&D campus in Seoul's Umyeondong district, while the plant in Gumi is building a "data factory" to collect data on how robots operate. Samsung plans to open several more research centers in the United States, China and Japan. "Through quick decisions and swift execution, the company expects to accelerate the pace of technology development, respond promptly to market changes, and create distinctive experiences and new business areas in robotics," Samsung's press release [says](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202607211603001).

Samsung sources hardware for its humanoids through its subsidiary Rainbow Robotics. On December 31, 2024, Samsung Electronics raised its stake in the company to 35%, Money Today [reported](https://www.mt.co.kr/industry/2024/12/31/2024123109022789286) at the time. That is also when Samsung's Advanced Robotics Center was formed, led by KAIST professor emeritus Oh Jun-ho, one of Rainbow Robotics' co-founders.
