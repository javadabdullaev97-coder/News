The Tashkent Main Department of Ecology and Climate Change confirmed on August 4 that 45 trees were felled in the Djararyk area of Shaykhantahur district — rumors of 400 felled trees did not hold up.

## What the Inspection Found

According to the department, [as published on its Telegram channel](https://t.me/Ecopoytaxt/36001), 45 fruit and ornamental trees were illegally felled on a plot in the Djararyk area. Another 35 saplings were transplanted elsewhere without authorization. The plot, according to the department, was acquired in 2017, when it had no trees. Fruit and ornamental plantings were later planted on it at private expense and subsequently cut down.

The environmental damage was estimated at about 50 million soums (roughly $4,100 at the Central Bank exchange rate as of early August). An administrative violation report was filed against the offender under [Article 79, Part 1](https://t.me/Ecopoytaxt/36001), of the Code of Administrative Responsibility — "illegal felling, uprooting, damage, destruction or replanting of trees, shrubs, other plants and young growth." The offender was fined 10.3 million soums (about $860).

## Rumors of 400 Felled Trees Debunked

The department launched the inspection after social media posts claimed about 400 trees had been felled in the Djararyk area. It cross-checked the data with the [Tashkent Electronic Register of Green Plantations](https://green.tashkent.uz), which listed 112 trees on the territory. The final confirmed count of violations was 45 felled and 35 transplanted trees.
