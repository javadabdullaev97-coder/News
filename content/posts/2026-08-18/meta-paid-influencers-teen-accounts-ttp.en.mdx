The nonprofit Tech Transparency Project published a report on August 18, 2026: Meta systematically hires influencers and funds parent groups whenever authorities discuss banning social media for children.

One example is Australia. In July 2025, Meta invited a group of Australian parent influencers to an "Instagram Safety Camp" event, [the report says](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/inside-metas-global-playbook-for-fighting-child-safety-laws). The event took place five months before Australia's ban on social media for children under 16 — the first law of its kind in the world — took effect. The venue, overlooking Sydney Harbour, greeted participants with desserts and branded travel merchandise.

Ravi Sinha, head of Meta's child safety unit, told participants that blanket social media bans do not work. Parental controls on Instagram, he said, protect children more effectively. "What concerns us is that bans push teens toward places that are less safe," Sinha said, adding that Meta's approach "strikes the right balance."

Instagram Teen Accounts is a feature with default restrictions for users under 18, including content and communication controls. The company promotes it as an alternative to age-based social media bans.

## Similar campaigns in other countries

TTP found [similar campaigns](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/inside-metas-global-playbook-for-fighting-child-safety-laws) in New Zealand, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Brazil, the UAE and several European countries in 2025-2026. According to the organization, the pattern repeats: Meta holds closed-door events for influencers and promotes its position before regulatory debate intensifies in a country.

"From Asia to the Middle East and Europe, Meta has used Instagram influencers and paid parent groups," the report says. The company sought to present its child safety work favorably and influence the regulatory debate, the authors note.

## Meta's response

In a statement to the report's authors, Meta said it has held more than 40 events worldwide since 2024 to promote teen safety tools. The company works with influencers "because they are local voices trusted by parents in their communities," [Meta said in its response](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/inside-metas-global-playbook-for-fighting-child-safety-laws).

According to Meta data cited by TTP, the company removed about 750,000 Instagram and Facebook accounts belonging to Australians under 16. The removals took place in the first months after the local ban took effect.
