The World Health Organization said the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is accelerating: confirmed cases have neared 4,000, and WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus visited Kinshasa on August 5.

## Cases Near 4,000

According to the latest data from the DR Congo's Ministry of Health, cited on August 6 by [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8863562), 3,874 confirmed cases and 1,751 deaths have been recorded since the outbreak began. Between 60% and 80% of new cases are identified outside known transmission chains, which complicates contact tracing. Nearly 90% of all cases are still concentrated in Ituri province, and the armed conflict in the country's east continues to limit medical access to affected areas, the outlet [notes](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8863562). The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, for which no vaccine or specific treatment has yet been approved.

## Tedros: Outbreak Is Outpacing the Response

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus visited Kinshasa on August 5, Deutsche Welle [reported](https://www.dw.com/en/who-s-tedros-visits-dr-congo-as-ebola-outbreak-accelerates/a-78258054). "The outbreak is spreading faster than we are scaling up the response," Tedros said. He said the number of new cases had doubled in some hotspots over the past week.

"We need to urgently and massively scale up efforts across every area of the response," Tedros said, DW [reported](https://www.dw.com/en/who-s-tedros-visits-dr-congo-as-ebola-outbreak-accelerates/a-78258054). The current outbreak, the 17th in DR Congo's history, remains the fastest-growing and the second-largest on record, the outlet notes.

## Moderna Starts Trials of Bundibugyo Strain Vaccine

Moderna and the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI) began Phase 1 clinical trials in early August of a candidate vaccine against the Bundibugyo virus, the pathogen behind the current outbreak, Euronews [reported](http://ru.euronews.com/health/2026/08/05/moderna-has-started-human-trials-for-the-bundibugyo-ebola-vaccine-as-the-outbreak-in-congo). The trials are taking place in Canada, where about 80 volunteers have received their first dose. The vaccine is built on the same mRNA platform Moderna used for its COVID-19 vaccine; researchers are assessing its safety, tolerability and the immune response in participants.

"The fact that Moderna's vaccine candidate has moved into Phase 1 trials so quickly is an important step forward in fighting this deadly outbreak," CEPI CEO Richard Hatchett [said](http://ru.euronews.com/health/2026/08/05/moderna-has-started-human-trials-for-the-bundibugyo-ebola-vaccine-as-the-outbreak-in-congo). No approved vaccine or specific treatment against the Bundibugyo virus exists yet — Moderna's trial remains at the earliest, Phase 1 stage.

## What's Next

On August 4, LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-04/drc-ebola-outbreak-1700-deaths-who-trials) that the outbreak's death toll had passed 1,700 and that WHO was accelerating trials of candidate vaccines. Since then, the death toll has risen to 1,751 and confirmed cases have grown to nearly 3,900. Moderna's vaccine trial remains at Phase 1, and the armed conflict in the country's east continues to limit medical access to affected areas of Ituri province.
