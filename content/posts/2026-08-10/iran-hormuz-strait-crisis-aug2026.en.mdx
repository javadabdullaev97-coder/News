Iran set six conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz on August 9, while US President Donald Trump said Washington is negotiating at half strength and is counting on economic pressure rather than new strikes.

## Tehran's six conditions

Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, on August 9 named six conditions under which the strait could be fully reopened: an end to threats and "insults to Iran's national and religious values," a definitive end to attacks on Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq, withdrawal of US naval and air forces from the Iran region, compensation for damage from "two imposed wars," the lifting of sanctions and the unconditional unfreezing of Iranian assets — [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/iran-issues-new-demands-as-pezeshkian-seeks-deal).

Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said: "As soon as the US accepts Iran's conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will definitely be opened." The new demands go beyond the memorandum of understanding the two sides signed in June: Tehran for the first time directly included the situation in Lebanon, Yemen and Iraq in the list of conditions.

## Tankers keep coming under attack

The strait remains a risk zone for shipping. The UAE said on August 8 that Iran struck a tanker belonging to state-owned company ADNOC with a missile in the Strait of Hormuz; there were no casualties, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/8/uae-says-iran-targeted-adnoc-tanker-in-hormuz-no-casualties-2). According to ADNOC, 15 of the company's vessels have been attacked since the war began, three of them in the week before August 8; one crew member was killed and about 20 people were injured in that series of attacks.

## Oman route "in the final stage"

Iran and Oman are negotiating a temporary maritime corridor for vessels entering and leaving the Persian Gulf. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the deal "very close" to completion but stressed: "Reopening the strait also depends on other conditions, including compensation for US violations of the Islamabad agreement," [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-09/wait-for-hormuz-deal-stretches-on-as-iran-says-terms-must-be-met). The reference is to the memorandum signed in June.

In parallel, Iranian lawmakers are discussing a bill that would bar US and Israeli vessels from passing through the strait while setting a transit fee for other countries; the bill is tied to the deal with Oman and [is being discussed against the backdrop of negotiations over the strait's status](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/iran-seeks-bar-on-us-ships-in-hormuz-as-deal-with-oman-advances). Any final agreement requires approval from Iran's supreme leadership — public consent has not yet been given.

Brent crude traded above $83 a barrel, with traders weighing the chances of a swift recovery in shipping through the Persian Gulf, Bloomberg reported.

## Trump opts for economic pressure

In an interview with Axios cited by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/9/iran-war-live-tehran-demands-end-to-us-blockade-to-reopen-strait-of-hormuz), Trump said: "We're only half-negotiating with them. We're just watching Iran go through massive inflation, and they just don't have the money." The US president described Washington's current approach to dialogue with Tehran as "throttling back."

The bet on economically exhausting Iran instead of launching new strikes is not new: back in May, Trump had already shifted priorities — first securing the reopening of the strait, then postponing negotiations on Iran's nuclear and missile programs for later, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-08/trump-punts-thorniest-iran-challenges-in-push-to-reopen-hormuz).

For his part, Araghchi said no direct talks are being held with the US — only an exchange of messages through intermediaries, with "efforts underway to create conditions for negotiations."

## Conflict status: "Phase II," day 29

The US and Israel launched a military operation against Iran on February 28, 2026, [Al Jazeera notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/why-is-pezeshkian-urging-an-end-to-irans-no-war-no-peace-status). In June, the two sides signed a memorandum of understanding meant to halt fighting and reopen the strait. The agreement did not hold for even a month: on July 8, Iran attacked three vessels in the strait, the US struck back at more than 80 Iranian targets, and Iran struck facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait. After that, at the NATO summit in Ankara, Trump said the ceasefire was "over," [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/8/trump-says-ceasefire-over-after-us-iran-trade-attacks). Al Jazeera labels the current stage of the war "Phase II" and keeps a separate day count — it marks August 9 as day 29.

According to Al Jazeera, the death toll since the war began has surpassed 3,400 (estimate as of August 9). The Strait of Hormuz has remained under a de facto Iranian blockade since the conflict began: it normally carries about one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil supplies.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian on August 9 called for ending the "no war, no peace" state with the US: "With God's help, we will reach a point where we can get out of this no-war, no-peace state," he said, calling the current moment the "best time for a deal" amid Iran's "unity and strength."
