The Supreme Court of Uzbekistan on August 5 [published](https://t.me/oliysuduz/51349) on its Telegram channel an analysis of a law that for the first time bars early release for those convicted of bribery — the law has been in effect since June 23, 2026.

The analysis was prepared by Sherzod Tukhtayev, a civil court judge at the Kashkadarya Regional Court, and by Khusan Tursunov and Nodir Khodjakulov, economic court judges at the same court. President Shavkat Mirziyoyev [signed](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026) the law itself on June 22 — Law No. O'RQ-1155, "On Amendments and Additions to Certain Legislative Acts of the Republic of Uzbekistan Aimed at Improving the Effectiveness of the Fight Against Corruption and Ensuring the Inevitability of Liability for Corruption Offenses." It amends three codes — the Criminal Code, the Criminal Procedure Code and the Code of Administrative Liability — and two laws: on state youth policy and the 2017 law on combating corruption.

## Ban on early release

The key innovation is a new legal concept — "corruption offenses" — introduced into the Criminal Code with a closed list of crimes: bribery, abuse of power or official authority, mediation in bribery and related grave articles of the Criminal Code, according to [the text of the law](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026).

For those convicted under this list, the law rules out parole and the replacement of a sentence with a milder form of punishment — previously such an option was available on general grounds, as for most other crimes. This is the "inevitability of liability" referenced in the law's title: those convicted must now serve the full term set by the court.

## Electronic registry and compliance units

The law requires the maintenance of an electronic registry of individuals convicted of corruption offenses — a corresponding provision has been added to the law on combating corruption. In addition, the law requires state bodies, organizations and enterprises with state participation to set up compliance units — internal control divisions tasked with identifying corruption risks and monitoring compliance with anti-corruption procedures. The law assigns coordination of their work to the level of state bodies and establishes guarantees of independence and protection from pressure for compliance staff.

## Risk maps and new powers for the Anti-Corruption Agency

The law expands the powers of the Anti-Corruption Agency: its new functions include [drawing up and updating corruption risk maps](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026) by region and industry, creating national and regional anti-corruption councils, systematically analyzing the corruption situation, and developing social partnership programs to build public intolerance toward corruption.

## What else the law changes

A separate provision of the Criminal Procedure Code establishes security measures for people who report corruption offenses. The law also introduces a fine into the Code of Administrative Liability — up to 30 base calculation units — for exceeding the permissible gap between the starting and the actual market price in public procurement. The law on state youth policy gains a new focus area: building public intolerance toward corruption.
