US President Donald Trump signed an executive order on August 6 imposing a 15% tariff on polysilicon and minimum import prices on products made from it — a move against China's dominance of the market.

## What was signed

The order takes the form of a proclamation under Section 232 of the US Trade Expansion Act of 1962, a provision that allows trade restrictions to be imposed on national security grounds. The document [sets a 15% tariff on polysilicon and products made from it, along with a minimum import price program](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/trade-war/trump-tariffs/us-imposes-15-tariff-on-polysilicon-in-pushback-against-china) for silicon wafers and solar cells.

According to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/us-considers-delaying-collection-of-planned-polysilicon-tariffs), the tariff and minimum prices are set to take effect on December 4 — 120 days after the order was signed. Some US solar panel manufacturers had pushed for a shorter, [90-day window](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/us-considers-delaying-collection-of-planned-polysilicon-tariffs), fearing that Chinese suppliers would use the four months to stockpile shipments to the US in advance. The administration chose the longer timeline.

US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has said the measure is meant to stop dumping and boost US production.

## How the story evolved

[As LEAP reported earlier](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/06/us-china-polysilicon-tariffs-xi-visit), Bloomberg said on August 5 that the Trump administration was preparing a tariff of at least 15% and minimum prices on polysilicon, with a decision possible as early as August 6 — ahead of Chinese President Xi Jinping's expected visit to Washington. At the time, the parameters of the order were still being finalized, and analysts at Roth Capital Partners suggested that, factoring in the minimum prices, the actual burden on importers could reach 25–35%. The document signed on August 6 confirmed the 15% base rate and the minimum-price mechanism but, according to Bloomberg, with the effective date pushed back 120 days.

## Dependence on China

The tariff follows an earlier Section 232 investigation into polysilicon, in which the US Department of Commerce examined whether imports of the material posed a threat to national security. Polysilicon is the raw material used to make solar panels and semiconductor chips. According to [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/economy/trade-war/trump-tariffs/us-imposes-15-tariff-on-polysilicon-in-pushback-against-china), Chinese companies account for 96% of global polysilicon production.

## What's next

Until December 4, the tariff and minimum prices will not be collected or applied — importers have a four-month window. There has been no official reaction from Beijing to the signed order as of publication.
