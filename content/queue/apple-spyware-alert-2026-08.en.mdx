Apple on Friday, August 14, sent a new wave of threat notifications — warnings about possible surveillance by commercial spyware — to users in 110 countries, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/unprecedented-number-of-apple-users-received-recent-spyware-alert-say-investigators/) reported, citing cybersecurity researchers.

Citizen Lab researcher John Scott-Railton told the outlet that "the scale and geographic diversity of public reports of received notifications are unprecedented." According to him, for every public report there is "a huge iceberg of notifications the public will never learn about." He added that overall, what's happening is "a clear sign that something bigger is going on." Apple did not respond to TechCrunch's request for comment.

Mohammed Al-Maskati of the human rights organization Access Now told TechCrunch that after the notifications went out, the number of inquiries to the organization rose by 30-40%. That is higher than the usual level Access Now recorded after previous waves of Apple notifications. According to him, the new way of delivering the notifications "helped raise awareness of the issue and made it harder for users to ignore it." Cybersecurity firm iVerify told TechCrunch it had seen an increase in the number of Apple notifications tracked by its service.

Starting this year, Apple displays the warning not only in the email tied to an Apple ID. It also appears on the iPhone lock screen, in the Settings app, and when signing into an Apple account through a web browser, TechCrunch reported. Previously, notifications arrived only by email. Over the past several years, according to Apple's own data, the company has warned users in more than 150 countries.

## How Apple Defines Threat Notifications

On its [support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102174), Apple describes threat notifications as "high-confidence warnings that a user has been the target of a commercial spyware attack." Such notifications, according to the company, should be treated very seriously. The company states that such attacks cost millions of dollars, are used against a small number of people — journalists, activists, politicians and diplomats — and occur worldwide. As a historical example, Apple cites Pegasus, developed by Israeli company NSO Group. Materials about the August 14 wave of notifications do not name a specific spyware vendor.

## Who Spoke About Their Notification

Users in various countries wrote publicly about receiving the warning, including a Ukrainian Armed Forces service member fighting against Russia, TechCrunch reported. According to him, he knows other service members who received the same notification. The outlet does not give his name.

The assessment of an "unprecedented" scale came from researchers interviewed by TechCrunch, not from Apple itself. The company does not publish the exact number of people who received the warning on August 14 and did not respond to the outlet's request.
