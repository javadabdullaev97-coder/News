Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on August 9, 2026, rejected the "15-point plan" at a cabinet meeting — the Trump administration's proposed scheme for disarming Hamas and withdrawing Israeli troops from the Gaza Strip.

## "Real" Disarmament Versus "Fictitious"

"Israel rejects the 15-point document," [Netanyahu said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-for-gaza) at the cabinet meeting. According to him, the Israel Defense Forces "will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas disarms for real."

The prime minister clarified the demand: ["when I say Hamas must disarm, I mean heavy weapons, light weapons, all weapons. This is about real disarmament, not fictitious disarmament,"](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-gaza-pm-benjamin-netanyahu-says-rcna591555) he said. Netanyahu also repeated his longstanding position on Palestinian statehood: ["as long as I am prime minister, there will be no Palestinian state"](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-gaza-pm-benjamin-netanyahu-says-rcna591555) — neither in Gaza nor in the West Bank, NBC News and [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8875810) report.

## What Trump's Plan Proposed

The "15-point plan" is an initiative of the Board of Peace, founded by Trump in January 2026, which Hamas [approved in July](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-for-gaza). According to [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8875810), the document envisioned a phased handover of weapons by Hamas, the transfer of maps of tunnels under Gaza, and a transition of power in the strip to a technocratic government. The plan also envisioned a possible amnesty for the group's leadership and a pullback of the Israeli army to the positions it held before the ceasefire took effect in October 2025. Under the plan, the withdrawal of Israeli troops was to proceed in parallel with Hamas's disarmament, not after its completion — it is precisely this sequence that Netanyahu rejected.

This is not the prime minister's first public rejection of the plan: on August 5, he already [said in a video address](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/netanyahu-gaza-peace-plan-rejection) that troops would not withdraw until Hamas fully disarmed. The August 9 cabinet meeting and the phrase "15-point document" mark a new, more formal round of the same dispute.

Implementation of the plan was already underway: on August 6, the Board of Peace signed its first contract to build a military base for international stabilization forces in Gaza. Uganda's parliament approved sending its troops there, [LEAP News reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/gaza-peace-council-military-base-uganda-aug2026). Under the plan, the deployment of these forces depended on the phased withdrawal of Israeli troops and Hamas's disarmament.

## Hamas and White House Reaction

Hamas politburo member Bassem Naim said the group remains committed to the agreed scheme and [said he expected](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/benjamin-netanyahu-us-gaza-peace-plan-reject/) mediators and the United States to pressure Netanyahu to stick to the plan. Hamas had previously conditioned the handover of heavy weapons on a full withdrawal of Israeli troops, an end to hostilities, and the establishment of a Palestinian state, [NBC News notes](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-gaza-pm-benjamin-netanyahu-says-rcna591555).

The White House [did not respond](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-gaza-pm-benjamin-netanyahu-says-rcna591555) to a request for comment on Netanyahu's statement.

## Situation in Gaza

According to Gaza health authorities cited by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/israel/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-gaza-pm-benjamin-netanyahu-says-rcna591555) and [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/israel-rejects-trumps-15-point-plan-for-gaza), more than 1,200 people have died in Gaza since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025; the total death toll since October 7, 2023, has exceeded 73,000 (as of August 9). Exact casualty figures on both sides of the conflict cannot be independently verified.
