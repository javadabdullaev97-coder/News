The Cabinet of Ministers of Uzbekistan on July 29 approved a list of 49 foreign countries and territories that provide personal data protection at a level equivalent to Uzbekistan's — [Resolution No. 415](https://lex.uz/docs/8369688) took effect on August 3.

## How Data Transfers Abroad Now Work

The resolution is based on Article 27-1 of the Law "On Personal Data," which divides cross-border data transfers into two regimes. For countries on the approved list, personal and anonymized data are transferred automatically through information systems built on international agreements. This happens [without additional permits and without notifying the authorized state body](https://lex.uz/docs/8369688) — provided the operator has taken measures against data leaks.

For countries not on the list, data transfers of any kind are permitted only under one condition. The operator and owner of the personal data base must meet the legal, organizational and technical requirements set by the authorized body.

The resolution separately introduces an obligation to report leaks during cross-border data transfers. If a leak occurs, the database operator must [notify the authorized body within 24 hours](https://lex.uz/docs/8369688) of discovering it. Within the following 72 hours, the operator must provide detailed information about the causes of the leak and the measures taken to address it.

The Department of Migration and Personalization under the Ministry of Internal Affairs has been designated as the authorized body that maintains the list and decides on adding or removing countries. It must publish the list on its official website within a week.

## Which Countries and Territories Made the List

The list includes all European Union countries, the United Kingdom, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Andorra. It also includes Russia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Israel, Canada, New Zealand, Uruguay, Argentina and Brazil. Several territories are listed separately — Hong Kong as a special administrative region of China, as well as Guernsey, Jersey, the Isle of Man and the Faroe Islands.

The United States is included on the list with a caveat: the country is recognized as providing adequate protection [only for companies operating under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework](https://lex.uz/docs/8369688). Mainland China, Turkey, the UAE, Kazakhstan and other Central Asian countries are not on the list.

## What Comes Next

The resolution requires the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Uzbekistan, together with the authorized body, to prepare a proposal within three months on joining the Council of Europe Convention. This refers to the Convention for the Protection of Individuals with Regard to Automatic Processing of Personal Data, signed in Strasbourg in 1981. The same deadline applies to preparing a proposal for the Cabinet of Ministers on getting Uzbekistan included on the lists of countries that other states themselves recognize as providing adequate data protection.

Within the same three-month period, the Ministry of Digital Technologies, the State Security Service and the National Agency for Prospective Projects, together with the Ministry of Internal Affairs, have been instructed to approve standard contractual terms for data transfers to countries not on the list. The agencies must also set requirements for binding corporate rules governing such transfers. Ministries and agencies have been given two months to bring their regulations in line with the resolution. Compliance will be overseen by Interior Minister Abdusalom Tashpulatov.

The option to transfer data to countries recognized as reliable without a permit appeared after March amendments to the Law "On Personal Data." [Law No. ZRU-1125 of March 26, 2026](https://lex.uz/docs/8104580) introduced mandatory storage in Uzbekistan of biometric and genetic data, as well as telecommunications user data. At the same time, the law permitted cross-border transfers of other data categories, subject to established conditions.
