The National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan said on July 31 that gross domestic product (GDP) in January-June 2026 totaled 1,072.7 trillion soums and grew 8.5% in real terms.

## What Drove the Growth

Gross value added across sectors contributed 8.1 percentage points of growth, and net taxes on products added another 0.4 percentage points, [according to the release](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69576-zbekiston-yalpi-ichki-ma-suloti-azhmi-8-5-foizga-sdi-3) from the National Statistics Committee. The data are preliminary.

At the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan's exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-07-31/) (the Central Bank) for July 31 — 12,006.39 soums per dollar — six-month GDP is equivalent to approximately $89.3 billion.

## Services Grow Faster Than Industry

Separate releases from the National Statistics Committee show output volumes by sector. These figures do not add up to GDP: it is calculated based on gross value added, not gross output.

- Market services — [664.9 trillion soums](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69510-zbekistonda-bozor-khizmatlari-azhmi-664-9-trln-s-mga-etdi-3) (≈$55.4 billion), up 16.9%.
- Industrial output — [637.2 trillion soums](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69506-sanoat-ma-sulotlari-ishlab-chi-arish-azhmi-637-trln-s-mdan-oshdi-3) (≈$53.1 billion), up 8%.
- Agriculture, forestry and fisheries — [about 271 trillion soums](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69536-ishlo-rmon-va-bali-chilik-kh-zhaligi-4-7-foizga-sdi-3) (≈$22.6 billion), up 4.7%.
- Construction work — [192 trillion soums](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69429-zbekistonda-yarim-jilda-192-trln-s-mlik-urilish-ishlari-bazharildi-3) (≈$16 billion), up 13.8%.

Within market services, the largest categories are trade (170 trillion soums, ≈$14.2 billion) and accommodation and food services (115.2 trillion soums, ≈$9.6 billion). Financial and transport services follow, then communications and information services.

Industrial output per capita amounted to 16.6 million soums. As of July 1, 2026, 63,300 industrial enterprises were operating in the country. In agriculture, crop and livestock production accounted for the bulk of output — 254.7 trillion soums (≈$21.2 billion); the rest came from forestry and fish farming.

## Full-Year Forecasts Below the First-Half Pace

The Central Bank's board on July 29 [kept](https://cbu.uz/ru/monetary-policy/publications/press-releases/4256870/) the key rate at 14% per annum and cited the same first-half growth figure — 8.5% in real terms. The regulator's full-year forecast is around 7.5-8%.

Inflation in June stood at 6.4% year-on-year, with core inflation at 5.7%. The Central Bank's forecast for the end of 2026 remained at 6.5%. Household inflation expectations in June stood at 10.1%, and business expectations at 9.9%. In the same release, the regulator noted growth in retail trade and services and high investment activity.

The Executive Board of the International Monetary Fund (IMF) on June 12 [concluded](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/06/18/pr-26214-uzbekistan-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation) its Article IV consultation and projected Uzbekistan's GDP growth at 6.8% for 2026 and 6% for 2027. For 2025, the National Statistics Committee [estimated](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/66555-2025-jilda-zbekiston-yalpi-ichki-ma-suloti-azhmi-7-7-foizga-sdi-3) economic growth at 7.7%.

## What's Next

The GDP estimate for January-June remains preliminary. The Central Bank board's next meeting on the key rate is scheduled for September 16.
