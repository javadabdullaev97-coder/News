Alibaba Cloud, the cloud unit of China's Alibaba Group, increased revenue 45% year over year to $7.14 billion for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, according to a [filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm) submitted to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on August 20.

Revenue from artificial intelligence-related products has grown at triple-digit rates year over year for a twelfth consecutive quarter, the [company said](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-2026456290057781248) in its earnings release.

Alibaba's capital expenditure on AI infrastructure rose 75% year over year to 67.7 billion yuan (about $9.97 billion). The company attributed the increase to fluctuations in equipment procurement cycles, expanded computing capacity and higher chip prices.

The cloud segment's adjusted operating margin (adjusted EBITA) rose year over year. Segment profit increased 133% to 5.63 billion yuan (about $830 million).

## Growing share of in-house chips

Alibaba merged its Cloud Intelligence Group cloud unit with T-Head, its in-house chip division, into a single segment called AI Cloud and Compute Services. T-Head designs processors, accelerators and networking chips for the company's data centers.

According to the filing, more than 650 external customers across more than 20 industries use chips from the Zhenwu line, including the latest M890 model. By comparison, more than 120,000 customers use Amazon Web Services' own Graviton processors, [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/08/21/alibaba-cloud-plans-to-use-fewer-western-chips-to-boost-its-already-huge-ai-margins/5290815) noted.

The Register links Alibaba Cloud's accelerated margin growth to expanded use of in-house chips instead of pricier foreign alternatives. Alibaba itself does not name reducing purchases of Western chips as an official goal in its filing — the company only provides figures on the number of customers that have switched to T-Head processors.
