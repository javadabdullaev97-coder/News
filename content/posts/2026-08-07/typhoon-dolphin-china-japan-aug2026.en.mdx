Typhoon Dolphin halted operations at the ports of Zhoushan, Ningbo, and Changjiangkou in Zhejiang province near Shanghai on August 6-7 and forced the cancellation of more than 500 flights in Japan, Bloomberg [reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/key-chinese-ports-near-shanghai-halt-operations-ahead-of-typhoon-dolphin). The cyclone is expected to make landfall on China's east coast overnight into Monday, August 10.

## China: ports near Shanghai closed, bunkering halted

The ports of Zhoushan, Ningbo, and Changjiangkou — part of the port complex in Hangzhou Bay near Shanghai — have temporarily suspended operations, Bloomberg [writes](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/key-chinese-ports-near-shanghai-halt-operations-ahead-of-typhoon-dolphin). Ships are permitted to leave the ports but not to enter them.

Zhoushan — China's largest ship-refueling hub — has suspended bunkering at both offshore and inshore anchorages since the start of the week; fuel suppliers have not yet said when operations will fully resume.

In Zhejiang province, 162 passenger ferry routes have been suspended, and 474 construction vessels have been moved to safe anchorages.

## Japan: over 500 flights canceled, 260,000 evacuated

Japan canceled more than 500 flights on August 7, and authorities ordered evacuations for nearly 260,000 residents of Kagoshima and Okinawa prefectures, Reuters [reports](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/07/japan-orders-evacuations-as-typhoon-dolphin-approaches-cancels-500-flights). Japan's Meteorological Agency (JMA) recorded sustained winds of up to 144 km/h with gusts up to 198 km/h in the typhoon — by Reuters' classification, equivalent to a Category 1 hurricane.

Naha Airport in Okinawa closed entirely on August 7: all domestic and international flights were canceled, AP [reports](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/airport-closes-okinawa-typhoon-dolphin-hits-parts-southern-japan-rcna591312). By Friday afternoon, 240 people were in evacuation centers in the prefecture. Toyota suspended operations at nine plants.

An additional risk stems from the overlap with the aftermath of a magnitude-7.1 earthquake that struck Kumamoto prefecture a week earlier: about 6,700 residents are still living in evacuation centers after their homes collapsed, and temperatures of up to 36°C are forecast there as the typhoon approaches, Reuters [notes](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/08/07/japan-orders-evacuations-as-typhoon-dolphin-approaches-cancels-500-flights).

## Track: 820 km offshore, landfall expected this weekend

As of Friday morning, August 7, the typhoon's center was located about 820 km east of Wenzhou in Zhejiang province, Bloomberg [reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/typhoon-dolphin-heads-for-china-bringing-heavy-rain-risk-to-east-coast). Sustained winds near the cyclone's center reached 45 m/s (162 km/h), with a minimum pressure of 950 hectopascals. China's National Meteorological Center maintains a yellow alert — the third of four levels on China's scale. The agency forecasts the typhoon will make landfall between southern Zhejiang and northern Fujian from Sunday, August 9, through early Monday morning, August 10. By the time it makes landfall, sustained winds will have weakened to 35-42 m/s (126-151 km/h).

Dolphin became the 13th named tropical cyclone in the western Pacific this season.

## What's next

Zhejiang and Fujian provinces, as well as the city of Shanghai, have already switched to emergency typhoon-response mode. Chinese meteorologists expect strong winds and heavy rain in these regions closer to Sunday, as the cyclone approaches the coastline. In Japan, the typhoon will continue moving toward Kyushu and Okinawa, keeping the risk of structural damage and flooding through the weekend.
