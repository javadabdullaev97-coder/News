The death toll from the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has surpassed 1,800 as of August 4, according to the World Health Organization (WHO) and Africa CDC, as a health workers' strike and a boat quarantine compound the epidemic.

## Health Workers in Ituri Strike Over Unpaid Wages

Dozens of health workers protested outside the governor's office in Bunia, the capital of Ituri province — which accounts for about 90% of the outbreak's cases — [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/dr-congo-ebola-health-workers-protest-unpaid-wages-as-deaths-surpass-1800). The protesters said they had not received salaries or allowances since the outbreak was declared in mid-May. "With this kind of management, Ebola will never end in this province," said one protester, Eduige Makosi. DR Congo authorities attributed the payment delays to logistical problems and did not comment further on the protesters' complaints.

According to WHO and Africa CDC data as of August 4, the outbreak has produced 3,973 confirmed cases, 1,801 deaths and 776 recoveries since it began, with 674 people currently under observation, [the organizations said](https://www.who.int/news/item/06-08-2026-africa-cdc-and-who-call-for-urgent--community-led-action-to-contain-ebola-in-the-drc) in a joint statement. The outbreak has spread across 51 health zones in five of the country's provinces. Only 75% of case contacts are being monitored daily by health workers — against a 95% target — while isolation center capacity in North Kivu has reached 139% of normal levels.

## Africa CDC Does Not Rule Out That the Virus Has Changed

The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, for which no vaccine or specific treatment has yet been approved. Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya said the agency plans research to determine whether the virus has changed, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/06/ebola-virus-drc-mutating). Kaseya said the concerns stem from the disease's unusually severe course for the Bundibugyo strain, as well as the fact that more than two-thirds of deaths occur outside health facilities — among patients health workers failed to identify and hospitalize in time. About 90% of patients admitted to one Doctors Without Borders treatment center had no documented contact with already known cases. There is no official laboratory confirmation of a viral mutation yet — Africa CDC and WHO are describing plans for research, not an established fact.

## Boat Quarantined on the Congo River After Suspected Ebola Case Aboard

DR Congo authorities intercepted a river boat that had been traveling for about three weeks down the Congo River from Tshopo province — in the Maluku area, near the port of Bende-Bende, roughly 65 kilometers from Kinshasa — and quarantined the passengers on board. One passenger disembarked in the town of Pimu in Mongala province and died with symptoms consistent with Ebola; according to secondary sources, this occurred on July 25. The case is currently classified as suspected, not confirmed. According to the BBC, five people died on and around the boat — the passenger who disembarked and four others who died on board: two adults, a child and a newborn. Other news agencies reported a single confirmed death among nearly 200 passengers under observation. DR Congo Health Minister Roger Kamba said authorities are "proceeding cautiously" with the case's classification; reports say a sanitary cordon has been set up around the boat, and a mobile laboratory is testing all passengers and crew members. There are no confirmed cases in Kinshasa so far.

## International Aid Is Declining

Humanitarian aid to the region continues to decline as the outbreak accelerates, [Le Monde reported](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/le-monde-africa/article/2026/08/06/as-ebola-spreads-in-drc-declining-international-aid-exposes-the-vulnerable_6756213_124.html): the United States, previously the largest donor for outbreak response in Central Africa, has sharply cut funding following decisions by President Donald Trump's administration since early 2025. In their joint statement of August 6, WHO and Africa CDC specifically called on donors to ensure that already-pledged funding reaches operational teams without delay, alongside expanding laboratory and treatment infrastructure and paying health workers on the ground on time.

## WHO Director-General's Visit

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus visited Uganda and DR Congo on August 4-5 as part of a joint mission with Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya and WHO Regional Director for Africa Mohamed Janabi, [Deutsche Welle reported](https://www.dw.com/en/who-s-tedros-visits-dr-congo-as-ebola-outbreak-accelerates/a-78258054). "In some parts of eastern DR Congo, the Ebola outbreak is outpacing our response efforts, so we urgently need to scale up work across the board," Tedros said. The delegation also studied Uganda's experience, where an outbreak — 20 cases and 2 deaths — ended on July 28 and is being cited as an example of successful early containment.

On August 6, LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-06/drc-ebola-outbreak-tedros-visit-moderna-trials) on the outbreak's acceleration and the start of trials for Moderna's experimental vaccine against the Bundibugyo strain. Since then, complicating factors have grown to include the health workers' strike, the boat quarantine on the Congo River, and the question of a possible viral change — which has yet to be confirmed in the lab.
