The UNESCO World Heritage Committee on July 26, 2026, added ten Tashkent modernist buildings constructed between the 1960s and 1990 to the World Heritage List.

## What Was Added to the List

The decision was made at the [48th session of the UNESCO World Heritage Committee](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1766/) in Busan, South Korea. The list now includes the serial nomination "Tashkent Modernist Architecture: Modernity and Traditions of Central Asia," which [brings together ten](https://acdf.uz/ru/news/tashkent-modernist-architecture-inscribed-on-the-unesco-world-heritage-list) landmark sites.

The nomination includes:

- the "Zhemchug" (Pearl) residential building;
- the State Museum of History of Uzbekistan;
- the State Museum of Arts of Uzbekistan;
- the "Friendship of Peoples" Palace;
- the Tashkent State Circus;
- the Central Exhibition Hall of the Academy of Arts of Uzbekistan;
- the Palace of Cinema Art of Uzbekistan;
- the Eski Juva market (Chorsu);
- the "Cosmonauts" metro station;
- the "Sun" Heliocomplex.

The full list was [published](https://acdf.uz/ru/news/tashkent-modernism-inscription-on-the-unesco-world-heritage-list-crowns-years-of-dedicated-work) by the Art and Culture Development Foundation of Uzbekistan (ACDF), the organization that prepared the nomination dossier. According to ACDF, the Committee's decision [was based on an assessment](https://acdf.uz/ru/news/tashkent-modernist-architecture-inscribed-on-the-unesco-world-heritage-list) of the site's authenticity and integrity, which meets the criteria of outstanding universal value.

## Context: The City After the Earthquake

Most of the listed buildings were constructed after the devastating earthquake of April 26, 1966, when the center of Tashkent was rebuilt from scratch.

Uzbekistan [became](https://acdf.uz/ru/news/tashkent-modernist-architecture-inscribed-on-the-unesco-world-heritage-list) the first country in Central Asia and the CIS whose 20th-century architecture was added to the UNESCO World Heritage List. According to ACDF, this is the first national nomination from Uzbekistan to receive this status in the past 25 years.

Before this decision, five sites in Uzbekistan were already on the World Heritage List: the historic center of Khiva (Itchan Kala), the historic center of Bukhara, the historic center of Shakhrisabz, Samarkand — Crossroad of Cultures, and the transboundary natural site "Western Tien-Shan." With the addition of [Tashkent modernism](https://acdf.uz/ru/news/tashkent-modernist-architecture-inscribed-on-the-unesco-world-heritage-list), their number has grown to six.

## What's Next

According to ACDF, the foundation will join the expert council managing the "Tashkent Modernist Architecture" site — the council will oversee the preservation of the buildings, monitor their condition, and ensure that future urban development does not undermine the significance of the modernist landmarks.
