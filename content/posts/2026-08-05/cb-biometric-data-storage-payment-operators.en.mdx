The Central Bank of Uzbekistan on August 1, 2026, required operators of payment systems, payment service providers, electronic factoring platforms and credit bureaus to store customers' biometric data only within the country.

## What the Resolution Introduces

The requirement was introduced by a resolution of the Central Bank Board, [registered by the Ministry of Justice on August 1, 2026, under number 3914](https://t.me/centralbankuzbekistan/13400). According to the Central Bank's publication on its Telegram channel, biometric data of individuals used by payment operators and other supervised organizations for customer identification and authentication must be stored exclusively within Uzbekistan.

Non-biometric personal data of customers, the Central Bank clarifies, can still be stored and processed outside the country — but only if the conditions of Part 3 of Article 27¹ of the Law "On Personal Data" are met. The resolution took effect on the day of its official publication; the Central Bank's publication does not specify a separate transition period for bringing data storage systems into compliance.

Liability for violating the new requirements and the procedure for monitoring compliance are not detailed in the text available on the Central Bank's Telegram channel.

## Why This Requirement Was Singled Out

Mandatory storage of biometric data within Uzbekistan is not a new rule as such, but its targeted extension to the payment sector. The general localization rule is contained in Article 27¹ of the Law ["On Personal Data"](https://lex.uz/docs/-4396419): previously, the general localization requirement covered personal data more broadly than it does now. Under a law dated March 26, 2026 (ZRU-1125), according to lex.uz, this requirement was eased, narrowing mandatory localization to specific categories of data — in particular biometric data, genetic data and data of telecommunications operators' subscribers.

The Central Bank's resolution transfers this rule to a specific list of organizations under its supervision — payment operators, payment service providers, factoring platforms and credit bureaus — establishing for them a direct obligation to store precisely customers' biometric data within the country.
