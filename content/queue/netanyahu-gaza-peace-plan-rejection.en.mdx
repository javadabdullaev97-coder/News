Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on August 5 rejected the Gaza settlement plan proposed by US President Donald Trump and said Israeli troops would not withdraw until Hamas fully disarms.

## Netanyahu's statement

"The [Trump] administration sent us a draft. We disagreed with it. This is not our draft. We sent our comments… That is our position," Netanyahu said in a video address posted on social media, [Euronews reports](http://ru.euronews.com/2026/08/05/netanyahu-says-no-withdrawal-from-gaza), on August 5.

"President Trump and his team believe they can achieve the disarmament of Hamas and the demilitarization of Gaza. We are studying this... we sent our comments. By the way, we sent them before any media campaign on this began, because that is our position. We firmly stand up for our interests — and, I believe, we do so wisely and decisively," he added.

According to Netanyahu, the Israel Defense Forces will continue "doing whatever is necessary to protect ourselves, our territory and our citizens," and Hamas's disarmament must precede all other steps, including a troop withdrawal.

## Board of Peace and Hamas position

The statement came a day after Board of Peace director Nikolai Mladenov met with Netanyahu in West Jerusalem. The two discussed friction over the disarmament roadmap, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/board-of-peace-says-no-israeli-withdrawal-from-gaza-before-hamas-disarms). The Board of Peace insists on a different sequence of steps: "The withdrawal of Israeli forces behind the yellow line will happen only after disarmament is complete, as Hamas has committed [to do]," the body said in a statement. Its stated goal is "the complete disarmament of weapons in the territory and a transition from rule by force to civilian governance."

At the meeting, Mladenov called on Netanyahu to halt strikes on the Strip. Since October 2025, Israel has killed more than 1,200 Palestinians, according to the Board of Peace, including 36 people after the disarmament agreement was announced on July 30. The overall death toll in Gaza since October 2023 has surpassed 73,000, Al Jazeera notes.

Ghazi Hamad, a member of Hamas's negotiating delegation, called the movement's agreement to disarm a step necessary to save Gaza, according to a video [cited by Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/4/hamas-negotiator-says-deal-to-disarm-was-necessary-to-save). Hamas insists the agreement will not be implemented until Israel halts its strikes and begins withdrawing troops from occupied territories.

## Split within Israel

Netanyahu's office said the published version of the agreement "does not reflect Israel's position." Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called the document "completely different" from the version approved by the government and demanded a new cabinet vote, Al Jazeera reports. Euronews also notes that Netanyahu faces a difficult Knesset election in October — a tough stance on Gaza strengthens his position ahead of the vote.

## What's next

On August 3, LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-03/hamas-disarmament-deal-gaza-strikes-continue) that Hamas and mediators — Egypt, Qatar, Turkey and the United States — agreed on a disarmament plan on July 30, which Israel has not officially confirmed. Under the terms of the agreement reached during the October truce, Israel was to withdraw its forces behind the so-called yellow line and hand control of the Strip to the Palestinian administration with the participation of an international stabilization force. Israel currently controls a significant part of Gaza's territory beyond the line stipulated by the truce terms. Netanyahu's statement means Israel is postponing the withdrawal of troops from positions occupied after October 2025 until Hamas's disarmament is complete, rather than carrying it out in parallel, as the document had envisioned. Neither the Board of Peace, Hamas, nor Israel has yet named a new timeline for resolving the disagreements.
