Marvell Technology on August 18 gave Google a warrant to purchase up to 58,970,907 shares of common stock at $206.58 per share — part of an expanded agreement on custom chip development. The terms of the deal [are disclosed in a Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001835632/000119312526356217/d412696d8k.htm) filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) on August 19. If fully exercised, the warrant is worth about $12.2 billion at Marvell's current share price, [Reuters reports](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/marvell-gives-google-option-to-buy-122-billion-stake-in-custom-chip-deal-4867472).

Of the 58.97 million shares, 1,360,867 vest in equal quarterly installments during the first year after the agreement was signed. The remaining shares are allocated across 240 tranches of $500 million in Custom Products revenue each. The tranches vest through the end of Marvell's fiscal year 2033, [the document states](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001835632/000119312526356217/d412696d8k.htm).

The agreement covers development for Google of "artificial intelligence (AI) inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers, memory controllers and near-memory compute," according to the Form 8-K. The document was signed on August 19 by Mark Casper, Marvell's executive vice president, general counsel and secretary. The warrant is valid through August 18, 2033.

## What This Means for Marvell

If Google meets all the procurement targets tied to the warrant, Marvell's revenue from Custom Products could reach about $120 billion by fiscal year 2033 — an estimate cited by [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/marvell-gives-google-option-to-buy-122-billion-stake-in-custom-chip-deal-4867472). The estimate is based on the warrant's vesting structure: 240 tranches of $500 million in revenue each.

Marvell shares rose nearly 8% on news of the deal, according to Reuters. [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/marvell-google-ai-chips.html) the gain reached as much as 10%. "This is a big win for Marvell," said Morningstar analyst William Kerwin, quoted by Reuters.

Demand for custom AI chips is growing as companies seek cheaper alternatives to Nvidia's graphics processing units, Reuters notes. Marvell and Google signed a commercial agreement on chip development on July 29, 2026 — the warrant was structured as part of that deal.
