Nvidia on August 10 [announced](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-partners-with-apollo-blackrock-blackstone-brookfield-goldman-sachs-and-kkr-to-establish-ai-compute-infrastructure-financing-platforms-to-mobilize-over-500-billion-of-third-party-capital) a partnership with six Wall Street investment firms — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR — to finance AI data centers worth more than $500 billion.

The companies signed memoranda to establish independent financing platforms for AI compute infrastructure. The platforms are meant to mobilize third-party capital "over time" — the release gives no exact timeline or schedule.

Nvidia's press release does not disclose how much each of the six partners will invest. It also does not specify the ratio of debt to equity capital in the deal structure.

"In AI, compute is revenue. Nvidia compute is uniquely suited for this role," Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said, according to the press release.

BlackRock Chairman and CEO Larry Fink said the partnership "deepens BlackRock's relationship with Nvidia." Apollo President Jim Zelter called modern compute capacity "a scarce, mission-critical asset class."

By August 2026, Nvidia had already struck deals worth hundreds of billions of dollars with companies across the AI ecosystem, [the Financial Times reported, as cited by the South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/article/3363577/nvidia-team-wall-street-us500-billion-package-ai-infrastructure-projects). According to the outlet, this is fueling concern among some investors that the company may be inflating demand and valuations across the industry through the circular nature of such deals. In these arrangements, Nvidia both supplies chips and participates in financing its own customers.

Nvidia has not issued an official response to these concerns in the sources reviewed.
