An accident at Kyrgyzstan's Toktogul hydropower plant on August 14 caused power outages in several Central Asian countries — in Uzbekistan, Surkhandarya region was left without electricity for about an hour.

## What happened at Toktogul HPP

At 14:37 on August 14, two hydro generators with a combined capacity of 600 MW shut down at the Toktogul hydropower plant in Kyrgyzstan, [Interfax reported](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1109391), citing Kazakhstan's system operator, Kazakhstan Electricity Grid Operating Company (KEGOC), a joint-stock company. According to KEGOC, this overloaded the "North-East-South" transit line of Kazakhstan's Unified Energy System and caused the Southern zone to separate from both Kazakhstan's power system and the Central Asian Unified Power System, which includes Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan.

In [KEGOC's official press release](https://www.kegoc.kz/ru/press-center/press-releases/168624/), the cause is described differently and without mentioning Toktogul HPP: the technological disruption was accompanied by the shutdown of the 500 kV "Agadyr—Ulken" and "Aktogay—Taldykorgan" transmission lines, and the company attributes it to a "power surge from the Central Asian Unified Power System." According to KEGOC, the volume of restrictions in Kazakhstan's power system totaled about 2,810 MW. [Interfax](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1109391) cited KEGOC for the full list of affected regions — Almaty, Zhambyl, Turkistan, Kyzylorda, Zhetysu, Karaganda, Ulytau and Abai. By 16:26, [KEGOC restored](https://www.kegoc.kz/ru/press-center/press-releases/168625/) parallel operation of its Southern zone with the Central Asian Unified Power System.

## How the outages affected Uzbekistan

At 10:57, [Uzbekistan's Ministry of Energy said](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20737) that an accident in the neighboring country's power grid had affected the Central Asian power system and caused power outages in Surkhandarya region and a number of other regions of Uzbekistan. On the evening of August 14, when the outages first became known, the Ministry of Energy did not specify which country's power grid the accident had occurred in — the cause was named only by Kazakhstan's KEGOC and Interfax the following day.

By 11:32, [power supply in Surkhandarya was restored to 80%](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20738), and by 12:22 [the Ministry of Energy declared the remaining affected regions of the country fully restored](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20739) — in that statement, the ministry gave the accident's start time as 14:38. Power supply in Surkhandarya [was fully restored by 15:38](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20740) — meaning the outage in the region lasted about an hour.

Electricity was out the longest in Surkhandarya specifically because of the way the region's power supply is configured: the region receives power via two 500 kV lines — one from the neighboring country, the other from Kashkadarya region — and at the time of the accident one of the lines was undergoing scheduled maintenance, [the Ministry of Energy said](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20740).

## How a blackout was avoided

According to the Ministry of Energy, emergency automatic protection systems stopped the accident from spreading through Uzbekistan's power grid, while the resulting power imbalance and drop in grid frequency were offset by energy storage systems (BESS) operating in the country — they promptly fed reserve energy into the grid without starting additional generating capacity, [the ministry said in its statement](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20740).

## What's next

[KEGOC said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1109391) a special commission would determine the causes of the technological disruption in Kazakhstan's power system. As of publication, there have been no official statements about the formation of a similar commission or an investigation into the causes by Uzbekistan's Ministry of Energy or the Kyrgyz side.
