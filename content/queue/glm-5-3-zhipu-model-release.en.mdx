Chinese startup Z.ai (Zhipu AI) opened access to GLM-5.3 on August 19 at $1.4 per million input tokens and $4.4 per million output tokens. [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/glm-5-3-tops-the-open-model-rankings-and-undercuts-rivals-on-price-but-its-release-is-delayed/) and [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/glm-5-3-hits-the-api-at-1-4-4-4-per-million-tokens) reported the news. The model is available through an API — the programming interface that lets third-party applications connect to a neural network. Tokens measure the volume of text a model processes. The price undercuts rival open models — neural networks whose developers publish their parameters publicly.

According to Artificial Analysis, which independently tests language models across dozens of tasks, GLM-5.3 scored 60 points on the Intelligence Index. The result matched the Kimi K3 model and beat the previous GLM-5.2 version by 7 points, [The Decoder reported](https://the-decoder.com/glm-5-3-tops-the-open-model-rankings-and-undercuts-rivals-on-price-but-its-release-is-delayed/).

On the GDPval-AA v2 agentic benchmark, GLM-5.3's score rose from 1,524 to 1,770 Elo points, according to the same data. Elo is a rating system that compares models through head-to-head matchups. Artificial Analysis estimated the cost of completing one task at $0.68. That's 19% cheaper than Kimi K3 ($0.84), but one and a half times more expensive than GLM-5.2 ($0.44).

Z.ai positions GLM-5.3 as an open model. The company is expected to publish the neural network's parameters (weights) — that would let developers run it on their own servers instead of only through Z.ai's paid access. The company delayed the release of the weights by about two weeks after the model's August 14 debut. According to The Decoder, the developer attributed the delay to additional security review — the model turned out to be too effective at finding vulnerabilities in code.

## Claim of a vulnerability found in Cursor

According to several outlets, Z.ai unveiled the debut version of GLM-5.3 on August 14 with an emphasis on autonomous vulnerability discovery. A Z.ai employee who runs the Lou account on X said the model was tasked with reverse engineering. It found a potentially serious vulnerability in the Cursor code editor, [VentureBeat reported](https://venturebeat.com/technology/glm-5-3-is-here-with-advanced-cyber-capabilities-and-reportedly-already-found-a-serious-vulnerability-in-cursor). The company notified Cursor's developers and is working with them on a fix.

As of August 19, there is no independent confirmation from Cursor or outside security researchers. No CVE number — the identifier assigned to vulnerabilities in the international registry — has been assigned to the claim, so for now it remains Z.ai's own assertion rather than a confirmed fact.
