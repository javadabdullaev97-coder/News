A unified palliative and hospice care system will begin operating in Uzbekistan on September 1, 2026, and is meant to cover at least 80% of the population by 2030. The Telegram channel of the Ministry of Health of Uzbekistan (Ministry of Health) [reported](https://t.me/ssvuz/22422) this, citing a presidential decision from June 24.

## How the System Will Work

The first mobile palliative care teams will begin operating in the Mirzo Ulugbek district of Tashkent, visiting patients with cancer and other severe diagnoses at home. This [is stated](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9299) in a report on the president's website about a June 11 meeting where proposals for reforming the oncology and palliative care service were discussed. Hospices are planned to be established in stages across all regions of the country as needed, with an interregional hospice in Tashkent to serve as the lead institution for methodological oversight of the new system.

A separate palliative care center for children will open in the Samarkand region. Patients and their families are expected to be provided with functional beds for home care through a voucher system. A unified electronic information system will be created to identify, register and support people in need of palliative care. The specific name of the platform and the number of mobile teams are not specified in the presidential website material.

The Ministry of Health's Telegram channel named the Republican Specialized Scientific and Practical Medical Center of Oncology and Radiology as responsible for implementing the initiative. Details on the rollout were provided by Yakhyo Ziyaev, an employee of the center.

## A Broader Reform of Cancer Care

The unified palliative care system is part of a broader package of measures [discussed](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9299) at the June 11 meeting. Among the target indicators for the coming years:

- population coverage with targeted screenings for common cancer types — up to 60%;
- five-year survival rate for cancer patients — rising from 35% to at least 45%;
- early-stage detection of hematological diseases in 2026-2030 — from 30-40% to 60%, with coverage of patients with hematological diagnoses reaching up to 100%;
- reducing the time between diagnosis and transplantation — from 6 to 3 months.

The national cancer control program is planned to be rolled out in stages starting in 2027.

Medical workers in the palliative and oncology care system with at least 15 years of experience have been offered compensation for their children's education expenses and help with mortgage down payments. These measures were also discussed at the June meeting.

## What's Next

The unified palliative and hospice care system is set to launch on September 1, 2026. The target population coverage — at least 80% — is set for the period through 2030; the Ministry of Health has not yet disclosed a phased timeline between these dates.
