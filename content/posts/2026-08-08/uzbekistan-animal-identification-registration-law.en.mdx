The Law "On Identification, Registration and Tracking of Animals" took effect in Uzbekistan on August 7, 2026, but the article of the law instructing the Cabinet of Ministers to determine the procedure for free registration remains unfulfilled so far.

## What Has Already Been Established

The Law [ZRU-1079](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7676785) requires individuals and legal entities to register cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, camels, dogs and cats in an electronic system — with different deadlines for each species, from 14 days after birth for livestock to 4 months for horses. LEAP News examined the law's requirements in detail in a story published August 1. The authorized body is the Committee for Veterinary Medicine and Livestock Development under the Ministry of Agriculture of the Republic of Uzbekistan; registration itself is handled by the Center for Animal Identification, Registration and Monitoring set up under the committee.

## Free or Not

The question of payment is the key point of divergence between the Oliy Majlis's statement and the text of the law. Official sources give two different answers to it.

When the law was adopted, the Oliy Majlis [said](https://parl.uz/oz/articles/economics/hayvonlarni_identifikatsiya_qilish_ro_yxatga_olish_va_kuzatish_yuzasidan_qonun/) that the document provides for the identification and registration of animals without charging owners. At the same time, the law's own article on financing (part 4 of article 9) lists "owners' own funds, charitable donations, funds of the State Budget" and other sources not prohibited by legislation as sources of funding — meaning it does not explicitly rule out payment out of the owner's pocket.

As LEAP News reported August 1, an earlier [presidential resolution No. PP-285](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/6583139) dated August 24, 2023, provided for the rollout of the identification system "free of charge," but only in districts specializing in livestock farming — first in 25 of them in 2024–2025, then in the rest in 2026–2027, with a budget of $33 million. This provision does not extend to the entire country or the entire period.

## What's Next

The law instructs the Cabinet of Ministers of the Republic of Uzbekistan (Cabmin) to separately determine the procedure and stages of free registration — part 5 of article 9 of the law. As of the law's entry into force on August 7, 2026, no such resolution had been published separately: specific deadlines and stages for free registration are not yet available in open sources.

Fines for violating identification rules are set out in a separate article of the law, but their exact amounts could not be found in open sources. Lex.uz also lists a second version of the law, which will take effect January 1, 2028; which provisions it changes is not yet clear.
