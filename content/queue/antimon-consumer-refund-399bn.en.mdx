The Competition Development and Consumer Rights Protection Committee [said in a Telegram post](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12603) on August 20 that from 2023 through July 2026 it recalculated 399.3 billion soums in favor of 11.7 million consumers. According to the committee, it received 137,785 complaints during that period.

At the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/) for August 21, 2026 (11,847.16 soums per dollar), that is about $33.7 million. These are excess charges. Suppliers of housing and utility services, transport, communications, retail and other sectors billed consumers above what was due. Following Committee inspections, the suppliers recalculated the charges in consumers' favor.

The number of complaints grew every year: 20,691 in 2023, 30,070 in 2024, 53,670 in 2025, [according to the committee's publication](https://t.me/RaqobatGovUz/12603). In the first seven months of 2026, the committee received 33,354 complaints — more than in all of 2023.

The three main categories, according to the committee: 68,326 complaints concerned housing and utility services and transport, 39,397 concerned retail and catering, and 25,965 concerned communications, financial and social services. The total for these three categories is 4,097 less than the overall number of complaints: the agency did not detail the remaining categories.

The committee reported on the results of the first half of 2026 on August 1: 23,200 complaints and nearly 59 billion soums returned, [LEAP News wrote](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/06/raqobat-consumer-refunds-h1-2026). The figure of 10.5 million people refers to that first half alone, while the 11.7 million consumers figure refers to the period since 2023. How these figures relate to each other does not follow from the committee's publications: the agency does not disclose its counting methodology.
