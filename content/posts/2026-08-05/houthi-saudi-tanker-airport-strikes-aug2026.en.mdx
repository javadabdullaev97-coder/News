Yemen's Houthi movement said on August 5 that it struck the Saudi oil tanker Wafa off the coast of Yanbu in the northern Red Sea with ballistic missiles — the eighth such strike since July 22.

## Strike on the Tanker Wafa

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree [said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107567) the tanker Wafa was hit by several ballistic missiles off Yanbu, a Saudi port on the Red Sea. The strike, he said, hit its target precisely. The spokesman gave no information on casualties or an oil spill, [Kommersant reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8862853), citing AFP.

According to Saree, Wafa was the eighth Saudi tanker hit by the Houthis since July 22, when the series of strikes on Saudi-flagged vessels began. Another 29 tankers were forced to change course or turn back, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107567). The Houthis declared a maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia on July 20.

The movement's spokesman described what is happening as implementing a "blockade for blockade" formula. The Houthis said operations against Saudi oil vessels in the northern Red Sea would continue and intensify. The goal, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/yemen-s-houthis-say-they-ll-target-tankers-in-northern-red-sea), is to "close off all outlets" and prevent tankers from passing through.

## Attack on Najran Airport

A day earlier, on August 4, the Houthis [said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107383) they struck a "significant target" with a drone at Najran airport in southwestern Saudi Arabia. The Houthi spokesman cited violations of Yemeni airspace by Saudi drones over the provinces of Saada and Hajjah as the reason for the attack. The Houthis gave no details about the nature of the target or the consequences of the strike, [Interfax reports](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107383), citing Al Jazeera.

As of publication, Saudi Arabia had not officially confirmed or commented on the strikes on the tanker or the airport.

## Risks to Shipping and the Oil Market

The Houthis explained the shift of strikes to the northern Red Sea by saying vessels had started bypassing the previous strike zone near the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait to the south of the route, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/yemen-s-houthis-say-they-ll-target-tankers-in-northern-red-sea). The outlet notes that the expanding geography of the strikes creates additional risks for global shipping and energy markets.

Saudi Aramco has said the blockade declared by the Houthis has not yet led to a reduction in oil export volumes. Saudi Arabia is working on contingency plans in case of further escalation, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-05/yemen-s-houthis-say-they-ll-target-tankers-in-northern-red-sea).

## What's Next

The Houthis warned that strikes on Saudi tankers in the northern Red Sea would continue and intensify. As of publication, Saudi Arabia had issued no official response to the strike on the tanker Wafa or the attack on Najran airport.
