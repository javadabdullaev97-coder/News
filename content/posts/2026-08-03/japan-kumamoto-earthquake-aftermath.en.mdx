A magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck southern Kyushu in Japan on July 28 — Kumamoto Prefecture authorities confirmed on July 31 that 34 people had died, with more than 9,000 residents still in evacuation shelters.

## What Happened on July 28

The quake was [recorded](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/japan-kumamoto-earthquake-what-happened-damage-victims-latest-updates) at around 4:30 p.m. local time in Kumamoto Prefecture in southern Kyushu. According to the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA), the magnitude was 7.1; the U.S. Geological Survey later estimated it at 6.8. Authorities issued a tsunami warning immediately after the quake, which was later lifted.

The earthquake triggered an explosion at the Aeon Mall shopping center in the city of Kashima, collapsing the building's second floor. A smokestack collapsed at the Nippon Paper Industries plant in the city of Yatsushiro, and residential buildings were destroyed in affected areas, [PBS NewsHour reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/quake-survivors-in-southwestern-japan-sleep-in-cars-in-sweltering-heat-as-death-toll-rises-to-34).

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi [said](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/japan-kumamoto-earthquake-what-happened-damage-victims-latest-updates) the day after the earthquake: "Even now there are people waiting to be rescued, and this is a race against time."

## Casualties and Damage

Kumamoto Prefecture authorities [confirmed the deaths of 34 people](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/news-wrap-death-toll-from-japan-earthquake-rises-to-34) on Friday, July 31; one additional death was being investigated for a link to the earthquake at the time of publication. At least 96 people were injured, six of them severely, [PBS NewsHour reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/quake-survivors-in-southwestern-japan-sleep-in-cars-in-sweltering-heat-as-death-toll-rises-to-34).

Most of the deaths are linked to two sites. At the Nippon Paper Industries plant in Yatsushiro, 11 people were rescued from under the collapsed smokestack and nine died. The explosion at the Aeon Mall shopping center in Kashima has left seven confirmed dead — authorities have not disclosed the number of people missing. The earthquake damaged or destroyed more than 1,500 buildings, 179 of them completely.

About 80,000 households in the region remained without water at the end of the week, [PBS NewsHour reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/quake-survivors-in-southwestern-japan-sleep-in-cars-in-sweltering-heat-as-death-toll-rises-to-34); power supply in the affected areas was fully restored by Friday evening.

## Life in Evacuation Shelters

More than 9,000 people [are sleeping in evacuation shelters](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/quake-survivors-in-southwestern-japan-sleep-in-cars-in-sweltering-heat-as-death-toll-rises-to-34), where authorities are installing additional power sources for air conditioners. Some survivors prefer to sleep in their cars for privacy but face a shortage of fuel to run air conditioning. Daytime temperatures reach as high as 36 degrees Celsius — authorities have issued an emergency heat warning.

One survivor, Haruto Kubo, has been living in his car since the earthquake: he drives out for food during the day and sleeps in the car at night, [PBS NewsHour reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/news-wrap-death-toll-from-japan-earthquake-rises-to-34).

## What's Next

[The Japan Meteorological Agency warned](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/japan-kumamoto-earthquake-what-happened-damage-victims-latest-updates) that strong aftershocks are possible within a week of the main shock, with the highest risk in the next two to three days. The Aeon Mall shopping center in Kashima, where the explosion occurred, had opened just a month before the earthquake. The building had been rebuilt after the 2016 Kumamoto earthquakes, [Al Jazeera notes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/japan-kumamoto-earthquake-what-happened-damage-victims-latest-updates).
