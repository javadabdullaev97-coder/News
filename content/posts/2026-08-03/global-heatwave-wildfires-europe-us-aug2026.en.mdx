Wildfires and record heat swept Europe and the western United States in early August: two helicopter pilots died in a collision near Athens on August 2, and damage across Europe topped €3.1 billion.

## Greece: Helicopter Collision and Casualties

Two Bell helicopters collided in midair on August 2 near Psatha, west of Athens, as crews battled a wildfire fanned by strong winds. One helicopter crashed and caught fire. Two people were killed — a Greek national and a Danish national — while two others (a Greek and a Briton) aboard the second helicopter survived, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/firefighting-helicopters-collide-near-athens-as-greece-battles-wildfires).

About 500 firefighters and 23 aircraft were deployed to fight the main blaze in Attica. Earlier this week, two firefighters died battling fires on Crete and another on the Peloponnese. Fires were also burning at the same time on the island of Kefalonia and in Halkidiki, in the north of the country. Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis was briefed on the firefighting effort, [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/europe-wildfire-crisis-helicopter-collision-greece-athens/).

Local farmer Minas Tzortzanis described the aftermath of the Attica fire this way: "A pristine forest, a paradise, was given over to the fire. Destruction. Complete destruction," [CBS News quotes him as saying](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/europe-wildfire-crisis-helicopter-collision-greece-athens/).

## France and Spain: Hundreds of Thousands Evacuated

In France and Spain, wildfires have forced more than 325,000 people to leave their homes and vacation spots since the start of the year. In France, about 115,000 hectares of forest have burned since the beginning of the year, and roughly 220,000 people have been evacuated. In Spain, 119,458 hectares had burned as of July 22 (more than 150,000 hectares since the start of the year), about 100,000 people have been evacuated, and another 30,000 were ordered to shelter in place. At least 12 people died in a fire in Andalusia on July 10, and on July 24 Spain declared a nationwide state of emergency, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/wildfires-in-southwest-europe-what-we-know-so-far).

## Damage to Europe's Economy Tops €3.1 Billion

According to Financial Times calculations based on data from the European Commission and the French government, damage from wildfires and heat in Europe this season has reached €3.1 billion. Over the first two months of the season, France, Spain, Portugal, Greece and Romania were hit hardest, with almost 0.5 million hectares burned in those countries. Over the past decade, damage above €3 billion was recorded only once, in 2021; in 2025 it stood at about €0.5 billion, while the EU's average annual figure is €2.5 billion, [Kommersant reports](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8861186).

The EU's Copernicus climate change service recorded that June 2026 was the hottest June in Western Europe since records began, with an average temperature of 20.74°C — 3.05°C above the 1991-2020 baseline. In Germany, the Koschen station recorded 41.7°C on June 28, and Germany's national weather service called the event "historic," with 252 stations across the country setting all-time temperature records. In Vienna, the temperature reached 40.0°C, a new June record for Austria, [the World Meteorological Organization reports](https://wmo.int/media/news/record-breaking-heat-spreads-through-europe).

## Hungary's Paks Nuclear Plant Shut Down Amid Danube Drought

Record-low water levels on the Danube have forced Hungary to fully shut down its only nuclear plant, Paks, for the first time in its 44-year operating history. The plant supplies almost half of the country's electricity. Water levels at the plant fell below the critical threshold of 134 cm on August 2, below the 2018 record of 98 cm; the plant's output dropped from its usual 2,000 MW to 240 MW during nighttime hours. Prime Minister Peter Magyar announced the shutdown on August 2, and Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico said his country was closely monitoring the situation and stood ready to help its neighbor's power grid, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/hungary-plans-to-shut-down-only-nuclear-power-plant-amid-drought).

## United States: Wildfires in Washington State

In the western United States, around Spokane, Washington state's second-largest city, three wildfires are burning simultaneously — Old Trails, Fairview and Autumn Lane. The fires have destroyed at least 600 homes, businesses and other structures: according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spokane-washington-wildfire-evacuations-old-trails-fire/), about 60,000 people have been evacuated from the city and its suburbs, while [PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/eastern-washington-fires-have-burned-600-structures-and-forced-the-evacuation-of-5000-homes) puts the evacuation at about 5,000 households — the two outlets use different units of measurement, residents versus households. None of the fires had been contained as of publication.

Governor Bob Ferguson declared a state of emergency on Saturday, August 1. Spokane Mayor Lisa Brown called the situation "the most severe natural disaster our region has faced," [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spokane-washington-wildfire-evacuations-old-trails-fire/). Benjamin Cossel, a spokesperson for the federal response team, said, "We have both weather and terrain working against us," [PBS NewsHour reports](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/eastern-washington-fires-have-burned-600-structures-and-forced-the-evacuation-of-5000-homes). No official figures on deaths or injuries were available as of publication.
