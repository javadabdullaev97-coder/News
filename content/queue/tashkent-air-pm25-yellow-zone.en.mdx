Air quality in Tashkent entered the "yellow" zone on the morning of August 15: the AQI reached 66, with PM2.5 concentration 3.4 times above the World Health Organization's (WHO) annual average guideline, according to the [IQAir monitoring service](https://www.iqair.com/ru/uzbekistan/toshkent-shahri/tashkent).

## What IQAir showed

According to IQAir data as of 10:00 on August 15, the concentration of fine particulate matter PM2.5 in Tashkent stood at [17 µg/m³](https://www.iqair.com/ru/uzbekistan/toshkent-shahri/tashkent) — 3.4 times above the WHO's annual guideline of 5 µg/m³. The AQI (on the US scale used by IQAir) equals 66 and falls into the "moderate" category — on the service's color scale, this is the "yellow" zone: air quality is acceptable for most people but may affect sensitive groups — children, the elderly, and people with chronic respiratory and cardiovascular conditions.

## Uzhydromet: exceedance recorded for PM10, not PM2.5

Separately, [Uzhydromet](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21982) — the Agency of Hydrometeorological Service under Uzbekistan's National Committee for Ecology and Climate Change — published its own state bulletin the same day. According to automated monitoring stations, as of 09:00 on August 15, the concentration of PM10 particles in Tashkent was 89 µg/m³ — 1.18 times above the maximum permissible concentration (MPC) set for Uzbekistan at 75 µg/m³. PM2.5 concentration, according to the same data, was 20 µg/m³ — within the national standard of 60 µg/m³.

The discrepancy with IQAir's PM2.5 data (20 µg/m³ from Uzhydromet versus 17 µg/m³ from IQAir) is likely explained by different measurement stations and close but not identical time points — 09:00 for Uzhydromet versus 10:00 for IQAir. IQAir compiles its index from a network of 67 stations run by different operators, including government and community sensors.

The PM10 exceedance recorded by Uzhydromet is not a one-off. According to the same channel, the national PM10 standard has been violated in Tashkent for a third consecutive day: on [August 13](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21963) at 09:00 the reading was 78 µg/m³ (1.04 times the limit), and on [August 14](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21972) at 09:00 — 97 µg/m³ (1.29 times the limit). PM2.5 concentration remained within the national MPC throughout this period.

## Two different standards, not a contradiction

The reports use two non-matching methodologies, and this is not a discrepancy about the same fact but two different ways of measuring.

Uzhydromet compares one-off (daily) readings against the national maximum permissible concentration (MPC) — Uzbekistan's sanitary standard designed for short-term exposure. By this standard, only PM10 exceeded the limit on August 15; PM2.5 was within range.

IQAir calculates the AQI and compares PM2.5 concentration to the WHO's annual average guideline of 5 µg/m³ — a standard designed for chronic, year-long exposure and stricter than any one-off MPC. By this standard, Tashkent's one-off PM2.5 concentration on August 15 was several times above the WHO's long-term guideline, even though it did not exceed Uzbekistan's daily standard.

Uzhydromet, the Ministry of Ecology and the Ministry of Health had not published any official health recommendations or restrictions related to air quality as of August 15.
