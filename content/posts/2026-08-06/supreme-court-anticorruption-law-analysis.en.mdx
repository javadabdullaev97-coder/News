The Supreme Court of Uzbekistan on August 5 [published](https://t.me/oliysuduz/51349) an analysis on its Telegram channel of a law that for the first time bars early release for people convicted of bribery — the document has been in force since June 23, 2026.

The analysis was prepared by Sherzod Tukhtaev, a civil case judge of the Kashkadarya Regional Court, and Khusan Tursunov and Nodir Khodjakulov, economic case judges at the same court. The law itself was [signed](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026) by President Shavkat Mirziyoyev on June 22 — it is Law No. O'RQ-1155, "On Introducing Additions and Amendments to Certain Legislative Acts of the Republic of Uzbekistan Aimed at Increasing the Effectiveness of the Fight Against Corruption and Ensuring the Inevitability of Liability for Corruption Crimes." It amends three codes — the Criminal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Code of Administrative Liability — and two laws: on state youth policy and the 2017 law on combating corruption.

## Ban on early release

The key novelty is the legal concept of "corruption crimes" introduced into the Criminal Code, with a closed list of offenses: bribery, abuse of power or official authority, mediation in bribery, and related grave articles of the Criminal Code, according to the [text of the law](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026).

For those convicted under this list, the law rules out parole and the replacement of a sentence with a lighter form of punishment — previously such an option was available on general grounds, as it is for most other crimes. This is the "inevitability of liability" referenced in the law's title: people convicted of these crimes must now serve their court-ordered sentence in full.

## Electronic registry and compliance units

The law requires an electronic registry of people convicted of corruption crimes — the corresponding provision has been added to the law on combating corruption. It also requires state bodies, organizations and enterprises with state participation to set up compliance units — internal control divisions tasked with identifying corruption risks and monitoring compliance with anti-corruption procedures. The law assigns coordination of their work to the level of state bodies and establishes guarantees of independence and protection from pressure for compliance unit staff.

## Risk maps and new powers for the Anti-Corruption Agency

The law expands the powers of the Anti-Corruption Agency: its new functions include [compiling and updating corruption risk maps](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/8282286?ONDATE=23.06.2026) by region and industry, setting up national and regional anti-corruption councils, conducting systemic analysis of the corruption situation, and developing social partnership programs to build zero tolerance for corruption in society.

## What else the law changes

A separate provision of the Code of Criminal Procedure establishes security measures for people who report corruption offenses. The law also introduces into the Code of Administrative Liability a fine — up to 30 basic calculation values — for exceeding the permissible difference between the starting price and the actual market price in public procurement. The Law on State Youth Policy gains a new area of focus — building zero tolerance for corruption in society.
