The Pharmaceutical Product Safety Center under the Ministry of Health [said](https://t.me/uzpharmcontrol/4683) on August 14 that an inspection of Tashkent pharmacies found 86 drugs without compliance certificates and another 5 with expired shelf life.

## What the Inspection Found

According to the center, some of the violations involved not only individual drugs but also the operation of the sales points themselves. Some pharmacies continued serving customers even though their pharmaceutical activity license had expired. The Ministry of Health's Telegram channel [republished](https://t.me/ssvuz/22517) the same report.

The agency described the situation not as a routine trade violation but as a risk to public health. Drugs without compliance certificates and expired drugs may not meet their stated quality and safety characteristics.

The center did not name the specific pharmacies where violations were found or give the exact date of the raid. It also did not say what measures — fines, license suspensions or the removal of drugs from circulation — were taken following the inspection.

## What Comes Next

The Pharmaceutical Product Safety Center is a state agency under the Ministry of Health responsible for the safety of pharmaceutical products in the country. As of publication, there have been no official statements about further inspections or final sanctions in this case.
