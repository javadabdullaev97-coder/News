Automatic Uzhydromet stations twice recorded PM10 particle concentrations above the norm in Tashkent's air on August 4 — by 21% in the morning and by 17% in the evening, while PM2.5 remained within normal limits.

## What the stations showed

The Agency of Hydrometeorology of Uzbekistan (Uzhydromet) publishes data from automatic air-monitoring stations in Tashkent twice a day. According to the [09:00 reading on August 4](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21863), PM10 concentration stood at 91 µg/m³ — exceeding the maximum permissible concentration (MPC) of 75 µg/m³ by a factor of 1.21. Finer PM2.5 particles measured 25 µg/m³ — within the norm (60 µg/m³).

By the [18:00 reading](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21869), PM10 had dropped to 88 µg/m³ but still exceeded the norm — by a factor of 1.17. PM2.5 at the same hour stood at 23 µg/m³, within the norm.

A day earlier, on August 3, readings were lower and did not exceed the norm: PM10 was [66 µg/m³ in the morning and 64 µg/m³ in the evening](https://t.me/uzgydromet/21852), PM2.5 stood at 17 µg/m³ at both readings.

## Air Pollution Index remains low

Uzhydromet assesses overall air pollution not only by PM10 but by the Air Pollution Index (API) — a composite indicator covering several substances. The agency's [interactive monitoring map](https://monitoring.meteo.uz/ru/map) showed API values ranging from 0.72 to 2.57 at Tashkent observation posts during the day on August 4 — corresponding to the "low" category (range of 0–5).

The agency identifies four API categories with separate [recommendations for the public](https://monitoring.meteo.uz/ru/menu/rekomenduemye-mery-predostorozhnosti-v-zavisimosti-ot-iza-):

- "low" (0–5) — no restrictions on being outdoors;
- "elevated" (5–7) — children, older adults and people with increased sensitivity are advised to reduce time outdoors and the intensity of physical activity;
- "high" (7–14) — vulnerable groups should avoid prolonged outdoor activity;
- "very high" (14 and above) — the agency recommends that everyone reduce outdoor activity and wear masks when leaving indoor spaces.

Some local Telegram channels reported a more pronounced exceedance of the norm during this period and described the city's air as "yellow." No such term appears in Uzhydromet's published reports — the agency provides only numerical concentration values and the API.

## What's next

Uzhydromet publishes data from automatic stations in Tashkent twice a day — at 09:00 and 18:00. Current readings by city district are available on the agency's [interactive monitoring map](https://monitoring.meteo.uz/ru/map).
