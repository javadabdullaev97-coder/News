Xiaomi reported on August 18 that its net profit for the second quarter of 2026 fell 20.3% year-on-year to 9.46 billion yuan (about $1.4 billion). The company attributed the drop to rising memory prices and intensifying competition.

The company's revenue fell 6.1% to 108.9 billion yuan ($16.1 billion), according to [Xiaomi's report to the Hong Kong Stock Exchange](https://ir.mi.com/static-files/4a85fc36-8a6d-4c24-b45b-b18d5d162e6c). Adjusted net profit — a measure that excludes one-off items such as share-based payments — plunged 42.6% to 6.22 billion yuan.

For the first half of 2026, Xiaomi's revenue totaled 208.1 billion yuan versus 227.2 billion a year earlier — an 8.4% decline. Profit for the period came to 14.2 billion yuan versus 22.8 billion a year earlier — a 37.6% decline.

In the report, the company links the decline to rising prices for key components, including memory, and intensifying competition. Gross margin in the smartphone segment fell to 8.5% from 11.5% a year earlier. Device shipments dropped 26.5% to 31.2 million units. The average smartphone price rose to a record 1,351 yuan (+25.9%).

## What Xiaomi said about AI

On the August 18 investor earnings call, Xiaomi Chief Financial Officer Alan Lam said the company is "in no rush" to profit from its investments in artificial intelligence. "Our AI investments are currently still in a phase of large-scale investment. However, as a large corporation, Xiaomi is in no hurry for immediate monetization," Lam said, according to [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-evs/article/3364458/xiaomi-no-rush-turn-vast-ai-spending-profits-despite-earnings-slump).

According to him, Xiaomi's research and development spending in the first half rose 25.6% year-on-year to 18.2 billion yuan ($2.7 billion). About 30% of that went to AI. The exchange filing itself puts it more cautiously: the artificial intelligence industry "has entered a phase of rapid development." Xiaomi, per the filing's wording, is focusing on "fundamental technological capabilities, including AI," to develop its "Human × Car × Home" ecosystem.

Xiaomi President Lu Weibing said pressure on the business will persist, according to South China Morning Post. "Memory prices remain high, competition remains fierce. But short-term pressure will not change our long-term strategy," he said.

## EVs grow, smartphones get cheaper

The electric vehicle, AI and other new-business segment brought Xiaomi 24.9 billion yuan in revenue at a 19.2% gross margin. EV deliveries in the second quarter rose 28.2% to 104,199 units, generating 23.9 billion yuan in revenue. The smartphone and AIoT devices segment brought in 84.0 billion yuan at a 20.0% margin.

This is the second consecutive quarter in which Xiaomi's net profit has fallen year-on-year: in January-March 2026 it dropped 56.5%.
