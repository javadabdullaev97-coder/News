President Shavkat Mirziyoyev on August 14 held a meeting with heads of Uzbekistan's judicial system, approved the draft "Justice – 2030" strategy and signed a decree on its implementation.

## What the strategy provides for

At the meeting, [the draft](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9498) of the strategy for developing court operations through 2030 was presented. The document was drafted based on public appeals, dialogue with scholars, practicing lawyers and attorneys, and foreign experience. The strategy sets out **57 tasks**, while the action program for its implementation for 2026-2028 includes **160 specific measures**. It also establishes **33 goals** to be achieved by 2030.

At the meeting it was noted that the outcome of the reforms should be that "a citizen can turn to the court without unnecessary difficulties, have a violated right restored in a timely manner and be confident in the fairness of the decision made."

## New courts and review of rulings

To improve the objectivity of reviewing lower court rulings, five interregional courts will be established starting **July 1, 2027**. Regional courts will retain their appellate and cassation instances, while the powers of the revision instance will be transferred to the interregional courts. This is meant to eliminate the practice of a court reviewing its own rulings and reduce the number of citizens' appeals to courts in the capital.

Civil courts, which handle everyday matters, will be gradually established in nearly all districts and cities of the country by 2030. All courts will offer the ability to file appeals and receive digital services under a "single window" principle. If an application falls under the jurisdiction of another court, it will not be returned to the citizen — the court will forward it to the appropriate venue itself.

## Jury trials and the "reasonable suspicion" standard

The strategy envisions increasing to at least three the number of crime categories tried with a jury.

To strengthen human rights guarantees at the investigation and inquiry stage, a "reasonable suspicion" (Prima Facie) standard will be introduced. When considering a sanction, the investigating judge will be able to verify the legality of detention, the sufficiency of grounds for suspicion or for filing charges, and request additional materials.

## Economic disputes and digitalization

To resolve economic disputes more quickly, a "proactive management of economic cases" standard will be introduced — meant to spare entrepreneurs from going from one authority to another and resubmitting documents.

The "Digital Court" concept will be fully implemented in all courts, and the my.sud.uz portal and its mobile app will be upgraded. The strategy envisions raising the share of electronic appeals to at least 50% and ensuring audio recording of 100% of court hearings. The number of users of the Supreme Court's information systems is also planned to increase at least fivefold.

To ensure uniform judicial practice, an open "Register of Precedents" with rulings of the Supreme Court's Presidium will begin operating. Starting in 2028, a judicial administration institute is planned to be introduced, freeing judges from organizational functions unrelated to the administration of justice.

## What's next

The president approved the draft strategy and signed the corresponding decree — its number and full text had not yet been posted on the lex.uz portal at the time of publication. The meeting also reviewed a sample uniform for court staff and proposals for improving constitutional court proceedings and upgrading the qualifications of judicial personnel abroad.
