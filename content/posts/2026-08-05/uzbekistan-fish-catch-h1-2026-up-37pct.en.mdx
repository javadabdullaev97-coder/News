Farms of all categories in Uzbekistan caught 75,300 tons of fish in January-June 2026 — up 37.1% from the first half of 2025, the National Statistics Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan (National Statistics Committee) [said](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69613-zbekistonda-yarim-jilda-75-3-ming-tonna-bali-ovlandi-3). Based on the published growth rate, the catch in the first half of 2025 amounted to about 54,900 tons.

## Khorezm and Namangan lead the catch

The National Statistics Committee [provided](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69613-zbekistonda-yarim-jilda-75-3-ming-tonna-bali-ovlandi-3) a breakdown by 13 regions. Khorezm region caught the most fish for the half-year — 12,600 tons. It was followed by Namangan (8,400 tons), Tashkent (7,500 tons) and Andijan regions (7,100 tons).

Next by volume were Syrdarya (6,500 tons), Jizzakh (6,000 tons) and Fergana regions (5,800 tons), Karakalpakstan (5,400 tons), Navoi (5,200 tons) and Samarkand regions (4,100 tons). The smallest catches were in Bukhara (2,900 tons), Surkhandarya (2,400 tons) and Kashkadarya regions (1,500 tons).

## Fish farming within the agricultural sector

By the value of its output, fish farming remains a small part of the agricultural sector. In January-June 2026, the total volume of goods and services in agriculture, forestry and fisheries [amounted to](https://stat.uz/ru/press-tsentr/novosti-goskomstata/69536-ishlo-rmon-va-bali-chilik-kh-zhaligi-4-7-foizga-sdi-3) about 271 trillion soums (≈$22.6 billion at the [Central Bank's rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-08-04/) of August 4 — 11,989.46 soums per dollar). That is 4.7% more than in the first half of 2025. Crop and livestock farming accounted for 254.7 trillion soums, forestry for 14.3 trillion soums. Fish farming accounted for 1.9 trillion soums (≈$158.5 million).

The 4.7% growth reflects the sector's output value in current prices. This is not the same indicator as the 37.1% growth in physical catch volume — the two figures measure different things over the same period.
