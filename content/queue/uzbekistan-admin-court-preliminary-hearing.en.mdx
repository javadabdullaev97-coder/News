President Shavkat Mirziyoyev on August 14 [signed Law ZRU-1165](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8405608), which introduces preliminary hearings in Uzbekistan's administrative courts — according to its lex.uz record, it took effect on the day of publication.

## How the preliminary hearing works

Under new Article 140¹ of the Code of Administrative Court Proceedings, a preliminary hearing is held for disputes over decisions, actions or inaction by state bodies, citizens' self-government bodies and their officials. The grounds are that these decisions or actions do not comply with legislation and violate the rights of citizens or legal entities. A judge must hold the hearing [no later than 20 days](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8405608) from the date the application reaches the court — this period is not included in the overall deadline for preparing the case for trial.

At the hearing, the judge clarifies the claimant's demands and the respondent's objections, points out deficiencies in the application and explains to the parties what evidence must be submitted. The judge also gives a preliminary legal assessment of the claims — a final opinion on the merits of the case cannot be given at this stage. The parties are notified of the time and place of the hearing by registered letter or another means of communication that confirms notification; the judge draws up minutes based on the results.

Based on the results of the preliminary hearing, the court issues one of three rulings: leave the application without consideration, approve a settlement between the parties, or schedule a trial. If the parties fail to appear, no minutes are drawn up and the judge proceeds under the general rules for case preparation.

## Mandatory attendance of state body representatives

The law adds a new Article 148¹ to the code: when a complaint against a decision or action (inaction) of a state body is being reviewed, the attendance of that body's official or its representative at the hearing becomes mandatory. If the representative fails to appear, the proceedings are postponed. If the court finds the absence unjustified, it may impose a court fine on the official. The court may hear the case without the state body's representative only when their absence does not prevent a comprehensive and full resolution of the dispute.

## State fee refunds and other changes

The law [amends](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8405608) the Law "On the State Fee": if an application is left without consideration following a preliminary hearing, the state fee paid is refunded to the applicant. A similar rule applies to cases where insolvency proceedings have been opened against the respondent and the claim, by law, must be considered within those proceedings.

The law adds Article 11¹ on "legal protection of trust" to the Code of Administrative Court Proceedings. This is a principle under which the law protects a person's good-faith belief in the legality of a state body's decision or an official's action. Administrative courts also gained the right to hear cases of administrative offenses uncovered during the review of an administrative case. These are offenses under Articles 180 and 181 of the Code of Administrative Liability. Certain provisions of the law merge inter-district, district and city economic courts into inter-district economic courts. These amendments are purely terminological — they are made to the Code of Criminal Procedure, the Code of Economic Procedure and the Law "On Courts".

## What came before

The preliminary hearing institution in administrative courts had been in preparation for more than a year. Under [Resolution PP-33 of January 30, 2025](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7351827), the president instructed the Supreme Court, together with the Ministry of Justice and the Prosecutor General's Office, to develop a concept for the future law. The concept included a preliminary hearing of up to 20 days, mandatory attendance of state body officials at hearings, and protection of trust in the lawful acts of state bodies. The Legislative Chamber passed the bill on February 10, 2026, the Senate approved it on May 18, and the president signed it on August 14.
