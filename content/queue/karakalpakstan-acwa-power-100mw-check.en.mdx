A government commission led by Deputy Prosecutor General Dilmurod Kasimov and Deputy Prime Minister Jamshid Khodjaev inspected progress on ACWA Power's 100-megawatt wind energy project in Karakalpakstan on August 8. The Prosecutor General's Office of Uzbekistan [reported](https://t.me/uzbprokuratura/87933) the visit.

## What the Commission Inspected

The delegation visited Karauzyak district in the Republic of Karakalpakstan, where ACWA Power (Saudi Arabia) is building the wind power station. The commission [reviewed](https://t.me/uzbprokuratura/87933) construction progress and also examined investment projects and the operations of exporting enterprises in the region as a whole, including the launch of equipment imported from abroad.

Following the visit, the commission ordered that the station be brought into full and timely operation. In the prosecutor's office statement, this is worded verbatim as «tegishli ko'rsatmalar berildi» ["relevant instructions were given"] regarding the timely and full launch of the project, [the statement said](https://t.me/uzbprokuratura/87933).

## Project Parameters

The station's capacity is 100 megawatts, and annual output once fully operational is expected to reach 328 million kWh of electricity, the Prosecutor General's Office [said](https://t.me/uzbprokuratura/87933). The station is expected to supply electricity to an additional 113,000 households, and generate roughly 10 billion soums (≈$840,000 at the Central Bank of Uzbekistan's August 7 exchange rate of 11,915.64 soums per dollar) in budget revenue.

## Context: ACWA Power's Regional Partnership

President Shavkat Mirziyoyev [launched](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/5006) construction of the republic's first ACWA Power wind station, with a capacity of 100 megawatts, in the Beruniy district of Karakalpakstan on February 24, 2022. At the time, the project was valued at $108 million in foreign direct investment, and the company offered a tariff of 2.56 cents per kilowatt-hour — the lowest among 20 tender participants.

According to [data](https://acwapower.com/en/what-we-do/projects/karatau-wind-ipp/) on ACWA Power's official website, a project of the same capacity — Karatau Wind IPP, which the company's website places in Nukus, Republic of Karakalpakstan — is valued at $117 million in investment, with electricity purchased under a 25-year agreement by JSC National Electric Grid of Uzbekistan. The company's investor materials date the station's entry into commercial operation to 2025. The prosecutor's office names Karauzyak district as the site of the visit, while ACWA Power's website lists Nukus for a project of the same capacity — the sources name different districts, and it has not been established whether the facility inspected by the commission is the same as the already-announced "commercial launch" of Karatau Wind IPP.

In total, according to the presidential administration, three wind power stations with a combined capacity of about 1,800 megawatts were planned to be built with ACWA Power in Karakalpakstan over five years.
