President Shavkat Mirziyoyev [announced](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9510) a package of business support measures on August 18 at an open meeting with entrepreneurs in the Khorezm region — halving fines and introducing a three-year moratorium on small business inspections.

The president regularly holds such meetings with entrepreneurs in an open dialogue format. The Telegram channel of the Presidential Press Service also [reported](https://t.me/shmirziyoyev/35206) on the new initiatives.

"We are announcing a three-year moratorium on any inspections within small business operations that are not related to causing harm to human health or the interests of other entrepreneurs," Mirziyoyev [said](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9510).

He also [said](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9510) that fines of all kinds will be reduced by an average of half. A different mechanism applies to medium and large enterprises. If a business undergoes a voluntary preventive audit and the tax authority accepts its findings, an entrepreneur who has corrected the violation is exempt from the fine. Repeat inspections within a year afterward are allowed only with the business ombudsman's permission.

The president explained why some inspecting agencies are more interested in fines than in correcting violations. According to him, 51 agencies have the right to fine businesses, and part of the fine goes to the agency itself. Some officials operate on the principle: "if inspected, they'll get scared; if scared, they'll pay."

Half of all government services for entrepreneurs will switch to the "silence means consent" principle, Mirziyoyev [said](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9510). If an application is not reviewed within the established deadline, the request is automatically considered resolved in the entrepreneur's favor.

He also announced the automatic return of deposits for accepted goods in government procurement — without the need for separate applications from suppliers.

As of August 21, no decree or resolution formalizing these measures had been published in open sources.
