Rights groups Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International and Lebanon's Legal Agenda said on August 6 that the Israeli airstrike that killed journalist Amal Khalil in southern Lebanon on April 22 bears the hallmarks of a war crime.

## What the rights groups found

Khalil, a correspondent for the Lebanese newspaper Al-Akhbar, and camerawoman Zeinab Faraj had taken shelter in a building in the village of Al-Tiri after an Israeli strike hit a car in front of their vehicle. Two men were killed, [according to](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/08/lebanon-deadly-israeli-attack-on-journalist-amal-khalil-must-be-investigated-as-a-war-crime/) a statement by Amnesty International. Israeli drones circled the site for the next two hours, followed by two more strikes — on the journalists' car and on the building where they were sheltering.

"The drones came very close, flying through the breach in the roof — less than a meter away," Amnesty International [quoted](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/08/lebanon-deadly-israeli-attack-on-journalist-amal-khalil-must-be-investigated-as-a-war-crime/) Zeinab Faraj as saying. Faraj suffered a skull fracture, injuries to her leg and eye, and a crushed hand. Khalil died at the scene.

The Lebanese Red Cross, through a monitoring mechanism involving UNIFIL, requested a safe corridor for evacuation as early as 3 p.m., but was not cleared to approach the site until after 5 p.m. — more than an hour after the second strike and about half an hour after the third, Human Rights Watch [wrote](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/08/06/lebanon-israels-killing-of-journalist-an-apparent-war-crime).

"The continued killing of journalists by Israeli forces shows a willingness to commit atrocities without fear of consequences," Human Rights Watch Lebanon researcher Ramzi Kaiss [said](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/08/06/lebanon-israels-killing-of-journalist-an-apparent-war-crime). He said "Israeli forces knew or should have known that Faraj and Khalil were civilians but struck anyway."

Lebanon's Legal Agenda concluded that the Israeli military's actions "constitute war crimes and grave breaches of international humanitarian law," Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/rights-groups-call-israels-killing-of-journalist-amal-khalil-a-war-crime), citing the organization's statement.

## Israel's position

The Israeli military said the strikes targeted "Hezbollah militants," expressed condolences over the journalist's death and said it was conducting a "preliminary review" of the incident, [according to](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/08/lebanon-deadly-israeli-attack-on-journalist-amal-khalil-must-be-investigated-as-a-war-crime/) Amnesty International. The military did not respond to detailed questions from the rights groups about the course of the strikes or the role of the drones.

## Context: escalation continues amid Rome talks

The rights groups' findings were published amid a new wave of Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon: on August 5, the Israeli military carried out a series of strikes on the region and ordered the evacuation of the village of Al-Mansouri, accusing Hezbollah of violating the ceasefire after two of its soldiers were killed in an explosion in the village of Majdal Selem, LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/06/israel-lebanon-strikes-ceasefire-violation). The strikes coincided with the seventh round of U.S.-mediated talks between Lebanon and Israel in Rome on implementing the June framework agreement — the withdrawal of Israeli forces from southern Lebanon and the disarmament of Hezbollah — the outlet [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/05/lebanon-israel-rome-talks-round7-2026).

Since the start of 2026, Israeli strikes have killed 9 Lebanese journalists (according to the Committee to Protect Journalists) and at least 8 Palestinian journalists (according to the Palestinian Journalists' Syndicate), Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/rights-groups-call-israels-killing-of-journalist-amal-khalil-a-war-crime).
