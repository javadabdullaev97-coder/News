Starting with the 2026/2027 academic year, Uzbekistan has introduced an education loan of up to 20,000 dollars a year for study at QS/THE top-300 universities — the Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Innovations [announced](https://t.me/eduuz/50007).

## Who can get the loan and on what terms

The legal basis for the mechanism is [Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 88 of March 2, 2026](https://lex.uz/docs/8070240) "On measures for financial support of study abroad for youth and teaching staff," which took effect on March 4, 2026.

Uzbek citizens applying for bachelor's or master's programs at a foreign university ranked in the QS or THE top-300 can apply for the loan:

- young people — up to and including 30 years old;
- teaching staff — with no upper age limit. Borrowers over 30 additionally need an employment record extract confirming teaching experience.

The maximum loan amount is up to 20,000 US dollars a year (about 240 million soums at the Central Bank's official rate on August 4). The text of the resolution does not appear to set a limit for the entire period of study — the cap applies only per academic year.

Interest on the loan accrues from the date the funds are transferred to the university. The borrower starts repaying the principal from the seventh month after officially completing their studies — that is, after a six-month grace period — and pays it off over 7 years.

## Rate and collateral

Funding for the loans comes from a specially created Education Loan Financing Fund under the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The Fund places funds with selected commercial banks as deposits — at a rate 4 percentage points below the Central Bank's key rate. As of August 4, the Central Bank's key rate [stands at 14%](https://cbu.uz/ru/monetary-policy/publications/press-releases/4256870/) — meaning the cost of funding for banks is currently around 10% a year. The resolution does not directly fix the final interest rate charged to the borrower — it is determined by the loan agreement with a specific bank.

The program's collateral is a guarantee deposit: 50% of the funds placed by the Fund with a bank serve as collateral. A co-borrower — a family member or another person with steady, verifiable income — may additionally be attached to the loan agreement.

Not just any bank can participate — only those meeting the resolution's requirements: a rating no lower than B on the Fitch or S&P scale (or B2 on Moody's), regulatory capital of at least 1 trillion soums, positive audit opinions for the past three years, and no arrears on state loans.

## How to apply

The resolution does not provide for a competitive selection process or quotas for applicants — the decision to issue the loan is made by the bank based on whether the applicant meets the program's conditions. The application is submitted directly to one of the selected commercial banks, not through a government agency.

The basic package of documents includes:

- a loan application;
- a copy of the passport;
- a university admission letter (for first-year students) or an academic transcript (from the second year onward);
- the study contract and an invoice with the university's payment details;
- a co-borrower's income statement for the past 12 months.

The bank reviews the documents according to its internal procedures, and the Fund confirms that the application meets the program's conditions within 5 business days.

The ministry and the Fund have not yet published the list of specific participating banks or the timeline for accepting applications.
