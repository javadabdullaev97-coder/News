The Namangan Region hokimiyat [announced](https://t.me/Namanganpress/7) on July 26 plans to build a new bridge over the Katta Namangan canal at the intersection of the D-110 highway and Oromgoh Street, replacing a narrow crossing about 1 meter wide.

## Project Parameters

According to the [channel](https://t.me/Namanganpress/7), the new structure will be 16 meters wide and 24 meters long. The hokimiyat estimated the project's cost at 6 billion soums (about $495,800 at the [Central Bank of Uzbekistan exchange rate](https://cbu.uz/ru/arkhiv-kursov-valyut/json/USD/2026-07-24/) for July 24, 2026 — 12,101.84 soums per dollar, the latest published figure before the post went out).

The hokimiyat links the need for construction to reducing congestion and improving traffic safety at the intersection of the D-110 road and Oromgoh Street.

The publication does not give a start or completion date for the construction work, the contractor, or the funding source — the regional budget, the national budget, or another source. As of publication, the hokimiyat had not disclosed these details.

## Context: Regional Infrastructure Investment

The Katta Namangan canal and the surrounding road network have already appeared in a broader regional development program. In September 2025, during a visit to Namangan Region, the program [mentioned](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/8494) lining 7 km of the banks of the Chartaksay and Katta Namangan canals with concrete, using 30 hectares of land for the work. The same program cited a 480-meter bridge over the Norin costing 63 billion soums and the reconstruction of a 180-meter, 63-year-old bridge over the Karadarya — the presidential website does not give a cost for the reconstruction. These projects are unrelated to the D-110/Oromgoh Street intersection: they are separate projects within the region's broader road and water infrastructure development program.

## What's Next

The hokimiyat has not published a construction schedule for the new bridge and had not reported the start of work as of publication. LEAP News will follow the project's progress.
