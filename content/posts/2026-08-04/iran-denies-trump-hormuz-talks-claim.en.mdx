Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said on August 3 that Tehran is not holding direct talks with the US on the Strait of Hormuz — contradicting Trump's claim that they had begun.

## Trump: talks are underway, this is Iran's last chance

US President Donald Trump said on August 3 that talks with Iran are already [underway](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/trump-blasts-duplicitous-iran-after-tehran-denies-it-is-in-talks-with-the-us), calling the situation Tehran's "last chance" before strikes resume. According to Trump, the talks began at Iran's own request and with the support of Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar.

The statement came a day after Trump [wrote](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-02/trump-halts-iran-strikes-hormuz-deal) that he was halting new strikes on Iran contingent on a quick deal on the strait.

## Tehran: no talks with Washington, only with Oman

At a briefing for journalists, Baghaei [denied](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/trump-blasts-duplicitous-iran-after-tehran-denies-it-is-in-talks-with-the-us) Trump's claim of direct talks with the US. According to him, Iran is in dialogue with "Oman, countries of the region and countries beyond it" to reduce tensions, and is separately discussing with Oman the creation of a temporary route for safe shipping through the strait.

"As long as the naval blockade and US actions continue, nothing will substantially change in the Strait of Hormuz," Baghaei said.

This clarifies, but does not reverse, Tehran's earlier statement. On August 2, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said talks with Oman on a new route had [reached their final stage](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-03/iran-oman-hormuz-talks-final-stage). That referred to an arrangement with Muscat on a specific stretch of ship passage — not to opening the entire strait at Washington's demand, and not to direct talks with the US.

The context of the dispute is a memorandum of understanding that the US and Iran signed in June with Pakistan's mediation; the document envisaged opening the strait and resuming shipping, but fell through in July over disagreements on routes, [LEAP News reported earlier](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-02/trump-halts-iran-strikes-hormuz-deal).

## Trump calls Iran's leadership "duplicitous"

In response to the denial, Trump wrote on Truth Social that Iran's leadership is "unbelievably duplicitous." According to him, Tehran asked for talks to end the war and then publicly stated that no talks were underway, Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/trump-blasts-duplicitous-iran-after-tehran-denies-it-is-in-talks-with-the-us).

The Russian business outlet Kommersant also [recorded](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8861513) Tehran's denial the same day, August 3: according to the outlet, Trump spoke of resumed talks, while Iran's Foreign Ministry said no talks with the US were underway.

## Markets react to conflicting signals

The S&P 500 index [rose](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/4/us-stocks-near-record-high-oil-falls-as-trump-claims-iran-talks-underway) 1.5% on Monday, August 3, nearly reaching the record high of 7,620.90 points set on June 2. Brent crude fell by about 5% the same day on investor hopes for de-escalation.

On Tuesday morning, August 4, at 07:05 GMT, Brent [recovered](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/4/us-stocks-near-record-high-oil-falls-as-trump-claims-iran-talks-underway) part of that drop — quotes rose more than 1% from Monday's closing price, to 84.65 dollars a barrel. The rise reflects uncertainty after Tehran denied Trump's claim of direct talks.

## What's next

Neither Washington nor Tehran has named a date or format for a possible next meeting. Iran continues to name only Oman as its negotiating partner, while the US insists dialogue with Tehran is underway. The strait remains under the [US naval blockade](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-03/iran-oman-hormuz-talks-final-stage) in place since July 14.
