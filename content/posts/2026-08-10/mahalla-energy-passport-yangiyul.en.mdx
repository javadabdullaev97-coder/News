The Ministry of Energy of Uzbekistan is introducing a "mahalla energy passport" — a database on the energy supply of residential neighborhoods that was piloted in the Yangiyul district of the Tashkent region following a presidential directive issued on July 27, 2026. The ministry's Telegram channel [reported](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20710) the move.

## What the passport contains

According to the description on the Ministry of Energy's channel, the [mahalla energy passport](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20710) records the number and composition of electricity and gas consumers in each neighborhood and their consumption volumes. The passport also accounts for available network capacity, the technical condition of infrastructure, and the possibility of connecting new subscribers. The goal is to give local energy companies and hokimiyats a unified picture for planning repairs and identifying losses at the level of a specific mahalla rather than the district as a whole.

The system is tied to the decision of the July 27 [video conference](https://president.uz/oz/lists/view/9456) on the fuel and energy sector. President Shavkat Mirziyoyev called Yangiyul district exemplary and ordered a complete metering system to be built there covering all 51 mahallas in the district. The work must be completed by October 1, 2026, after which the approach is to be extended to all districts and cities of the republic.

## Audit in Yangiyul: first figures

The practical content of the future passport comes from a comprehensive inspection that the Ministry of Energy [published](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20695) for Yangiyul district: the district has 41,874 gas consumers in total, of which 41,176 are households and 698 are wholesale consumers. During the inspection of 51 mahallas, 1,305 households, 38 enterprises and 3 social facilities were registered.

At the same time, 86 gas distribution units were modernized (14 were dismantled), 12.21 km of gas networks were reconstructed and another 1.43 km were repaired. Inspectors checked 1,671 gas meters, restored data transmission from 975 devices, and examined the internal gas and heating systems of 9 apartment buildings.

From 303 gas debtors, 1 billion 85 million soums (about $90,800 at the Central Bank's exchange rate for August 10) were collected. During inspections, the prosecutor's office found 11 cases of illegal gas use totaling 18,565 cubic meters, with damage estimated at 55.6 million soums (about $4,700). Another 1,128 residents across 51 mahallas received guidance on economical gas use.

## The experience is being extended to Fergana

Building on the Yangiyul model, the Ministry of Energy [launched](https://t.me/minenergy_uz/20711) a training seminar in Kushtepa district, Fergana region. The goal is to introduce the "Tumanbay" and "Mahallabay" systems in the region's cities and districts, which manage energy supply at the district and mahalla level, respectively. Seminar participants exchanged experience in identifying energy supply problems and resolving them quickly on the ground — the channel did not specify how many mahallas were covered.

## What's next

Following the [meeting](https://president.uz/oz/lists/view/9487) with the president on August 6, a similar accounting and management system is already being built in 13 other districts of the country based on the Yangiyul model. The goal is preparation for the autumn-winter season. In Yangiyul itself, where a special task force on electricity and gas supply is currently operating, all work to form the energy passport must be completed by October 1. After that, the approach is planned to be introduced in all cities and districts of the republic.
