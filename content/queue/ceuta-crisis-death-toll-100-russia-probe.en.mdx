The death toll from the migrant crossing from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta, which began on the night of July 31, [reached about 100 by August 6](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864102), according to Spanish and Moroccan authorities.

## Death Toll Continues to Climb

LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-05/ceuta-crisis-toll-rises-eu-solidarity-split) on the crisis on August 5, when the official toll exceeded 80 people. Since then the figure has risen: according to [data](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864102) from Spanish and Moroccan authorities as of August 6, about 100 people have died attempting to reach the enclave. Since the crisis began, 72,000 people [have entered](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864102) Ceuta, of whom 2,000 remain in the enclave — the rest have returned to Morocco.

## European Commission Finds No Link Between Crisis and Russia

European Commission spokesperson Guillaume Mercier [said](https://www.interfax.ru/world/1107914) the EC found no trace of Russian information interference before the events in Ceuta began. "Before July 30, we did not detect any information activity on their part," Mercier [said](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864102). According to him, starting July 30 certain Russian channels broadcast a simplified version of events, but were not the initiators of the crisis.

Mercier [clarified](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/06/1219337-ne-nashla-dokazatelstv) that "there is not yet a complete picture" and the European Commission continues to be in contact with Madrid. According to the EC, disinformation on social media about the conditions for crossing the border was spread primarily by human-trafficking networks — this was one of the factors that triggered the mass border crossing.

## Brussels Does Not Rule Out a Repeat

European Commissioner for Home Affairs and Migration Magnus Brunner addressed an extraordinary session of the relevant European Parliament committee via video conference. He [warned](https://www.dw.com/ru/сто-мигрантов-погибли-при-попытке-прорыва-в-испанскую-сеуту/a-78269401) that what happened in Ceuta "was capable of discrediting migration policy across the entire European Union." According to him, "it cannot be ruled out that a number of migrants will attempt to repeat such a crossing" — not only in Ceuta, but also on Italy's Lampedusa or Greece's Crete.

Italy, Finland and Denmark [called](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/8864102) for Spain's exclusion from the Schengen area. The EU Council for Justice and Home Affairs had already rejected this proposal at a meeting on August 4, which LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/posts/2026-08-05/ceuta-migrant-crisis-death-toll-75-eu-schengen) on earlier.

## Issue Reaches US Congress

The crisis has also reverberated in American politics. US Representative Thomas Massie [criticized](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/whats-behind-us-representative-massies-claims-over-crisis-at-ceuta) the wording in a House Appropriations Committee report on the HR 8595 bill. The bill provides for $40 million in security assistance to Morocco. The report describes Ceuta and Melilla as "territory administered by Spain but located on Moroccan soil" and the subject of Rabat's "longstanding claims." Massie said the wording "lends legitimacy" to Morocco's claims to the Spanish enclaves.

Spanish media outlets cited by Al Jazeera [reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/whats-behind-us-representative-massies-claims-over-crisis-at-ceuta) that Moroccan intelligence officers were present at the border, and some migrants claimed Moroccan authorities encouraged the crossing. Rabat has not officially confirmed this.

Bloomberg, in an [overview](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-06/ceuta-and-michigan-expose-political-fault-lines-playing-out-in-germany) published August 6, notes that there is still no definitive answer to what exactly triggered the mass border crossing. The issue has become one of the factors in Germany's migration debate ahead of elections in three federal states.

## What's Next

The European Commission [continues](https://www.vedomosti.ru/politics/news/2026/08/06/1219337-ne-nashla-dokazatelstv) to be in contact with Madrid to obtain a complete picture of what happened. Brussels does not rule out a repeat of similar mass border-crossing attempts in other locations.
