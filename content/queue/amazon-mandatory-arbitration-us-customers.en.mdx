Amazon on Friday, August 14, reinstated mandatory arbitration in its terms of use for US customers and barred class actions against the company. The changes took effect immediately, [Reuters reported](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/14/amazon-reinstates-binding-arbitration-bars-class-a/).

The company notified customers of the new terms by email. Continued use of Amazon's services constitutes agreement with them — no separate confirmation from users is required.

Under the new terms, almost any dispute over Amazon services or products purchased on Amazon.com must be resolved through individual arbitration rather than in court. The right to file a claim in small claims court remains — according to [Bloomberg](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/08/17/339571.htm), that option applies to disputes involving up to a few thousand dollars. Before arbitration, a customer must contact Amazon support and file a "Notice of Dispute." The parties then negotiate in good faith for 60 days — only after that can arbitration begin.

## Amazon Will Group Mass Claims Into Batches

The key difference from the previous terms is the rules for "mass arbitration." If 25 or more identical, coordinated claims on the same issue are filed within six months, it counts as "mass arbitration." Amazon can bundle such cases into batches of at least 25 instead of hearing each one separately. If more than 500 claims are filed, the minimum batch size rises to 100 cases; above 2,500 claims, it rises to 500, [the updated Conditions of Use state](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/amazon-arbitration-class-action-waiver/). An Amazon spokesperson told Reuters: "We determined that reinstating the arbitration clause would give customers a fast, inexpensive way to resolve disputes while preserving the ability to go to small claims court."

## Why the Company Dropped Arbitration in 2021

Amazon removed mandatory arbitration from its terms of use in 2021. At the time, about 75,000 users of Echo smart speakers filed individual arbitration claims over the Alexa voice assistant recording conversations without consent. Handling that volume of claims was costly for the company.

The new terms do not affect prior court disputes and class actions filed before August 14.
