Nvidia said on August 17 it would provide up to $105 billion in financing for the construction of an OpenAI data center in Pike County, Ohio, according to filings submitted to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/nvidia-investing-1-5b-in-softbank-data-center-developer-behind-openai-project/) reported the news.

The data center will be built, owned and operated by SB Energy, a company linked to Japan's SoftBank Group, under a [lease agreement](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-invest-105-billion-openai-141151330.html) with OpenAI. Nvidia's loan covers the first phase, with a capacity of 4.25 gigawatts, with an option for the remaining 3.75 gigawatts — bringing the site's total potential capacity to 8 gigawatts. The first 800 megawatts are expected to come online in 2028.

Separately, Nvidia will [invest $1.5 billion directly in SB Energy](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/nvidia-investing-1-5b-in-softbank-data-center-developer-behind-openai-project/) and will become the exclusive supplier of computing infrastructure for the site.

The arrangement — in which Nvidia simultaneously finances the construction and supplies equipment for it to OpenAI, its own customer and a company it partially controls — has raised questions about circular financing. Nvidia [clarified](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-invest-105-billion-openai-141151330.html) that OpenAI itself pays the lease, and that payments will begin only as capacity comes online.

Bloomberg independently confirmed the deal — its report, citing sources and the filed documents, was published on August 17.
