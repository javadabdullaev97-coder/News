Qatar on August 15 denied Iran's claims that it is detaining three military pilots whose Su-24 bombers Qatari air defenses shot down in March, in the early days of the U.S. and Israeli war against Iran.

Iranian Armed Forces Brigadier General Mohammad Bagherzadeh appealed to the International Committee of the Red Cross to secure the pilots' release. He said Qatar has held them without contact with their families or diplomats for six months. According to him, the pilots are Javad Salehi, Abdolmajid Dashtiyani and Omran Behraveshian, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/qatar-denies-it-is-detaining-three-iranian-bomber-pilots).

Qatar Foreign Ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari called the claims misleading. "We are surprised by the misleading statements about the Iranian pilots amid ongoing diplomatic efforts," he said in a statement [cited by Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/qatar-denies-it-is-detaining-three-iranian-bomber-pilots) and [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj4kk8kz271o).

According to Qatar, its air defenses made contact with two Iranian Su-24s on March 2 after the jets entered the kingdom's airspace without authorization. Qatar opened fire after receiving no response to warnings. The body of one of the pilots, Majid Kazemi, was found and handed over to the Iranian side, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/15/qatar-denies-it-is-detaining-three-iranian-bomber-pilots).

## The March 2 Incident

Two Iranian Su-24s were shot down on March 2 amid the escalation of the war the United States and Israel launched against Iran in late February 2026. The same day, Qatar's air defenses intercepted seven ballistic missiles and five drones Iran fired at the country's territory, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/2/qatar-says-it-downed-two-iranian-fighter-jets-as-conflict-widens) at the time. Iran's Permanent Representative to the UN, Amir Saeid Iravani, said at the time that Tehran was targeting only military sites.

General Bagherzadeh's appeal to the ICRC was Iran's first public step in the pilots' case since the March incident.
