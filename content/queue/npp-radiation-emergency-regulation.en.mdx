The Industrial, Radiation and Nuclear Safety Committee under the Cabinet of Ministers of Uzbekistan approved instructions for responding to a radiation emergency at a nuclear power plant — the [Justice Ministry registered the document](https://www.lex.uz/pdffile/-8419999) on August 19, 2026, under number 3928.

The committee adopted Resolution No. 49 on July 29, 2026; the document was coordinated with the Ministry of Emergency Situations, the Atomic Energy Agency under the Cabinet of Ministers, and the Academy of Sciences. The legal basis for the document is the [law "On the Use of Atomic Energy for Peaceful Purposes"](https://lex.uz/docs/4506948). The committee also cited presidential decree UP-224 of December 24, 2024, on additional measures to control industrial, radiation and nuclear safety.

The instructions apply at every stage of the nuclear plant's life cycle — from siting and design to decommissioning.

## What Emergency Regimes Are Introduced

The document establishes two internal, site-level regimes — regimes introduced directly at the plant itself. "Emergency Preparedness" is declared when safe operating conditions are violated or the threshold dose rate is exceeded — measured in microsieverts per hour (μSv/h). "Emergency Condition" is introduced if the dose rises to a higher threshold or the head of the operating organization decides to apply the severe accident management plan.

Threshold values differ across three zones:

| Zone | "Emergency Preparedness" | "Emergency Condition" |
|---|---|---|
| Premises with permanent staff presence | 10 μSv/h | 600 μSv/h |
| NPP site and sanitary protection zone | 2.5 μSv/h | 200 μSv/h |
| Observation zone | 0.1 μSv/h above background | 20 μSv/h |

The decision to introduce a regime is made by the head of the operating organization or, if the head cannot be reached, the station's shift supervisor.

## Who Is Responsible for the Response

The operating organization must set up in advance a scientific and technical working group, an emergency assistance group, and internal and external emergency management headquarters. The internal headquarters is located at a protected control point on the plant's territory; the external one is in a safe location outside it.

After a regime is declared, the operating organization must submit data on the radiation situation to state authorities every 12 hours. Plant staff must receive information at least once a day. Before the first nuclear fuel is delivered to the plant, a standing commission for accident prevention and fire safety must be established.

The operating organization must hold at least one full-scale emergency drill every year involving the emergency assistance group and other forces engaged in the response.

## When It Takes Effect and Who It Concerns

The instructions take effect on the day of official publication. They apply to the nuclear power plant's operating organization and all structures involved in responding to a radiation emergency — from the plant's design to its decommissioning.
