Nevada Transportation Authority approved permits for Tesla, Uber and Waymo to commercially operate robotaxis in Clark County (Las Vegas) on Thursday, August 20, TechCrunch reported. The agency regulates Nevada's transportation network companies — it had not officially confirmed the move as of publication.

Combined, the three permits allow up to [8,000 robotaxis over the next 12 months](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/tesla-uber-and-waymo-all-get-the-ok-to-operate-thousands-of-robotaxis-in-nevada/) onto the county's streets. Tesla received a cap of 5,000 vehicles, Waymo up to 1,000, and Uber also up to 1,000. Uber plans to deploy vehicles through partners Motional (a Hyundai unit) and Zoox, which already holds a separate permit for 100 vehicles, according to TechCrunch.

## From 10 Vehicles to 5,000

The August 20 approval expands on an earlier regulatory decision that [LEAP News reported on August 18](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/18/tesla-robotaxi-nevada-permit-cybercab-launch). At the time, the NTA had granted Tesla a test permit for just 10 vehicles. According to [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/08/17/tesla-nevada-robotaxi-permit-10-vehicles-las-vegas/) and [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/14/tesla-sought-permit-for-5000-robotaxis-in-las-vegas-it-got-10), that permit came with a 45 mph (about 72 km/h) speed limit. It did not cover the airport and applied only within an agreed geofence. Tesla's application had initially covered all of Clark County and requested a cap of 5,000 vehicles.

Tesla's Cybercab chief engineer Eric Early told the regulator's meeting that the figure of 5,000 vehicles "has always been a ceiling for us." The company would be satisfied with around 2,500 robotaxis, TechCrunch reported. That is the company's own estimate, not a regulatory confirmation.

## Taxi Companies Object

The Livery Operators Association, which represents the county's local limousine and taxi companies, opposed the approval. Its representative Kimberly Maxson-Rushton said the applications "raise two serious concerns," TechCrunch reported. The concerns involve oversaturation of Nevada's commercial transportation market and road congestion, particularly in the Golden Triangle area.
