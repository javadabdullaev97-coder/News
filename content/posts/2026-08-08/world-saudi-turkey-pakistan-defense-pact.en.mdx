Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed a mutual defense agreement in Mecca on August 7 — an attack on any one of the three countries will be treated as an attack on all of them.

## What was signed and by whom

LEAP News [reported](https://leap.uz/en/2026/08/07/turkey-saudi-pakistan-defense-pact-jeddah) on preparations for the meeting of the three countries' leaders the day before: at that point the sides had not officially confirmed either the format of the document or the exact location of the ceremony, which was expected to take place in Jeddah. In fact, the ceremony was held at the Al-Safa Palace in Mecca as part of a summit the sides called the Makkah Al-Mukarramah Summit for Joint Defence, [according to a press release from Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/makkah-al-mukarramah-summit-for-joint-defence). The summit was held at the invitation of Saudi King Salman bin Abdulaziz Al Saud. The Turkish and Pakistani leaders were received in Mecca by Saudi Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman. The document was signed on behalf of the three countries by Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/saudi-arabia-turkey-pakistan-defense-agreement/).

The agreement's key provision is a collective defense clause: "any armed attack against one of the three states shall be considered an attack against all," [Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs quotes the text of the document as saying](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/makkah-al-mukarramah-summit-for-joint-defence). Beyond that, the pact provides for expanding military cooperation among the three countries, joint development of defense systems, intelligence sharing and coordination in the energy sector, [Al Jazeera writes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it).

## How the sides explain the pact

Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs described the agreement as grounded in the "brotherhood and Islamic solidarity" of the three states and their "longstanding defense cooperation," [according to the ministry's press release](https://mofa.gov.pk/press-releases/makkah-al-mukarramah-summit-for-joint-defence).

A representative of the Turkish side described the pact as "purely defensive in nature" and "not directed against any particular player." Saudi Deputy Foreign Minister Rayed Krimly said the agreement "does not represent an attempt to create a military axis or a sectarian bloc," [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/saudi-arabia-turkey-pakistan-defense-agreement/).

Iranian lawmaker Ebrahim Rezaei criticized the document: "The Saudis should know that a paper agreement with Turkey and Pakistan will not bring them security," [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/turkiye-saudi-arabi-pakistan-sign-joint-defence-agreement-whats-in-it).

## Context: The US and Israel's war against Iran

The sides signed the pact against the backdrop of an ongoing military conflict between the US, Israel and Iran. On February 28, 2026, the US and Israel struck 24 Iranian provinces, killing Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei; according to Iranian media and the Red Crescent, at least 201 people died, [Al Jazeera reported at the time](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/28/irans-supreme-leader-ali-khamenei-killed-in-us-israeli-attacks-reports).

In response, Iran blocked the Strait of Hormuz to unauthorized shipping and is using its control over the strait as leverage, warning of consequences for the global economy and the US energy market. As of early August, the sides have been exchanging threats of new strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/iran-warns-against-fire-of-war-amid-us-strike-threats-on-energy-sites).
